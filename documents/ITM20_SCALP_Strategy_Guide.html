ITM20 Scalp

Mark the high and low of the first 15 minutes. When a 5-minute candle closes outside that box, wait for price to come back and touch the edge, then for one more strong candle. Buy an option one strike in the money. Take +20 on the premium, or get out at −10.

1. In one minute

  • Mark the high and low of the three 5-minute candles 09:15, 09:20 and 09:25. That is the opening range. Nothing trades before 09:30.
  • Breakout: a 5-minute candle closes above the range high, above VWAP, and EMA9 is above VWAP and rising. (For a put: below the range low, below VWAP, EMA9 below VWAP and falling.)
  • Retest: in the next 3 candles, price comes back to within 5 points of the range edge — but no candle may close back inside the range.
  • Confirmation: inside those same 3 candles, a green candle closes above the previous candle's high (red and below the previous low for a put), with the VWAP and EMA9 checks true again.
  • Buy the option one strike in the money right after the confirmation candle closes.
  • Exit the whole position at premium +20 or premium −10. Anything still open is closed at 15:15.
  • The confirmation must close by 11:30. At most 2 trades a day. Stop after a win, after 2 losses, or at −20 premium points for the day. Weekly expiry day is skipped.

Read this first. This strategy has never traded — no paper sessions and no live orders. There is no live trading for it at all: only Paper and Backtest exist. The only numbers we have come from a backtest with simulated option prices (Section 10), and that backtest lost money.

3. Step 1 — The opening range

  1. At 09:30 the bot takes the three 5-minute candles 09:15, 09:20 and 09:25.
  2. The highest high is the range high. The lowest low is the range low.
  3. If one of the three candles is missing, the bot will not trade on a guessed range. A wrong range is worse than no trade.
  4. The first candle that can break out is the 09:30 candle.

Example: on 15 Jul 2025 the three candles made a range of 25,088.45 – 25,151.15. On 1 Jun 2026 it was 23,587.75 – 23,733.7, a much wider morning.

4. Step 2 — VWAP and EMA9

Every decision candle is checked against two lines. Both must agree with the direction.

CheckFor a CEFor a PE
Candle close vs VWAPclose above VWAPclose below VWAP
EMA9 vs VWAPEMA9 above VWAPEMA9 below VWAP
EMA9 directionhigher than last candle (rising)lower than last candle (falling)
  • VWAP here is not volume-weighted, because the index has no volume. It is the plain average of (high + low + close) ÷ 3 of every candle since 09:15.
  • EMA9 is warmed up on the previous day's candles. If the previous day's data is incomplete, the bot does not trade — an EMA it cannot trust is no use.
  • The checks are made on the breakout candle and again on the confirmation candle.

5. Step 3 — Breakout, retest, confirmation

  1. Breakout. From 09:30, a candle closes above the range high (CE) or below the range low (PE), and the three checks above are true.
  2. Retest. In the next 3 candles, a candle's low comes within 5 points of the range high (CE) — or its high within 5 points of the range low (PE). The breakout candle itself does not count.
  3. Never back inside. If any of those 3 candles closes at or below the range high (CE) or at or above the range low (PE), the setup is dead. A wick back inside is fine; only the close counts.
  4. Confirmation. The retest candle itself or a later one, still inside the 3 candles, must be green and close above the previous candle's high (CE), or red and close below the previous candle's low (PE), with the three checks true again.
  5. The confirmation candle must close by 11:30.
  6. If 3 candles pass without a confirmation, the setup has expired.

What you see: 15 Jul 2025. The opening range is 25,088.45–25,151.15. At 10:35 a candle closes at 25,156.9, above the range high. VWAP (25,124.01) is below it, and EMA9 (25,129.81) is above VWAP and higher than the 25,123.04 before it: breakout (1). The 10:40 candle dips to 25,155.15 — anything at 25,156.15 or lower counts — so that is the retest (2). It is green, but its close 25,160.35 is not above the 10:35 high 25,165.15, so it does not confirm. The 10:45 candle is green too, but closes 25,167.45, just under the 10:40 high 25,168.75. The 10:50 candle, the third and last one allowed, closes 25,191.4 — above the 10:45 high 25,167.9 — with EMA9 25,152.06 above VWAP 25,130.92 and still rising: confirmation (3). None of the three candles closed back inside the range. BUY CE at 10:55 with NIFTY at 25,190.4, the 25150 strike. The simulated premium starts at ₹240 and reaches ₹260 at 11:05, when NIFTY touches 25,226.77: target. After slippage that is +18 points, or +₹1,078 on one 65-unit lot after charges. Why it matters: the confirmation can come two candles after the retest, as long as it is inside the 3-candle window.

After a setup is used up. A setup that expired, fired a signal, or ended in a trade does not reset by itself. Price must first close back inside the range; only then can a new breakout count. A setup that died by closing back inside is already inside, so the bot is ready again at once. A trade's own candles are skipped: the search starts again with the first candle that closes after the exit.

6. Step 4 — Strike, stop and target

  • When: Paper buys on the first option quote after the confirmation candle closes. The backtest uses the next candle's open — the same moment.
  • Strike: exactly one strike in the money. CE = the highest strike below NIFTY. PE = the lowest strike above NIFTY. The strike step comes from the instrument settings (50 for NIFTY).
  • Target: entry premium +20. Stop: entry premium −10. Both close the whole position.
  • Example: bought at ₹150 → target ₹170, stop ₹140.
  • The stop is checked before the target. In the backtest, a candle that touches both counts as the stop.
  • Anything still open at 15:15 is closed.

What you see: 1 Jun 2026, a put. The opening range is 23,587.75–23,733.7. At 09:40 a candle closes at 23,576.7, below the range low. VWAP (23,610.13) is above it, and EMA9 (23,600.81) is below VWAP and lower than the 23,606.84 before it: breakout (1). The 09:45 candle's high reaches 23,594.5 — back inside the range, and well past the 23,582.75 it needed — so that is the retest (2). It still closes at 23,580.2, below the range low, so the setup lives: a wick inside is fine. The 09:45 and 09:50 candles are green, so neither can confirm a put. The 09:50 close, 23,585.55, is only 2.2 points under the range low — one close at or above 23,587.75 would have killed the setup. The 09:55 candle is red and closes at 23,561.8, below the 09:50 low 23,572.05, with EMA9 23,587.93 below VWAP 23,599.44 and still falling: confirmation (3). BUY PE at 10:00 with NIFTY at 23,561.05, the 23600 strike. At 10:30 NIFTY touches 23,524.66, the simulated premium reaches ₹260: target, +18 points after slippage, +₹1,078 after charges. Why it matters: a put is the exact mirror of a call — every "above" becomes "below".

When a valid signal is still not filled (Paper)
  • No valid weekly expiry for the strike, or no option price → the entry is skipped. If the entry fails, the bot retries every 5 seconds until the next candle closes.
  • If the confirmation candle reaches the bot late, in a delayed batch of candles, it is not filled at a stale price. The setup is used up.
  • The capital pool must be able to pay for the lot.
  • The shared "wait after any exit" rule (EXIT_REENTRY_COOLDOWN_CANDLES, 2 candles by default) also applies.

7. Every way a trade ends

While a trade is open, the bot watches the option premium. It checks the stop first, then the target. At 15:15 everything is closed.

What you see: 27 May 2026. The opening range is 23,869.65–23,950.15. At 10:15 a candle closes at 23,951.95 — just 1.8 points above the range high — with VWAP 23,907.84 below it and EMA9 23,920.69 rising from 23,912.88: breakout (1). The 10:20 candle dips to 23,942.45 (anything at 23,955.15 or lower counts): retest (2). It closes at 23,953.65, still above the range, but not above the 10:15 high 23,955.6. The 10:25 candle is green and closes at 23,963.4, above the 10:20 high 23,959.05, with EMA9 23,934.51 above VWAP 23,914.4 and rising: confirmation (3). BUY CE at 10:30 with NIFTY at 23,964.8, the 23950 strike. The 10:30 candle's low, 23,947.45, just misses the stop. The 10:40 candle falls to 23,934.45, and the simulated premium hits ₹230 when NIFTY passes 23,946.63: stop. After slippage it is booked at ₹228: −12 points, or −₹868 after charges. The 10:40 candle closes at 23,936.2, back inside the range, so the bot is ready again. After one loss a second trade is still allowed. At 10:50 a new breakout closes at 23,966.95 (grey dot), but no confirmation comes in its 3 candles and it expires at 11:05. Price never closes back inside the range before 11:30, so no new breakout can count. The day ends with one loss. Why it matters: a breakout by less than 2 points, with a stop worth about 18 NIFTY points, is easily shaken out.

8. Day limits

After…What happens
a winning tradeDone for the day. Example from the engine: +18 points → "Winning trade booked — done for the day".
one losing tradeOne more trade is allowed, but its stop is tightened so the day cannot lose more than 20 points.
two losing tradesDone for the day.
−20 points for the dayDone for the day.
2 tradesDone for the day (the most allowed).

How the second stop is tightened. Normal stop = entry − 10. Day cap = entry − (20 − points already lost). The bot uses whichever is closer to the entry — it never widens the stop. Example: the first trade lost 12 points (the usual loss in the backtest, after slippage). The second trade is bought at ₹240. The normal stop would be ₹230; the day cap gives 240 − (20 − 12) = ₹232. So the stop is ₹232, 8 points away. With slippage that trade loses 10 points, so the day ends at −22 — a little worse than −20.

10. What the backtest said

These numbers come from running the backtest (runItm20ScalpBacktest) over the cached NIFTY 50 5-minute data from 1 Jan 2024 to 31 Jul 2026, with the app's own backtest settings. The option premium is SIMULATED: there is no historical option-chain data. Every trade starts at a ₹240 premium, which moves 0.55 rupee per NIFTY point (delta), minus a tiny time-decay allowance (₹10 a day spread over the 65-unit lot, about ₹0.15 of premium a day). Each trade pays 2 points of slippage (1 each way) and full Zerodha charges on one 65-unit lot. So the rupee figures show a direction, not an exact result. The profit lock was off.

In plain words: a win books +18 points (+₹1,078 after charges) and a loss books −12 points (−₹868). To break even you need to win about 45% of trades (40% before charges). The backtest won 27.2%. In the model, a −10 stop is only about 18 NIFTY points away, while +20 needs about 36 points — and more than a quarter of all trades were over inside their first candle. Every year lost money. There is no edge here on the data we have.
A warning from another strategy. In July 2026 a test on the ORB strategy found that adding a retest made ORB worse: waiting for the retest tended to pick weak breakouts and skip the strong ones that never came back. ITM20 Scalp is built around a retest, so the same thing may be happening here.

Deliberately left out. No volume rule (the index has none), no VIX filter, no OI filter, no ATR, no RSI, no ADX, no SuperTrend, no trailing stop, no partial exit, no averaging, no extra re-entries beyond the day limits. The common 10:00–14:00 trade window is not applied — this strategy's own 09:30–11:30 window is part of its rules. Adding any of these makes it a different strategy that needs its own testing.

12. Paper, Backtest & Replay

ModeWhat it does
PaperLive Fyers data, pretend orders, real option quotes for the premium. This is the official version of the rules; the other modes are made to match it.
BacktestPast 5-minute NIFTY candles. Buys at the next candle's open. Inside a candle it checks the stop before the target. The premium is simulated (Section 10).
ReplayRe-runs a recorded paper day tick by tick, so a change can be tested on a real session.
LiveDoes not exist. There is no live route and no live order code for this strategy.

Every decision reads only closed 5-minute candles. The live option price is used only for the stop and the target.

14. What's still missing

  • It has never traded. No paper session, no live order.
  • There is no live mode. Real orders cannot be placed for this strategy at all.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated. Every trade starts at the same ₹240 premium and moves 0.55 per NIFTY point. Real ITM premiums, delta and spreads change during the day, so real +20 / −10 exits will come at different NIFTY levels.
  • No historical option data. The +20 / −10 rule is about the premium, but the backtest can only guess the premium from NIFTY.
  • On the data we have, the rules lose money (27.2% wins against about 45% needed). The retest rule may be part of the problem (see the ORB warning above).
  • Next step: collect clean paper sessions, check them on /replay, and compare real premiums with the backtest's guess before trusting any number here.
