COMMODITY (MCX) — EMA_RSI_ST & EMA_RSI_ST_V2

Six paper-trading pages that run the two EMA_RSI_ST strategies on MCX crude oil, gold and silver. The bot reads the price of the commodity future in the evening, and when the rules agree it buys a Call (CE) or a Put (PE) option on that future — on paper only.

Updated 2026-10-08  ·  plain-English edition  ·  every chart is an illustration built from made-up candles at realistic prices, run through the real strategy rules — not a real trade

In one minute
  • What: the same two strategies you know from NIFTY — EMA_RSI_ST (we call it V1) and EMA_RSI_ST_V2 — copied onto three MCX commodities. Three commodities × two strategies = six pages.
  • Paper only. No real order is ever sent. There is no Live page and no Backtest page for commodities.
  • When: MCX is open 09:00–23:30. The bot only buys between 15:00 and 22:30, closes anything still open at 23:00, and switches itself off at 23:30.
  • What it watches: 5-minute candles of the commodity future. What it buys: the at-the-money option on that future, 1 lot.
  • V1 buys when the trend, RSI, SuperTrend and the candle close all agree; its stop starts at the previous candle and then rides the EMA21 line.
  • V2 buys on three simpler rules; its only stop is the SuperTrend line.
  • Separate from NIFTY: its own price feed (asked every 3 seconds), its own files, its own money pool and its own daily limits. A crude loss can never stop a NIFTY strategy, and the other way round.
  • Every page ships OFF. You switch each one on in Settings.
Read this first. These pages are new and have no track record yet. There is no backtest for commodities and no Replay. Every rupee figure in this guide comes from an illustration, not from real trading. Option prices in the examples (crude ₹165, gold mini ₹1,150, silver mini ₹2,400) are typical-looking numbers picked for teaching. Real premiums change every day.

1. What MCX commodities are

MCX is the Multi Commodity Exchange of India. On MCX you trade raw materials, not company shares. This bot covers three of them:

CommodityPrice is quoted perTypical price levelMoves most when
Crude Oil1 barrelabout ₹5,500 – ₹6,500US market hours and US oil data (Indian evening)
Gold10 gramsabout ₹70,000 – ₹78,000US data, US dollar moves (evening)
Silver1 kgabout ₹85,000 – ₹95,000same as gold, but it swings harder

Each commodity trades in two forms:

  • A future — a contract to buy or sell the commodity on a set date. Its price follows the commodity minute by minute. The bot reads the future's price to make its decisions.
  • Options on that future — a CE (Call) gains when the future goes up, a PE (Put) gains when it goes down. The bot buys options. It never sells options and never buys the future itself.

2. Words you'll see

  • Candle — one bar on the chart. A 5-minute candle shows the open, high, low and close price of those 5 minutes. A finished candle is one whose 5 minutes are over.
  • Signal candle — the finished candle on which all the entry rules are true.
  • EMA20 / EMA50 — Exponential Moving Average: the average closing price of the last 20 (or 50) candles, with recent candles counting more. EMA20 above EMA50 means the short-term trend is up.
  • EMA21 (V1 only) — a 21-candle average of each candle's (open + high + low + close) ÷ 4. V1 uses it only as a trailing stop line, never to enter.
  • RSI(14) — Relative Strength Index: a 0–100 score of recent buying vs selling over 14 candles. 50 is neutral. Above 52 = buyers in control; below 48 = sellers in control.
  • ATR — Average True Range: how far price usually moves in one candle.
  • SuperTrend — a line that sits below price in an up-trend and above price in a down-trend. It is drawn a few ATRs away from price. V1 uses SuperTrend(10, 3) to confirm the trend. V2 uses SuperTrend(10, 2) only as its stop.
  • CE / PE — Call option (profits when price rises) / Put option (profits when price falls).
  • Premium — the price of the option. You pay it when you buy and receive it when you sell. P&L comes from the change in premium.
  • ATM (at-the-money) — the option whose strike price is closest to the future's current price. A rough rule of thumb used in this guide's examples: an ATM premium moves about half as much as the future.
  • Lot / multiplier — the size of one contract. P&L per lot = premium change × multiplier (for example ×100 for crude oil).
  • Stop (stop-loss) — a price on the future where the bot gives up and sells. Trail / trailing stop — a stop that moves with the trade, closer and closer, but never backwards.
  • Trigger — the close of the signal candle. The next candle must cross it before the bot buys (see section 9).
  • Square-off — closing every open trade at a fixed time. Here: 23:00.
  • Paper trade — a pretend trade. The bot records it as if it happened, using live prices, but sends nothing to the exchange.

3. The trading hours

MCX keeps much longer hours than NSE. It opens at 09:00 and closes at 23:30. The bot uses that day in four parts:

  1. 09:00 – 15:00 · watch only. The page can run, keep candles up to date and show prices, but it does not buy. Commodity prices are usually quiet in the Indian morning.
  2. 15:00 – 22:30 · entry window. New trades are allowed. The signal candle must start inside this window. This is when Europe and then the US are trading, and commodities move.
  3. 22:30 – 23:00 · manage only. No new trades. An open trade keeps its stops.
  4. 23:00 · square-off. Anything still open is sold. Nothing is ever held overnight. 23:30 · the page stops itself and sends the day report.

What you see: one crude oil day in 30-minute candles. Grey = the bot watches but will not buy. Green = the 15:00–22:30 entry window. Amber = no new trades. Red = the 23:00 square-off and the 23:30 close. Why it matters: most of the day's big moves come after 15:00. Every commodity page follows this clock unless you change it in Settings.

Winter hours. MCX closes at 23:30 during Indian summer and at 23:55 when US clocks go back (about November to March). The bot does not switch by itself. Change CMX_SESSION_END to 23:55 in November and back in March.
  • Weekends: pressing Start on Saturday or Sunday is refused — MCX is closed.
  • After 23:30: Start is refused — the session is over for the day.
  • Auto-start (off by default): when on, every switched-on page starts by itself on weekdays between 09:00 and 23:00. It tries once every 5 minutes. A page you stop by hand stays stopped for the rest of that day.
  • Start All (Commodity): a button on the Dashboard and on the Real-Time page starts every switched-on commodity page at once.
  • Login: the NIFTY side clears the Fyers login at about 4 PM. When any commodity page is switched on, that clear waits until 15 minutes after MCX closes, so the evening session keeps its data.

4. Which contract the bot picks

MCX expiry dates do not follow a simple rule like NSE's. So the bot does not calculate them. It reads Fyers' own list of MCX contracts (the "symbol master") and picks from what is really listed.

  1. When you press Start, the bot loads the MCX contract list. It downloads it at most once a day and keeps a copy. If the download fails, it uses yesterday's copy.
  2. It looks at the commodity you trade (for example Gold Mini) and finds the nearest option expiry after today. It never trades options on their expiry day.
  3. It finds the future those options are written on. That future's 5-minute candles become the signal source for the whole session.
  4. At the moment of each entry, it picks the listed strike closest to the future's current price — the at-the-money CE or PE.
Example (crude oil). Crude options for a month expire a few days before the crude future. Say the October options expire on 15 Oct and the October future on 19 Oct. On 8 Oct the bot trades October options and reads the October future. On 15 Oct (options expiry day) it moves to the November options and reads the future they are written on.

The contract is fixed for the session. A contract change in Settings applies at the next Start. The page shows which future and which expiry it is using.

Example premiums are made-up teaching numbers. Multipliers come from the code.

How P&L is counted

P&L = (exit premium − entry premium) × multiplier × lots − ₹60 charges.

Example: crude CE bought at ₹165, sold at ₹184.08, 1 lot. (184.08 − 165) × 100 = ₹1,908. Minus ₹60 = ₹1,848.

The ₹60 is a flat estimate for brokerage and taxes on the buy and the sell together (Settings: charges per trade). It is not the exact MCX charge sheet.

The money pool

  • All six commodity pages share one paper money pool, ₹1,00,000 by default. It is separate from the NIFTY pools.
  • Before each entry the bot checks the pool can pay for it: premium × multiplier × lots. While a trade is open, that money is held. When it closes, the money is freed and the P&L is added or taken away.
  • If the pool cannot pay, the entry is refused — just like a broker rejecting an order.
A blocked trade: full Gold. If you switch the Gold pages to the full GOLD contract, one ATM lot at ₹1,150 costs ₹1,150 × 100 = ₹1,15,000. That is more than the whole ₹1,00,000 pool, so every gold signal is skipped with a "pool cannot pay" note in the log. Raise the paper capital or stay on Gold Mini.

6. How a V1 (EMA_RSI_ST) trade starts

V1 is the original EMA_RSI_ST rule set, used as-is. The bot checks it when each 5-minute candle of the future finishes. All four rules must be true on that one finished candle.

#Buy a CE (price going up) when…Buy a PE (price going down) when…
1EMA20 is above EMA50EMA20 is below EMA50
2RSI is above 52 and below 80RSI is below 48 and above 20
3SuperTrend(10, 3) is green (line below price)SuperTrend(10, 3) is red (line above price)
4the candle closes above EMA20the candle closes below EMA20
  • The 80 and 20 limits stop V1 from chasing a move that has already run too far ("overbought" / "oversold").
  • The first stop is the low of the candle before the signal candle for a CE, or its high for a PE.
  • After each candle closes, the stop moves up to the EMA21 line if that is tighter. It never moves back.
  • The bot then waits for the confirmation candle (section 9) before buying.

What you see: crude oil, V1, a CE winner. At 18:45 the candle closes at 5,903: EMA20 (5,886.9) is above EMA50, RSI is 66.9, SuperTrend is green, and the close is above EMA20. The 18:50 candle crosses 5,903, so the bot buys the CE at 5,904. The first stop (yellow) is 5,893 — the low of the 18:40 candle. From 19:15 the stop climbs with the purple EMA21 line. The premium rises from ₹165 to a peak of ₹187.50, the profit lock takes it at ₹184.08 at 20:15. Result: +₹1,848 after charges. Why it matters: this is a normal V1 trade — four rules on one candle, a tight first stop, then a trail.

7. How a V2 (EMA_RSI_ST_V2) trade starts

V2 is a simpler version of V1. It also reads finished 5-minute candles of the future. Three rules must be true on one finished candle:

#Buy a CE when…Buy a PE when…
1EMA20 is above EMA50EMA20 is below EMA50
2the candle closes above EMA20the candle closes below EMA20
3RSI is above 52 (no upper limit)RSI is below 48 (no lower limit)
  • No SuperTrend rule on entry. V2 uses SuperTrend(10, 2) only as its stop.
  • The first stop is the SuperTrend line at the signal candle. After each candle closes, the stop moves to the new SuperTrend value if that is tighter.
  • Safety check: if the SuperTrend line is on the wrong side of price (above the close for a CE, below it for a PE), the stop would already be broken. V2 then skips the trade and writes why.

What you see: crude oil, V2, a PE winner. At 19:45 the candle closes at 5,811: EMA20 is below EMA50, the close is below EMA20 (5,835) and RSI is 31.5. The 19:50 candle crosses below 5,811 and the bot buys the PE at 5,810. The first stop is the red SuperTrend line at 5,830.03. As crude falls, the stop follows SuperTrend down, candle by candle. At 22:40 crude bounces up to the stop (5,704.85) and the trade closes at 5,705. The premium went from ₹165 to ₹217.50. Result: +₹5,190 after charges. Why it matters: V2 has no target. It holds as long as SuperTrend is not touched, so a long trend pays well.

What you see: gold mini, V2. At 18:40 all three CE rules pass, but the SuperTrend line (75,917) is above the close (75,892). A CE stop above the buy price makes no sense, so the bot skips it (red arrow). The same happens at 18:50. At 19:00 the rules pass again and this time SuperTrend (75,795) is below price — the bot buys at 19:05 at 75,913 and the profit lock sells at 19:50. Result: +₹834. Why it matters: V2 never opens a trade it cannot protect.

9. The confirmation candle

Both strategies use a confirmation candle, which is on by default (the same setting as on NIFTY).

  1. The signal candle finishes. Its close becomes the trigger price.
  2. During the very next candle, the bot checks the future's price every few seconds.
  3. CE: as soon as price goes above the trigger, it buys. PE: as soon as price goes below the trigger, it buys.
  4. If that next candle ends without crossing, the signal is cancelled. A new signal needs a new signal candle.

Just touching the trigger is not enough — price must go past it.

What you see: crude oil, V2. Signal 1 closes at 17:20 at 5,831 (amber line). The 17:25 candle only reaches 5,831 — it touches the trigger but never goes above — so signal 1 is cancelled and nothing is bought. Signal 2 closes at 17:30 at 5,829. The 17:35 candle goes above 5,829 and the bot buys the CE at 5,830 (stop = SuperTrend 5,812.23). The profit lock sells at 18:25. Result: +₹1,253. Why it matters: the confirmation candle filters out signals that fade straight away, at the cost of buying a little later.

10. Every way a trade ends

While a trade is open, the bot asks Fyers for the latest future and option prices every 3 seconds. The quick checks below run on every one of those price checks. A few more checks run only when a 5-minute candle finishes.

Exits checked every 3 seconds (in this order)

ExitV1V2What happens
Profit lockyesyesOnce the premium has been 8% above the buy price, it may never be sold below +5%. After that the floor also follows the best premium: it keeps 85% of the best gain while the gain is under 20%, 80% while under 40%, and 70% above that.
Breakeven stopyesnoOnce the premium has been 5% above the buy price, the trade is sold if the premium falls back to the buy price.
Option stopyesnoSold if the premium falls 15% below the buy price.
Stop on the futureyesyesSold when the future's price reaches the stop. It is called "Initial SL" while the stop is still at its first level and "Trail SL" once it has moved.

Exits checked when a candle finishes

ExitV1V2What happens
Negative-candle stopyesnoFrom the 2nd candle close onward, if the option is worth less than you paid, the trade is closed.
Trail updateEMA21SuperTrendThe stop moves to the new EMA21 (V1) or SuperTrend (V2) value if that is tighter. It never loosens.
EMA21 touch-backyesnoIf a candle (not the entry candle) touches the EMA21 line, the trade closes at that candle's close. In practice the EMA21 trailing stop usually fires first, because it sits on the same line and is checked every 3 seconds.
Opposite signalyesyesA full signal the other way (inside the 15:00–22:30 window) closes the trade.
23:00 square-offyesyesAnything open at 23:00 is sold. Nothing is held overnight.

You can also close a trade yourself with the Exit button on the page, and pressing Stop (or the 23:30 automatic stop) closes any open trade first.

Exits are checked every 3 seconds, not on every tick. If price jumps past the stop between two checks, the trade is sold at the price of the next check. So an exit can land a little past the stop. You can make the checks faster (down to 2 seconds) in Settings, at the cost of more calls to Fyers.

1. The first stop is hit (V1)

What you see: gold mini, V1, a PE that loses. At 17:25 the candle closes at 76,497: EMA20 (76,779) is below EMA50, RSI is 20.3 (just inside V1's 20–48 band), SuperTrend is red. The bot buys the PE at 17:30 at 76,496. Its first stop is 76,562 — the high of the 17:20 candle, the candle before the signal. Gold turns up and reaches the stop at 17:55. Sold at 76,563; premium ₹1,150 → ₹1,116.37. Result: −₹396.30. The 17:55 candle also gives a fresh PE signal, but V1 refuses the same side for 3 candles after a stop-out (amber dot). Why it matters: V1's first stop is close to the entry, so losses are small — but it is hit often.

2. The trailing stop is hit (V1, EMA21)

What you see: gold mini, V1, a CE. Signal at 20:55 (close 76,813, RSI 67.1); bought at 21:00 at 76,814 with a first stop of 76,696. From 21:15 the yellow stop climbs with the purple EMA21 line, one candle at a time. The premium peaks at ₹1,239 (+7.7% — just short of the +8% profit-lock level). At 22:30 gold dips to the stop at 76,877 and the trade is sold at 76,875, premium ₹1,180.60. Result: +₹246. Why it matters: the EMA21 trail turned a trade that started with a 118-point risk into a small locked-in win.

3. The negative-candle stop (V1)

What you see: silver mini, V1, a CE. Bought at 18:15 at 91,083 after an 18:10 signal (RSI 73.1). The first stop, 90,718, is far away — the 18:05 candle was long. At the 18:15 close the option is ₹3 down, but one candle is allowed. At 18:20 and 18:25 it is in profit. At the 18:30 close it is ₹54 below the ₹2,400 paid and the trade has lived through more than 2 candles, so the bot gets out at 90,975. Result: −₹330. If it had waited for the 90,718 stop, the loss would have been roughly ₹900. Why it matters: when the first stop is far away, this rule cuts a trade that is not working.

4. The profit lock (both strategies)

What you see: the option premium of the crude V1 trade from section 6. Bought at ₹165. When the premium touches ₹178.20 (+8%) the lock arms and the trade can no longer be sold below ₹173.25 (+5%). The best premium is ₹187.50, a gain of ₹22.50 (13.6%). Under 20% gain the lock keeps 85% of it, so the green floor ends at ₹165 + 85% × ₹22.50 = ₹184.13. The premium slips to ₹184.08 and the trade is sold. Result: +₹1,848. Why it matters: once a trade is clearly winning, most of the gain is protected, but a runner is never capped while it keeps climbing.

5. The breakeven stop (V1)

What you see: the premium of a crude V1 PE bought at 18:20 for ₹165 (it is the first trade on the busy-evening chart in section 11). The premium rises to ₹173.50 — more than +5% (₹173.25), so the breakeven arms. It never reaches the +8% profit-lock level (₹178.20). At 19:00 it falls back to ₹164.80, at or below the buy price, and the bot sells. Result: −₹80 (−₹20 on the premium plus ₹60 charges). Why it matters: a trade that was up 5% is not allowed to turn into a full loss. The cost is the charges.

6. The SuperTrend stop is hit (V2)

What you see: silver mini, V2, a PE that loses. At 21:40 the candle closes at 91,337: EMA20 (92,069) is below EMA50, the close is below EMA20, RSI is 22. The bot buys the PE at 21:45 at 91,329. Its first stop is the SuperTrend line at 91,839.49 — about 510 points away. Silver jumps back up and touches it at 22:00. Sold at 91,842; premium ₹2,400 → ₹2,143.50. Result: −₹1,342.50. Why it matters: V2's stop gives the trade more room than V1's, so its losing trades are bigger. It has no option stop or negative-candle stop to cut it early.

7. The 23:00 square-off

What you see: gold mini, V2, a CE in a slow climb. Signal at 20:00 (close 76,141, RSI 73.0); bought at 20:05 at 76,142, first stop = SuperTrend 76,063.87. The stop climbs all evening but gold never comes back to it. The premium peaks at ₹1,317.50 (+14.6%), so the profit lock arms with a floor near ₹1,292 — but the premium never falls that far. No new trades are allowed after 22:30 (amber). At 23:00 the bot sells whatever is open: gold 76,466, premium ₹1,312. Result: +₹1,560. Why it matters: no trade is ever carried into the next day, even a winning one.

After an exit

  • Same-side pause: after a stop on the future or the option stop, the same side (CE or PE) is refused for 3 candles (V1) or 2 candles (V2).
  • Opposite-side cooldown: after most exits, the other side is refused for 3 candles (V1) or 2 candles (V2), so a stopped-out CE cannot flip straight into a PE. It does not apply after an opposite-signal exit, the 23:00 square-off, a session stop or a manual exit.

12. What is different from NIFTY

The entry rules are the same code as the NIFTY strategies, called without changes. Everything around them — timing, size, data and daily limits — is separate, so a commodity page can never change a NIFTY decision.

13. Where the data is kept

Everything lives in ~/trading-data/cmx/, outside the code folder, so updates never wipe it. Each page has an id such as cmx_gold_ema_rsi_st_v2.

  • {id}_paper_trades.json — every day's trades and P&L. The page's History view reads this.
  • .active_{id}_position.json — the open trade, saved after every change. After a restart on the same day, the page picks the trade up again and keeps managing it. A saved trade from an earlier day is dropped.
  • .running_{id}.json — marks the page as running. If the app restarts mid-evening (for example after a deploy), the page starts itself again about 20 seconds later — if its switch is still on.
  • MCX_COM.csv — the day's copy of the Fyers MCX contract list.

15. How to switch a page on

  1. Make sure the Fyers login is fresh (the page loads its candles from Fyers).
  2. Open Settings → Menu Visibility. Turn on the switch for the page you want, for example CMX_CRUDE_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_MODE_ENABLED. Save.
  3. (Optional) In Settings → COMMODITY, check the contract size, lots, times and money pool.
  4. The sidebar now shows COMMODITY → Crude Oil → EMA_RSI_ST_V2 Paper. Open it.
  5. Press Start on a weekday before 23:30 — or use Start All (Commodity) on the Dashboard, or turn on CMX_AUTO_START.
  6. The page shows the future it reads, the option expiry, the live price, the open trade, the activity log and a chart with the EMAs, SuperTrend and trade markers. Buttons: Stop, Exit (close the open trade), Manual CE / PE (a paper entry at the current price, same stops and limits; the first stop is the last finished candle's low for CE or high for PE), Reset and History.

Turning a switch off hides the page and blocks a new Start. A page that is already running keeps trading until you stop it, and it stays visible on the monitors until then.

16. Limits and what is missing

  • No track record. No backtest, no replay, no live trading. Judge the pages only on paper days you collect.
  • Stops are checked every 3 seconds. A fast spike can pass the stop between checks, so exits may land past the stop.
  • Fills use the last traded price. Real MCX options, especially gold and silver, can have wide gaps between buy and sell prices. Real fills would often be worse.
  • Charges are a flat ₹60. The real MCX charge sheet is not modelled.
  • The strategy settings are shared with NIFTY. Tuning RSI or SuperTrend for crude also changes NIFTY.
  • Winter close time must be changed by hand (23:30 → 23:55).
  • One trade at a time per page, at most 3 per day, always closed by 23:00.
