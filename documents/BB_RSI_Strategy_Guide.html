This guide describes V8 — and V8 trades the OPPOSITE side to V7

On 2026-08-14 BB_RSI was rebuilt as V8, a mean-reversion engine. If you learned this strategy before that date, the single most important thing to un-learn is the side:

  • Calls (CE) — V7 bought when price closed above the upper band with RSI above 70. V8 buys when price closes at or BELOW the LOWER band with RSI at or below 25.
  • Puts (PE) — V7 bought below the lower band with RSI under 40. V8 buys at or ABOVE the UPPER band with RSI at or above 75.
  • SuperTrend no longer exists in this strategy, and neither does the band-re-entry or trend-flip exit. The middle band is now the profit target, and the stop line is the signal candle's own extreme.
  • New guards refuse the sideways market: a minimum band width, a minimum recent RSI range, and an optional divergence requirement.

Everything below has been rewritten against the running V8 engine. BB_RSI.md in the repo root carries the same spec in developer form.

01 The idea in one minute

Picture a rubber band drawn around NIFTY's recent price — a stretchy upper edge and a stretchy lower edge that move with the market. Most of the time price bounces around inside that band. Once in a while it stretches far enough to close outside it. This strategy bets that such a stretch is overdone and snaps back — it trades the return to the middle, not the push outwards.

  • If a candle closes below the bottom edge and RSI is deeply oversold, we buy a CE (Call option) — a bet the drop is exhausted and price rebounds.
  • If a candle closes above the top edge and RSI is deeply overbought, we buy a PE (Put option) — a bet the rip is exhausted and price falls back.

Read those twice: the side is the opposite of what a breakout strategy would do with the same candle. Because we are fading a move, there is deliberately no trend filter — a trend tool agrees with the breakout, so it would veto every fade we want to take. What protects us instead is a pair of chop guards: the band must be genuinely wide, and RSI must have actually been moving. The target is the middle band — the mean price is reverting to. All terms are explained in Section 2.

The version running today: "V8" (mean reversion)

On 14 August 2026 this strategy was rebuilt and its direction inverted. V7 bought the break — a close above the upper band bought a Call. V8 fades that same bar: a close above the upper band now buys a Put. SuperTrend has been removed outright (a trend filter agrees with the breakout, so it vetoed every fade), and the RSI thresholds were flipped from "strong enough to continue" to "extreme enough to snap back". If you have read an older copy of this guide describing PSAR, SuperTrend, a band re-entry exit or a profit lock, ignore it — everything below describes V8. BB_RSI_DIRECTION=breakout can put the old side mapping back for an A/B test.

A word on "points". Every price on these charts is the index level itself — not rupees, and not the price of the option you buy. So "+30 points" means the index moved 30 points our way. As a rough rule of thumb the option we buy gains a little over half a point for each point the index moves, and one lot is about 65 units — so a 30-point index move is roughly ₹1,050 on one lot before costs.

For most of the session price wanders quietly inside the blue Bollinger band and there is nothing to do. One candle finally closes outside it with RSI at an extreme, the next candle confirms, and a trade is on. It lasts under an hour and ends at the middle band. Then back to waiting — BB_RSI takes small, quick bites and sits out the rest of the day.

02 Words you'll see (plain meanings)

A handful of terms come up again and again. Here's what each one means in everyday language. Keep this section handy and the rest of the guide reads easily.

TermWhat it really means
CE / PECE (a Call) makes money when NIFTY goes up. PE (a Put) makes money when NIFTY goes down. We only ever buy one of them at a time — never both.
PremiumThe price you pay for the option, in ₹ per unit. If you buy a Call at a premium of ₹120 and later sell it at ₹175, you made ₹55 per unit. Options trade in fixed "lots" (NIFTY is about 65 units per lot), so ₹55 × 65 ≈ ₹3,575 for one lot.
CandleA 5-minute price bar. It shows where NIFTY opened, where it closed, and the highest and lowest it reached in those 5 minutes. A "green" candle closed higher than it opened; a "red" one closed lower. We make decisions when a candle closes.
Bollinger Bands (BB)Two stretchy lines drawn above and below a moving average of price — like an elastic channel that widens when the market is jumpy and narrows when it's calm. Price closing outside a band is a sign of an unusually strong push (a "breakout"). We use a tight setting so the bands hug the price closely.
BreakoutWhen price shoves past a barrier — here, closing above the top band or below the bottom band. It's the spark that starts a trade.
Mean reversionThe bet this strategy makes: that a price stretched unusually far from its recent average is overdone, and is more likely to come back to that average than to keep going. "Fading" a move means trading against it on that logic.
Middle bandThe plain average line running down the centre of the Bollinger channel. It is the price the market has been hovering around lately — and it is what this strategy aims at as its profit target.
RSIA momentum meter that runs from 0 to 100. High readings (near 70+) mean strong upward push; low readings (near 40 or below) mean strong downward push; the middle is indecision. We use it to make sure a breakout has real force behind it.
Initial stop-loss (SL)A pre-set price line where, in principle, we'd give up on the trade. Here it is the signal candle's own extreme — its low for a Call, its high for a Put. It is mainly a reference for measuring risk; the real exits are the ones in Section 7.
Hard stopA last-resort safety exit measured in points. If the trade goes badly wrong — NIFTY moves 30 points against us — we bail no matter what. It's set deliberately wide so it only ever catches a genuine disaster, not normal wiggles.
Profit lockA way of protecting a gain. Once a trade is nicely in profit, we watch the peak, and if it gives back half of that peak we take the money and leave. As the peak grows, the protected floor rises with it (it "ratchets"). Explained with numbers in Section 7.
Confirmation candleOne extra candle we insist on before buying. The candle that lights up all three signals only "arms" the trade; we buy on the next candle only if it keeps pushing the same way. This weeds out fake pokes.
Bid-ask spreadThe gap between the price to buy an option and the price to sell it. A wide gap quietly eats your profit just from entering and exiting, so we skip options with a wide spread.
VIXThe market's "fear gauge." A high VIX means jumpy, expensive, unpredictable conditions. We can tell the strategy to sit out when VIX is too high.
ATM (at-the-money)The option whose strike price is closest to where NIFTY is trading right now. This strategy buys the ATM Call or Put.

03 The things we watch

Every 5 minutes, when a candle closes, the strategy re-reads two signal tools — the Bollinger Bands and RSI — plus two guards whose only job is to keep it out of a dead, chopping market. There is no trend tool. Here they are, one at a time.

1. Bollinger Bands — the stretchy channel

Setting: 30-candle average, band width 2

The middle line is the average price over the last 30 candles. The upper and lower bands sit two "standard deviations" away — a fancy phrase that just means "a typical amount of wiggle". At width 2 a close outside the band is a genuinely unusual stretch, which is exactly what we want to fade. When a candle closes below the lower band, that is the raw spark for a Call. Closing above the upper band is the spark for a Put. The middle line matters too — it is the target the trade is aiming at.

The tools on one screen — read it top to bottom. The blue channel is the Bollinger Band and the purple line in the strip underneath is RSI. (This chart is from the V7 era, so it also draws a SuperTrend line and marks the old breakout entries — V8 has no SuperTrend, and would take the opposite side at each yellow dot.) What still reads true is the rhythm: for long stretches price sits inside the channel with RSI mid-range and there is nothing to do; the strategy only wakes up when a close lands outside the band with RSI at an extreme.

2. RSI — is there real force behind the push?

Calls need RSI at or below 25  |  Puts need RSI at or above 75

RSI is a momentum meter from 0 to 100. We are not asking "is there force behind this push?" — we are asking "is this push exhausted?". For a Call, RSI must be 25 or lower (deeply oversold, a drop with nothing left). For a Put, RSI must be 75 or higher (deeply overbought). One number per side, not a range. (An optional extra, off by default, also requires RSI to have already turned back.)

3. The chop guards — what replaced the trend tool

Band width ≥ 50 points  |  RSI range ≥ 30

V8 has no trend filter, and that is deliberate: a trend tool agrees with the breakout, so requiring it would veto every fade. But that leaves a real failure mode — a sideways tape pins price to a band that has collapsed to noise width, the fade signal repeats every few candles, and each one bleeds. Two guards, both on by default, close that hole:

  • Band width (BB_RSI_MIN_BAND_WIDTH_PTS, 50). Upper minus lower must be at least 50 points. A skinny band is not a stretch, so there is nothing to fade.
  • RSI range (BB_RSI_RSI_RANGE_MIN, 30). Over the previous 20 candles — deliberately excluding the signal candle, whose own spike would otherwise satisfy the test — RSI's high minus its low must be at least 30. A tape whose RSI has been pinned mid-range is dead, and its band touches mean nothing.

A third guard, an ADX ceiling (BB_RSI_ADX_ENABLED, off), blocks entries when ADX is above 30. Note the direction: a strong trend runs a fade over, so here a high reading is the danger — the opposite of how V7 used ADX. An optional divergence filter (off) can also require that price made a new extreme RSI did not confirm.

Where the stop line comes from now

With SuperTrend gone, the initial stop reference is the signal candle's own extreme — its low for a Call, its high for a Put. BB_RSI_MAX_ENTRY_SL_PTS (50) rejects a signal candle wider than that, since it would mean risking too much on one bar.

Before any of this runs

The strategy needs some history to compute these tools, so it waits for at least 30 candles after the open before it will take any signal, and it only opens new trades between 9:21 AM and 2:30 PM IST. Outside that window it just watches.

04 The entry checklist

For the strategy to arm a trade on a candle close, all of the boxes below have to say "yes." Miss one and it waits for the next candle. Here they are for both sides, side by side.

Arming a CE (bullish)

1
The candle closes at or below the lower Bollinger Band — an overdone drop.
2
RSI is 25 or lower — deeply oversold, the fall is exhausted.
3
The band is wide enough — upper minus lower is at least 50 points.
4
RSI has been moving — its range over the previous 20 candles is at least 30.
5
Stop line = the signal candle's low, and no wider than 50 points; we'll buy an ATM Call.

Arming a PE (bearish)

1
The candle closes at or above the upper Bollinger Band — an overdone rip.
2
RSI is 75 or higher — deeply overbought, the push is exhausted.
3
The band is wide enough — upper minus lower is at least 50 points.
4
RSI has been moving — its range over the previous 20 candles is at least 30.
5
Stop line = the signal candle's high, and no wider than 50 points; we'll buy an ATM Put.

The Put side, so you can see both halves. Another V7-era chart: it shows a close below the band buying a Put. In V8 that same candle buys a Call — the drop is what we fade. The mechanics either way are the mirror of the other side: signal candle, confirming candle, then a target and a stop. If you understand one side you understand both.

Why the "not too wide" check matters (step 5)

The initial stop line is the signal candle's own extreme — its low for a Call, its high for a Put. A violently wide signal candle therefore means a violently wide stop, and far more risk than the trade is worth. So the strategy simply skips any entry whose signal candle spans more than 50 points from its close (BB_RSI_MAX_ENTRY_SL_PTS, 0 = off).

Optional filters (off unless you switch them on): an ADX ceiling (BB_RSI_ADX_ENABLED, with BB_RSI_ADX_MAX at 30) blocks entries when the trend is too strong to fade — note this is the opposite direction to V7, which used ADX to demand a strong trend. A divergence filter (BB_RSI_DIVERGENCE_ENABLED) can also require price to make a new extreme that RSI refuses to confirm. Leave divergence off in breakout mode: it is a mean-reversion tell, so requiring it there would keep only the breakouts already failing.

When there is nothing to trade. On a calm, low-VIX day the bands (tight setting of 1) collapse right onto the price — the whole channel is barely 12 points wide. Price keeps nudging its edges, and the yellow dot marks a candle that pokes a whisker above the top band. But that "break" is just noise inside a 20-point range, not a real push — so no trade. This is the regime the strategy sits out; the ADX chop gate above exists precisely to skip days like this.

05 Wait for proof, then buy (the confirmation candle)

Here's the important part: the candle that ticks all the boxes above does not buy anything. It only arms the trade — like cocking a spring. We then look at the very next candle, and we buy only if it keeps pushing the same way.

What "the next candle confirms" means

With BB_RSI_CONFIRM_CANDLE_ENABLED and BB_RSI_CONFIRM_ON_CLOSE both on (the defaults), the next candle must close beyond the signal candle's close, in the direction of our trade — the first real sign the stretch is snapping back. If it does, we buy at that candle's closing price. If it stalls or keeps falling, the spring is released and nothing happens: a knife that is still falling costs us nothing.

This replaced the old BB_RSI_CONFIRM_OUTSIDE_BAND on 14 August 2026, and the reason matters. That rule additionally demanded the confirming candle still close outside the band. It made sense when the engine bought breakouts, but a mean-reversion entry wants price coming back toward the band — so the old test would have vetoed every valid V8 confirmation. Turning BB_RSI_CONFIRM_ON_CLOSE off enters the moment price crosses the level intra-bar; turning BB_RSI_CONFIRM_CANDLE_ENABLED off buys straight on the signal candle. The defaults are the careful setting.

The trade that never happened — and this is the point of the rule. A candle closes outside the band with RSI at an extreme and both chop guards satisfied, so the trade is armed (yellow dot). The amber line is the level the next candle has to clear: it must close beyond the signal candle's close, in our direction. Instead the next candle keeps going the original way — the stretch was not exhausted after all — so the spring is released and we buy nothing. This poke cost us exactly zero. Without this one-candle wait we would have caught the falling knife at full speed.

A fade trade, start to finish. This chart is drawn from the retired V7 engine, so it shows the entry mechanics — signal candle, then a confirming candle — with the sides reversed from what V8 would do. Read it as the shape of the sequence, not the direction: in V8 the yellow dot would be a candle closing below the lower band with RSI at or under 25, the next candle closing back up confirms it, and the trade aims at the middle band.

06 Pick the option

Once we've decided to trade, we still have to choose which option to buy and check it's worth buying.

CheckSettingIn plain words
Which strikeATMWe buy the at-the-money Call (for CE) or Put (for PE) — the strike closest to where NIFTY is right now.
Buy/sell gap≤ 2 pointsSkip the option if the gap between its buy and sell price is wider than 2 points (MAX_BID_ASK_SPREAD_PTS) — a wide gap quietly eats profit. If quotes are missing, this check lets the trade through rather than blocking it.
One at a time—Only one BB_RSI option is ever open at once. No pairs, no stacking.

In the backtest there are no real option prices. History doesn't record option quotes, so the backtest estimates how the option would have moved with NIFTY (using a delta of about 0.55 and a daily time-decay of ~10 points). Backtest profit is therefore a good approximation, not the exact fills you'd get live — always sanity-check against paper and replay.

The backtest now models the rough edges too. If price gaps straight past the hard stop — a candle opens well beyond it — the exit is filled at that worse gap price, not pretended at the stop line, so a jumpy bar's real loss isn't understated. And every exit now pays a small slippage haircut (BB_RSI_SLIPPAGE_PTS, default 1.5 points) on top of the entry-side one, because options fill a touch worse than the spot-implied price. Both changes make backtest results a little more honest and a little less rosy.

07 When we sell

Once we're in a trade, several exit rules watch it at once, and whichever one triggers first closes the position. They split into two groups: rules checked on every price tick (fast), and rules checked only when a candle closes (slower). The fast group runs first, so most exits happen intra-candle; the candle-close group is the backstop.

1
Middle-band target — checked every tick

This is the objective of the whole trade. The mean is what price is reverting to, so when price reaches the middle Bollinger line the fade has done its job and we take the money — at the tick, not waiting for the candle. Toggle: BB_RSI_TARGET_MIDDLE_BAND (on). It is skipped automatically in breakout mode, where the mean sits behind the entry.

2
Wide hard stop — checked every tick

The disaster brake. If NIFTY moves 30 points against our entry (BB_RSI_STOP_LOSS_PTS), we are out no matter what. It sits underneath the candle-close stop below, because a single violent bar against a fade can travel a long way before that close arrives. Set to 0 to disable.

3
Two opposite candles — the stop, then the trail (candle close)

The everyday exit, and it changes job as the trade matures. Before the trade has run 10 points in our favour (BB_RSI_TRAIL_ARM_PTS) it acts as the stop: two consecutive candles closing against us (BB_RSI_OPP_CANDLE_SL_COUNT) says the fade is not working, so we leave. Once past that mark the trail takes over with its own count — two opposite closes now bank a winner instead. Both halves are separately toggleable.

4
End of day

Anything still open is sold near 3:30 PM IST (TRADE_STOP_TIME), with an earlier safety square-off just before. We never hold a BB_RSI option overnight.

Gone in V8: the profit lock, the band re-entry exit, the trend flip

V7 banked profit with a ratcheting profit lock, bailed out on a band re-entry, and rode winners until a SuperTrend flip. None of those exist here. The profit lock (BB_RSI_PROFIT_LOCK_TRIGGER_PTS) still exists as a key but defaults to 0 = off, deliberately: firing it before the mean is reached would cut the trade short of its whole objective. Band re-entry is meaningless for a fade — re-entering the band is what we are betting on — and the trend flip left with SuperTrend.

The exit stack in one picture. The chart below is drawn from the retired V7 engine and is kept only to show the shape of an exit stack — in V8 the first line checked is the middle-band target, then the 30-point hard stop, then the two-opposite-candle stop or trail on the close. The profit-lock floor it draws no longer exists.

A losing trade, and the brake that stops it getting worse. The hard stop behaves identically in V8, so this picture still reads true — only the reason for being long changes. We bought a Call, price kept falling instead of rebounding, and the red line is the hard stop: 30 points below where we bought. Price touches it and we are out, no argument, no waiting for the candle to finish. That is the entire job of the hard stop — not to make money, but to stop one bad trade ruining a week. A fade that keeps going is exactly the failure this catches.

The honest trade-off — a fade caps its own upside. This V7-era chart makes a point that still holds. BB_RSI aims at the middle band and leaves; it will never ride a trend. That is deliberate: the whole premise is that the stretch is overdone, so the trade is over once price is back at its average. If you want the big move, that is what the trend strategies are for — and if a strong trend is running, the optional ADX ceiling is there to keep this strategy out of its way.

Real numbers — how the two-candle exit switches job. Bought a Call at 23,600 with the signal candle's low at 23,585. The trade is only +4 points up, so the two-candle rule is still the stop: two consecutive red closes and we leave near 23,590. Now suppose it instead runs to +14 points — past the 10-point arm level — so the same rule becomes the trail: two red closes now bank roughly +10 rather than cutting a loss. Same test, opposite purpose, decided purely by whether the trade cleared 10 points.

What we deliberately do NOT do: there is no tick-by-tick creeping trailing stop and no "move the stop to break-even" step, and no time-based exit. The stop line taken at entry (the signal candle's extreme) is a reference for sizing risk — it does not trail. Profit is taken at the middle band; the two-opposite-candle rule handles everything in between.

And a losing example, kept small

Say instead the drop was not exhausted at all and simply keeps going. The Call is bought at 23,556 and price slides. The trade never reaches +10 points, so the two-candle rule is still acting as the stop: after two consecutive candles close against us, we leave near 23,535 for a small loss. Only if a single violent bar runs 30 points against the entry before that second close arrives does the wide hard stop fire first. That is the division of labour — the candle rule handles the ordinary "I was wrong" case, and the hard stop exists purely for the rare bar that gaps straight through it.

08 The "market too jumpy" filter (VIX)

VIX is the market's fear gauge. When it's high, options get expensive, breakouts fail more often, and swings turn violent — a poor time for this strategy. The optional VIX filter lets it sit out those conditions.

VIX ≤ 16all signals allowed
16 – 20best setups only
VIX > 20no trades

VIX 16 or below — calm

Low fear, cleaner trends, tighter option prices. All valid breakout signals are allowed. This is the ideal backdrop for BB_RSI.

VIX 16 to 20 — elevated

Moderately nervous. Only the strongest setups are allowed through; borderline breakouts that would likely fail in the chop are skipped.

VIX above 20 — high

Fear zone. All entries blocked. Wide whipsaws and inflated premiums erode the edge; the strategy waits for VIX to cool.

How it's switched on. BB_RSI has its own VIX toggle, BB_RSI_VIX_ENABLED (off by default). Its two levels, BB_RSI_VIX_MAX_ENTRY (the "no trades above" ceiling) and BB_RSI_VIX_STRONG_ONLY (the "best only" line), fall back to the shared values 20 and 16 if you don't set them. If the VIX feed is unavailable, VIX_FAIL_MODE decides whether to block or allow (blocks by default).

New: a whole-book daily loss cap (off by default)

Every limit above only watches BB_RSI on its own. But you run several strategies at once, and on a bad day each one could hit its own cap while the account, all added together, loses far more. A new cross-strategy brake — PORTFOLIO_MAX_DAILY_LOSS (default 0 = off) — adds up today's realised profit-and-loss across all six paper strategies together. Once that combined loss reaches the cap, no strategy (BB_RSI included) may open a new trade for the rest of the day; trades already open are left alone. It only ever blocks new entries — it never places or changes an order — and if it can't read the day's numbers it lets trades through (fails open). Leave it at 0 to keep it off; set it (e.g. 12000) to arm it.

10 Paper, Live & Backtest

  • This is the official version of the strategy: whenever live or backtest behaves differently, paper is treated as correct.
  • Every trade is saved to disk, so it survives a restart.

Live — real money

bbRsiLive.js • page /bb_rsi

  • Real orders through Fyers.
  • Locked behind two switches so it can't fire by accident: BB_RSI_ENABLED must be on and the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN must be off.
  • While dry-run is on (the default), it logs the order it would place but sends nothing.

Backtest — test on the past

bbRsiBacktest.js • page /bb_rsi-backtest

  • Runs the strategy over past dates using cached historical candles.
  • Since history has no option prices, it estimates the option (delta ~0.55, time-decay ~10 pts/day) and includes charges.
  • Gives a downloadable report; treat the profit as an approximation.

Replay — re-run a recorded day

page /replay

  • Takes a day you recorded in paper mode and runs it again tick by tick.
  • Replay it with the original settings to reproduce exactly what happened, or with your current settings to test a tweak against the same real prices.
  • The safe way to try changes after market hours.

Three live-mode fixes you should know about (26 July 2026)

None of these changed a trading rule. All three were about live mode doing something different from practice mode — and practice is always treated as the correct one.

1. Stopping a live session could lose the final trade from your books. When you pressed Stop while holding a position, the sell order was sent but not waited for — the code carried straight on to save the session and send the day report while the order was still at the broker. The saved session was missing its last trade and its profit. A ₹240→₹300 exit on 65 units is ₹3,814 that never reached the books. Stopping now waits for the exit to complete before saving anything.
2. …but that wait is now capped, or a dead broker could hang the whole app. Neither broker SDK sets a network timeout, so a dead connection can hang for minutes. The wait is now capped at LIVE_EXIT_WAIT_MS (default 20,000 ms). A healthy order takes well under a second, so this never fires in normal use — only during a real outage, where you get a Telegram alert instead of a frozen process. Important: the timeout cancels nothing. A market order that has left the process cannot be recalled, so the alert says "may still be in flight, verify the dashboard" rather than "exit failed". Set it to 0 to opt out of the cap.
3. The portfolio-wide loss cap was missing from live mode entirely. PORTFOLIO_MAX_DAILY_LOSS was wired into all six practice routes and none of the live ones. So with the cap armed, practice would stop taking trades while live — the side with real money — carried on. All ten routes now check it, at the same point in the sequence. It can only ever block a new entry; it never places or changes an order.

If you find an old guide or config mentioning these, they were removed and do nothing now: BB_RSI_PSAR_STEP / BB_RSI_PSAR_MAX and every BB_RSI_SUPERTREND_* key (V8 has no trend tool at all), BB_RSI_BB_REENTRY_EXIT / BB_RSI_BB_REENTRY_ARM_PTS (re-entering the band is what a fade is betting on, so it cannot also be an exit), BB_RSI_CONFIRM_OUTSIDE_BAND (replaced by BB_RSI_CONFIRM_ON_CLOSE), BB_RSI_ADX_MIN (the gate is now a ceiling, BB_RSI_ADX_MAX), and a long list of earlier filters (BB-squeeze, CPR-narrow, approach-angle and the rest) that were tried and dropped.

12 Notes & what's still missing

  • Paper is the source of truth. If live or backtest ever disagree with paper, paper is treated as correct and the other side is fixed to match — never the other way round.
  • Backtest option prices are estimated. History has no option quotes, so backtest profit is an approximation. Always confirm against paper and replay before trusting a result.
  • This strategy lives and dies by mean reversion. It makes money when an overdone move snaps back to its average, and it bleeds when a market picks a direction and simply keeps going — a fade is run over by a strong trend. The optional ADX ceiling exists to sit those trending days out.
  • The hard stop is wide on purpose. 30 points sounds loose, but the two-opposite-candle stop catches the common small failures first on the close — the wide stop exists only for the violent bar that arrives before any close.
  • It is regime-bimodal. In a low-volatility market the band collapses toward noise width and every touch becomes meaningless. That is exactly what the on-by-default band-width and RSI-range guards are for; they, or a VIX floor, are the lever worth tuning — not the RSI thresholds.
Check this before you trust any BB_RSI session from before 26 July 2026. A stale option-expiry date was being traded as if the contract still existed. BB_RSI was one of the two strategies that entered the trade anyway: it logged its profit-and-loss as pnlMode: "spot proxy" (priced off NIFTY points, because no option premium ever arrived), and — because the percentage option stop needs an entry premium to measure against — it ran with no option stop-loss at all for the whole life of the position.

This is now blocked: an expiry date is treated as dead once its own expiry day has closed at 15:30, and a dead date refuses the entry and names the setting to fix rather than quietly switching to a different expiry (a different expiry means different premium, different decay, different risk — your call, not the software's).

How to spot an affected session in your logs: trades stamped pnlMode: "spot proxy" together with "no_data" errors on the option price. Discard those sessions — they are a data hole, not a strategy result.

The big correction in this edition

Older copies of this guide described a trend tool — first Parabolic SAR (PSAR), later SuperTrend (10, 3). Both are out of date. V8 has no trend tool at all, by design: a trend filter agrees with the breakout and would therefore veto every fade this engine is built to take. Its job is done instead by the band-width and RSI-range chop guards, with an optional ADX ceiling for strongly trending days.
