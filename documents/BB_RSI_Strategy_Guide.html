BB_RSI — fade the stretch

When NIFTY closes outside its Bollinger Band and RSI says the move is exhausted, the bot bets on the snap back to the middle. It buys a Call after a sharp drop and a Put after a sharp rise.

Updated 2026-10-09 · engine V8 (mean reversion) · every chart is an example built from made-up candles and checked against the real engine code

In one minute
  • Candles: 5-minute NIFTY 50 candles (follows the global candle size unless you set a BB_RSI-only size).
  • CE (Call) setup: a candle closes at or below the lower Bollinger Band (30, 2) and RSI (14) is 25 or lower. The bet: the drop is overdone and price bounces.
  • PE (Put) setup: the mirror. A candle closes at or above the upper band and RSI is 75 or higher.
  • Proof first: the next candle must close back the other way (above the signal close for a CE, below it for a PE). The bot buys at that close.
  • Three chop guards skip dead markets: the band must be at least 50 points wide, RSI must have moved at least 30 over the last 20 candles, and the signal candle may not be more than 50 points long.
  • Exits: the middle band is the target. Two candles in a row closing against you end the trade. A 30-point hard stop caps a violent move. The app-wide profit lock and breakeven stop also protect the option premium. Anything still open is closed at 15:20.
  • Limits: new signals only from 09:21 to 14:30. Max 30 trades a day, a daily loss cap (₹4,000 in Settings), and a short pause after every stop-loss.
Read this first. This is a mean-reversion strategy: it trades against the move that just happened. A 2018–2026 backtest of this fade over 510 trades lost money (34% wins, profit factor 0.50), as recorded in the developer notes. Treat it as a strategy under study, not a proven earner. Every chart below is an Example — not a real trade.

2. The band and the momentum meter

The bot watches two things on every finished candle.

  1. Bollinger Bands (30, 2). Price lives inside the bands most of the time. A close outside means price ran further than usual.
  2. RSI (14). It tells you whether the run was extreme. A close below the lower band and RSI at 25 or less is a real washout. A close above the upper band and RSI at 75 or more is a real blow-off.

There is deliberately no trend filter. A trend tool agrees with the move, so it would block every fade this strategy wants to take.

What you see: a morning of NIFTY with the three band lines. Yellow dots mark the 19 closes that landed outside a band. The band is only 10 points wide in the quiet stretch and opens to about 110 points during the rally and the drop. Why it matters: closes outside a band are common. On their own they are not a signal — RSI must be extreme too, and the band must be wide enough. Example — not a real trade.

3. The entry, step by step

On every finished candle, the engine runs these checks in this order. The first one that fails ends the check for that candle.

  1. Enough history. At least 35 finished candles. Paper loads earlier candles at start, so this is ready from the open.
  2. Time window. The candle must start at or after 09:21 and before 14:30.
  3. Band-width guard (on). Upper minus lower band must be 50 points or more.
  4. RSI-range guard (on). Over the 20 candles before this one, RSI's highest minus lowest value must be 30 or more. The signal candle itself is left out, so its own spike cannot pass the test.
  5. ADX ceiling (off by default). If switched on, ADX(14) at 30 or above blocks the trade — a strong trend runs over a fade.
  6. The setup. CE: close ≤ lower band and RSI ≤ 25. PE: close ≥ upper band and RSI ≥ 75.
  7. RSI turning (off by default). If on, RSI must already be turning back (rising for a CE, falling for a PE).
  8. Divergence (off by default). If on, price must make a new low (CE) or new high (PE) while RSI does not. The bot compares with the most recent confirmed swing (2 candles either side) within 20 candles. No swing found = blocked.
  9. Wide-candle guard. The distance from the close to the candle's low (CE) or high (PE) must be 50 points or less. That extreme is the trade's "stop line" shown on screen.

If all pass, the paper route then checks its own gates: the daily loss cap, the whole-portfolio cap, the trade count, the per-side pause, the VIX filter (if on) and the OI filter (if on). Only then is the signal armed.

4. Wait for proof, then buy

An armed signal does not buy yet. It waits exactly one candle.

  • CE: the next candle must close above the signal candle's close. The bot buys at that close.
  • PE: the next candle must close below the signal candle's close.
  • If the next candle fails, the signal expires. But if that candle is itself a new setup, it arms again and the wait starts over.
  • The gates are not checked again at confirmation. They were all passed when the signal armed.

What you see: at 10:30 a candle closes at 24,606.40, under the lower band (24,609.30), with RSI 21.3 — a CE signal. The 10:35 candle closes lower (24,597.35), so no proof — but it is a fresh signal (RSI 18.5). Same again at 10:40 (RSI 16.6). The 10:45 candle finally closes above 24,589.85, so the bot buys a CE at 24,597.90. Why it matters: you never catch the exact bottom. You buy the first candle that shows buyers are back. Example — not a real trade.

Two switches change this step.
  • Confirm on close (on). Turn it off and the bot buys during the next candle, the instant price crosses the signal close. Faster, but a candle that pokes up and then closes red still gets you in.
  • Confirmation candle (on). Turn it off and the bot buys right at the signal candle's close, with no proof at all.

5. Which option it buys

  • Strike: at the money, or one step in the money — never out of the money. For a CE the strike is at or below spot. For a PE it is at or above spot. You can shift this with the strike-offset settings (0 = ATM).
  • Size: one lot (65 units) times the lot multiplier (1 by default).
  • Spread check: if the option's buy/sell gap is wider than 2 points, the entry is skipped. If the broker shows no buy/sell prices at all, the check lets the trade through.
  • Money check: Paper draws from a shared practice-capital pool (₹1,00,000 by default). If the pool cannot pay for the option, the entry is refused.
  • Futures mode: if the app's instrument is set to NIFTY futures, the bot trades the month futures contract instead.

The profit or loss is the change in the option's premium times the quantity, minus Fyers charges.

6. How a trade ends

Most rules are checked on every price tick, in this order. The first one that fires closes the trade.

  1. Middle-band target. CE: price reaches the middle band. PE: price falls to it. The middle band used is the one from the last finished candle. This is the main way to win.
  2. App-wide profit lock. Once the option premium has gained 8%, the trade may not fall back below a locked floor. The floor starts at +5% and rises with the best price (it keeps 85% of the gain while the gain is under 20%, 80% under 40%, then 70%).
  3. App-wide breakeven stop. Once the premium has gained 5% (but the lock is not armed), the trade may not be sold below the entry premium.
  4. Hard stop. If NIFTY moves 30 points against the entry price, the trade is closed at once.
  5. BB_RSI's own profit lock (off by default, trigger 0). If you set it, it locks a share of the best NIFTY-point gain.
  6. End of day. At 15:20 (10 minutes before the 15:30 stop time) anything still open is sold.

One rule is checked only when a candle closes:

  • Two opposite candles. For a CE, two red candles in a row since entry. For a PE, two green candles in a row. Before the trade has gone 10 points your way, this is the stop. After that, it is the trail. Each has its own on/off switch and count (both 2).

7. Winning trades — CE and PE

What you see: a CE signal at 10:40 (close 24,631.40, under the lower band, RSI 21.1, band 64 points wide). The 10:45 candle closes higher, so the bot buys at 24,643.10. Price climbs and touches the middle band (24,665.47) at 11:05. Exit at the band: about +22 NIFTY points. Why it matters: the target is not a fixed number — it is wherever the 30-candle average is. Example — not a real trade.

Notice the premium had risen more than 8% before the target, so the profit lock was armed. Price never dropped back to its floor, so the target came first.

What you see: a rally pushes three closes in a row above the upper band (12:20, 12:25, 12:30 — the last with RSI 83.9). Each next candle closed higher, so each signal expired and re-armed. At 12:35 a candle closes below 24,488.15 and the bot buys a PE at 24,475.20. Price falls to the middle band (24,436.25) at 12:55: about +39 points. Why it matters: the PE is the exact mirror of the CE. Example — not a real trade.

8. Losing trades

What you see: CE bought at 24,584.15 (11:15). It rises only 7.5 points, so the trail never arms. Then two red candles close in a row, and the second one closes the trade at 24,567.85 at 11:30: about −16 points. The shaded area is the CE pause that follows. Why it matters: this is the normal, small loss. Two red candles mean the bounce has failed. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: PE bought at 24,496.55 (11:45) after a rip above the upper band. At 11:55 one big green candle runs straight up. The moment NIFTY reaches 24,526.55 (entry + 30), the hard stop sells — before the candle even closes. Why it matters: the two-candle stop only acts on a close. The hard stop is the seat belt for one violent candle. Example — not a real trade.

9. The trail, the profit lock and breakeven

What you see: CE bought at 24,592.60 (11:25). It gets 10.1 points ahead, which arms the trail (purple line). Two small red candles follow and the trail exits at 24,593.10 at 11:40 — about break-even. The middle band (about 24,651) was never reached. Why it matters: the trail turns a "nearly worked" trade into a scratch instead of a loss. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: CE bought at 24,645.45 (11:45). The lower panel is the option premium at each close (example: ₹150 at entry, 0.55 premium per NIFTY point). Inside the 12:00 candle it peaks at about ₹162.3 (+8.2%, green dot), which arms the lock. The floor is set to keep 85% of that gain: about ₹160.5. In the same candle the premium falls back to the floor (red dot) and the bot sells near 24,664: about +19 points. Why it matters: the lock banks most of a gain even when the middle band (about 24,686) is never reached. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: PE bought at 24,501.60 (10:55). NIFTY falls 18 points and the premium peaks at about ₹160.1 (+6.7%, green dot). That is more than 5%, so the breakeven stop arms — but less than 8%, so the profit lock does not. The bounce takes the premium back to ₹150 (red dot) and the bot sells at 11:15, at about the entry price. Why it matters: once a trade has been 5% up, it can no longer turn into a real loss. Example — not a real trade.

Where these three come from. The two-candle trail is BB_RSI's own rule (NIFTY points). The profit lock and breakeven stop are app-wide rules (Settings → Security & Safety) that watch the option premium. They apply to every strategy, and BB_RSI checks them right after its middle-band target.

10. Trades it refuses

Each of these charts shows a candle that closed outside a band with an extreme RSI — and still no trade.

What you see: a sleepy morning. At 10:05 a candle closes at 24,535.60, under the lower band, with RSI 23.2. But the band is only 21 points wide — far below the 50-point minimum. No trade. Why it matters: in a skinny band, "outside the band" is just noise. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: price creeps up and then stalls. Over the 20 shaded candles RSI stays stuck in a narrow range — only 25.6 points from its high to its low. Then one sharp red candle at 11:05 closes under the lower band with RSI 17.9. The RSI-range guard needs 30, so the trade is skipped. Why it matters: a stalled market that jerks once is not an exhausted move. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a steady climb. At 11:00 a candle closes at 24,492, above the upper band, with RSI 79.3 — a PE setup. But its high (24,544) is 52 points above the close. The limit is 50, so no trade. The next five setups (11:05 to 11:25) also carry wicks longer than 50 points, so they are refused too. Why it matters: a huge candle means the stop line is too far away and the risk is too big. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: the lower panel is India VIX, between 17 and 19 all morning. At 11:00 a PE setup appears with RSI 78.4, and at 11:55 a CE setup with RSI 23.9. With VIX above 16, only STRONG signals pass (RSI 80+ for a PE, 20 or less for a CE). Both are blocked. Without the filter, the PE would have been bought at 11:05. Why it matters: the VIX filter is off by default; when on, it keeps this fade out of jumpy markets. Example — not a real trade.

  • VIX above 20 (when the filter is on): no new trades at all.
  • VIX between 16 and 20: only STRONG signals — RSI at least 5 points past the threshold.
  • VIX not available: blocked by default ("fail closed").
  • OI filter (off by default, and needs the app-wide OI switch too): blocks a buy that fights a confirmed open-interest build-up.
  • Expiry-day only (off): when on, BB_RSI trades only on NIFTY expiry day.

11. Time rules

  • First signal: the candle must start at 09:21 or later. With 5-minute candles the first one that counts is the 09:25 candle.
  • Last signal: the candle must start before 14:30. The last one that counts is the 14:25 candle. Its confirmation (the 14:30 candle) can still buy.
  • Square-off: anything still open is sold at 15:20.

What you see: CE signals at 14:20 and 14:25 (closes 24,574.70 and 24,565.35). The 14:30 candle closes lower (24,558.90), so the 14:25 signal is not confirmed. The 14:30 candle is itself under the band with RSI 17.2 — but it starts at 14:30, so the window is shut. The bounce that follows is never traded. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a CE bought at 24,615.20 at 14:10. Price then goes sideways: never two red candles in a row, never 10 points ahead, never 30 points behind, and the middle band stays out of reach. At 15:20 the bot sells at 24,620.35: about +5 points. Why it matters: no BB_RSI trade is ever held overnight. Example — not a real trade.

12. Safety limits and the pause

  • Max trades a day: 30.
  • Daily loss cap: once the day's net loss reaches the cap, no more entries today. Settings shows ₹4,000. (If the key has never been saved, the code uses ₹2,000.) Earlier paper sessions on the same day count too.
  • Portfolio cap: an app-wide loss cap across all strategies (0 = off). It only blocks new entries.
  • Pause after a stop-loss: after the two-candle stop or the hard stop, that side (CE or PE) is paused for 3 candles (Settings value; 2 if never saved). A second stop in a row on the same side adds 2 more candles, a third adds 4, and so on. A winning trade on that side resets the count. The other side keeps trading.

Trail exits, target exits, lock exits and the 15:20 square-off do not start a pause.

What you see: CE bought at 24,620.40 (10:45) and stopped out by two red candles at 11:00 (24,607.85). Price keeps falling, and the 11:05 and 11:10 candles are fresh CE signals — both ignored (grey dots) because CE is paused. The pause ends, the 11:15 candle arms a new signal, and the bot buys at 11:20 (24,596.85). That trade ends by the profit lock at 11:45 near 24,615.80. Why it matters: the pause stops the bot buying every candle of a falling knife. Example — not a real trade.

13. A day with no trades

What you see: NIFTY wanders all morning. The band is only 9 to 39 points wide and RSI stays between about 44 and 68. Price sometimes pokes outside a band, but nothing comes close to a setup. Zero trades. Why it matters: on quiet days BB_RSI is meant to do nothing. That is the chop guards working. Example — not a real trade.

14. Paper, Live, Backtest & the breakout switch

  • Paper (the reference): runs on the live NIFTY feed, records every trade, and uses real option prices for profit and loss. Everything in this guide describes Paper.
  • Live: the same rules, placing Fyers orders. Real orders need all of: BB_RSI live orders on, BB_RSI "allow real orders" on, the global dry-run switch off, and the BB_RSI dry-run override off. Otherwise Live only logs what it would have done. Live also rests a stop order at the broker as a safety net.
  • Backtest: replays history with candles only. It estimates the option price from NIFTY moves (0.55 per point, minus 10 points of time decay a day) and charges 1.5 points of slippage per side.
  • Replay: re-runs a recorded day through the paper logic.
The breakout switch. Trade direction can be set to breakout. The same signal candles are then traded the other way: a close above the upper band buys a CE, a close below the lower band buys a PE. Everything else stays the same, except the middle-band target is skipped (the average is behind you) and "STRONG" means RSI further past the threshold in the breakout direction. Leave divergence off in breakout mode. The default is fade.

16. What we do not know yet

  • Is there an edge? The long backtest of the fade lost money. Paper results since the V8 rebuild (14 August 2026) are the real test. Judge it on clean post-change sessions only.
  • Profit lock vs target. With a ₹150 premium, about 22 NIFTY points arms the app-wide lock. Many fades will be closed by the lock or breakeven stop before the middle band. Nobody has yet measured whether that helps or hurts this strategy.
  • Fade or breakout? The direction switch exists so both can be compared on the same signal candles. That comparison has not been settled.
  • Option prices in the charts are modelled (₹150 entry, 0.55 per point) only to show how the premium rules work. Real premiums vary.
