EMA_RSI_ST_V2

A stripped-down version of EMA_RSI_ST. Three rules on a closed 5-minute candle decide the trade. One candle of proof triggers the buy. One line — SuperTrend — is the stop. V2 is a separate strategy with its own settings; changing V2 never changes V1.

Updated 2026-10-08 · every chart is an example built from made-up candles and checked against the real engine code

In one minute
  • CE (Call) when all three are true on a closed 5-minute candle: EMA20 is above EMA50, the candle closed above EMA20, and RSI is above 52.
  • PE (Put) is the mirror: EMA20 below EMA50, close below EMA20, RSI below 48.
  • One more condition: the SuperTrend line must be on the protective side of the close (below it for CE, above it for PE). If not, there is nowhere to put a stop, so the setup is skipped.
  • Wait for proof: the bot buys during the next candle, the moment price goes past the signal candle's close.
  • The stop is SuperTrend(10, 2). It starts at the SuperTrend value and moves only in the trade's favour at each candle close.
  • Other exits: the app-wide profit lock (+8%), an opposite signal, and the 14:00 square-off. No target.
  • Tight limits: new trades only 10:30–13:00. Max 2 trades a day, ₹1,000 daily loss, stop after 2 losses in a row. VIX above 20 blocks entries.
Read this first. V2 ships switched off and has never traded live. Every chart below is an example — not a real trade: invented candles, with indicators computed from them and trades produced by running V2's own rule code. Option prices in the examples use a simple model (₹180 at entry — the backtest's seed premium — moving ₹0.55 for each NIFTY point). The real bot reads the live option price.

Words you will see

WordPlain meaning
CandleOne bar on the chart: 5 minutes of NIFTY price — open, high, low, close.
EMAExponential Moving Average, a smooth line of the average closing price. EMA20 follows the last ~20 candles, EMA50 the last ~50. EMA20 above EMA50 = uptrend.
RSIRelative Strength Index, 0 to 100, over 14 candles. Above 50 = buyers pushing; below 50 = sellers pushing.
SuperTrendA line drawn from ATR (average candle size) over 10 candles × 2. It sits below price in an uptrend (green) and above price in a downtrend (red). In V2 it is only the stop, not an entry rule.
CE / PECE = Call, gains when NIFTY rises. PE = Put, gains when NIFTY falls. V2 only buys.
PremiumThe option's price in rupees per unit. Profit = (sell − buy) × quantity (1 lot = 65).
Stop / trailThe stop is the price where the bot sells to cap a loss. A trailing stop follows price as the trade goes your way.
VIXIndia VIX, the market's "fear gauge". High = jumpy prices.

Step 1 — The three rules

#CE (Call)PE (Put)
1. TrendEMA20 above EMA50EMA20 below EMA50
2. CloseCandle closed above EMA20Candle closed below EMA20
3. MomentumRSI above 52RSI below 48
Stop checkSuperTrend below the closeSuperTrend above the close

The bot reads only closed candles. It needs 55 candles of history (EMA50 + 5), more than one day, so it loads the previous 5 trading days at start.

What you see: at 10:30 EMA20 (24,480.65) is above EMA50 (24,465.40), the candle closes at 24,513.60, above EMA20, and RSI is 69.1. SuperTrend (24,487.48) is below the close, so a stop exists. The next candle crosses the close and the bot buys a CE at 24,517.95. The orange line is the stop climbing behind price. At 11:35 the profit lock sells it at 24,595.65. Why it matters: three plain rules, one candle, one stop. Example — not a real trade.

Step 2 — One candle of proof

  1. The signal candle's close becomes the trigger.
  2. During the very next candle, the bot buys the moment price goes strictly past it (above for CE, below for PE).
  3. If that candle never crosses, the setup is dropped. If that candle is itself a new signal, it re-arms with its own close.

What you see: a close-up of the same trade. Yellow = the trigger (the 10:30 close). Orange = the first stop, the SuperTrend value on the signal candle. During 10:35 price trades above the trigger and the bot fills at 24,517.95. Why it matters: a signal that price will not follow costs nothing. Example — not a real trade.

Step 3 — The SuperTrend stop

  • Start: the SuperTrend value on the signal candle. If the fill landed on the wrong side of it, the bot uses the nearest earlier low (CE) or high (PE) that does protect the trade.
  • Move: at each candle close the stop is set to the new SuperTrend value — but only if that is tighter (higher for CE, lower for PE). It never moves back.
  • Hit: checked on every price tick. A touch sells the trade.

What you see: a CE bought 10:35 at 24,521.60 on a trend day, drawn with the profit lock switched off so the trail can be seen on its own. The stop steps up under price all morning. At 13:05 price falls into it and the trade is sold at 24,733.56 — about 212 points. In the model, premium ₹180 → ₹296.24. Why it matters: with no target, the trail is what lets a trend pay. With the lock on (the default), this trade would have been sold earlier, once premium fell back from its peak. Example — not a real trade.

Winning trades

What you see: the premium of the CE from Step 1. Bought ₹180. It passes +8% (₹194.40), which arms the profit lock, and peaks at ₹234.61. With the peak gain between +20% and +40%, the lock keeps 80% of the gain: floor ≈ ₹223.69. Premium dips to ₹222.74 and the trade is sold — about ₹2,698 net on one lot. Why it matters: the lock banks most of a run before the slower SuperTrend line would. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a falling market. EMA20 under EMA50, a close under EMA20, RSI below 48 and SuperTrend red above price. Bought a PE at 11:50 (24,660.05). The stop follows from above. At 12:55 the profit lock sells it at 24,578.20 (premium ₹180 → ₹225.02, about ₹2,846 net). Why it matters: PE is the exact mirror of CE. Example — not a real trade.

A losing trade

What you see: a CE bought at 11:40 (24,638.70), first stop 24,618.68. Price stalls and turns down; at 12:00 it touches the stop and the trade is sold — about −₹900. After this, new CE trades are paused for 2 candles. Why it matters: the loss is set by how far SuperTrend sits from price, which is why V2 caps the day at 2 trades and ₹1,000. Example — not a real trade.

Every way a trade ends

  1. Profit lock (every tick) — premium rose +8%, then fell to the locked floor. The floor is the higher of entry +5% and a share of the best gain (85% under +20%, 80% under +40%, else 70%).
  2. SuperTrend stop (every tick) — price touched the stop line.
  3. Opposite signal (candle close) — a full PE signal while holding a CE, or the reverse.
  4. 14:00 square-off (candle close) — anything open is sold.

V2 has no option-price stop, no fixed-point stop, no "still losing after N candles" stop, no EMA21 exit, no breakeven stop and no target.

What you see: a CE bought at 12:50. No new trades start after 13:00 (shaded). The trade is still open when the 13:55 candle closes at 14:00, so it is sold at that close, 25,220.20 (small profit, about ₹257). Why it matters: V2 closes much earlier than most strategies to avoid the last-hour time decay. Example — not a real trade.

Trades it refuses

What you see: at 10:30 and 10:35 all three rules pass (10:30: RSI 52.3, close 24,607.25 above EMA20), but SuperTrend (24,618.33) is above the close. A CE stop must be below price, so both are skipped. Later, at 10:50, a setup with SuperTrend below price is taken. Why it matters: V2 never opens a trade it cannot protect. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: at 12:35 EMA20 is above EMA50 and the close (24,729.60) is above EMA20, but RSI is exactly 50.0. A CE needs RSI above 52. No trade. Why it matters: trend without push is not enough. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: at 12:50 the trend is up (EMA20 above EMA50) and RSI is 52.2, but the candle closed at 25,322.35, below EMA20 (25,328.24). No CE. Why it matters: a dip under the fast line is a pause, not a buy signal. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: the Step 1 day again, but India VIX is 22 until 11:20 (shaded). The 10:35 cross is refused (red dot). When VIX drops to 15, the next setups trade: a CE at 11:20 wins via the profit lock, a CE at 12:00 is stopped out. Why it matters: V2 uses the same VIX switch and limit (20) as V1. Example — not a real trade.

Time rules

  • The signal candle must close inside 10:30–13:00.
  • The confirmation candle must also start inside the window. A 12:55 signal's next candle starts at 13:00 — too late.
  • At 14:00 anything open is sold. Nothing is held overnight.

What you see: the three rules are already true at 10:10 (red dot), but that is before 10:30. The first allowed signal is the 10:30 candle; the CE bought at 10:35 is later closed by the profit lock. Why it matters: the first hour is noisy and its crosses often reverse. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a CE signal at 12:55 (RSI 78.7 — V2 has no upper RSI cap). Its confirmation candle is 13:00, outside the window, so the setup is dropped. Why it matters: a late trade has little time left before the 14:00 square-off. Example — not a real trade.

Bad days and quiet days

What you see: a CE at 10:55 is stopped at 11:30 (−₹922). CE crosses at 11:35 and 11:40 are refused by the 2-candle same-side pause (red dots). A second CE at 11:45 is stopped at 12:10 (−₹405). That is 2 losses in a row and −₹1,327 for the day, so the bot stops for the day (shaded). Why it matters: on a choppy day V2 loses small and quits early. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a PE bought at 10:35 is stopped at 10:40. A CE cross at 10:45 is refused (red dot) by the opposite-side pause of 2 candles. At 10:50 a CE is allowed and wins via the profit lock. Why it matters: it stops instant CE↔PE flip-flopping without blocking a real turn for long. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: the only setup of the day is a PE at 10:30 (trigger 25,002.30). The 10:35 candle's lowest price is 25,004.25 — it never trades below the trigger — so the setup is dropped. No trade all day. Why it matters: zero trades is a normal, correct day. Example — not a real trade.

Safety limits

  • Max 2 trades a day, max ₹1,000 loss a day (counted with earlier trades today), and stop after 2 losses in a row.
  • Same-side pause: after a SuperTrend stop, that side is paused 2 candles.
  • Opposite-side pause: after any exit except an opposite signal, the square-off or a manual exit, the other side is paused 2 candles.
  • No new entry on the candle where a stop or the profit lock fired.
  • VIX: uses V1's VIX_FILTER_ENABLED / VIX_MAX_ENTRY — blocks when India VIX is above 20.
  • Strike: at the money or one step in the money, never out of the money.

Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

  • Paper — simulated trades on the live feed; the reference the other modes must match. Needs EMA_RSI_ST_V2_PAPER_ENABLED on.
  • Live — real Zerodha orders only when EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_ENABLED is on and the app-wide LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN brake is off.
  • Backtest — same rules on past candles. No option history, so each trade starts from a seed premium and pays a points haircut each way for spread and slippage.
  • Replay — a recorded day replayed tick by tick through the paper engine.
  • V2 has a NIFTY BANK twin, BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2, with its own guide and settings.
