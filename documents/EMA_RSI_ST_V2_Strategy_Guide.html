EMA_RSI_ST_V2

A stripped-down rewrite of EMA_RSI_ST. Three things have to line up on one finished five-minute candle — the trend, the close, and the momentum reading — and then a single SuperTrend line does all the stopping. No target, no second stop, no third opinion.

Updated 2026-09-05  ·  plain-English edition  ·  every chart below was produced by running the real engine on real 2025 NIFTY candles

Read this first. V2 has never traded live and has zero paper sessions. The only evidence that exists is a backtest, and the backtest says two opposite things depending on how you measure it: +2,255 spot points when you count NIFTY points and ignore costs, but −₹77,978 when you run the real backtest route with modelled option premiums and a slippage haircut. Both numbers are in this guide. The second one is the one that matters, and it is negative.

1. The idea in one minute

Buy in the direction the market is already going, but only once, and only when three cheap tests agree.

The first test is the trend: the 20-candle average has to be above the 50-candle average for a Call. The second is the price itself: the candle has to finish above that 20-candle average, not just poke at it. The third is momentum: the RSI reading has to be on the right side of the middle. For a Put, flip all three.

That is the whole entry. There is no profit target. The trade is held until a SuperTrend line — one line, drawn from the candles themselves — catches up to the price, or until 14:00 comes and everything is closed regardless.

Why a V2 at all? V1 (EMA_RSI_ST) grew a lot of parts — an optional EMA9 triple stack, an RSI ceiling, an EMA21 trail, a negative-candle stop, an option-premium stop, a points stop. Every one of those is a knob, and every knob is a chance to fit the past instead of the future. V2 keeps three entry rules and one stop, so there is far less to tune and far less to fool yourself with. V1 is untouched. They run under separate settings and share nothing.

2. Words you'll see

  • EMA20 / EMA50 — exponential moving averages of the closing price over 20 and 50 candles. The recent candles count for more than the old ones, so they turn faster than a plain average. EMA20 above EMA50 is the usual shorthand for "the short-term trend is up".
  • RSI(14) — a 0–100 momentum reading over the last 14 candles. 50 is the neutral middle. Above it means buyers have had the upper hand recently; below it means sellers have.
  • SuperTrend(10, 2) — a line that sits below price while the trend is up and flips above it when the trend turns. The 10 is how many candles of range it measures; the 2 is how many of those ranges it stands away from price. In V2 it is only a stop.
  • Finished candle — a five-minute bar that has closed. V2 never reads the bar that is still forming, which is exactly why Paper and Replay give the same answer twice.
  • Trigger — the closing price of the candle that passed all three rules. It is the price the next candle has to cross before anything is bought.
  • Tighten-only trail — a stop that is allowed to move closer to price but never further away. If the SuperTrend line slips back down mid-trade, the stop stays where it was.
  • Spot points — movement measured in NIFTY index points, not in rupees of option premium. The two are not the same thing, and the gap between them is the central problem in this guide.
  • CE / PE — a Call option (you profit if NIFTY rises) and a Put option (you profit if it falls).

3. The entry — three rules, one candle

Everything is decided on a finished five-minute candle. All three tests are read off that same candle. Not one of them is allowed to have been true an hour ago.

 CE — buy a CallPE — buy a Put
1. TrendEMA20 above EMA50EMA20 below EMA50
2. Closethe candle closes above EMA20the candle closes below EMA20
3. MomentumRSI(14) above 52RSI(14) below 48

The PE side is the exact mirror of the CE side. There is no asymmetry anywhere in V2 — no side gets an extra filter or a different stop.

Rule 2 is not the same as rule 1. EMA20 can sit above EMA50 (an uptrend) while the candle itself closes below EMA20 (a pullback). That is a common state, and V2 does not buy it. It wants the trend up and price back on top of the fast average on the same bar.
52 and 48, not 70 and 30. Those are barely off the neutral 50. This is on purpose: rules 1 and 2 already establish direction, so the RSI test is here to veto a signal firing into obvious weakness, not to hunt for extremes. Raise EMA_RSI_ST_V2_RSI_CE_MIN and you get fewer, later, stronger entries — and you will miss the start of most moves.

4. The confirmation candle

The candle that passes all three rules does not buy anything. It only arms the trade. Default: ON.

How it actually works.
  1. A finished candle passes all three rules. Call its closing price the trigger.
  2. During the immediately next candle, price must cross that trigger — strictly above it for a CE, strictly below for a PE.
  3. If it does, the fill happens inside that candle, at the crossing price. Not at the next close.
  4. If that one candle never crosses the trigger, the setup is dropped. It does not carry over.

Paper and Live fill on the first tick past the trigger. The backtest fills at the same market moment through a shared helper, so all three modes agree by construction rather than by luck.

What this buys you and what it costs you. It throws away the signals that were a one-candle spike and immediately snapped back — the classic way a moving-average rule gets whipsawed. The cost is that you never get the first candle of a move, and on a gap-and-run day the crossing price can be well above where the signal candle closed. Turning it off (EMA_RSI_ST_V2_CONFIRM_CANDLE_ENABLED=false) enters on the signal candle itself.

5. The stop — SuperTrend and nothing else

  • The initial stop is the SuperTrend value at the moment of entry — below price for a CE, above it for a PE.
  • The trail is the same line, re-read at every candle close.
  • Tighten-only. A CE stop may only move up; a PE stop may only move down. A SuperTrend that loosens is ignored.
  • Enforced intra-candle. The execution layer checks every tick. You do not wait for a candle to close to be stopped out.
  • Hard close at 14:00, stop or no stop. Nothing is carried past it and nothing is carried overnight.

What is deliberately absent

No profit target. No negative-candle stop. No option-premium stop. No fixed-points stop. No EMA21 trail. No breakeven jump, no partial booking. If a trade is open, the only two things that can close it are the SuperTrend line and the clock.

No target means the exit is always late. A SuperTrend trail gives back part of every winner — that is how a trail works. The trade that made +360.1 points in the worked example below did not exit at its high; it ran to 14:00 and closed there. In exchange, nothing caps how far a winner can run. The backtest bears this out: the average win is +48.5 points against an average loss of −25.7 points, which is where the whole spot-points edge comes from.

6. What makes V2 different from V1

These are deliberate removals, not oversights. If you are reading this later and think one of them should be "added back", that is V1 — and V1 already exists, untouched, right next to this.

The one honest caveat, and it is not a small one.

SuperTrend is V2's only stop, and V2 does not test SuperTrend on entry. Those two facts collide. A setup can pass all three entry rules while the SuperTrend line is sitting on the wrong side of price — above the close on a CE. If that trade were opened, its stop would already be behind it: there would be no protective stop at all.

V2 skips those trades and writes the reason to the log. Measured over 2024–25 NIFTY data, that is 1,555 of 10,429 qualifying setups — 14.9%. The remaining 8,874 fired.

The side effect is worth saying plainly: in practice this means a CE is never taken while SuperTrend is red. So SuperTrend still behaves like a directional filter on roughly one setup in seven, even though it is not written as an entry rule. If you ever describe V2 as "no SuperTrend on entry", that is true of the rules and not quite true of the behaviour.

7. A winning trade, start to finish

Real NIFTY 5-minute candles from 17 April 2025, run through the real engine. Nothing below was placed by hand.

The Call that ran to the bell. At 11:15 the candle closes at 23482.35. EMA20 sits at 23410.83, above EMA50 at 23391.19 — trend up. The close is above EMA20 — rule two passes. RSI is 64.4, past 52 — rule three passes. That close becomes the trigger. The very next candle, 11:20, reaches a high of 23485.40, crossing it, so the fill happens intra-bar at 23482.35. The initial stop is the SuperTrend value at the signal bar, 23401.48 — about 81 points below entry. From there the stop ratchets up all afternoon and never once loosens: 23401.48 → 23420.78 → 23436.82 → 23468.04 … and by 13:55 it has climbed to 23790.20. It is never hit. At 14:00 the hard square-off closes the trade at 23842.40 — +360.1 spot points, held 32 candles.

Read the lower panel. RSI is only 64.4 at entry — nowhere near an extreme. It then climbs into the mid-80s and stays there for hours. Under V1's rules this trade would have been blocked outright once RSI passed 80. That is the single clearest argument for removing the cap — and also the clearest argument for why removing it will hurt on the days the market turns straight back down from an extreme. This chart is one day, not evidence.

8. A losing trade, start to finish

Real candles from 8 April 2025. Same engine, same settings, a much shorter story.

Stopped in three candles. At 11:05 a large green candle closes at 22481.15. EMA20 at 22363.99 is above EMA50 at 22271.33, the close is above EMA20, and RSI is 66.3. All three rules pass, so 22481.15 becomes the trigger. At 11:10 price reaches 22487.75, crosses it, and the fill is at 22481.15. The initial stop is the SuperTrend at the signal bar, 22318.34. But that same 11:10 candle is a large red one, and its close pulls the SuperTrend up to 22342.93 — a tighten, so the stop moves. Price never recovers. At 11:25 the candle trades down to a low of 22323.15, through the stop, and the trade is closed at 22342.93 — −138.2 spot points, held 3 candles.

This is what "median 25 points" hides. Across the 2025 backtest the median initial stop was 25.1 points. This trade's initial stop was 163 points away, and it still lost 138. SuperTrend spacing depends on how wide the recent candles were, so a strategy with no fixed stop has no fixed risk per trade. On a volatile morning the stop is far away and the loss is large; on a quiet one it is close and you are stopped by noise. Both extremes are in the numbers below.

A 0.2-point stop is not a stop. That trade is closed by the next tick of noise, and it is a real entry in these results, not a rounding artefact. A 307.9-point stop at the other end is a position whose worst case is enormous. Nothing in V2 clamps either end.

Measurement two — the real backtest, in rupees

Over 2024–25, with simulated option premiums and a 2-point slippage haircut both ways, the backtest route produced 552 trades, a 33.5% win rate, and a net of −₹77,978.

So the spot-points figure is positive and the money figure is firmly negative. There is no clever reading that reconciles them — the difference is the answer. Being right about direction in index points does not pay for the option you had to buy to express it. Premium decay and dealing costs eat the edge, and the win rate drops from 42.5% to 33.5% once every trade has to clear a spread before it counts as a winner.

Do not quote the +2,255 figure on its own. It is the strategy's signal quality, not its profit.

10. The trades it refuses

The most interesting refusal is the one described in section 6 — the setup that passes every entry rule but has nowhere to put a stop.

All three rules pass, and it still does not trade. Real candles from 8 April 2025, the same session as the losing trade above. At 10:55 the candle closes at 22374.40. EMA20 is 22347.70, above EMA50 at 22257.60 — trend up. The close is above EMA20. RSI is 56.6, past 52. Three for three, and the clock is inside the 10:30–13:00 window. But the SuperTrend line is at 22394.36 — above the close, on the wrong side. A CE opened here would have its only stop already behind it, so V2 declines and logs why. The same thing is true at 10:50 and again at 11:00. Not until 11:05, when the big candle closes at 22481.15 and leaves SuperTrend below it, does a trade become possible — and that is the trade that lost 138 points.

The other refusals.
  • Outside 10:30–13:00. No entries before or after, whatever the candle says.
  • The confirmation candle never crossed the trigger. One candle to cross, and only one.
  • Not enough history. V2 refuses outright until it has EMA_SLOW + 5 candles — 55 bars at the defaults, which is more than a whole session, so previous days are preloaded before the day starts.
  • The day's budget is spent — 2 trades done, or the loss cap hit, or two losses in a row.
  • Still in a cooldown after a recent stop-out, on either side.

11. Safety limits

  • Entries only between 10:30 and 13:00 IST. The open is skipped deliberately — its crosses reverse — and a late entry has no room left before the 14:00 close.
  • Maximum 2 trades a day. Deliberately tight. V2 is meant to take a couple of good crosses, not grind the session.
  • ₹1,000 daily loss cap. Reach it and the day is over. This is small — one ordinary loser can end the session, which is the intent while the strategy is unproven.
  • Stop for the day after 2 consecutive losses. The usual sign the market is chopping and every cross is fake.
  • 2-candle pause on the same side after a stop-out, so the engine cannot re-buy the identical failed signal ten minutes later.
  • 2-candle opposite-side cooldown, so a stopped-out CE cannot flip straight into a PE on the next candle.
  • Hard square-off at 14:00. Nothing overnight, ever.
  • The shared portfolio-wide cap applies on top of all of this.
Note how the guards interact with the loss cap. At 2 trades a day and a ₹1,000 cap, a single bad trade can end the day before the second is ever taken. That is a deliberate throttle on an unproven strategy, not a tuned number — and it means the live trade count will be lower than the backtest's 1.66 per day suggests.

12. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

Paper is the canonical surface. Every decision, fill and exit rule lives there; the other modes follow it, never the reverse.

Live is a harness that wraps Paper and forwards its decisions to the broker, so Live equals Paper by construction rather than by two implementations agreeing. It is gated three ways: EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_ENABLED must be on, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN must be off, and EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_DRY_RUN must not be on. All three ship in the safe position, so nothing places a real order out of the box.

Two brokers. Candles and quotes come from Fyers; orders go to Zerodha — the same split V1 uses. That is why starting Live checks both logins.

Replay. Because nothing in V2 reads a forming candle or live spot, a recorded session replays bit-for-bit. That is the tool to use before touching a live gate: replay a paper day and confirm Live and Paper made the same decisions.

What the backtest can and cannot tell you. The entries are honest — the backtest drives the same signal engine and fills the confirmation candle at the same market moment through a shared helper. The rupees are not. There is no historical option chain in this repo, so the premium is modelled from a seed price and decay, and the −₹77,978 figure is therefore a modelled loss, not a recorded one. It is still the more truthful of the two measurements, because at least it charges you for the spread — but treat its exact size as an estimate and its sign as the finding.

14. What's still missing / not validated

Be honest with yourself about what you are looking at.
  • It has never traded live. Not one order, not one rupee.
  • Zero paper sessions. There is no forward-tested record at all — nothing has been observed running against a live tick feed.
  • The backtest rupees are simulated. There is no historical option chain in this repo, so premiums are modelled from a seed price and decay. The −₹77,978 is a modelled loss.
  • And that modelled loss is the headline. +2,255 spot points and −₹77,978 are the same 2024–25 behaviour measured two ways. The one that includes what you actually pay is negative. Nothing here has been shown to make money.
  • The 0.2-point stop is real. The median initial stop is 25.1 points, but the minimum in the 2025 run was 0.2 points — noise level, stopped out instantly, and V2 has nothing that clamps a stop to a sensible floor. The 307.9-point maximum is the same problem at the other end.
  • The 42.5% win rate is a losing win rate on its own. It only works because the average win (+48.5) is nearly twice the average loss (−25.7). Anything that shrinks that ratio — a wider spread, a worse fill, a tighter multiplier — turns the whole thing negative, and in the rupee backtest it already has.
  • No threshold has been fitted. 52 and 48, SuperTrend 10 and 2, 2 trades a day, the ₹1,000 cap — these are stated rules and repo conventions, not values that earned their place from data.
  • V2 and V1 share no settings. Tuning one does nothing to the other. Do not assume a number that worked in V1 transfers.
  • What to do next. Run paper sessions with the tick recorder on. Replay them and confirm Paper and Live agree on a recorded day. Then decide whether the spot-points signal is worth trying to express in something cheaper than a bought option — because as an option-buying strategy, the only measurement that charges you for costs says no.
