RSI Pivot ST

Before the market opens, the bot draws two lines from yesterday's prices. It buys a Call when price breaks up through the top line with strong momentum. It buys a Put when price breaks down through the bottom line with weak momentum. Then it holds until a stop catches it.

Updated 2026-10-08 · every chart is an example built from made-up candles and checked against the real engine code

In one minute
  • Two lines, fixed for the whole day. R1 (above) and S1 (below) come from yesterday's high, low and close. They never move during the day.
  • CE (Call) entry: a 5-minute candle closes above R1 (the one before closed at or below it). The next candle must close above that breakout candle's high. On that next candle, RSI must be above 70.
  • PE (Put) entry: the mirror. A candle closes below S1, the next candle closes below its low, and RSI is below 40.
  • Which option: by default 1% of spot out of the money (about 5 strikes away).
  • Stops: CE has two — the SuperTrend line under price, and a floor 25% below the best option price. PE has only the 25% floor by default. The app-wide profit lock and breakeven stop also apply.
  • No profit target. Entries from 09:30 to 15:00. Everything is closed at 15:15. Max 5 trades and ₹5,000 loss per day.
Read this first. This strategy has not been proven with real trades. The numbers in its rules are your stated rules or round numbers, not values fitted to data. Every chart below is an example made from invented candles. The bot's real code was run on those candles to make sure each entry and exit is drawn where the code would put it. Option prices in the examples are a simple model (the option moves 0.3 rupee per NIFTY point). They show how the rules work, not whether they make money.

2. Step 1 — The two lines, drawn before the open

Each morning the bot takes yesterday's daily candle. In all the examples in this guide, yesterday's NIFTY had a high of 24,520, a low of 24,290 and a close of 24,455. The math is the classic "floor pivot" formula:

  1. PP (the middle) = (high + low + close) ÷ 3 = (24520 + 24290 + 24455) ÷ 3 = 24,421.67
  2. R1 (the CE line) = 2 × PP − low = 24,553.33
  3. S1 (the PE line) = 2 × PP − high = 24,323.33

R2 (24,651.67) and S2 (24,191.67) are also worked out and shown on the bot's own chart, but no trade uses them. The bot only trades R1 and S1.

  • The lines are worked out once and do not move all day.
  • The bot always uses the last finished day. It never uses today's still-forming daily candle.
  • If yesterday's prices are missing, there are no lines, and the bot does not trade at all that day.

What you see: the three lines for the day. At 10:15 a candle spikes above R1 with its wick, but it closes back below. Why it matters: the bot only looks at where a candle closes. A wick through the line is not a cross, so nothing happens here.

3. Step 2 — The momentum check (RSI)

A break of a line is only half the story. The bot also wants proof that the move has real force behind it. It uses RSI(14) for that.

  • For a CE: RSI must be above 70. Buyers have clearly been in control.
  • For a PE: RSI must be below 40. Sellers have clearly been in control.
  • RSI is read on the candle that makes the decision. With the default confirmation rule (next section) that is the confirming candle, not the breakout candle.
  • RSI being high on its own does nothing. Price must also cross the line. You can see RSI above 70 for an hour with no trade, because price never reached R1.

Look at the lower panel of the next chart. RSI climbs past 70 at 10:10, more than half an hour before price reaches R1. The bot waits.

4. Step 3 — Cross, then confirm

This is the entry rule for a CE, step by step. The PE rule is the exact mirror (S1 instead of R1, low instead of high, RSI below 40 instead of above 70).

  1. The breakout candle. The candle before it closed at or below R1. This candle closes above R1. That is a fresh cross. The setup is now "armed". The bot does not buy yet.
  2. The confirming candle. The very next candle must close above the breakout candle's high. Only the next candle counts. If it fails, the setup is dead. A new entry then needs a new fresh cross.
  3. RSI check. On that confirming candle, RSI must be above 70.
  4. Buy. The bot buys the CE right after the confirming candle closes.

What you see: the 10:45 candle closes at 24,561, above R1 (24,553.33). The candle before it closed at 24,543, below R1, so this is a fresh cross. The setup is armed. The 10:50 candle closes at 24,576, above the breakout high of 24,564.24, and RSI is 91.98. All checks pass, so the bot buys a CE. Why it matters: two candles in a row beyond the line show the move is really going somewhere.

Why wait for the second candle? Many breakouts poke a few points past the line and then stall. Waiting for a close above the breakout high filters out many of those. The cost is a slightly later, slightly higher entry price.

What you see: the 10:25 candle closes at 24,557, above R1. Armed. But the 10:30 candle closes at 24,551, below the breakout high of 24,560.66. The setup is dead and price fades away. RSI was above 70 the whole time, but that does not save it. Why it matters: this is exactly the "poke and stall" breakout the confirmation rule is there to skip.

Turning confirmation off. The setting RSI_PIVOT_ST_CONFIRM_ENABLED is ON by default. If you switch it OFF, the bot buys on the breakout candle itself (and reads RSI on that candle). That means earlier entries, but more false breakouts.

Small extra details:

  • The bot only decides on closed 5-minute candles. A price that moves through R1 for a few seconds inside a candle does not count.
  • A buffer setting (default 0 points) can demand that the close is some points beyond the line. At 0, any close beyond the line counts.
  • The bot needs about 20 candles of history before it can decide anything (14 for RSI, 10 for SuperTrend, plus a margin). It loads earlier candles at start-up, so normally this is ready by the open.

5. Step 4 — Which option it buys

The strike is set as a percentage of spot (default 1%), rounded to the nearest 50-point NIFTY strike. The mode decides the direction:

ModeWhat it buysExample: spot 24,576 (ATM 24,600), 1% ≈ 246 pts ≈ 5 strikes
OTM (default)1% away from the money. Cheaper, needs a bigger move.CE strike 24,850 · PE strike 24,350
ITM1% into the money. Dearer, moves more with NIFTY.CE strike 24,350 · PE strike 24,850
ATMThe strike nearest to spot. The % is ignored.24,600 for both

If the percentage is so small that it rounds to zero strikes, the bot uses ATM. The strike is worked out again from the live price at the moment of buying.

6. Step 5 — How it gets out

There is no profit target. A trade stays open until one of these exits fires. The bot checks them every couple of seconds while a trade is open.

ExitCE (default)PE (default)
App-wide profit lock / breakeven — shared by every strategyYesYes
25% premium floor — sell if the option falls 25% below its best price so farYesYes
SuperTrend stop — sell if NIFTY falls to the line (CE), or the line flipsYesNo
Square-off at 15:15YesYes

The SuperTrend stop (CE side)

What you see: SuperTrend(10, 2) on a day that goes up, down, then up again. The green line sits under price while the trend is up. When price closes below it, it flips above price and turns red. Why it matters: for a CE, the green line is the stop. A flip to red means the up-trend is over, and the bot sells at once.

  • When a CE is bought, the SuperTrend line must be under price (green). If it is above price, or still warming up, or already at or above the entry price, the bot skips the trade. It never enters a CE without this stop.
  • After each candle closes, the stop moves up to the new SuperTrend value. It never moves down, even if the line dips.
  • If NIFTY touches the stop at any moment, the bot sells. If the line flips to the down side at a candle close, the bot sells.
  • By default only CE uses SuperTrend. The setting RSI_PIVOT_ST_ST_SIDES can give it to PE too (mirrored: the line must sit above price, and a flip up is the exit), to both, or to neither.

The 25% premium floor (both sides)

  • At entry the floor is 25% under the price paid. Buy at ₹120 → floor ₹90.
  • When the option makes a new high, the floor moves up to 25% under that high. Best price ₹132.53 → floor ₹99.4.
  • The floor never comes down. If the option price touches it, the bot sells.

The app-wide profit lock and breakeven stop

These two rules live in the app's global safety settings and apply to every strategy, including this one. With the default settings:

  • Breakeven: once the option has been up 5%, the bot will not let it close below the price paid.
  • Profit lock: once the option has been up 8%, the bot keeps at least +5%. The kept amount also rises with the best price: under +20% it keeps 85% of the best gain, under +40% it keeps 80%, and above that 70%.

In practice, these two rules decide most winning exits. A 25% trail is very wide. The profit lock usually takes the trade out much earlier, with most of the gain kept.

A trade with no stop is possible — be careful. RSI_PIVOT_ST_PREMIUM_SL_SIDES (default BOTH) can switch the 25% floor off for a side. A PE has no SuperTrend by default. So if you turn the floor off for PE, a PE trade has no stop at all, except the profit lock (which only works after a gain) and the 15:15 square-off. The bot will still take the trade. It only writes a loud warning in the log.

7. A winning CE trade, start to finish

What you see: the CE from the entry chart above. The bot buys at 24,576 (the 24,850 CE at an example price of ₹110). The first stop is the SuperTrend line at 24,532.41. The orange stop line steps up after every candle: 24,547 → 24,560 → 24,575 → 24,588 → 24,598. The option reaches ₹130.42 (+18.6%), so the profit lock is armed. At 11:20 the price dips and the option falls to the locked floor of ₹127.31. The bot sells at 24,633.69. Result: +₹1,125 on one lot, before charges. Why it matters: with no fixed target, the profit lock is what turns a good move into money in the bank.

  1. 10:45 — breakout candle closes above R1. Armed.
  2. 10:50 — confirming candle closes above the breakout high. RSI 91.98. Buy.
  3. 10:55 to 11:15 — strong rise. The SuperTrend stop climbs behind it.
  4. 11:20 — a pullback. The profit lock sells. The SuperTrend stop (24,598.44) was never touched.

8. A losing CE trade

What you see: a normal-looking setup. The 10:30 candle breaks R1, the 10:35 candle confirms at 24,570, RSI is 93.51. The first stop is the SuperTrend at 24,528.25; after one candle it moves up to 24,538.32. Then price turns down. At 10:50 NIFTY falls to the stop and the bot sells at 24,538.11. The option went from ₹115 to ₹105.43. Result: −₹622. Why it matters: the stop keeps a failed breakout to a small, known loss.

9. PE trades — a win and a loss

The PE side uses S1 (24,323.33) and needs RSI below 40. By default it has no SuperTrend stop, only the 25% premium floor plus the app-wide profit lock and breakeven.

What you see: price slides all morning. The 11:00 candle closes at 24,321, below S1. Armed. The 11:05 candle closes at 24,307, below the breakout low of 24,317.68, and RSI is 7.34 (far below 40). The bot buys the 24,050 PE. Price falls further, then bounces, and the profit lock sells at 24,271.5. Result: +₹692. Why it matters: the PE rule is the mirror of the CE rule.

What you see: the same trade, but drawn as the option's price. Bought at ₹120. The red line is the 25% floor; it rises from ₹90 as the option climbs. The green line is the app-wide breakeven, then profit-lock floor. The option peaks at ₹132.53; the profit-lock floor rises to ₹130.65, and when the option falls back to it, the bot sells. Why it matters: on a winner, the green floor sits far above the red one, so the profit lock almost always exits first.

What you see: the PE is bought at 24,307 for ₹95. The floor is 25% lower, at ₹71.25. Right away price jumps back up through S1. There is no SuperTrend stop on the PE side, and the option never got 5% into profit, so the breakeven never armed. At 10:50 the option drops to ₹71.11, through the floor, and the bot sells. Result: −₹1,553. Why it matters: on a PE the 25% floor is the only real stop, and 25% of the premium is a big loss.

10. Trades it refuses

Most of the time the bot says no. Each "no" is written to the skip log with the reason.

What you see: two hours of chop just under R1. Then the 11:10 candle jumps to 24,556 (above R1) and the 11:15 candle confirms at 24,565. The line part passes. But all that chop kept RSI low: it is only 62.42, not above 70. No trade. The log says: "Confirmed above R1 … but RSI 62.42 is not above 70". Why it matters: a break with no momentum behind it often fails.

What you see: NIFTY opens above R1 and keeps rising. RSI is above 70 all morning. But no candle ever crosses R1 — price was already above it. No trade. Why it matters: buying here would be chasing a move that already happened.

Other reasons it says no:

  • The confirming candle does not close beyond the breakout candle's high (CE) or low (PE).
  • Yesterday's high/low/close is missing, so there are no lines.
  • CE setup, but SuperTrend is not under price, or is already at or above the entry price. (Same for PE if you gave PE the SuperTrend stop.)
  • The candle closes at or before 09:30, or after 15:00.
  • The day is closed: 5 trades already taken, ₹5,000 lost, or the weekly or app-wide loss cap is hit.
  • No option price could be fetched, or there is not enough free capital for the trade.

11. Time rules

  • The bot starts watching at 09:15.
  • A candle can only trigger an entry if it closes after 09:30 and by 15:00. So the 14:55 candle (which closes at 15:00) is the last one that can enter.
  • At 15:15 any open trade is sold, win or lose. Nothing is held overnight.

What you see: a late breakout. The bot buys a CE at 14:25 at 24,569. Price then drifts sideways. The stop creeps up but is never hit, and the option never gets 5% into profit. At 15:15 the bot sells anyway, at 24,569.3. Result: +₹6 before charges — after charges, a small loss. Why it matters: late entries have little time to work.

12. Bad days and quiet days

What you see: a choppy day that keeps crossing R1. Trade 1 (10:00, at 24,565) is stopped at 24,532.53. Price recovers and gives a second valid setup with RSI just 70.27 (11:25, at 24,567). That one is stopped too, at 24,531.06. Day result: −₹1,334 before charges. Why it matters: this is the normal cost of a breakout strategy. The daily limits (5 trades, ₹5,000) stop a bad day from becoming a disaster.

What you see: NIFTY moves up and down between S1 and R1 all day. RSI pokes above 70 once, right at the open, but price never gets close to R1. Zero trades. Why it matters: on a day like this the best result is no trade. The bot is quiet on many days.

15. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

  • Paper (/rsi-pivot-st-paper) — real live prices, pretend orders. This is the master copy of the rules. The other modes follow it.
  • Live (/rsi-pivot-st-live) — runs Paper and sends the same decisions to Zerodha. Prices come from Fyers; orders go to Zerodha, so both logins are checked. A real order needs all of: RSI_PIVOT_ST_LIVE_ENABLED=true, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false, and RSI_PIVOT_ST_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. By default no real order is placed.
  • Backtest (/rsi-pivot-st-backtest) — uses the same entry rules over past days. If a candle touched both a stop and a better price, it books the stop. There is no history of option prices, so the premium is a model starting at ₹180 (setting below). Treat its rupees as a rough guide, especially for PE trades whose only stop is the premium floor.
  • Replay — plays back a recorded day tick by tick, to check that Live and Paper made the same decisions.
  • Crash safety — an open trade is saved to disk. After a restart the bot checks it against the broker and resumes with the same stops.

17. What we do not know yet

  • No proven track record. There is no measured win rate, profit factor or drawdown for this strategy.
  • No tuned numbers. RSI 70/40, SuperTrend 10/2, the 25% floor and the 1% strike are rules and round numbers, not results of testing.
  • Backtest rupees are a model. There is no history of option prices, so the premium floor can only be estimated in a backtest.
  • The PE side is weaker protected. By default its only stop before a gain is the 25% premium floor.
  • Next step: run Paper for many days, replay the recorded days, and only then think about Live with the dry-run brake on.
