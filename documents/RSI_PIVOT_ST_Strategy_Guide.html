RSI Pivot ST

Two lines are drawn on the chart before the market even opens, and they never move again. This strategy waits all day for a five-minute candle to push through one of them while the momentum reading agrees — and then it buys an option and holds it until a stop catches up.

Updated 2026-08-18  ·  plain-English edition  ·  every chart below was produced by running the real engine

Read this first: this strategy has never traded. Not one paper session, not one live order, and no backtest has been run against it either. Every number in the rules is either your own stated rule or a repo convention — nothing here has been fitted to data, because there is no data. The charts below are the real engine run on constructed candles, for illustration only. They show you what the rule does; they say nothing about whether the rule makes money.

1. The idea in one minute

Floor traders have used the same three-number arithmetic for decades. Take yesterday's high, low and close, average them, and you get the pivot point — a rough centre of gravity for today. Reflect yesterday's low across it and you get R1, a resistance line above. Reflect yesterday's high across it and you get S1, a support line below.

Those two lines are the strategy's entire map of the day, and the reason they are useful is precisely that they are boring: they are computed once, from bars that have already finished, and they do not move. Every other line this app draws — an EMA, a Bollinger band, a VWAP — shifts under your feet as the session runs. R1 and S1 do not.

The idea is then simple. If price is strong enough to push through R1 and close there, and the momentum reading agrees that it is genuinely strong, buy a Call. If it is weak enough to break down through S1 and close there with momentum agreeing, buy a Put. There is no profit target — you hold until a stop trails into you, or until the day ends.

2. Step 1 — The two lines, drawn before the open

The arithmetic, with the numbers this guide uses throughout. Yesterday (17 Aug 2026 in the examples) NIFTY made a high of 24520, a low of 24290, and closed at 24455. So:
  • PP = (24520 + 24290 + 24455) ÷ 3 = 24421.67
  • R1 = 2 × 24421.67 − 24290 = 24553.33  ← the Call trigger
  • S1 = 2 × 24421.67 − 24520 = 24323.33  ← the Put trigger

R2 and S2 come out of the same formula and are drawn on the chart for context, but nothing trades off them.

The bar that feeds this must be YESTERDAY, not today. A daily candle that is still forming would make R1 and S1 drift all session, and then no replay and no backtest could ever reproduce an entry. The engine walks backwards through the daily series to the newest completed bar strictly before the day being traded, which is also what lets a backtest step through history without peeking at the day it is trading. If there is no usable prior daily bar, the answer is "cannot trade" — never zero.

3. Step 2 — The entry needs both halves

An entry is only ever decided on a closed five-minute candle, and both tests must pass on that same candle. Reading the RSI on the very bar that does the crossing is deliberate: it removes any lag between the two halves of the rule.

SideMomentum halfLevel half
CE (Call)RSI(14) above 70 on the signal candleThe candle crosses and closes above R1 — the previous close sat at or below R1, this one closes above
PE (Put)RSI(14) below 40 on the signal candleThe candle crosses and closes below S1 — the previous close sat at or above S1, this one closes below
"Crosses" is doing real work in that sentence. A candle that is already sitting above R1 and closes higher still is not a cross. Taking it would be chasing a move that already happened — the level was breached some candles ago and you would be paying a worse price for the same idea. The engine requires the transition itself: below-or-at, then above.

4. Step 3 — Which option gets bought

The strike is chosen from the signal candle's closing price, as a percentage of spot, in the direction the setting picks. The default is OTM at 1%.

ModeWhat it doesWorked example at spot 24566
OTM default1% away from the money — CE above spot, PE below1% of 24566 = 245.66 pts → 5 strikes of 50 → CE strike 24800
ITM1% into the money — the mirrorCE strike would be 24300
ATMNearest strike to spot; the percentage is ignored24550

The raw distance is rounded to the nearest 50-point NIFTY strike. If the percentage is so small that it rounds to zero steps, the engine falls back to ATM rather than inventing a strike that does not exist.

5. Step 4 — The stops, and why CE and PE differ

The two sides are stopped differently, and that is the rule — not a bug and not an oversight. If you are reading this later and thinking of "balancing" the two sides, don't. It is written this way on purpose, and it is stated identically in the engine header, in the paper route header and here.

CE — two stops, both live, whichever fires first

  • SuperTrend(10, 2) on the five-minute spot chart. It is both the initial stop and the trail. It only ever moves in the trade's favour — a SuperTrend that ticks down mid-trade is ignored, because a trail that can loosen is not a trail. And if the line flips bearish, that flip is itself an exit: the reason for being long has gone.
  • A 25% premium floor. The option's own price is watched, and the exit sits 25% below the highest premium the trade has seen. Buy at ₹180 and the floor starts at ₹135; if the premium runs to ₹240 the floor ratchets up to ₹180, which is your entry price. It never comes back down.

PE — one stop only

  • The 25% premium floor, and nothing else. There is no SuperTrend on the Put side.
A CE with no valid stop is refused, not entered. If the SuperTrend is still warming up, or has flipped bearish, or already sits at or above the entry close — meaning the trade would be stopped out on its very first tick — the engine declines the setup and says so in the skip log. It will not open a position it cannot protect.

What is NOT here

There is no profit target at all, on either side. The trade runs until a stop trails into it or the 15:15 square-off. And deliberately absent, so nobody adds them back in later "helpfully": no VIX gate, no Open Interest filter, no ADX, no volume test, no EMA, no VWAP, no ATR sizing, no multi-timeframe bias, no extra confirmation candle, no breakeven jump, no partial booking, no re-entry after a stop, no expiry-day rule.

6. A CE trade, start to finish

This is the real engine, fed the candles you see, with every setting left at its default. Nothing below was placed by hand.

The Call side. Price grinds down all morning, turns, and at 11:15 a candle opens at 24549 and closes at 24566 — the first close above R1 at 24553, with the previous candle having closed at 24549, just below it. RSI on that same bar is 87.27, comfortably past 70, so both halves pass and the engine buys the 24800 CE (OTM, 5 strikes out). The initial stop is the SuperTrend line at 24509.95, 56 points below entry. Over the next seven candles the trail ratchets up 24525 → 24538 → 24548 → 24556 → 24562 → 24565 → 24570.49, and there it sticks. The rally stalls near 24618, rolls over, and at 12:05 the candle's low of 24569 reaches the trail — EXIT 24570.49, +4 spot points. A trade that looked like a clean 50-point winner at its best gave nearly all of it back, because there is no target and no breakeven rule to bank anything on the way.

Read the lower panel. The RSI sub-chart shows the momentum half of the rule doing its job: RSI only clears 70 at 10:45, several candles before the level is broken. Being above 70 is not itself a signal — the cross is. That is why the strategy is quiet for most of a session even on a strongly trending day.

7. A PE trade, start to finish

The Put side uses a different prior day — high 24640, low 24410, close 24470 — which puts S1 at 24373.33.

The Put side, and the premium stop alone. Price chops sideways in a narrow drift for two hours, then rolls. At 11:10 the candle opens at 24382 — the previous close, sitting just above S1 at 24373 — and closes at 24356, below it. RSI on that bar is 19.3, far under 40, so the engine buys the 24100 PE (OTM, 5 strikes out). No spot stop is set at all: the Put side has no SuperTrend, so the only protection is the 25% premium floor. Price falls another 78 points to 24278 by 11:40 before turning; then it climbs back, and the exit is decided entirely by the option's own price rather than by any line on this chart. The dashed line marks 24278, the low that set the trade's best premium.

The premium floor, step by step on that trade. Assume the 24100 PE was bought at ₹120 with a delta around 0.45 — a plausible simulation, not a recorded quote:

The premium peaks at ₹155.10, which pins the floor at ₹116.32. It never moves down again. At 12:10 the premium prints ₹112.80, under the floor, and the trade is closed — a small loss on an entry that was 78 points in profit at its best. That is the cost of a 25% giveback rule with no target.

8. The trades it refuses

Most of what an engine does is decline. Here is the most common refusal, again from the real engine.

The level broke, and it still did not trade. Price saws in a tight range just under R1 for two hours, then at 11:15 a single big candle opens at 24500 and closes at 24560, decisively above R1 at 24553. The level half of the rule passes. But the sideways grind kept RSI pinned in the 40s, and that one candle only lifts it to 64.96 — short of 70 — so the engine refuses and logs exactly why: "Closed above R1 24553.33 (24500 → 24560) but RSI 64.96 is not above 70." A breakout out of a range with no momentum behind it is precisely the breakout that fails.

The other refusals, and what each one is protecting you from.
  • RSI is past 70 but there is no fresh cross — price is already above R1. Entering here is chasing.
  • No prior daily bar — there is no R1 or S1 to trade, so there is no trade. Never treated as zero.
  • CE setup, SuperTrend not bullish — no initial stop can be placed, so the entry is declined rather than opened unprotected.
  • CE setup, SuperTrend at or above entry — the trade would stop out on its first tick.
  • Outside 09:30–15:00, or the daily trade budget is spent.
  • A candle with unusable OHLC, or a series that turns out to carry one bar per day rather than intraday bars — the engine refuses to decide rather than compute nonsense.

Every refusal names which half failed. A skip log that only says "no signal" teaches nothing.

9. Safety limits

  • Max 5 trades a day. The pivot levels are fixed all day, so only a handful of genuine crosses can honestly occur — a higher number would mean the rule is firing on noise.
  • Max ₹5,000 loss in a day, then the day is over.
  • Weekly loss cap is off by default (0). It reads the per-day log files, so it survives a restart.
  • Entries only between 09:30 and 15:00. The first candles of the day are noisy and often break a pivot for no reason; late entries have no room to work out. The bar's close time is what is gated, so the 14:55 bar — closing at 15:00 — is still legal.
  • Everything is squared off at 15:15. Nothing is carried overnight, ever.
  • The shared portfolio-wide cap applies on top of all of this.

10. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

Paper (/rsi-pivot-st-paper) is the canonical surface. Every decision, fill and exit rule lives there; the other two modes follow it, never the reverse.

Live (/rsi-pivot-st-live) does not re-implement anything — it wraps Paper and forwards its decisions to the broker, so Live equals Paper by construction. It is triple-gated to dry-run: a real order needs RSI_PIVOT_ST_LIVE_ENABLED=true and LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false and RSI_PIVOT_ST_LIVE_DRY_RUN not-true, plus a logged-in broker. By default nothing places a real order.

Two brokers. The candles and quotes come from Fyers, as everywhere in this app, but the orders go to Zerodha — the same split EMA_RSI_ST uses. That is why starting Live checks both logins.

Crash recovery. An open position is snapshotted to .active_rsi_pivot_st_position.json and reconciled against the broker's book on the next boot. Both stops are recomputed from values the route already persists, so a recovered position resumes on exactly the same numbers.

What the backtest can and cannot tell you. It drives the same signal engine, so the entries are honest, and it is conservative about intra-bar order: the adverse stop is tested on the bar's high/low before anything favourable, so a bar that touched both books the loss; a bar that opened beyond a level fills at the open, never at the better level; and the SuperTrend trail advances only on a bar close, matching Paper. It also fetches a separate daily series padded a week before the range, so the very first session still has a yesterday to compute R1/S1 from.

But the rupees are simulated. There is no historical option chain, so the premium is modelled from delta and theta seeded at ₹180, and the 25% floor is applied to that model rather than to a real quote. Since the premium stop is the only stop on the PE side, PE results in particular should be read as directional, not as a P&L you could have collected.

12. What's still missing / not validated

Be honest with yourself about what you are looking at.
  • It has never traded. Not once. Zero paper sessions, zero live orders. There is no win rate, no profit factor, no drawdown figure, because there are no trades.
  • No threshold here has been fitted or backtested. RSI 70 and 40, SuperTrend 10 and 2, the 25% floor, 1% OTM, 5 trades a day — every one is either your stated rule or a round number borrowed from the rest of this repo. None of them earned its value from data.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated, not observed. There is no historical option chain, so the premium is a delta-plus-theta model seeded at ₹180. The premium stop is the weakest part of that simulation — and on the PE side it is the only stop, so a PE backtest is essentially a test of the model rather than of the market.
  • The core claim is untested. Does a five-minute close through yesterday's R1, with RSI above 70, actually lead anywhere? Nobody here knows. The charts in this guide demonstrate the mechanics on constructed candles; they are not evidence.
  • The CE example above is a warning, not a showcase. It reached roughly +50 points and exited at +4, because there is no target and no breakeven rule. Whether that giveback is acceptable is exactly the kind of question only real sessions can answer.
  • What to do next. Run paper sessions with the tick recorder on. Diff them against /replay to confirm Paper and Live agree on a recorded day. Only then consider touching a live gate — and even then, remember trades produced before any bug fix were produced by different code.
