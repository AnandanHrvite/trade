HA Scalp

A Heikin Ashi candle with no wick, in the direction of the 50-candle average, on the 15-minute NIFTY chart. The bot buys one CE or PE, puts the stop under (or over) that candle, and gets out when the candles say the push is over.

In one minute

  1. The bot reads 15-minute candles of the NIFTY 50 index. It turns them into Heikin Ashi (HA) candles — smoothed candles that make a trend easy to see.
  2. Pick the side: if the last real close is above the 50-candle simple moving average (50 SMA), only a CE (call, gains when NIFTY rises) is allowed. Below it, only a PE (put, gains when NIFTY falls).
  3. The signal: a green HA candle with no bottom wick (for CE) or a red HA candle with no top wick (for PE). Its body must be at least 5 points and it must not be a doji.
  4. Entry: at the very next price after that candle closes (the next candle's open). One lot of a strike one step in-the-money.
  5. Stop-loss: the signal candle's real low minus 15 points (CE) or real high plus 15 points (PE). It moves to breakeven at +20 points and trails 20 points behind the best price from +25 points.
  6. Exit on the stop, a doji HA candle, an opposite-colour HA candle, a weak HA candle, the all-strategy profit lock, or the 15:15 square-off. There is no fixed target.
  7. Day limits: at most 3 trades, stop after 2 stop-outs or a ₹3,000 loss.

1 What a Heikin Ashi candle is

"Heikin Ashi" is Japanese for "average bar". Each HA candle is built from the normal (raw) candle with four short sums.

HA close = (open + high + low + close) ÷ 4      ← average of this candle's 4 prices
HA open  = (previous HA open + previous HA close) ÷ 2   ← middle of the last HA body
HA high  = the highest of: high, HA open, HA close
HA low   = the lowest of:  low,  HA open, HA close
  • The candle remembers. HA open comes from the HA candle before it. That is why colours change less often and trends look clean. The bot waits until at least 20 candles of history exist before it trusts the colours, and it needs 51 candles in total (for the 50 SMA), so it loads about 15 days of history when it starts. The HA chain also runs across nights, just like TradingView draws it.
  • An HA price never traded. HA close is an average — nobody bought or sold there. So the bot decides with HA candles (colour, wick, doji, weak) but uses raw prices for the entry and the stop.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: one day of normal 15-minute NIFTY candles. Colours flip often, and wicks stick out on both ends. It is hard to say when the push really started. Why it matters: this is the chart the exchange prints. The bot's entry and stop prices come from here.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: the same day as Heikin Ashi candles. The midday rise becomes a clean run of green candles. The 50 SMA (blue) is below price, so only CE trades are allowed. At 11:00 a green candle still has a bottom wick, so the bot skips it. The 12:00 candle is green with no bottom wick — the signal. The bot buys a CE at the next open, 24,402. The stop starts at 24,366, then jumps up as the trade gains. At 13:00 the option's premium slips back to the locked floor and the profit lock sells: EXIT 24,436, +34 points (roughly ₹1,328 on one lot). Why it matters: one picture, the whole idea.

3 Step 1 — Which side we may trade

Before looking at any candle shape, the bot asks one question: is the last raw close above or below the 50 SMA?

Close above the 50 SMA → CE only

Only green HA candles with no bottom wick can open a trade.

Close below the 50 SMA → PE only

Only red HA candles with no top wick can open a trade.

If the close sits exactly on the SMA, the bot stands aside. The SMA is computed on raw closes, the way a normal chart draws it. You can switch it to an EMA in Settings, but the rules were written for a plain SMA.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: NIFTY bounces hard in the morning. At 11:00 the HA candle is green, has a 23-point body and no bottom wick — on shape alone, a perfect CE signal. But the raw close (24,334) is still below the 50 SMA (24,347). The trend says PE only, so the bot refuses: "no entry against the trend". Later, price is above the SMA, but those green candles all have bottom wicks. Why it matters: the SMA gate is the rule that stops the bot from buying bounces inside a downtrend.

Not too far from the average

The bot also skips a setup when the raw close is more than 150 points away from the 50 SMA. A move that far from its average is already stretched.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a strong rally day. At 09:45 a big green HA candle with no bottom wick forms — but the close is 159.2 points above the 50 SMA. The purple line marks SMA + 150. Every candle this day sits above it, so the bot logs "move already stretched, no entry" all day. Why it matters: late entries into an over-extended move often catch the top. (Paper trades entered 150–220 points from the SMA all lost, which is why the 150 default exists.)

4 Step 2 — The candle with no wick

With the side settled, the bot looks at the HA candle that has just closed. It checks these in order. The first "no" ends the check.

  1. Is it a doji? If the body is 20% of the range or less, it is a doji — never an entry.
  2. Is it the right colour? Green for a CE, red for a PE.
  3. Is the body big enough? At least 5 points. (A flat candle has "no wick" by accident.)
  4. Is the wick zero on the side that matters? CE: no bottom wick. PE: no top wick. The default allows 0% of the range — exactly zero.
  5. Is there room for the stop? The raw close must be above the stop (CE) or below it (PE). An optional maximum stop distance can also skip very wide stops (off by default).
Why "no wick" means something

On a green HA candle the bottom of the body is the HA open. No wick below it means that for the whole 15 minutes, the real price never went under that level. Sellers never got a look in. Because of how HA candles are built, this happens quite often in a real trend — the rule is strict, but it does fire.

When signals are allowed

The candle must close after 09:30 and by 15:00. So the first possible signal candle is 09:30–09:45, and the last is 14:45–15:00. The 09:15 candle can never be a signal.

The overview chart in Section 1 shows both a refused candle (a bottom wick at 11:00) and the accepted one at 12:00.

5 Step 3 — Entry and stop

Both numbers come from the raw candle, never the HA one.

Rule
EntryThe first NIFTY price after the signal candle closes — the open of the next candle. If that price is already past the stop, the entry is cancelled.
Stop (CE)Signal candle's raw low − 15 points.
Stop (PE)Signal candle's raw high + 15 points.
TargetNone.

The 15-point cushion exists because stops right on the candle's low were hit by normal noise within minutes in paper trading.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: NIFTY is below the 50 SMA, so only PE trades count. The 09:45 HA candle is red with no top wick (body 15 pts). The bot buys a PE at the next open, 24,226. The stop is that candle's raw high 24,241 + 15 = 24,256. At 10:30 the HA candle is still red but its body is only 29% of its range — a weak candle — so the bot exits at 24,217, +9 points. Why it matters: for a PE everything is upside-down: below the SMA, red candle, stop above.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a valid CE signal at 11:30. Raw low 24,391 (grey line) − 15 gives a stop at 24,376. The bot buys at 24,395. In the same 15 minutes NIFTY drops and touches the stop: −20 points, about ₹762 lost. Why it matters: the risk of each trade is set by how tall the signal candle was. This counts as a stop-out for the day limit.

6 Step 4 — How a trade ends

First one to happen wins.

ExitCheckedPlain words
Profit lockEvery tickAll-strategy rule on the option premium (on by default). Once the premium is 8% above the buy price, the trade may never be sold below +5%. The floor then rises with the best premium: it keeps 85% of the gain while the gain is under 20%, 80% under 40%, and 70% above that.
StopEvery tickNIFTY touches the current stop (first, breakeven or trailed).
BreakevenCandle closeOnce the best NIFTY price is 20 points in our favour, the stop moves to the entry price.
TrailCandle closeOnce the best price is 25 points in our favour, the stop sits 20 points behind the best price. It only tightens.
DojiCandle closeAn HA candle with a body of 20% of its range or less — of any colour.
Opposite colourCandle closeA red HA candle while holding a CE (or green while holding a PE).
WeakCandle closeRight colour, but the body is under 40% of the range.
Square-offClockAt 15:15 anything still open is sold.

The candle-close exits never look at the signal candle itself — only candles after it. They can fire on the very candle the trade was bought in, when it closes.

Doji exit

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a CE bought at 24,388. The next HA candle (10:30) has a body of only 6 points in a 31-point range — 18%, a doji. The bot exits at that candle's close, 24,384 (-5 pts). Why it matters: the trade was opened on one-sided strength. A doji says that strength is gone, so the bot does not wait for the stop.

Opposite-colour exit

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a CE bought at 24,412. The push fades and the 13:00 HA candle closes red. The bot exits at 24,391 (-20 pts, about ₹797 lost). Why it matters: the stop was 34 points away; the red candle got the bot out earlier, for a smaller loss.

Breakeven

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a CE bought at 24,368. NIFTY reaches 24,392, 23 points in our favour. At that candle's close the stop moves up from 24,341 to the entry price, 24,368. Price falls back and touches it: the trade ends flat (only costs lost). Why it matters: without breakeven this trade could have become a full loss. Note: a breakeven exit still counts as a stop-out for the day limit.

Trailing stop

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a CE bought at 24,380. NIFTY reaches 24,406 (+26 points). That is past 25, so at the candle close the stop jumps straight to best − 20 = 24,385 — tighter than breakeven. The next dip touches it: EXIT 24,385, +5 points. Why it matters: the trail was added after the first 14 paper trades all gave back their open profit. Here it banks a small win instead of a loss.

Profit lock

In the overview chart, the option was bought at about ₹240 (example price). It peaked near ₹264 (+10%), which armed the lock. The floor became ₹260 (keeping 85% of the gain). When the premium fell back to it, the bot sold. In NIFTY terms that was 24,436, 6 points below the best price. This exit does not count as a stop-out.

Square-off at 15:15

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a late CE signal at 14:30, bought at 24,359 at 14:45. No stop, doji or weak candle ends it. At 15:15 the bot sells: 24,380, +21 points. After 15:00 no new signal is allowed (red shade). Why it matters: nothing is held overnight.

8 A no-trade day

Example — not a real trade
What you see: NIFTY drifts sideways around the 50 SMA. Candles flip colour, many are dojis, and the few strong ones have wicks on the wrong side. The bot logs a reason at every candle close but takes no trade. Why it matters: sideways days are where the SMA gate and the no-wick rule earn their keep — by doing nothing.

9 Worked example

The overview trade from Section 1, step by step.

  1. 12:00 candle closes. Raw close 24,402 is above the 50 SMA (24,322) — CE side, and only 80 points away (under 150).
  2. The HA candle is green: HA open 24375.2, HA close 24395.8. Body 20.6 pts out of a 35.4-pt range (58% — not a doji). HA low = HA open, so no bottom wick. Signal!
  3. 12:15 — the first price of the next candle is 24,402. Buy one lot of the CE one strike in-the-money.
  4. Stop = raw low 24,381 − 15 = 24,366 (37 points of risk).
  5. NIFTY climbs to 24,442. The stop moves up with the trail. The option premium rises over 8%, so the profit lock arms.
  6. 13:00 — NIFTY slips back; the premium touches the locked floor. Sold at about 24,436: +34 points, roughly ₹1,328 on one lot before costs (using 0.6 rupee per point × 65 units).

10 Paper, Live, Backtest and Replay

All four call the same engine file, so the decisions match. Paper is the reference.

ModeWhat it does
Paper /ha-scalp-paperLive data, pretend orders. Candles come from the Fyers history feed at each 15-minute close (about 5 seconds after, so the candle is published). The live tick feed is used only to test the stop and to fill the entry. Real option quotes are used for P&L and the profit lock.
Live /ha-scalp-liveRuns the paper engine and copies each decision to Zerodha as a real order. Needs both logins (Fyers for data, Zerodha for orders). A real order needs all three: HA_SCALP_LIVE_ENABLED=true, LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false, and HA_SCALP_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. It has never placed a live order.
Backtest /ha-scalp-backtestPast 15-minute candles. Fills at the next candle's open. The stop is tested on the candle's low/high before the candle-close exits, so a candle that did both counts as the loss. A gap past the stop fills at the open. The option price is estimated (0.55 per point, about 8 points of decay a day, ₹240 starting premium) with 1.5 points of slippage each way. It does not model the all-strategy premium profit lock, so its exits can differ from paper.
Replay /replayPlays back a recorded day tick by tick, to compare Paper and Live decisions.

12 Good to know

  • Deliberately not here: no VIX gate, no OI filter, no RSI, ADX, ATR, VWAP or SuperTrend, no volume test, no second timeframe, no fixed target, no partial booking. VIX is only recorded with each trade.
  • Wide or tiny stops. The stop depends on how tall the signal candle was. The 15-point buffer fixes the tiny ones; HA_SCALP_MAX_SL_PTS can skip the very wide ones (off by default).
  • Live has never traded. Collect clean paper sessions, compare them with /replay, and only then think about live.
  • Backtest rupees are rough. No real option prices, and no premium profit lock. Use it for direction, not for exact P&L.
  • Tune on clean data. The trail (15 Sept 2026), the 15-point buffer and the 150-point SMA guard changed the rules. Older trades came from different rules.
