HA Scalp

Ordinary candles are noisy — every wick is a fight that may mean nothing. Heikin Ashi candles smooth that away and leave a simpler picture: a run of same-coloured candles is a trend, and a candle with no wick at all on one side is a candle where the other side never showed up. This strategy waits for exactly that candle, in the direction the 50-period average says the market is already going.

Read this first: this strategy has never traded. Not one paper session, not one live order, zero trades in existence. 1. The idea in one minute

A normal candlestick tells you four prices and a lot of noise. Price can spend fifteen minutes thrashing up and down, finish where it started, and leave a candle with long wicks on both ends that tells you almost nothing about direction.

Heikin Ashi — Japanese for "average bar" — replaces each candle with a smoothed version of itself. Colours stop flickering. A trend shows up as a long run of one colour, and the moments where one side of the market completely gives up show up as a candle with no wick on that side.

That wick-free candle is the whole signal. If a Heikin Ashi candle is green and has no bottom wick, then across that entire fifteen minutes, nothing traded below where the candle opened. Sellers never got a look in. This strategy buys a Call on that candle — but only if the market is already above its 50-period average, so it is pushing with the trend, not against it.

50 MA says UP → Calls only→ Green HA candle, no bottom wick→ Buy at the next candle's open→ Stop = that candle's real low→ Exit on a doji or a weak candle

Everything below the mirror-images for a downtrend: below the 50 MA, red Heikin Ashi candle with no top wick, buy a Put, stop at that candle's real high.

One instrument, one chart. Every decision is read off 15-minute NIFTY 50 spot candles. There is no VIX gate, no open-interest filter, no RSI, no ADX, no ATR, no volume test and no second timeframe. The 50 MA and the shape of one Heikin Ashi candle are the entire input.

2. What a Heikin Ashi candle is

A Heikin Ashi candle is built from the ordinary candle, using four short formulas. This is the only definition used anywhere in this repo:

haClose = (open + high + low + close) / 4        ← the average of this bar's four prices
haOpen  = (previous haOpen + previous haClose) / 2 ← the midpoint of the last HA candle
haHigh  = max(high, haOpen, haClose)
haLow   = min(low,  haOpen, haClose)

Two consequences matter, and they matter a great deal.

First: the candle remembers. haOpen is built from the previous HA candle, which was built from the one before it. Each candle carries an echo of everything that came before, which is exactly why the colours stop flickering — and also why the chain has to be warmed up before it means anything. The engine refuses to make any decision until HA_SCALP_HA_WARMUP_BARS (20) candles are behind it. It also keeps the chain running across the overnight gap rather than restarting each morning, because that is what TradingView draws, and the rules were read off a TradingView chart.

Second, and this is the trap: a Heikin Ashi price never traded. haClose is the average of four numbers. No buyer and no seller ever met there. So the engine splits the two jobs cleanly and never mixes them:
  • Decisions — colour, wick, doji, weakness — are read off the Heikin Ashi candle.
  • Prices — the entry reference and the stop level — are read off the raw candle.
Putting a stop at a Heikin Ashi low would place it at a price the market never printed. That is the classic Heikin Ashi mistake and it silently invents fills that could not have happened.

The raw chart — 20 March 2026. Wicks everywhere, colours alternating. Hard to say what the trend is doing at 11:00.

The same day in Heikin Ashi. The noise is gone and the afternoon downtrend is a clean run of red candles. This is the chart every rule in this guide is read from.

4. Step 1 — Which side we're allowed to take

Before any candle shape is considered, one question is asked: where is price relative to its 50-period moving average? The average is computed on raw closes, not Heikin Ashi ones, because that is what a charting platform draws by default.

Raw close ABOVE the 50 MA
CE only

Calls, and only on a green Heikin Ashi candle with no bottom wick.

Raw close BELOW the 50 MA
PE only

Puts, and only on a red Heikin Ashi candle with no top wick.

This is a hard gate, and it is the rule that does the most work. A textbook-perfect green wick-free candle sitting below a falling average is not a trade — it is a bounce inside a downtrend, and the engine says so in the log rather than quietly skipping it. Section 8 shows a real one.

There is deliberately no drawn trend line. A hand-drawn line depends on which swing points you pick, so two people get two different answers and nothing is reproducible. The 50 MA is a number anyone can recompute.

5. Step 2 — The candle with no wick

With the side settled, the strategy looks at the Heikin Ashi candle that has just closed and asks three things.

  1. Is it the right colour? Green for a Call, red for a Put. A candle of the wrong colour is never an entry, whatever else it does.
  2. Is it wick-free on the side that matters? For a Call, no bottom wick — nothing traded below the open. For a Put, no top wick. The tolerance is HA_SCALP_MAX_WICK_PCT, and it ships at 0: the wick must be exactly zero.
  3. Is the body real? At least HA_SCALP_MIN_BODY_PTS (5) points. Without this, a completely flat candle satisfies "no wick" trivially and would count as a signal.
Why "no wick" means something. On a green Heikin Ashi candle, the bottom of the body is the open. If there is no wick below it, then for the whole fifteen minutes, price never once dipped under where the candle started. Every attempt to sell was absorbed instantly. That is what the strategy is buying: not a prediction, but a candle where one side of the market visibly stopped participating.

The wick is measured as a percentage of the candle's own range, so the tolerance means the same thing on a quiet 20-point bar and a violent 200-point one. Raising HA_SCALP_MAX_WICK_PCT to 5 or 10 loosens the entry considerably; leaving it at 0 is the rule as specified, and it is strict on purpose.

6. Step 3 — Where we get in and where we're wrong

Both numbers come off the raw candle, never the Heikin Ashi one.

Where it comes from
EntryThe next candle's open. The signal fires the instant the 15-minute candle closes, and the fill is the very next price the market prints. Paper and Live take the first tick after the close; Backtest takes the next raw bar's open — the same event.
StopThe signal candle's own raw low for a Call, or its raw high for a Put, plus HA_SCALP_SL_BUFFER_PTS (0 by default). It is frozen at entry and never moved again.
TargetThere isn't one. No target, no trailing stop, no breakeven jump, no partial booking. The trade runs until a candle tells it to stop.

The logic of the stop is simple: the signal candle was one where price never traded below its open. If price now goes back below that candle's low, the thing that made it a signal is no longer true, and the reason for the trade is gone.

Because the stop is the candle's own extreme, the trade's risk is decided entirely by how tall that candle happened to be. That is not a detail — it is the strategy's biggest unresolved problem, and section 10 is about it.

7. Step 4 — How we get out

Four ways out, tested in this order:

ExitWhenChecked
StopSpot reaches the frozen level from section 6.Every tick
DojiAn HA candle closes with a body of 20% of its range or less (HA_SCALP_DOJI_BODY_PCT). Colour is irrelevant — a doji ends the trend question either way.Each 15-min close
Weak / oppositeAn HA candle closes the opposite colour, or stays the right colour but its body shrinks below 40% of range (HA_SCALP_WEAK_BODY_PCT).Each 15-min close
Square-off15:15 IST (HA_SCALP_FORCED_EXIT). Anything still open is closed.Clock

The reasoning is the same as the entry: the trade was opened because a candle showed one-sided strength, so it is closed as soon as a candle stops showing it. A doji means the fight is even again. A shrinking body means the push is running out. Neither waits for price to come all the way back to the stop.

Here is a real one, drawn from the engine's own output.

23 March 2026 — a Put that ran. Spot is far below its 50 SMA, so only Puts are allowed. The 09:30 HA candle closes red with a 225-point body and no top wick — buyers never got above the open. Entry at the next candle's open, 22,706; stop frozen at that candle's raw high, 22,743 (36 points). Five candles later the HA candle at 11:00 is a doji, and the trade is closed at 22,555 — 151 spot points in the trade's favour. Note the stop was never moved: it sat at 22,743 the whole way.

8. The trades it refuses

Over the six months measured, the single most common reason a good-looking candle was thrown away was the trend gate. Here is one of them.

20 March 2026, 09:30 — the best candle of the day, refused. A green Heikin Ashi candle with a 149-point body and no bottom wick: on shape alone, a textbook Call. But that candle closes at 23266.16 while the 50 SMA is up at 23469 — price is 203 points below its average. This is a bounce inside a downtrend, not a trend continuation, so the engine refuses it and logs why. The rest of the day proves the gate right: price drifts down to close at 23114.85.

The engine states every refusal in plain words rather than staying silent, so the log shows a setup was seen and rejected on purpose rather than missed. Across the six-month sample, 227 bullish wick-free candles were refused for sitting below the average — against 547 that were eligible, so the gate threw away nearly a third of the shapes that otherwise qualified.

10. The stop-size problem

This is the thing to watch in paper trading, and it is a direct consequence of the rule "the stop is the signal candle's own extreme".

Measured across the same 358 real signals, the distance from entry to stop was:

Smallest25th pctMedian75th pctLargest
0.2 pt10.75 pt23.25 pt37.9 pt129.4 pt

The median of 23 points is reasonable. The problem is the bottom of the distribution: 81 of the 358 signals — 23% — had a stop less than 10 points away, and the tightest was 0.2 points.

Ten points on a 15-minute NIFTY chart is noise. It is smaller than the ordinary bar-to-bar wobble, so a stop that close is not measuring "the trade idea was wrong" — it is measuring "the market breathed". Nearly a quarter of all signals were handed a stop that a random tick could take out.

Here is the extreme case, and it is a real trade the engine produced:

6 April 2026, 12:00 — stopped out on the entry candle itself. A perfectly valid signal: green HA candle, 30-point body, no bottom wick, price comfortably above the 50 SMA. But the signal candle's raw low was 22,659.40 and the next candle opened at 22,660.35 — a stop 0.2 points away. The trade was stopped within the same bar, for a loss of about one point of spot. Then price ran to 22,731, and later to 22,998 — the direction was right and the trade caught none of it.

This is the single clearest thing paper trading needs to answer. Two levers exist for it, both shipped OFF so the rules run exactly as specified until there is evidence:

  • HA_SCALP_SL_BUFFER_PTS — pushes the stop a few points past the candle's extreme, so a one-tick poke doesn't take it out.
  • HA_SCALP_MAX_SL_PTS — skips setups whose stop is too far away. Note this fixes the opposite end of the distribution: it does nothing about the 0.2-point stops.

There is currently no minimum stop-distance setting. Whether one is needed is exactly the sort of question a clean paper sample should decide, rather than a guess made in advance.

Position size is 1 lot, one strike in-the-money (HA_SCALP_ITM_STEPS = 1, roughly delta 0.6). Orders route to Zerodha; Fyers supplies the candles and the option premiums.

12. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

ModeWhat it does
PaperLive data, simulated orders. This is the canonical surface — every decision, fill and exit rule is defined here, and the other modes are made to match it, never the reverse.
BacktestHistorical candles. Applies a spread/slippage haircut of HA_SCALP_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS (1.5 points each way) and assumes a seed premium, because an option-buying backtest without costs always flatters.
LiveReal Zerodha orders. Double-gated: needs HA_SCALP_LIVE_ENABLED and the global dry-run switch turned off. It ships off, and should stay off until paper and replay agree.
ReplayRe-runs a recorded session tick by tick, so a settings change can be tested against a day that actually happened.
The four modes agree by construction. Every value a decision reads comes from a closed 15-minute candle fetched from the history endpoint. No live tick enters any signal calculation. The live price is used for exactly one thing: checking whether the already-frozen stop has been hit. That is what makes Paper, Backtest, Live and Replay compute identical numbers from the same session.

14. What's still missing

It has never traded. Not once. No paper session, no live order, zero trades in existence. Everything in this guide describes what the code does, verified by running it — not what it earns.
  • There is no win rate, because none has been measured. The 51% stop-out figure in section 9 is a count of how trades ended on spot, not a profit-and-loss result. Option premiums are not modelled in that measurement at all.
  • Any rupee figure from the backtest is simulated. The backtest assumes a seed premium of ₹240 and a flat 1.5-point haircut per side rather than replaying real option quotes. Treat its output as an order-of-magnitude sketch, never as a result.
  • The exact-zero wick rule is unproven in the wild. The six-month sample says about a quarter of Heikin Ashi candles qualify on shape, which suggests the rule fires often enough. But that was measured on cached history with a clean candle feed. Live, a bar arriving late or a partially-published candle could change the count, and nobody has watched it happen yet.
  • The tiny-stop problem in section 10 is unresolved. 23% of signals get a stop under 10 points. There is no minimum-stop setting, and whether one is needed should be decided by a clean paper sample rather than guessed.
  • Nothing about the exits has been compared to alternatives. The doji and weak thresholds (20% and 40%) are the values in the original rules. They have not been tested against anything, and the exit mix shows they close 44% of trades — so they matter a lot.
  • No regime analysis. Six months is one market. How the strategy behaves in a sustained low-volatility drift, where the 50 MA whipsaws and candle bodies shrink, is unknown.
The order of work. Collect clean paper sessions first. Then diff them against /replay to confirm Paper and Live make identical decisions on a recorded day. Only then consider touching a live gate — and even then, the tiny-stop question in section 10 should have an answer first.
