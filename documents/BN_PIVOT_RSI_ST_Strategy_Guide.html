BN Pivot RSI ST

RSI Pivot ST's rules, unchanged, on NIFTY BANK. Two lines are fixed before the open from yesterday's prices. When a 5-minute candle breaks through one of them with strong momentum, the bot buys an option and holds it until a stop or the clock closes it.

Updated 2026-10-08  ·  15 example charts  ·  every chart is an illustration, not a real trade

In one minute

  • The lines: from yesterday's high, low and close the bot works out R1 (above) and S1 (below). They stay fixed all day.
  • CE: a candle closes above R1 (after the one before closed at or below it), the next candle closes above that candle's high, and RSI on that next candle is above 70.
  • PE: the mirror — close below S1, next candle closes below its low, RSI below 40.
  • Option: the monthly NIFTY BANK option about 1% of the index away from the money (5 strikes out).
  • Exits: shared Profit Lock and breakeven stop, a 25% premium floor (both sides), a SuperTrend stop (CE only by default), and 15:15.
  • Limits: max 5 trades and ₹5,000 loss a day. Needs 20 candles of today first, so the earliest entry is about 10:55.
  • Status: never traded live; no recorded paper results. Every number here is a setting, not a result.
Read this first: nothing here has been measured.

BN Pivot RSI ST has never traded live and there are no recorded paper results or backtests on NIFTY BANK. There is no win rate and no profit figure for it.

About the charts. Every chart uses invented NIFTY BANK candles (around 54,000). The pivot lines, RSI, SuperTrend, and every entry, skip and exit on them were worked out by running the strategy's real engine code on those candles. Option prices are a simple model: a ₹185 option, 5 strikes out of the money, that moves ₹0.35 per NIFTY BANK point. P&L is for 1 lot (30) before charges. The charts show how the rules behave, not what the strategy earns.

1. The idea, with a full example trade

Yesterday's prices give two lines for today. A strong push through the top line is a reason to buy a Call. A strong push through the bottom line is a reason to buy a Put. "Strong" means RSI agrees and the next candle keeps going.

yesterday's H / L / C
→
R1 and S1 (fixed)
→
candle closes beyond the line
→
next candle closes beyond its high/low
→
RSI above 70 / below 40
→
buy the monthly option

What you see: NIFTY BANK climbs toward R1 (54,631.73). The 11:45 candle closes at 54,634.4 — above R1, after the previous close (54,620.9) was below it. That is the breakout candle. The 11:50 candle closes at 54,643.8, above the breakout candle's high (54,640.4), with RSI at 73.5 (above 70). The bot buys the 55,100 CE at that close. The first SuperTrend stop is 54,543.68. The shared Profit Lock books the trade at 12:05 for +₹566, even though the index went higher later. Why it matters: this is the full rule: break, confirm, RSI, buy, exit. P&L is for 1 lot (30) of a ₹185 option that moves ₹0.35 per index point, before charges.

2. Words you'll see

  • Candle — one 5-minute bar. The bot decides only when a candle has closed.
  • Pivot point (PP) — (yesterday's high + low + close) ÷ 3. A rough "middle" for today. Drawn for reference only.
  • R1 — 2 × PP − yesterday's low. The line above. A break above it can give a CE.
  • S1 — 2 × PP − yesterday's high. The line below. A break below it can give a PE.
  • Breakout candle — the candle that closes on the far side of R1 or S1 when the candle before it had not.
  • Confirmation candle — the next candle. It must close past the breakout candle's high (CE) or low (PE).
  • RSI(14) — a 0–100 score of recent buying vs selling. Above 70 = strong buying; below 40 = strong selling.
  • SuperTrend(10, 2) — a line below price in an uptrend, above it in a downtrend. Used as a CE stop.
  • Premium — the option's own price in rupees.
  • Premium floor — a sell level 25% below the best premium the trade has seen. It only moves up.
  • CE / PE — Call (gains when NIFTY BANK rises) / Put (gains when it falls).
  • OTM / ITM / ATM — a strike away from the price (cheaper), into the price (dearer), or at the price.
  • Lot — one NIFTY BANK lot is 30 units, so ₹1 of premium = ₹30.

3. What is different from RSI Pivot ST

The rules and every default are the same. Only the instrument changes.

 RSI Pivot STBN Pivot RSI ST
IndexNSE:NIFTY50-INDEXNSE:NIFTYBANK-INDEX — pivots, RSI and SuperTrend all use NIFTY BANK candles
Strike gap50 points100 points (BANKNIFTY_STRIKE_STEP)
Expiryweekly or monthlymonthly only (NSE stopped BANKNIFTY weeklies in Nov-2024)
Lot sizeNIFTY_LOT_SIZEBANKNIFTY_LOT_SIZE, 30
The 25% floor is looser here. A monthly option loses value to time slowly and costs more than a weekly. So "25% of the premium" takes a bigger move in NIFTY BANK to reach. On a PE that floor is the only stop. Nothing in the code adjusts for this; if you want a tighter stop, lower BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_PREMIUM_SL_PCT.
The engine is a copy. src/strategies/bn_pivot_rsi_st.js is a duplicate of rsi_pivot_st.js, not a shared file. A rule change must be made in both (a regression test checks they stay in step).

4. Step 1 — the lines, drawn before the open

PP = (H + L + C) ÷ 3  ·  R1 = 2×PP − L  ·  S1 = 2×PP − H  ·  R2 = PP + (H − L)  ·  S2 = PP − (H − L)

Example (the chart below): H 54,538.9, L 54,209.2, C 54,513.3 → PP 54,420.47, R1 54,631.73, S1 54,302.03, R2 54,750.17, S2 54,090.77.

  • Only R1 and S1 are traded. R2 and S2 are drawn for context.
  • The lines come from yesterday's finished day, never today's. They do not move during the day.
  • If there is no usable previous day, there are no lines and no trades that day.

What you see: yesterday (15 Sep) ended at 54,513.3. Its high was 54,538.9 and its low 54,209.2. From those three numbers the bot draws today's lines before the open: PP 54,420.47, R1 54,631.73, S1 54,302.03 (R2 and S2 are drawn only for context). On the right, today's first candles move between the lines — the lines do not move. Why it matters: R1 and S1 are the whole map for the day. A CE needs a break above R1; a PE needs a break below S1.

5. Step 2 — the entry: break, confirm, RSI

 CE (Call)PE (Put)
1. BreakA candle closes above R1, and the candle before it closed at or below R1A candle closes below S1, and the candle before it closed at or above S1
2. ConfirmThe next candle closes above the breakout candle's highThe next candle closes below the breakout candle's low
3. RSIRSI(14) on the confirming candle is above 70RSI(14) on the confirming candle is below 40
BuyAt the close of the confirming candle

What you see: a close-up of the same trade. Candle 1 closes above R1 — that only arms the setup. Candle 2 must close above candle 1's high (yellow line, 54,640.4). It closes at 54,643.8, so the bot buys at that close. RSI is read on candle 2, the candle that makes the decision. Why it matters: a candle that pokes just past R1 and stalls never gets a trade. The move has to keep going.

Each part can fail on its own. Here is what each failure looks like.

What you see: the 11:15 candle closes above R1 — a breakout. But the next candle (11:20) closes at 54,601, below the breakout high of 54,617.2. RSI was 72.05, above 70, so only the confirmation failed. No trade. The price then fades. Why it matters: only the very next candle counts. A failed confirmation needs a brand-new cross of R1 to try again.

What you see: a slow climb through R1. At 13:10 the cross is confirmed, but RSI is only 67.32 (grey dot) — not above 70. No trade all day. Why it matters: a slow drift through a line is the breakout that usually fails. RSI above 70 asks for real force behind the move.

What you see: NIFTY BANK opens at 54,706.25, already above R1 (54,646.27), and keeps rising. At 11:55 RSI is 70.61, above 70 — but price never crossed R1 from below today, so there is no fresh cross. No trade. Why it matters: the bot does not chase a move that already happened. It needs the moment of the break.

Turning confirmation off. With BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_CONFIRM_ENABLED=false the breakout candle itself is the entry: it must cross the line and RSI must agree on that same candle. BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_PIVOT_BUFFER_PTS (default 0) can demand the close be some points past the line.

6. Warm-up and the time window

  • The bot loads only today's 5-minute candles. RSI(14) and SuperTrend(10) need history, so it waits for 20 finished candles.
  • The 20th candle of the day is the 10:50 candle, which closes at 10:55. So the earliest possible entry is 10:55, even though the entry window in Settings starts at 09:30.
  • The window is checked on the candle's closing time: a candle must close after 09:30 and no later than 15:00. The 14:55 candle (closing at 15:00) is the last one allowed.
  • Anything still open is closed at 15:15.

What you see: NIFTY BANK breaks above R1 at 09:55, early in the day. The bot ignores it: it only uses today's candles and needs 20 of them, so the first decision is at the close of the 10:50 candle (10:55). By then the cross is old, so there is no fresh cross and no trade. Why it matters: the 09:30 start time in Settings is not the real start. In practice nothing can happen before 10:55.

7. Step 3 — which option is bought

The strike is set from the confirming candle's close. Default: OTM, 1%. 1% of the index is rounded to whole 100-point strikes.

ModeCE strikePE strike
OTM (default)55,10054,100
ITM54,10055,100
ATM54,60054,600

Worked out by the engine for NIFTY BANK at 54,643.80: ATM = 54,600; 1% = 546.4 points = 5 strikes; so 54,600 ± 500. If 1% ever rounds to zero strikes, the engine uses ATM.

  • Always the current monthly expiry.
  • Quantity: 1 lot of 30 × BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_LOT_MULTIPLIER (0 = use the global multiplier).

8. Step 4 — how a trade ends

There is no profit target. These are checked on every price update, in this order:

#ExitApplies toWhen it fires
1Profit Lock (shared)CE and PEOption up 8% arms it; it then sells if the option falls back to a floor that keeps 85% of the gain (under +20%), 80% (under +40%) or 70% (above).
2Breakeven stop (shared)CE and PEOption up 5% arms it; it then sells if the option falls back to the entry price.
325% premium floorCE and PEOption falls 25% below the best price it has reached. Starts at 75% of the entry price and only moves up.
4SuperTrend stopCE only (default)NIFTY BANK falls to the SuperTrend line. The line is re-read at each candle close and only moves up.
5SuperTrend flipCE only (default)At a candle close the line flips above price — the uptrend is over, so the CE is sold at that close.
615:15 square-offallAnything still open is closed.
CE and PE are stopped differently on purpose. By default a CE has the SuperTrend stop and the premium floor. A PE has only the premium floor (plus the shared lock and breakeven). This is the stated rule — do not "balance" it. BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_ST_SIDES can add SuperTrend to PE (the line then sits above price).

What you see: a CE bought at 54,659.2 (11:55). The green dashed SuperTrend line is its spot stop; it starts at 54,528.05 (orange line) and moves up at each candle close. The move fails. Price falls to the stop at 54,570.2 at 12:20: about 89 points, −₹935. The 25% premium floor was further away, so SuperTrend acted first. Why it matters: a CE carries two stops and the first one reached wins.

What you see: NIFTY BANK falls through S1 (the red line). The 11:50 candle closes below S1; the 11:55 candle closes below that candle's low (54,296.1) with RSI 29.7 (below 40). The bot buys the 53,800 PE at 54,265.15. The Profit Lock books it at 12:05 for +₹636. Why it matters: the PE rule is the mirror of the CE rule. But a PE has no SuperTrend stop by default — only the premium stops protect it.

What you see: a PE bought at 54,217.55 (12:00). NIFTY BANK turns back up. The option never gains 5%, so neither the Profit Lock nor the breakeven stop arms. At 12:35, after about 123 points against the trade, the option has lost 25% and is sold: −₹1,289. Why it matters: on a PE the 25% floor is the only stop. On a slow-decaying monthly option it can be a long way away.

What you see: the same PE on the option price. Bought at ₹185. The red line is the 25% floor: ₹138.75 at the start, then 25% below the best price (best ₹189.39). The floor only moves up. The option falls to ₹142.04 and is sold. Why it matters: the floor follows the option's best price, not the index.

How the 25% floor moves (engine output for a ₹185 entry):

Best price so farFloorVersus the ₹185 entry

On its own the floor needs the option to rise by a third before it protects any profit. That is why the shared Profit Lock and breakeven stop usually end winning trades first:

What you see: the winning CE on the option price (₹185 at entry). It reaches about ₹207.19. The lock armed at +8%; the green floor then keeps 85% of the gain. The price slips back to the floor and the option is sold at ₹203.86 (+₹566). The red 25% floor sits far below. Why it matters: the shared Profit Lock usually ends a winning trade long before the strategy's own 25% floor or SuperTrend would.

What you see: a CE bought at ₹185 (12:00). It rises to about ₹196.09 — more than +5%, so the breakeven stop arms. Then it falls back. When it touches the entry price the bot sells at ₹185: about zero P&L before charges (12:25). Why it matters: once a trade has been 5% up, the shared breakeven rule does not let it turn into a loss.

What you see: a late breakout. The bot buys a CE at 54,700.35 (14:45). Price drifts; no stop is hit and the lock never arms. At 15:15 the bot closes it anyway (+₹137). The grey area: no new entries after 15:00. Why it matters: nothing is carried overnight.

Deliberately absent: no VIX gate (VIX is only recorded), no OI filter, no ADX, no volume test, no EMA, no VWAP, no ATR sizing, no multi-timeframe check, no profit target, no partial booking, no expiry-day rule.

9. Trades it refuses

Every "no" is written to the skip log with the reason. In the order the engine checks:

  1. Fewer than 20 of today's candles (warm-up).
  2. Bad price data on the last candles.
  3. The candle closes at or before 09:30, or after 15:00; or the day's trade budget is used up.
  4. No previous day → no R1/S1 → no trade.
  5. RSI cannot be worked out.
  6. Breakout happened but the next candle did not close past its high/low (failed confirmation).
  7. Break confirmed but RSI does not agree.
  8. RSI is strong but there is no fresh cross (price already beyond the line).
  9. CE only (by default): SuperTrend is not below price, or sits at/above the entry close — no room for a stop, so no trade.

What you see: a full morning that stays between S1 and R1 — no candle even touches either line. With no cross there is nothing to confirm, and RSI stays near the middle anyway. The bot takes no trade. Why it matters: two fixed lines and a strict RSI test mean many days have no trade at all. That is the design, not a fault.

11. Paper, Live & Replay

Paper (/bn-pivot-rsi-st-paper) is the reference. All rules live there.

Live (/bn-pivot-rsi-st-live) wraps Paper and forwards its decisions to Zerodha. A real order needs BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_LIVE_ENABLED=true, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false, and BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. All ship in the safe position.

Two brokers. Prices from Fyers; orders to Zerodha.

Crash recovery. An open position is saved to .active_bn_pivot_rsi_st_position.json and checked against the broker on restart. Both stops are rebuilt from saved values.

Replay (/replay) re-runs a recorded day from data/ticks/. Keep TICK_RECORDER_ENABLED on for any day you want to check.

12. The backtest and its rupees

The backtest (/bn-pivot-rsi-st-backtest) uses the same engine, and like Paper it feeds only each day's own candles. It is careful about order inside a candle: stops are tested against the worst price of the bar first, and a bar that opens past a level fills at the open.

The rupees are modelled, and the defaults suit NIFTY weeklies, not BANKNIFTY monthlies.
KeyDefaultProblem here
BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_BT_SEED_PREMIUM180Starting option price. A BANKNIFTY monthly ATM option costs several times this.
BACKTEST_THETA_DAY8₹ lost to time per day. A monthly decays more slowly.
BACKTEST_DELTA0.55₹ of premium per index point. Not checked for a 5-strike-OTM monthly.
BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS2Cost per side for the spread.

The 25% floor is measured on this modelled price, and on a PE it is the only stop — so a PE backtest mostly tests the model. Treat backtest ₹ as a direction, not a P&L.

14. What is still missing

  • No live trades and no recorded results. No win rate, no trade count, no drawdown.
  • No setting was tuned — on NIFTY or NIFTY BANK. RSI 70/40, SuperTrend 10/2, 25%, 1% OTM, 5 trades: all inherited.
  • The 25% floor is looser on a monthly option, and on a PE it is the only strategy stop.
  • The 09:30 start is not the real start. With today-only warm-up the first decision is at 10:55.
  • Backtest ₹ use NIFTY-weekly assumptions. Fix the seed, theta and delta first.
  • Next step: run paper sessions with the tick recorder on, replay them, confirm Paper and Live agree, and only then think about a live switch.
