BN Pivot RSI ST

This is RSI Pivot ST's rule set, unchanged, pointed at a different index. Two lines are fixed before the open from yesterday's high, low and close; a five-minute NIFTY BANK candle has to push through one of them while the momentum reading agrees; then an option is bought and held until a stop catches up. Every threshold is identical to the NIFTY version. Only the instrument is different — and the instrument changes three things that matter.

Engine src/strategies/bn_pivot_rsi_st.js · every rule and every default below was read out of that file or out of the Settings schema, not copied from the NIFTY guide

  • There are no charts.   • Nothing was carried over from the NIFTY guide. RSI Pivot ST's charts and examples are NIFTY 50 data. Reproducing them here would be a lie about a NIFTY BANK strategy, so they are absent rather than borrowed.
  • Every rupee a backtest eventually prints is simulated. There is no historical option chain anywhere in this repo, so the backtest models the premium from delta and theta rather than reading a quote. See the backtest section before you believe a single ₹ figure.

1. The idea in one minute

Take yesterday's NIFTY BANK high, low and close, average them, and you get the pivot point — a rough centre of gravity for today. Reflect yesterday's low across it and you get R1, a line above. Reflect yesterday's high across it and you get S1, a line below. Those two lines are the strategy's whole map of the day, and their usefulness is precisely that they are boring: computed once from finished bars, and fixed until the close.

If a five-minute candle is strong enough to push through R1 and close there, and RSI agrees that the strength is real, buy a Call. If a candle breaks down through S1 and closes there with RSI agreeing, buy a Put. There is no profit target — the position is held until a stop trails into it or the day is squared off.

Yesterday's H / L / C
→
PP, R1, S1 (frozen)
→
5-min NIFTY BANK candle crosses & closes beyond
→
RSI agrees
→
buy the monthly option
→
hold until a stop

2. What differs from RSI Pivot ST

Exactly three things, and none of them is a rule change. Every threshold — RSI 70 and 40, SuperTrend(10, 2), the 25% premium floor, the 1% strike distance, the 09:30–15:00 entry window, the 15:15 square-off, five trades a day, the ₹5,000 day stop — is identical to the NIFTY engine, and so are the deliberate omissions. What changes is the instrument.

 RSI Pivot STBN Pivot RSI ST
SpotNSE:NIFTY50-INDEXNSE:NIFTYBANK-INDEX — pivots, RSI(14) and SuperTrend(10, 2) are all computed on NIFTY BANK candles
Strike gridevery 50 pointsevery 100 points (BANKNIFTY_STRIKE_STEP)
Expiryweekly or monthlyMONTHLY ONLY — NSE withdrew BANKNIFTY weekly options in November 2024
Lot sizeNIFTY_LOT_SIZEBANKNIFTY_LOT_SIZE, default 30
The monthly expiry makes the 25% premium floor a materially LOOSER stop, and nothing compensates for it. The only stop a Put ever carries is a floor set 25% below the best premium the trade has seen. On NIFTY that floor sits under a weekly contract, which bleeds time value fast, so 25% of the premium is a comparatively small number of index points. A BANKNIFTY monthly contract decays far more slowly: the same 25% is a much larger adverse move in the index before the stop is reached, and it stays that way all day. Same rule, same percentage, a different amount of risk — because the contract underneath it is different. This is a real behavioural difference between the two strategies, not a footnote, and the engine does not adjust for it. If you want a tighter stop on NIFTY BANK you have to say so in BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_PREMIUM_SL_PCT; the rule as written does not do it for you.
What the option symbol looks like. Symbols are built as NSE:{prefix}{expiry code}{strike}{CE|PE}, and a monthly expiry code is YYMMM — the year and the month, never a day. Called right now with no live quote available (it falls back to an index level of 54,000, because the Fyers token is expired), the repo's own symbol builder returns NSE:BANKNIFTY26SEP54000CE. A March-2026 contract would carry the code 26MAR. There is no weekly code to fall back to: BANKNIFTY_WEEKLY_EXPIRY_ENABLED ships false and must stay that way, because weekly BANKNIFTY symbols simply do not exist — switching it on would make every entry refuse.

3. Words you'll see

  • Standard (floor) pivot — three prices worked out from yesterday's high, low and close. They do not move once the day starts.
  • PP — the middle line, (High + Low + Close) ÷ 3. Drawn for context; nothing trades on it.
  • R1 — the first line above PP, 2 × PP − Low. The CE trigger.
  • S1 — the first line below PP, 2 × PP − High. The PE trigger.
  • Cross and close — the candle before must have finished on one side of the line and this candle must finish on the other. A candle that poked through and came back has not crossed, and neither has one that was already past the line.
  • RSI — a 0–100 momentum reading over the last 14 closes. Here it is the second opinion that has to agree with the break.
  • SuperTrend — a line that sits below price in an uptrend and flips above it in a downtrend. When it is below, it doubles as a stop that only ever moves up.
  • Premium — what the option itself costs, in rupees. Not the NIFTY BANK level.
  • Premium floor — an exit price set 25% below the best premium the trade has seen. It rises with the trade and never falls.
  • OTM / ITM — a strike above spot for a CE (out of the money, cheaper) or below it (in the money, dearer and closer-tracking). PE is the mirror.
Both halves, on the same candle. The RSI reading and the cross must happen on the same closed five-minute candle. RSI going high an hour before the break does not count, and neither does a break on a candle whose RSI has already faded. This is the single most common way to misread the rule.

4. Step 1 — The two lines, drawn before the open

The formula is the classic floor-trader arithmetic, taken from computePivots() in the engine:

PP = (H + L + C) / 3    R1 = 2·PP − L    S1 = 2·PP − H    R2 = PP + (H − L)    S2 = PP − (H − L)

Only R1 and S1 are traded. R2 and S2 are returned for context.

Worked example — arithmetic only, at a stated hypothetical daily bar. These are not observed prices; they are what the engine returns when it is handed a made-up previous-day bar, and they exist here purely to show the shape of the numbers on a ~54,000 index. Feed computePivots() a previous day with high 54,620, low 54,180 and close 54,500 and it answers:
  • PP = 54,433.33   (range 440)
  • R1 = 54,686.67  ← the Call trigger
  • S1 = 54,246.67  ← the Put trigger
  • R2 = 54,873.33, S2 = 53,993.33 — context only

In that example R1 sits 253.34 points above PP and S1 186.66 below it — the levels are asymmetric whenever yesterday closed off the middle of its range, which is the formula working, not a bug. Read those distances against the index they belong to: this repo's own fallback level for NIFTY BANK is 54,000 against 24,000 for NIFTY, so a 250-point break here is a much smaller percentage event than a 250-point break on NIFTY. Do not carry an intuition for "how far is far" across from the NIFTY guide.

The bar that feeds this must be YESTERDAY, not today. A daily candle that is still forming would make R1 and S1 drift all session, and then no replay and no backtest could ever reproduce an entry. The engine walks backwards through the daily series to the newest completed bar strictly before the day being traded, which is also what lets a backtest step through history without peeking at the day it is trading. If there is no usable prior daily bar, the answer is "cannot trade" — never zero.

5. Step 2 — The entry needs both halves

An entry is only ever decided on a closed five-minute candle, and both tests must pass on that same candle. Reading RSI on the very bar that does the crossing is deliberate: it removes any lag between the two halves of the rule.

SideMomentum halfLevel half
CE (Call)RSI(14) above 70 on the signal candleThe candle crosses and closes above R1 — the previous close sat at or below R1, this one closes above
PE (Put)RSI(14) below 40 on the signal candleThe candle crosses and closes below S1 — the previous close sat at or above S1, this one closes below
"Crosses" is doing real work in that sentence. A candle that is already sitting above R1 and closes higher still is not a cross. Taking it would be chasing a move that already happened. The engine requires the transition itself: below-or-at, then above.

The engine refuses to decide anything until it holds 20 closed five-minute candles — that is minBarsFor() at the shipped defaults, which takes the largest of RSI period + 2, SuperTrend period + 2, and a floor of 20. Until then it reports a warm-up reason rather than a signal, and the paper page shows the countdown from the same function so the badge can never disagree with the engine.

6. Step 3 — Which option gets bought

The strike is chosen from the signal candle's closing price, as a percentage of spot, in the direction the setting picks. The default is OTM at 1%, and the raw distance is rounded to the nearest 100-point BANKNIFTY strike. If the percentage is so small that it rounds to zero steps, the engine falls back to ATM rather than inventing a strike that does not exist.

Worked example — arithmetic only, at a stated spot of 54,387. Every row below is the literal return value of strikeForSide(54387, side, cfg) with the shipped defaults. 54,387 is a stated index level chosen to show the rounding, not an observed price and not a trade.
ModeCallPutSteps from ATM
OTM default54,900 (513 pts away)53,900 (487 pts away)5
ITM53,900 (487 pts away)54,900 (513 pts away)5
ATM54,40054,4000

The arithmetic: 1% of 54,387 is 543.87 points; 543.87 ÷ 100 is 5.44, which rounds to 5 strikes; ATM is 54,387 rounded to the nearest 100, i.e. 54,400; and the strike is 54,400 ± 5 × 100. Because ATM rounds down here, the CE ends up 513 points from spot and the PE 487 — the two are not symmetric, and that is the grid, not a bug.

Five strikes out is a real shift, and the ATM fallback never comes into play here. The "rounds to zero → use ATM" branch exists for degenerate configurations — a tiny percentage, or a coarse grid on a small index. At the shipped defaults it does not fire: 1% of an index near 54,000 is about 540 points, and 540 ÷ 100 rounds to five strikes. Do not read the fallback as a hint that the 1% default is marginal here — it is not.

7. Step 4 — The stops, and why CE and PE differ

The two sides are stopped differently, and that is the rule — not a bug and not an oversight. It is stated identically in the engine header, in the paper route header, in the NIFTY sibling and here. If you are reading this later and thinking of "balancing" the two sides, don't.

CE — two stops, both live, whichever fires first

  • SuperTrend(10, 2) on the five-minute NIFTY BANK spot chart. It is both the initial stop and the trail. It only ever moves in the trade's favour — a SuperTrend that ticks down mid-trade is ignored, because a trail that can loosen is not a trail. And if the line flips bearish, that flip is itself an exit: the reason for being long has gone.
  • A 25% premium floor on the option's own price, set 25% below the highest premium the trade has seen, ratcheting up and never down.

PE — one stop only, by default

  • The 25% premium floor, and nothing else. Out of the box the SuperTrend is applied to CE only (BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_ST_SIDES=CE), so a Put is held by the premium floor alone — and on a slow-decaying monthly contract that floor is a long way away. Re-read the warning in section 2.
  • This is a setting, not a law of the engine. BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_ST_SIDES accepts BOTH, CE, PE or NONE; on PE the line is the exact mirror — it must sit above price to enter, and a flip to bullish is the exit.
How the floor ratchets — arithmetic only, at a stated entry premium of ₹100. Every figure is the literal return of premiumStop(entry, highWater, cfg, side) at the shipped 25%. ₹100 is a round number chosen so the percentages read cleanly; it is not a BANKNIFTY quote, and this repo currently has no way to fetch one.
Best premium so farFloor (25% under best)Where that sits vs the ₹100 entry
₹100 (entry)₹75.0025% below entry
₹120₹90.00still 10% below entry
₹133.33₹100.00exactly break-even
₹160₹120.0020% above entry — the first locked-in profit

Read the third row twice. At a 25% give-back the premium must run a third above entry before the floor even reaches your entry price. Anything that goes your way by less than that and then turns is a losing trade, however right the direction was. There is no target and no breakeven rule to bank anything on the way, so this rule needs big winners, not frequent ones — and that bar is higher here than on NIFTY. A monthly option costs more than a weekly at the same moneyness, so the same index move is a smaller percentage of its premium; "a third above entry" therefore asks for a larger move in NIFTY BANK than the identical sentence asks for in NIFTY.

You can configure a trade with no stop at all. BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_PREMIUM_SL_SIDES (default BOTH) can switch the premium floor off for a side. If that side also has no SuperTrend — a PE at the defaults, or any side under ST_SIDES=NONE — the position has no stop of any kind and can only be closed by the 15:15 square-off, with the full premium at risk. The engine does not quietly substitute a stop; isStoplessSide() is what the routes call to detect the case, and they log it loudly and keep going. Only turn the floor off for a side that still carries the SuperTrend.
A side that expects a SuperTrend stop is refused without one. On any side BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_ST_SIDES covers — CE at the defaults — if the SuperTrend is still warming up, has flipped the wrong way, or already sits through the entry close (so the trade would be stopped on its very first tick), the engine declines the setup and says so in the skip log.

Note the limit of this rule: it only fires where the SuperTrend applies. A side configured with neither the SuperTrend nor the premium floor is not refused — it is entered unprotected and logged as such.

What is NOT here

There is no profit target at all, on either side. The trade runs until a stop trails into it or the 15:15 square-off. And deliberately absent, so nobody adds them back in later "helpfully": no VIX gate, no Open Interest filter, no ADX, no volume test, no EMA, no VWAP, no ATR sizing, no multi-timeframe bias, no extra confirmation candle, no breakeven jump, no partial booking, no re-entry after a stop, no expiry-day rule, no quality score.

8. The trades it refuses

Most of what an engine does is decline, and every refusal here names which half failed — a skip log that only says "no signal" teaches nothing. These are the branches in getSignal(), in the order it tests them:

  • Fewer than 20 closed candles — warm-up. RSI(14) and SuperTrend(10) both need history.
  • The signal candle or the one before it has unusable OHLC — refuses to decide rather than compute nonsense.
  • The series carries one bar per IST day — someone handed daily candles to an intraday engine. Refused.
  • The bar closes before 09:30 or after 15:00, or the daily trade budget is spent. The bar's close time is what is gated, so the 14:55 bar — closing at 15:00 — is still legal.
  • No usable prior daily bar — there is no R1 or S1 to trade, so there is no trade. Never treated as zero.
  • RSI could not be computed, or resolved to a non-number — refused rather than quoted stale.
  • The level broke but RSI did not agree — "closed above R1 but RSI is not above 70". A breakout out of a range with no momentum behind it is precisely the breakout that fails.
  • RSI is past the threshold but there is no fresh cross — price is already beyond the level. Entering here is chasing.
  • The SuperTrend is not on the right side of price (CE at the defaults) — no initial stop can be placed, so the entry is declined rather than opened unprotected.
  • The SuperTrend already sits through the entry close — the trade would be stopped on its first tick.

9. Safety limits

  • Max 5 trades a day. The pivot levels are fixed all day, so only a handful of genuine crosses can honestly occur — a higher number would mean the rule is firing on noise.
  • Max ₹5,000 loss in a day, then the day is over.
  • Weekly loss cap is off by default (0). It reads the per-day log files, so it survives a restart.
  • Entries only between 09:30 and 15:00 (IST), gated on the bar's close time.
  • Everything is squared off at 15:15. Nothing is carried overnight, ever.
  • The shared portfolio-wide cap applies on top of all of this — bn_pivot_rsi_st is registered in the portfolio-risk paper-mode list and draws on the Zerodha paper capital pool.
Sizing is a NIFTY BANK lot, and it is a different number from NIFTY's. One lot is BANKNIFTY_LOT_SIZE, which ships at 30 (NSE's Nov-2024 revision). getLotQty("BANKNIFTY") returns 30 at the shipped defaults. Every ₹1 of premium movement is therefore ₹30 per lot — and the global LOT_MULTIPLIER is applied on top, clamped by MAX_LOT_MULTIPLIER. A NIFTY BANK lot and a NIFTY lot are not interchangeable units of size, so "one lot here" is not the same rupee exposure as "one lot" on the NIFTY sibling. Read the capital strip on the paper page rather than assuming the two match.

10. Paper, Live & Replay

Paper (/bn-pivot-rsi-st-paper) is the canonical surface. Every decision, fill and exit rule lives there; the other modes follow it, never the reverse.

Live (/bn-pivot-rsi-st-live) does not re-implement anything — it wraps Paper through the shared live harness and forwards its decisions to the broker, so Live equals Paper by construction. There is no hand-written *Live.js for this strategy, on purpose.

Backtest (/bn-pivot-rsi-st-backtest) calls the same getSignal() and re-implements only Paper's exits. See the next section before reading any number it produces.

Two brokers. Candles and quotes come from Fyers, as everywhere in this app, but orders go to Zerodha — the same split the NIFTY sibling uses. That is why starting Live checks both logins, and it is why the crash-recovery snapshot is reconciled against the Zerodha book.

Crash recovery. An open position is snapshotted to .active_bn_pivot_rsi_st_position.json under ~/trading-data/ and reconciled against the broker's book on the next boot. Both stops are recomputed from values the route already persists, so a recovered position resumes on exactly the same numbers.

Live orders are triple-gated, and by default nothing places one. A real order needs all three: BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_LIVE_ENABLED=true and the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false and BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true — plus a logged-in Zerodha session. The shipped defaults leave BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_LIVE_ENABLED=false and the global harness in dry-run, so the live page logs the broker call it would have made and sends nothing. Given that this strategy has never produced a single paper trade, leave it that way.

Replay needs a recorded session, and there are none. /replay reproduces a day tick-by-tick from data/ticks/YYYY-MM-DD/, which is how Paper and Live are proven to agree before a live gate is touched. No NIFTY BANK session has been recorded, so there is nothing to replay yet. Keep TICK_RECORDER_ENABLED on for any session you intend to check later.

11. The backtest, and why its rupees are not comparable

The backtest drives the same signal engine, so the entries are honest, and it is conservative about intra-bar order: the adverse stop is tested on the bar's extreme before anything favourable, a bar that opened beyond a level fills at the open rather than the better price, and the trail only advances on a bar close, matching Paper. It also fetches a separate daily series padded a week before the range, so the very first session still has a yesterday to compute R1/S1 from.

But the rupees are simulated, and the defaults are the wrong shape for this instrument. There is no historical option chain in this repo, so the backtest never reads a premium — it models one from delta and theta and seeds it at a fixed number. Three defaults drive that model:
KeyDefaultWhy it is wrong here
BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_BT_SEED_PREMIUM180A NIFTY-weekly-sized premium. The backtest route says so itself — it warns on the console that a BANKNIFTY monthly ATM premium is several times ₹180. Nobody here has measured the real number (the token is expired), but a ₹180 seed is certainly the wrong order of magnitude, so every ₹ figure and the 25% floor are computed off a starting price the contract never had.
BACKTEST_THETA_DAY8Daily time decay in ₹, again a weekly-shaped number. A monthly contract decays far more slowly, so this over-charges the simulated position for holding.
BACKTEST_DELTA0.55How much premium moves per index point. Unverified for a 5-strike-OTM BANKNIFTY monthly.

Until those three are set for a BANKNIFTY monthly contract, backtest rupees here are not comparable to RSI Pivot ST's — not "roughly comparable", not comparable at all, because the premium series they are computed from is a different instrument's. The backtest page prints a warning to the console when BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_BT_SEED_PREMIUM is left unset for exactly this reason. And note what this does to the stop: the 25% floor is applied to the model, not to a quote, and on the PE side that floor is the only stop — so a PE backtest is largely a test of the premium model rather than of the market.

Slippage is charged separately: BN_PIVOT_RSI_ST_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS ships at 2 points each way. Without it, option-buying backtests always flatter.

None of this has been run. No backtest has been executed against this engine — the caches hold no NIFTY BANK candles and the Fyers token is expired, so a history fetch returns zero bars. When you do run one, fix the three keys above first, and read the result as directional rather than as a P&L you could have collected.

13. What's still missing / not validated

Be honest with yourself about what you are looking at.
  • It has never traded. Not once. Zero paper sessions, zero live orders, zero backtests. There is no win rate, no profit factor, no trade count and no drawdown figure on this page because there are no trades.
  • There is no market data to look at either. The Fyers token is expired, so every history fetch returns zero candles and every chart renders blank — that looks like a code bug and is not one. The backtest and candle caches hold no NIFTY BANK bars, and no session has been tick-recorded.
  • No threshold here has been fitted or backtested — on NIFTY or on NIFTY BANK. RSI 70 and 40, SuperTrend 10 and 2, the 25% floor, 1% OTM, five trades a day: every one is inherited from the NIFTY sibling, where it was also never validated. Inheriting an unvalidated number does not make it more likely to be right.
  • The transplant itself is the untested claim. The rules work on NIFTY BANK by construction, but whether a rule set designed around NIFTY's volatility and a weekly option behaves the same way on an index that moves twice as far, on a monthly contract, is exactly the question nobody here has answered.
  • The premium floor is looser here and nothing offsets it. Stated once more because it is the single most consequential difference: 25% of a slow-decaying monthly premium is a bigger adverse index move than 25% of a weekly. On the PE side it is the only stop there is.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated and mis-seeded. ₹180 seed, ₹8/day theta and 0.55 delta are all NIFTY-weekly numbers. Fix them before quoting any ₹ figure, and never compare an unfixed run against the NIFTY sibling's.
  • What to do next. Run paper sessions with the tick recorder on. Diff them against /replay to confirm Paper and Live agree on a recorded day. Only then consider touching a live gate — and even then, remember that trades produced before any bug fix were produced by different code.
