3M Gap Fix Scalp

Sometimes the NIFTY futures order book thins out and price jumps, leaving a small band of prices where nothing traded — a gap. Gaps tend to get filled. This strategy finds one on the 3-minute chart, waits for the very next candle to tell it whether the jump was real, and if it wasn't, sells the move back into the gap.

Updated 2026-08-09  ·  plain-English edition  ·  every number below is produced by running the live engine

Read this first: this strategy has never traded. Not one paper session, not one live order. Every figure in this guide comes from the engine running on constructed candles. There is no win rate here because none has been measured — and unlike the other eight strategies, even the frequency is unknown, because the futures gap distribution has never been sampled.

1. The idea in one minute

Price normally moves smoothly: each candle starts roughly where the last one finished. Occasionally it doesn't — a burst of orders lifts price so fast that one candle's low sits above the previous candle's high. Between those two levels is a band of prices where nothing changed hands. That band is a gap, and traders watch it because price often comes back to trade through it.

But not always. Sometimes the jump is the start of a real move, and fading it is expensive. So this strategy does not guess. It looks at the very next candle and asks one question: did that candle push on past the day's high (for a gap up) on heavy volume, or did it turn back?

Find a gap ≥ 20pt→ Next candle: break or return?→ Break on volume → do nothing · Return → fade it→ Target = the gap fills· Stop = the day's high

A gap up is faded by buying a PE. A gap down is faded by buying a CE. The target is the far edge of the gap; the stop is the day's high (for a PE) or the day's low (for a CE). Neither level ever moves once the trade is on.

2. Why the futures chart, not the index

This was measured, not assumed. On this app's own cached NIFTY 50 index candles — 39 sessions — there were only 12 intraday gaps in total, the largest was 2.1 points and the median was 0.45 points.

The reason is structural. The NIFTY index is not a thing you can buy; it is an average of 50 share prices, recalculated continuously. An average has no order book, so it never leaves a hole. NIFTY futures is a single contract with a real book, and that book is what gaps when it empties out. So this engine reads futures candles — a strategy about gaps cannot be built on an instrument that does not gap.

The index is still used, for exactly two things: picking which option strike to buy (strikes are struck on the index, not on the future), and drawing the second chart on the Paper page. No rule reads it.

3. Words you'll see

  • Gap / void — a price band nothing traded through. Formally: this candle's low is above the previous candle's high (gap up), or this candle's high is below the previous candle's low (gap down). Touching is not enough; it has to be a real hole.
  • Fill level — the far edge of the gap, i.e. the price that has to be reached for the gap to be "filled". That is the target.
  • Confirm candle — the one candle after the gap. It decides everything.
  • Day high / day low — the highest and lowest price of today's session so far, on the futures chart. Frozen at the moment the gap is found, and used as the stop.
  • Fading — betting against the direction of the jump.

4. Step 1 — Finding the gap

Every 3 minutes a candle closes. The engine compares it with the one before it. If the newer candle's low sits above the older candle's high by at least GAP3M_MIN_GAP_PTS (default 20 points), that is a gap up and the exact band is remembered.

Why the minimum is 20 points and not 5. The gap size is the target — you cannot make more than the gap. On one lot (65 qty) this app's own charges.js takes ₹89.51 in statutory charges on a round trip, and about 1.5 premium points of slippage each way on top. At an in-the-money delta of ~0.6, that is roughly 7.3 index points of move needed before the trade has made anything at all. A 5-point gap cannot pay for itself even when it fills perfectly. Twenty is the first round number safely clear of that floor.

5. Step 2 — The one candle that decides

Here is a gap up of 22 points. Candle A topped out at 24252; the next candle's low was 24274. Nothing traded in between. That candle also made the day's high at 24290.

The trade that works. The confirm candle poked at the highs, failed, and closed red at 24276 on below-average volume. That is a return, so the engine buys a PE. Target 24252 (the gap's far edge, 24pt away). Stop 24290 (the day's high, 14pt away). Two candles later the gap fills.

The engine's own description of that entry, verbatim:

3M GAP FIX PE: 22pt gap up (void 24252–24274) at 10:06, confirm bar poked the day high 24290 on WEAK volume (82000 < 1.5× avg 109705.88) and returned (close 24276) | target = gap fill 24252 (24pt) | SL = day high 24290 (14pt) | R:R 1.71

Two things had to be true for that entry:

  1. It did not break the day's high on volume. A candle that pushes past the day's extreme with real participation behind it is a breakout, not a failure — see section 8.
  2. It returned. By default (GAP3M_RETURN_MODE=reverse_close) that means the candle closed against the gap's direction and gave back ground compared with the gap candle's close. Both halves matter: a red candle that still finishes above the gap candle has not returned anywhere.

6. Step 3 — How we get out

There are exactly three ways out, and no others:

  1. The gap fills — price reaches the far edge. That is the whole trade. Take it.
  2. The day's extreme goes — price comes back through the day high (PE) or day low (CE). The fade was wrong; get out.
  3. The clock — square off at GAP3M_FORCED_EXIT (default 15:15) if the gap never filled.

Nothing here trails. There is no trailing stop, no break-even jump, no partial booking, no time stop and no premium stop. Both levels are fixed prices decided before the trade opens, and neither one ever moves. That is deliberate: the whole trade is a bet on one specific price being reached, so moving the goalposts mid-trade would be answering a different question.

7. The geometry that decides your reward

This is the most important thing to understand about the strategy, and it falls straight out of the rules rather than being a design choice.

The target is the gap's bottom edge (24252). The stop is the day's high (24290). The distance between them is fixed at 38 points — and your entry price sits somewhere in between. So target + stop always adds up to 38, and where the confirm candle happens to close decides how that 38 is split.

Read that table the right way round. A shallow return — one that only just turns down — gives the best reward-to-risk, because the stop is close by. A deep return that dives back inside the gap looks more convincing but is a worse trade, because you have already spent much of the move you were trying to capture and the stop is now far away. This is why the default return test is reverse_close (the shallow reading) rather than into_gap (the deep one), and why GAP3M_MIN_RR exists as a lever.

8. The trades it refuses

Same gap, different next candle:

A real breakout. The confirm candle blew straight through the day high on 4,20,000 contracts against an average of 1,09,706 — more than the 1.5× threshold. The engine takes no trade at all. Fading a genuine breakout is the expensive mistake this rule exists to avoid.

What if the volume is unknown? If the futures candles come back without volume — an expired Fyers token, a data hiccup — the engine cannot show the break was weak. It then treats every break of the day's extreme as real and stands aside. That is deliberate: it fails safe, never blind.

Other reasons it will stand aside on a day: no gap large enough all session; the confirm candle neither broke out nor returned (it just hung there); the confirm candle already closed past the fill level, so there is nothing left to make; or the day's high and low have not been established yet before GAP3M_ENTRY_START.

And because reward-to-risk swings with where the confirm candle closes (section 7), that break-even figure moves trade by trade — a run of deep returns can push it well past 60%.

10. Safety limits

  • Max 3 trades a day (GAP3M_MAX_DAILY_TRADES).
  • Day ends after 2 stop-outs (GAP3M_MAX_DAILY_LOSSES).
  • Day ends at ₹3,000 of loss (GAP3M_MAX_DAILY_LOSS).
  • Entries only between 09:30 and 15:00, everything squared off by 15:15.
  • Position size follows the global setting — GAP3M_LOT_MULTIPLIER defaults to 0, meaning "use LOT_MULTIPLIER", and the app-wide MAX_LOT_MULTIPLIER ceiling still applies.
  • The shared portfolio cap and the weekly loss cap both apply on top.
Three guards ship switched OFF, on purpose. GAP3M_MIN_RR, GAP3M_MAX_SL_PTS and GAP3M_MAX_EXTREME_DIST_PTS all default to 0. Out of the box the engine does exactly and only what the rules say — including taking a trade whose stop is a long way off when the gap forms far from the day's extreme. Those three settings exist so that behaviour can be reined in from the Settings page once there is real data to justify a number, rather than a guess being baked into the default.

11. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

ModeRouteWhat it does
Paper/gap-fix-3m-paperThe canonical engine. Real data, simulated orders. Every rule lives here.
Backtest/gap-fix-3m-backtestSame signal engine over a date range. Splits the range by front-month futures contract and rolls on the day before the last Thursday, exactly as the live path does. Only reaches back as far as contracts that still exist — see below.
Live/gap-fix-3m-liveWraps Paper, so Live and Paper cannot disagree. Triple-gated to dry-run.
Replay/replayRe-runs a recorded session bar by bar.
You cannot backtest this one over a long history, and that is not a limitation of the code. A NIFTY futures contract is delisted the moment it expires, and Fyers then answers Invalid symbol provided for that symbol — there is simply no history for a month that has already gone. Every other strategy here backtests on the NIFTY index, which never expires; this one needs futures, so it can only be run over contracts that are still listed. In practice that is roughly the current month, which is why the default range is the current contract's own window rather than the usual last-90-days.

A wider range still works — each contract is fetched separately, and anything Fyers refuses is reported as partial coverage naming the missing symbols, so an absent month is never quietly read as a quiet market. One caveat worth knowing: a block that comes back empty is reported differently from one that was refused, because an expired Fyers token also returns no data rather than an auth error — an all-empty run points you at re-logging in, not at delisting.

How the data actually arrives. Closed 3-minute futures candles come from the Fyers history endpoint, refreshed once per bar a few seconds after it closes — the same endpoint the backtest and replay read, which is what makes the four modes agree on a session. The live futures price used to check the two exit levels comes from a quote poll every GAP3M_FUT_POLL_MS (default 2 seconds), because the app's shared tick socket carries the index, not the future. That 2-second granularity is the resolution at which exits are checked, and it is worth knowing before you read a fill price too literally.

13. What's still missing

  • It has never traded. Zero paper sessions, zero live orders. Every number in this guide comes from the engine run on constructed candles, not from a market.
  • The futures gap distribution has never been measured. The 39-session measurement in section 2 was made on index candles, which is what proved the index unusable — the futures gap frequency, which decides how often this strategy trades at all, is unknown. It could be several setups a week or almost none.
  • 20 points is a floor, not an optimum. GAP3M_MIN_GAP_PTS was derived from the cost arithmetic in section 4. It has not been fitted to anything, and the right value may be well above it.
  • There is barely any backtest history to have. Expired futures contracts are delisted, so the usable range is roughly the current contract — not the years the other strategies can draw on. Whatever this strategy is judged on will mostly have to be forward-collected paper sessions.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated. There is no historical option chain, so premium is modelled from delta and theta with a slippage haircut each way. Treat backtest P&L as directional, not exact.
  • Exits are checked every ~2 seconds, not tick by tick. The backtest and replay check them once per bar. Expect fills to differ.
  • Reward-to-risk is not controlled. Section 7 shows it can land anywhere from 0.27 to 3.75 on the same gap. Until there is data, GAP3M_MIN_RR stays off and that variance is simply carried.
