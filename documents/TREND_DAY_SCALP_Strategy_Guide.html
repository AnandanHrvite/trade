Trend Day Scalp

On most days NIFTY wanders, and a bought option slowly loses value while it does. This bot looks at the first hour once, at 10:15, and asks: "Is today a real trend day?" Most days the answer is no, and it does nothing. When the answer is yes, it buys one dip in the day's direction, with a fixed stop and a fixed target.

Updated 2026-10-08 · every chart is an example built from made-up candles and checked against the real engine code

In one minute
  • 10:15 day gate, decided once. Three tests on the first hour (09:15–10:15): it moved at least 0.5%; the last 6 closes are all on one side of VWAP; and price is at least 0.20 × the first-hour range away from VWAP. Fail any one = no trade all day.
  • Direction is locked. Price above VWAP = CE (Call) only. Below = PE (Put) only. It never flips later.
  • Entry: price dips back to the "zone" (the nearer of VWAP and EMA20) and a strong candle closes back beyond it.
  • Stop = the low of the dip (CE) or high of the bounce (PE). At least 12 points; if it needs more than 40, the trade is skipped.
  • Target = 2.5 × the stop distance, fixed. At +1 × the stop distance the stop jumps once to entry + 3 points. A 25-minute time stop and a 25% premium stop guard the rest.
  • Entries until 14:00. Everything closed at 15:10. Max 2 trades a day; 2 stop-outs, a ₹3,000 loss or a ₹3,000 profit ends the day.
Read this first. This strategy has not been proven with real trades. Two of its numbers (the 40-point stop cap and the 0.20 extension) were set from 39 measured sessions; the rest are starting guesses. Every chart below is an example made from invented candles. The bot's real code was run on those candles to check each decision. Option prices use a simple model (one strike in the money, the option moves about 0.6 rupee per NIFTY point, entry price ₹240).

1. Why it is built this way

  • Most strategies in this app buy a breakout and let the winner run. Their results depend on a few very big winners.
  • This one aims for small but steady. It takes a fixed profit and does not wait for a huge winner.
  • Its biggest tool is saying no. A day with no trade costs nothing. A bought option on a sideways day loses money to time decay.
  • Costs matter. One round trip on one lot costs about ₹90 in charges plus slippage. That is why the target is 2.5 × the risk, not a tiny 10-point scalp.

2. Words you will see

CE / PECE is a Call option: it gains when NIFTY rises. PE is a Put option: it gains when NIFTY falls. The bot only buys options.
PremiumThe option's own price in rupees. One lot = 65 units, so ₹1 of premium = ₹65.

EMA20A smooth line that follows the last 20 candles. In a trend, price often dips back to it and bounces.
The zoneWhichever of VWAP and EMA20 is nearer to price. That is where the bot wants to buy a dip.
ATRAverage True Range: how big a normal candle has been lately (14 candles). Used to judge if a candle is strong.

3. Step 1 — Is today a trend day? (10:15, once)

At 10:15 the bot looks at the first hour (the candles from 09:15 up to the one that closes at 10:15). It runs three tests. All three must pass.

TestRuleWhyExample day below
1. Did it move?First-hour high − low ≥ 0.5% of the priceA quiet first hour usually means a quiet day. That is no good for an option buyer.129.31 pts = 0.53% ✅
2. One direction?The last 6 closes are all above VWAP (or all below)Price that keeps crossing its average is chop.all 6 above VWAP 24,441.04 ✅
3. Committed?Distance from VWAP ≥ 0.20 × the first-hour rangePrice can stay above VWAP while hugging it. That is not a trend.56.96 pts vs 25.86 needed ✅

Example — not a real trade. What you see: the first hour inside the dashed box. NIFTY climbs 129 points (0.53%). The six green dots mark six closes in a row above the purple VWAP line. At 10:15 the close is 24,498, which is 56.96 points above VWAP (25.86 needed). All three tests pass. Why it matters: from now on the bot will only look for CE trades. It will never buy a PE today, even if the market turns.

  • The verdict is made once and frozen for the day.
  • Side = CE if the 10:15 close is above VWAP, PE if below. The streak and the distance must point the same way.
  • Nothing can be bought before 10:15.

4. Step 2 — Buying the dip

On a CE day, the bot waits for a dip back to the zone that is bought straight back up. On each closed 5-minute candle (from 10:15 to 14:00) it checks four things. (PE days are the mirror: a bounce up to the zone, then a strong red candle closing back below it.)

  1. Touch. In the last 3 candles, the lowest low reached the zone. A wick is enough.
  2. Reclaim. This candle closes back above the zone.
  3. Fresh. Either this candle's low dipped into the zone, or the candle before closed at or below the zone. This stops it buying the 5th candle of a rally that left the zone long ago.
  4. Strong candle. It is green, and its body is at least 0.4 × ATR.

Example — not a real trade. What you see: after the gate passes, NIFTY drifts back down. The 10:35 candle dips to 24,446.58 and closes at 24,451, below the zone (VWAP 24,453.82, which is just above EMA20 here, so it is the nearer line). The 10:40 candle closes at 24,469, back above the zone, with a 18.48-point green body (needed: 6.98 = 0.4 × ATR 17.44). All four checks pass: BUY CE. Why it matters: the dip was bought, not continued. The stop goes at the lowest low of the last 3 candles, 24,443.92.

Where the stop goes

  • The stop is the dip's lowest low (last 3 candles) for a CE, or the bounce's highest high for a PE.
  • If that is closer than 12 points, the stop is moved out to 12 points.
  • If it is more than 40 points away, the trade is skipped. The stop is never squeezed inside the dip.
  • The stop, target and breakeven levels are set from the actual fill price, so the risk stays exactly the planned number of points.

Which option

One strike (50 points) in the money. Example: NIFTY at 24,469 → nearest strike 24,450 → the bot buys the 24,400 CE. For a PE at 24,475 → nearest strike 24,500 → the 24,550 PE.

5. Step 3 — How it gets out

Everything is decided at entry. The bot checks these on every price tick, in this order:

#ExitRule
1StopNIFTY reaches the stop (or the breakeven stop after the jump).
2TargetNIFTY reaches entry + 2.5R (CE) or entry − 2.5R (PE). Taken at once, never trailed.
3Breakeven jumpWhen the trade is +1R, the stop moves once to entry + 3 points (CE) or entry − 3 (PE). It never moves again.
4Time stopIf the breakeven jump has not happened within 25 minutes of entry, sell.
5Premium stopIf the option falls 25% below the price paid, sell. This catches a fall in option value that NIFTY's price alone would not show.
6Square-offAt 15:10 anything still open is sold.
No trailing stop, on purpose. A trailing stop often gets hit by a normal pullback just before the move carries on. Here the fixed target does that job. The stop moves exactly once, to breakeven. The app-wide profit lock used by some other strategies is not part of this strategy's exits.

6. Example trades — one entry, four endings

The next four charts use the same day and the same entry (CE at 24,469, stop 24,443.92, 1R = 25.08 points). Only what happens after the entry changes.

Example — not a real trade. Winner. What you see: NIFTY rises from 24,469. At 10:55 it reaches +1R (24,494.08), so the orange stop line jumps from 24,443.92 to 24,472 — entry + 3. It never moves again. At 11:10 NIFTY touches the target 24,531.7, and the bot sells. Result: +₹2,445 on one lot before charges. Why it matters: the whole plan worked: fixed risk, one stop move, fixed profit.

Example — not a real trade. Loser. What you see: same entry. NIFTY never reaches +1R. It falls back through the dip low, and at 10:55 the stop at 24,443.92 is hit. Result: −₹978 (1R). Why it matters: the loss is known before the trade starts. Losses range from about 12 to 40 points, depending on how deep the dip was.

Example — not a real trade. Scratch. What you see: NIFTY reaches +1R at 10:55, so the stop jumps to 24,472. Then it turns down and hits that stop at 11:00. Result: +₹117 before charges — about break-even after them. Why it matters: once a trade has been +1R, it cannot become a real loss. A breakeven exit does not count as one of the day's two stop-outs.

Example — not a real trade. Time stop. What you see: NIFTY goes sideways after the entry. It never reaches +1R and never hits the stop. 25 minutes after entry (the end of the 11:05 candle) the bot sells at 24,464. Result: −₹195. Why it matters: a stuck option only loses value to time. Getting out early frees the trade budget for a better chance. A time-stop exit does not count as a stop-out.

7. A PE day

Example — not a real trade. What you see: the first hour falls 123 points (0.5%), the last six closes are below VWAP, and price is 55.19 points under VWAP (24.65 needed). PE-only day. At 10:35 NIFTY bounces up and touches the zone (VWAP 24,489.33). The 10:40 candle is a strong red candle that closes back below it at 24,475: BUY PE. Stop = the bounce high 24,498.2 (23.2 pts). At 10:50 the trade reaches +1R and the stop jumps to 24,472. At 11:05 the target 24,417 is hit. Result: +₹2,262. Why it matters: the PE rules are the exact mirror of the CE rules.

8. Days it says no

This is what most days look like. A day with no trade makes ₹0, and on a bad day for option buyers, ₹0 is a good result.

Example — not a real trade. What you see: a big first hour (130 points, 0.53% — test 1 passes), but price swings across VWAP again and again. Test 2 fails (the last 6 closes are not all on one side). Test 3 also fails: at 10:15 price is only 19.35 points from VWAP (25.9 needed). No trade today. Why it matters: a big range alone is not a trend.

Example — not a real trade. What you see: a slow, gentle rise. The last 6 closes are above VWAP and price is far enough from it, so tests 2 and 3 pass. But the whole first hour moved only 35.61 points (0.15% — 0.5% needed). No trade today. Why it matters: one failed test is enough. A quiet morning rarely gives a move big enough to pay for an option.

9. Setups it skips on a good day

Example — not a real trade. What you see: a valid CE day. A sharp dip to 24,415.92 and a strong reclaim candle closing at 24,465. Every entry check passes. But the stop would have to sit 49.08 points away, more than the 40-point cap. Skipped. The bot does not tighten the stop to make it fit. Why it matters: a 49-point loss on one lot is about ₹1,900 of premium — too big for this strategy.

Example — not a real trade. What you see: the dip touches the zone and the 10:40 candle closes back above it — but with a body of only 5 points. The rule needs 0.4 × ATR (about 6.6 points here). Skipped. The 10:45 candle is also too small (5.9 pts vs 6.5 needed). After that, price runs away from the zone without dipping back, so there is no fresh setup. Why it matters: a small, unsure candle is not proof that buyers stepped in.

Other reasons it skips: the reclaim candle is the wrong colour; price is "already running" (no fresh dip); it is after 14:00; the day is closed by a limit; there is no option price or not enough free capital.

10. Safety limits

Example — not a real trade. What you see: trade 1 is stopped at 10:55 (−₹978). A second dip-and-reclaim gives trade 2 at 11:35, and it is stopped at 11:45 (−₹1,019). That is 2 real stop-outs, so the day is over. Day result: −₹1,997. Why it matters: two failed dips on a "trend day" means the read was wrong. Stopping protects the account.

LimitDefaultWhat happens
Max trades per day2Costs are paid per trade, so fewer is usually better.
Stop-outs that end the day2After 2 real stop-outs, no more entries. Breakeven and time-stop exits do not count.

Max weekly loss0 (off)If set, no entries for the rest of the week once the week's loss reaches it.

Square-off15:10Nothing is held overnight.

11. The money

One lot (65 units), entry premium ₹240, option moves 0.6 rupee per NIFTY point. Charges are from the app's own charges calculator (Fyers). Slippage is not included — in real trading it takes more off every trade.

Do the math. With a 2.5R target, the bot breaks even before costs if it is right about 29% of the time (1 win pays for 2.5 losses). After costs and slippage it needs more. Whether it reaches that is not known yet.

12. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

  • Paper (/trend-day-scalp-paper) — live prices, pretend orders. This is the master copy of the rules.
  • Live (/trend-day-scalp-live) — runs Paper and sends the same decisions to Fyers. A real order needs all of: TDS_LIVE_ENABLED=true, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false, and TDS_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. By default no real order is placed.
  • Backtest (/trend-day-scalp-backtest) — the same gate and entry rules over past days. If a candle touched both the stop and the target, it books the loss. A candle that opened past a level fills at the open. The breakeven stop can only be hit on a later candle than the one that armed it. There is no option price history, so premiums are a model (seed ₹240) and the premium stop is only an estimate.
  • Replay — plays back a recorded day tick by tick, to check that Live and Paper agree.
  • Crash safety — an open trade is saved to disk and checked against the broker after a restart.

14. What we do not know yet

  • No proven track record. No win rate or profit figure has been measured.
  • Few trades. The gate rejects most days. On the 39 measured sessions it passed about 31% of days, and about 10 trades followed. That sample's net was negative (about −₹738). It is not enough to judge.
  • Backtest rupees are a model. There is no option price history.
  • No VIX or open-interest filter. A wild day is treated like a calm one.
  • Next step: run Paper for a long time, replay the recorded days, and only then think about Live with the dry-run brake on.
