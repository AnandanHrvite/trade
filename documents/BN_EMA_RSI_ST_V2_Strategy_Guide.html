BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2

The NIFTY BANK twin of EMA_RSI_ST_V2. Same rules, same engine file, same default settings — but a bigger, slower-decaying option underneath, and that changes the risk.

Updated 2026-10-08  ·  15 example charts  ·  every chart is an illustration, not a real trade

In one minute

  • What it trades: buys one NIFTY BANK option (a CE when it thinks the index will rise, a PE when it thinks it will fall). Always the monthly contract, at the strike nearest to the index.
  • When it buys: on a finished 5-minute candle, three things must agree: EMA20 above EMA50, the candle closes above EMA20, and RSI is above 52. (PE: all three flipped, RSI below 48.)
  • Then it waits one candle: the next candle must cross the signal candle's close. Only then is the option bought.
  • How it gets out: the shared Profit Lock, the SuperTrend stop, an opposite signal, or 14:00 — whichever comes first. There is no fixed target.
  • Limits: entries only 10:30–13:00, at most 2 trades a day, stop for the day after a ₹1,000 loss or 2 losses in a row.
  • Status: never traded live, no paper sessions yet. Every number here is a setting, not a result.
Read this first: nothing here has been measured.

BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2 has never traded live and has no paper sessions and no backtests on NIFTY BANK data. There is no win rate and no profit figure for it anywhere.

About the charts. Every chart is made from invented NIFTY BANK candles (around 54,000). The EMA, RSI and SuperTrend lines, and every signal, skip and stop on them, were worked out by running the strategy's real engine code on those candles. Option prices are a simple model: a ₹360 at-the-money monthly option that moves ₹0.50 for each point NIFTY BANK moves. P&L is for 1 lot (30 units) before charges. They show how the rules behave — not how much money the strategy makes.

1. The idea, with a full example day

Buy in the direction NIFTY BANK is already moving. Do it only when three simple tests agree on the same finished 5-minute candle. Then wait one more candle to make sure the move is real.

EMA20 above EMA50
→
candle closes above EMA20
→
RSI above 52
→
next candle crosses that close
→
buy the monthly CE
→
exit on lock, stop, opposite signal or 14:00

What you see: a made-up NIFTY BANK morning. At 10:30 the candle closes above the orange EMA20, EMA20 (54,040.91) is above the purple EMA50 (54,014.45) and RSI is 56.4 — all three rules pass, so the candle is marked signal 1. The next candle (10:35) crosses that close and the bot buys a CE at 54,060.95. The shared Profit Lock sells it at 11:25 for +₹949. A second signal at 11:25 gives trade 2 (11:30–11:40, +₹1,321). After two trades the day is over, even though the market keeps rising. Why it matters: this is the whole strategy in one picture — three rules on one candle, one confirmation candle, then an exit. P&L is for 1 lot (30) of a ₹360 at-the-money monthly option, before charges.

2. Words you'll see

  • Candle — one bar on the chart. Here each candle is 5 minutes. A finished candle is one that has closed. The bot only decides on finished candles.
  • EMA20 / EMA50 — moving averages: the average closing price of the last 20 and last 50 candles, with recent candles counting more. EMA20 above EMA50 means "short-term trend is up".
  • RSI(14) — a 0–100 score of recent buying vs selling over 14 candles. 50 is neutral. Above 52 = buyers slightly in control; below 48 = sellers slightly in control.
  • SuperTrend(10, 2) — a line drawn from the size of recent candles. In an uptrend it sits below price (green); in a downtrend it sits above (red). Here it is only used as the stop.
  • Stop (stop-loss) — the price where the bot sells to cap a loss.
  • Trail — a stop that follows price in your favour. It may move closer, never further away.
  • Trigger — the close of the signal candle. The next candle must cross it before anything is bought.
  • CE / PE — a Call option (gains when NIFTY BANK rises) and a Put option (gains when it falls).
  • Premium — the price of the option itself, in rupees. Not the NIFTY BANK level.
  • Monthly contract — NIFTY BANK options expire once a month (NSE stopped the weekly ones in Nov-2024). A monthly option loses value to time slowly.
  • Lot — the trading unit. One NIFTY BANK lot is 30 units, so ₹1 of premium = ₹30.
  • VIX — India VIX, the market's "fear gauge". High VIX = wild swings.

3. Same rules as the NIFTY version

  • One engine file. Both strategies run src/strategies/ema_rsi_st_v2.js. The NIFTY BANK pages pass the prefix BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2, so they read their own settings.
  • A rule change hits both at once. There is only one copy of the rules, so they can never drift apart.
  • Settings are separate. Every key here starts with BN_. Changing the NIFTY version's RSI level does nothing here, and the other way round.
  • They can run side by side. Each has its own position file and its own lock slot.

Two facts stack up.
  1. NIFTY BANK moves about twice as many points as NIFTY for the same % move. The SuperTrend stop is measured in index points, so it usually sits further away.
  2. A monthly option decays slowly. A losing position does not lose value to time very fast, so it keeps moving with the index until the stop.

The guards (₹1,000 daily loss, 2 trades a day) are copied from NIFTY and not resized. Nothing in the code adjusts for this. See section 13.

5. Entry rule 1 — trend: EMA20 vs EMA50

  • CE: EMA20 must be above EMA50.
  • PE: EMA20 must be below EMA50.
  • If they are exactly equal, nothing happens.

What you see: while the orange EMA20 is above the purple EMA50 (green background) the bot may only look for CE trades. At 11:25 EMA20 drops below EMA50 (white dot). From then on (red background) only PE trades are allowed — and the bot takes two PE trades (11:30–11:40 +₹1,036, 11:45–12:05 +₹1,153). Why it matters: rule 1 decides the direction. The bot never buys against it.

6. Entry rule 2 — the candle closes beyond EMA20

  • CE: the candle must close above EMA20. Poking above and closing below does not count.
  • PE: the candle must close below EMA20.

What you see: the trend is up (EMA20 above EMA50), but at 11:25 the candles close below EMA20. That is a pullback, and the bot does nothing. At 11:50 a candle closes back above EMA20 with RSI above 52 — that is the signal. This one still lost (−₹1,089) — the rules pick the moment, they do not promise a winner. Why it matters: rule 2 is not the same as rule 1. An uptrend alone is not enough; price must be back on top of EMA20 on the same candle.

7. Entry rule 3 — RSI agrees

  • CE: RSI(14) must be above 52.
  • PE: RSI(14) must be below 48.
  • There is no upper cap. RSI 95 is still a valid CE. There is no lower cap for PE either.
  • 52 and 48 sit just off the middle on purpose. Rules 1 and 2 already give the direction; RSI only blocks entries with weak momentum.

What you see: at 11:40 the trend is up and the candle closes above EMA20, but RSI is only 51.6 (grey dot in the lower panel). That is not above 52, so no signal. At 11:50 RSI is 59.9 and the signal fires. Why it matters: RSI is the veto. It stops a buy when the push behind the candle is weak.

All three on the same candle. The three rules are read off one finished candle. RSI being high an hour ago does not count.

8. The confirmation candle

The candle that passes all three rules does not buy. It only arms the trade. This is ON by default.

  1. A finished candle passes all three rules. Its closing price becomes the trigger.
  2. During the very next candle, price must go past the trigger: above it for a CE, below it for a PE.
  3. The bot buys the moment that happens, inside that candle. It does not wait for the candle to close.
  4. If that candle never crosses, the setup is dropped. (If that candle is itself a new signal, it arms again for the candle after it.)

What you see: a close-up of trade 1. The signal candle closes at 54,053.8 — that close is the trigger (yellow line). During the very next candle price goes above the trigger, so the bot buys right there, at 54,060.95. It does not wait for that candle to close. The red line is the first stop: the SuperTrend value on the signal candle, 53,987.11. Why it matters: the signal candle only arms the trade. The next candle has to prove the move is real.

What you see: a CE signal at 12:00 sets the trigger at 54,115.8. The next candle (12:05) only reaches 54,112.45 — it never goes above the trigger. The setup is thrown away. Why it matters: there is exactly one candle to confirm. No cross, no trade.

Turning confirmation off (BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_CONFIRM_CANDLE_ENABLED=false) makes the bot buy at the close of the signal candle itself.

9. Which option is bought

  • The strike nearest to the NIFTY BANK price, on the 100-point grid (for example, NIFTY BANK at 54,061 → strike 54,100). The shared BANKNIFTY_STRIKE_OFFSET_CE / _PE settings can shift it.
  • Always the current monthly expiry. A weekly-style symbol is never built.
  • Quantity = 1 lot of 30 × the global lot multiplier.
  • In live data, if the option's bid-ask spread is wider than MAX_BID_ASK_SPREAD_PTS (default 2 points), the entry is skipped.

The Profit Lock books it at 11:40 for +₹1,036. A second PE (11:45–12:05) makes +₹1,153. Why it matters: PE rules are the exact opposite of CE rules. There is no extra filter on either side.

Deliberately absent (do not "add back" — that is the older V1 strategy):
  • No SuperTrend test on entry, no RSI cap, no EMA9, no EMA21.
  • No negative-candle stop, no option-price stop, no fixed-point stop, no time stop.
  • No profit target, no partial booking. (The shared Profit Lock is the only profit rule.)

11. Trades it refuses

  • SuperTrend on the wrong side of price — nowhere to put a stop, so the setup is skipped and logged.
  • Outside 10:30–13:00 — the signal candle must start between 10:30 and 12:50, because the confirmation candle after it must also start before 13:00. (With confirmation off, 12:55 is the last signal candle.)
  • India VIX above 20 — uses the shared VIX_FILTER_ENABLED (on unless set to false) and VIX_MAX_ENTRY (20).
  • The confirmation candle never crossed the trigger.
  • Not enough history — 55 candles are needed (EMA50 + 5). That is more than one day, so 5 earlier days are loaded first.
  • A daily limit is reached or a cooldown is running (next section).
  • The option spread is too wide or the option symbol does not exist.

What you see: at 11:15 all three entry rules pass (close 54,174.55, RSI 54.5). But SuperTrend is at 54,185.67 — above the close. A CE stop must sit below price, so there is nowhere to put it. The bot skips the trade and writes the reason in its log. Why it matters: SuperTrend is not an entry rule, but in practice a CE is never bought while SuperTrend is above price.

What you see: a strong morning. By 09:40 the three rules are already true, but the grey area (09:15–10:30) is closed for entries. The first candle allowed to be a signal is the 10:30 candle; the bot buys on the next candle (10:35) and the Profit Lock sells at 11:00 (+₹794). Why it matters: the opening hour is skipped on purpose — NIFTY BANK swings the most then, and early crosses often reverse.

What you see: the same candles as the first chart, with India VIX (lower panel) above 20 until 11:30. The 10:30 signal is confirmed at 10:35, but VIX is 21.12, so the buy is refused. Later VIX drops below 20 and the next signal trades normally (11:30–11:40, +₹1,321). Why it matters: on very nervous days the bot sits out. This uses the shared EMA_RSI_ST VIX settings, not a BN-only key.

What you see: a sideways day. CE #1 (10:35–11:15) loses ₹974, CE #2 (11:55–12:10) loses ₹1,089. Total −₹2,063. That is two losses in a row and more than the ₹1,000 daily limit, so the bot stops for the rest of the day (red area). Why it matters: on a choppy day every cross is fake. The guards make sure a bad day stays small.

13. Why the limits are a different size here

Simple arithmetic. One lot is 30 units. An at-the-money option moves roughly ₹0.50 for each NIFTY BANK point. So 1 point against you ≈ ₹15 per lot, and the ₹1,000 daily limit is used up by a move of only about 67 NIFTY BANK points. A single SuperTrend stop is often further away than that (the losing example above was 93 points).

So in practice one stop-out can end the day. That may be what you want on an untested strategy, but it is not a tuned risk budget. The numbers were copied from NIFTY and nothing in the code rescales them. Review lot size and the daily limit together before turning live orders on.

14. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

Paper (/bn_ema_rsi_st_v2-paper) is the reference. Every rule lives there; the other modes copy it.

Live (/bn_ema_rsi_st_v2-live-harness) wraps Paper and sends its decisions to the broker. A real order needs all three: BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_ENABLED on, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN off, and BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_DRY_RUN not on. All ship in the safe position.

Two brokers. Prices come from Fyers; orders go to Zerodha.

Backtest (/bn_ema_rsi_st_v2-backtest) uses the same engine and the same confirmation-fill helper, so its entries match Paper. Its rupees are modelled: there is no historical option chain, so premium = seed (₹180) moved by BACKTEST_DELTA (0.55) and reduced by BACKTEST_THETA_DAY (₹8/day), plus 2 points slippage each way. Those defaults are NIFTY-weekly shaped. A BANKNIFTY monthly costs much more than ₹180 and decays more slowly. Read a backtest as a test of the signal, not of money.

Replay (/replay) re-runs a recorded day tick by tick. Nothing reads a half-formed candle, so a recorded session replays exactly. Use it to check that Live and Paper agree before touching a live switch.

16. What is still missing

  • No live trades, no paper sessions, no NIFTY BANK backtest. There is no win rate, no trade count, no average win or loss.
  • No setting was tuned for NIFTY BANK. 52/48, SuperTrend 10/2, 2 trades, ₹1,000 — all copied from NIFTY.
  • The daily loss limit is small for this index (about 67 points on 1 lot).
  • Backtest rupees use NIFTY-weekly assumptions (₹180 seed, ₹8/day decay). Fix them before reading any ₹ figure.
  • One engine, two strategies. Changing a rule in ema_rsi_st_v2.js changes the NIFTY version too.
  • Next step: turn the mode on, run paper sessions with the tick recorder on, replay one of those days, and confirm Paper and Live agree. Only then is there data to judge.
