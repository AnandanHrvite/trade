BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2

The NIFTY BANK twin of EMA_RSI_ST_V2. The rules are identical — same engine file, same three entry conditions, same single stop, same guards, same defaults. What changes is the instrument underneath them, and that change is bigger than it looks.

Updated 2026-09-05  ·  plain-English edition  ·  no charts and no measured results — see the box below

Read this first: nothing here has been measured.

BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2 has never traded live, has zero paper sessions, and has zero backtests on NIFTY BANK data. There is not one recorded trade of this strategy on this instrument. Every number in this guide is either a configured setting (a value the Settings page ships) or an instrument fact (the strike grid, the lot size, the expiry cadence). None of them is a result.

There are no charts. The illustrative candle charts in the other guides were rendered from real engine output. Nothing has been run on NIFTY BANK candles here, so there is no real output to draw — and a hand-drawn trade would be a lie about a strategy that has never traded. A chart-free guide is the honest version.

Do not import the NIFTY twin's numbers. Where this guide mentions what the same rules did on NIFTY, it names NIFTY explicitly and says the NIFTY BANK equivalent is unknown. Those are two different instruments with two different option chains; a NIFTY figure is context, never a forecast.

1. The idea in one minute

Buy in the direction NIFTY BANK is already going, but only once, and only when three cheap tests agree on the same finished five-minute candle.

The first test is the trend: the 20-candle average has to be above the 50-candle average for a Call. The second is the price itself: the candle has to finish above that 20-candle average, not just poke at it. The third is momentum: the RSI reading has to be on the right side of the middle. For a Put, flip all three.

That is the whole entry. There is no profit target. The trade is held until a SuperTrend line — one line, drawn from the NIFTY BANK candles themselves — catches up to price, or until 14:00 comes and everything is closed regardless.

EMA20 above EMA50
→
candle closes above EMA20
→
RSI above 52
→
next candle crosses that close
→
buy the BANKNIFTY monthly
→
hold until SuperTrend, or 14:00

2. Identical to the NIFTY version — what that means

This is not a variant. It is the same strategy pointed at a different index.
  • One engine file. Both run src/strategies/ema_rsi_st_v2.js. There is no separate NIFTY BANK engine. Every call from the NIFTY BANK routes passes the prefix BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2, which is how the two read different settings out of one piece of code.
  • The same three entry conditions, in the same order, read off the same last fully-closed 5-minute candle.
  • The same single stop — SuperTrend(10, 2), initial and trail, tighten-only, enforced intra-candle.
  • The same guards, with the same numeric defaults: 10:30–13:00, 2 trades a day, ₹1,000 loss cap, 2 consecutive losses, 2-candle pauses, 14:00 square-off.
  • The same deliberate omissions — no SuperTrend entry gate, no RSI cap, no EMA21, no negative-candle stop, no premium stop, no points stop, no target.

The practical consequence cuts both ways. A rule change lands in both variants at once, because there is only one engine to change — the rules can never quietly drift apart. But the settings tune completely independently: every key here is BN_-prefixed, so raising the RSI threshold on NIFTY does nothing to NIFTY BANK, and vice versa. The two can run side by side, each holding its own position.

3. The instrument, and why it changes the risk

This is the part of the guide that actually matters. The rules are borrowed wholesale; the instrument is not.

ExpiryWeekly contracts availableMonthly only. NSE withdrew BANKNIFTY weekly options in Nov-2024, so there is no weekly contract to buy
Lot size6530
The risk that nobody adjusted for.

Two facts stack on top of each other here, and neither is compensated for anywhere in the code.

One: a monthly decays far more slowly than a weekly. Theta on a BANKNIFTY monthly is a fraction of what a NIFTY weekly pays per day. That sounds like good news — the position bleeds less per hour when it goes nowhere — and in one sense it is. But it also means the premium does not fall away when the trade is wrong. The option refuses to die. A losing position keeps most of its value while the index keeps moving against the spot stop.

Two: NIFTY BANK moves roughly twice NIFTY's points for the same percentage move. The SuperTrend stop is measured in index points, not in premium. So a SuperTrend distance that looks the same on a chart is a materially larger move — and a materially larger rupee loss — here than on NIFTY.

Put together: the same ₹1,000 daily cap and the same SuperTrend stop distance represent a materially LARGER rupee risk on NIFTY BANK than they do on NIFTY. Nothing in the code compensates. The guards are the NIFTY numbers carried over verbatim — they were not scaled for the bigger index or the slower decay. Sizing must be reviewed before the dry-run is ever switched off.

And the backtest's decay figure is the wrong one. The shared backtest models premium decay with BACKTEST_THETA_DAY, which is a NIFTY weekly decay figure. Applied to a BANKNIFTY monthly it overstates decay — a monthly loses value far more slowly than that number assumes. So a NIFTY BANK backtest is charging the trade for time-decay it would not actually pay. Combined with the seed-premium guess (see §13), it means the rupee output of a NIFTY BANK backtest is even less trustworthy than the NIFTY version's, and the NIFTY version's was already labelled a modelled estimate rather than a measurement. Do not read a NIFTY BANK backtest as money.

4. Words you'll see

  • EMA20 / EMA50 — exponential moving averages of the closing price over 20 and 50 candles. The recent candles count for more than the old ones, so they turn faster than a plain average. EMA20 above EMA50 is the usual shorthand for "the short-term trend is up".
  • RSI(14) — a 0–100 momentum reading over the last 14 candles. 50 is the neutral middle. Above it means buyers have had the upper hand recently; below it means sellers have.
  • SuperTrend(10, 2) — a line that sits below price while the trend is up and flips above it when the trend turns. The 10 is how many candles of range it measures; the 2 is how many of those ranges it stands away from price. Here it is only a stop. Note the range it measures is a NIFTY BANK range, so the same settings put the line further from price in points than they do on NIFTY.
  • Finished candle — a five-minute bar that has closed. The engine never reads the bar that is still forming, which is exactly why Paper and Replay give the same answer twice.
  • Trigger — the closing price of the candle that passed all three rules. It is the price the next candle has to cross before anything is bought.
  • Tighten-only trail — a stop that is allowed to move closer to price but never further away. If the SuperTrend line slips back mid-trade, the stop stays where it was.
  • Spot points — movement measured in NIFTY BANK index points, not in rupees of option premium. The two are not the same thing.
  • Monthly contract — the only kind of BANKNIFTY option that exists since Nov-2024. It expires at the end of the month, not the end of the week, and therefore loses value to time much more slowly.
  • CE / PE — a Call option (you profit if NIFTY BANK rises) and a Put option (you profit if it falls).

5. The entry — three rules, one candle

Everything is decided on a finished five-minute NIFTY BANK candle. All three tests are read off that same candle. Not one of them is allowed to have been true an hour ago.

 CE — buy a CallPE — buy a Put
1. TrendEMA20 above EMA50EMA20 below EMA50
2. Closethe candle closes above EMA20the candle closes below EMA20
3. MomentumRSI(14) above 52RSI(14) below 48

The PE side is the exact mirror of the CE side. There is no asymmetry anywhere — no side gets an extra filter or a different stop.

Rule 2 is not the same as rule 1. EMA20 can sit above EMA50 (an uptrend) while the candle itself closes below EMA20 (a pullback). That is a common state, and it is not bought. The rules want the trend up and price back on top of the fast average on the same bar.
52 and 48, not 70 and 30. Those are barely off the neutral 50. This is on purpose: rules 1 and 2 already establish direction, so the RSI test is here to veto a signal firing into obvious weakness, not to hunt for extremes. Raise BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_RSI_CE_MIN and you get fewer, later, stronger entries — and you will miss the start of most moves. Note these are the NIFTY thresholds carried over unchanged; no threshold has been fitted to NIFTY BANK behaviour, because no NIFTY BANK data has been run.

6. The confirmation candle

The candle that passes all three rules does not buy anything. It only arms the trade. Default: ON.

How it actually works.
  1. A finished candle passes all three rules. Call its closing price the trigger.
  2. During the immediately next candle, price must cross that trigger — strictly above it for a CE, strictly below for a PE.
  3. If it does, the fill happens inside that candle, at the crossing price. Not at the next close.
  4. If that one candle never crosses the trigger, the setup is dropped. It does not carry over.

Paper and Live fill on the first tick past the trigger. The backtest fills at the same market moment through a shared helper, so all three modes agree by construction rather than by luck.

What this buys you and what it costs you. It throws away the signals that were a one-candle spike and immediately snapped back — the classic way a moving-average rule gets whipsawed. The cost is that you never get the first candle of a move, and on a gap-and-run day the crossing price can be well above where the signal candle closed. That cost is larger on NIFTY BANK, where the index travels further inside a single five-minute bar. Turning it off (BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_CONFIRM_CANDLE_ENABLED=false) enters on the signal candle itself.

7. The stop — SuperTrend and nothing else

  • The initial stop is the SuperTrend value at the moment of entry — below price for a CE, above it for a PE.
  • The trail is the same line, re-read at every candle close.
  • Tighten-only. A CE stop may only move up; a PE stop may only move down. A SuperTrend that loosens is ignored.
  • Enforced intra-candle. The execution layer checks every tick. You do not wait for a candle to close to be stopped out.
  • Hard close at 14:00, stop or no stop. Nothing is carried past it and nothing is carried overnight.
The stop distance is not a fixed risk, and here it is a bigger one. SuperTrend spacing depends on how wide the recent candles were, so a strategy with no fixed stop has no fixed risk per trade. On a volatile morning the stop is far away and the loss is large; on a quiet one it is close and you are stopped by noise. Because NIFTY BANK candles are roughly twice as wide in points as NIFTY's, the whole distribution of stop distances sits further out — in points and, unless you resize, in rupees. Nothing clamps either end.
Nothing overnight matters more here. The 14:00 square-off is not just tidiness. A monthly BANKNIFTY option held over a gap can move a long way before you are able to act on it, and there is no stop that works while the market is closed.

8. What is deliberately absent

These are deliberate removals inherited from the NIFTY V2 design, not oversights. If you are reading this later and think one of them should be "added back", that is V1 — and V1 already exists, untouched, elsewhere in this app.

  • No SuperTrend gate on entry. SuperTrend is only the stop. A CE can legitimately fire while SuperTrend is red.
  • No RSI cap. One threshold per side and no ceiling — RSI 95 is a valid CE.
  • No EMA9 triple stack and no EMA21 anywhere — not in the entry, not in the exit, not in the trail.
  • No negative-candle stop.
  • No option-premium stop.
  • No fixed-point stop.
  • No profit target, no breakeven jump, no partial booking.

If a trade is open, the only two things that can close it are the SuperTrend line and the clock.

The one honest caveat, and it is not a small one.

SuperTrend is the only stop, and SuperTrend is not tested on entry. Those two facts collide. A setup can pass all three entry rules while the SuperTrend line is sitting on the wrong side of price — above the close on a CE. If that trade were opened, its stop would already be behind it: there would be no protective stop at all.

The engine skips those setups and writes the reason to the log. It does not open them unprotected. How often that happens on NIFTY BANK is unknown — the skip rate has never been measured on this instrument. (On NIFTY the same rule refused about one setup in seven over 2024–25; there is no reason to assume the NIFTY BANK proportion matches, and it has not been checked.)

The side effect is worth saying plainly: in practice a CE is never taken while SuperTrend is red. So SuperTrend still behaves like a partial directional filter, even though it is not written as an entry rule. If you ever describe this strategy as "no SuperTrend on entry", that is true of the rules and not quite true of the behaviour.

9. The trades it refuses

  • The setup with nowhere to put a stop — SuperTrend on the wrong side of price, as described just above. Skipped and logged, never opened unprotected.
  • Outside 10:30–13:00. No entries before or after, whatever the candle says. The open is skipped deliberately, and NIFTY BANK's opening swing is the widest of the day.
  • The confirmation candle never crossed the trigger. One candle to cross, and only one.
  • Not enough history. The engine refuses outright until it has EMA_SLOW + 5 candles — 55 bars at the defaults, which is more than a whole session, so previous days are preloaded before the day starts.
  • The day's budget is spent — 2 trades done, or the loss cap hit, or two losses in a row.
  • Still in a cooldown after a recent stop-out, on either side.
  • The option symbol does not validate. Because BANKNIFTY is monthly-only, the expiry code is always a monthly one; a weekly-shaped symbol would not exist and is never built.

10. Safety limits — and why they are the wrong size here

  • Entries only between 10:30 and 13:00 IST. The open is skipped deliberately — its crosses reverse — and a late entry has no room left before the 14:00 close.
  • Maximum 2 trades a day. Deliberately tight: a couple of good crosses, not a session-long grind.
  • ₹1,000 daily loss cap. Reach it and the day is over.
  • Stop for the day after 2 consecutive losses. The usual sign the market is chopping and every cross is fake.
  • 2-candle pause on the same side after a stop-out, so the engine cannot re-buy the identical failed signal ten minutes later.
  • 2-candle opposite-side cooldown, so a stopped-out CE cannot flip straight into a PE on the next candle.
  • Hard square-off at 14:00. Nothing overnight, ever.
  • The shared portfolio-wide cap applies on top of all of this.
Every one of those numbers is the NIFTY number, unchanged.

The ₹1,000 cap was chosen as a throttle on an unproven NIFTY strategy. It was copied here without being rescaled for an index that swings about twice as far in a day. The consequence is that the same ₹1,000 is reached by a much smaller move against you, in percentage terms, than it is on NIFTY. Expect it to end sessions earlier here — and with 2 trades a day allowed, a single bad trade can end the day before the second is ever taken.

That may be exactly what you want on an unvalidated strategy. It is not, however, a tuned risk budget for NIFTY BANK. Review the cap and the position size together before turning the dry-run off.

12. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

Paper is the canonical surface. Every decision, fill and exit rule lives there; the other modes follow it, never the reverse.

Live is a harness that wraps Paper and forwards its decisions to the broker, so Live equals Paper by construction rather than by two implementations agreeing. It is gated three ways: BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_ENABLED must be on, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN must be off, and BN_EMA_RSI_ST_V2_LIVE_DRY_RUN must not be on. All three ship in the safe position, so nothing places a real order out of the box.

Two brokers. Candles and quotes come from Fyers; orders go to Zerodha — the same split the NIFTY twin uses. That is why starting Live checks both logins.

Its own position. NIFTY BANK holds its own position file and its own mutex slot, so it can run alongside the NIFTY variant without either interfering with the other.

Replay. Because nothing reads a forming candle or live spot, a recorded session replays bit-for-bit. Replay pins the recorded day's monthly BANKNIFTY expiry — it never substitutes the NIFTY expiry. That is the tool to use before touching a live gate: replay a paper day and confirm Live and Paper made the same decisions.

What the backtest can and cannot tell you here. The entries are honest — the backtest drives the same signal engine and fills the confirmation candle at the same market moment through a shared helper. The rupees are not. There is no historical option chain in this repo, so the premium is modelled from a seed price and a decay figure — and as §3 explains, that decay figure is a NIFTY-weekly one applied to a BANKNIFTY monthly, which overstates the decay. The seed premium (₹180 by default) is a NIFTY-weekly-shaped guess too; a BANKNIFTY monthly ATM premium is typically well above it. Treat a NIFTY BANK backtest as a test of the signal, and not at all as a statement about money.

14. What's still missing / not validated

Be honest with yourself about what you are looking at.
  • It has never traded live. Not one order, not one rupee.
  • Zero paper sessions. There is no forward-tested record at all — nothing has been observed running against a live NIFTY BANK tick feed.
  • Zero backtests on NIFTY BANK. No historical run of these rules on this index exists. There is no win rate, no net figure, no trade count, no stop-distance distribution — not bad ones, none.
  • That is also why there are no charts. Every chart in the sibling guides was rendered from real engine output. There is no real NIFTY BANK output to render, and a hand-drawn illustration of a trade that never happened would be worse than an empty page.
  • One engine, two strategies. This shares src/strategies/ema_rsi_st_v2.js with the NIFTY variant, so a rule change lands in both at once — check the other one before changing anything here. The settings, by contrast, tune independently: nothing you change under BN_ affects NIFTY, and nothing you change on NIFTY affects this.
  • No threshold has been fitted to this instrument. 52 and 48, SuperTrend 10 and 2, 2 trades a day, the ₹1,000 cap — every one of them was carried over from NIFTY. They are repo conventions, not values that earned their place on NIFTY BANK data.
  • The guards are the wrong size and nothing in the code fixes that. A monthly option decays slowly and NIFTY BANK moves roughly twice NIFTY's points, so the same cap and the same stop distance are a materially larger rupee risk here. Review sizing before the dry-run is ever switched off.
  • A NIFTY BANK backtest, when one is run, will overstate decay. BACKTEST_THETA_DAY is a NIFTY-weekly figure and the seed premium is a NIFTY-weekly-shaped guess. Read the signal, not the rupees.
  • What to do next. Turn the mode on, run paper sessions with the tick recorder on, and let real NIFTY BANK signals accumulate. Replay one of those days and confirm Paper and Live agree. Only then is there any data to tune on — and only then does the question of whether these rules work on this index have an answer.
