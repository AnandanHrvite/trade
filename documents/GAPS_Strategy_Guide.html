GAPS

When the market has been stretched to an extreme and then opens the other way, that gap is usually the crowd changing its mind. GAPS takes one trade a day into that turn, risks exactly the size of the gap, and rides it with a moving line until the move gives up.

1. The idea in one minute

Imagine a stock that has gone up every day for weeks. Everybody is happy, nobody wants to sell. Then one morning it opens lower than where it closed yesterday. Nothing traded overnight — the market simply reopened at a worse price. Somebody, somewhere, changed their mind while you were asleep.

GAPS looks for exactly that combination:

Market is stretched (RSI at an extreme)
→
It opens the opposite way (a gap)
→
Buy one option, betting the turn continues
The whole strategy, in two sentences.
If yesterday-and-today's market is badly overbought and today opens below yesterday's close, buy a PE (a bet on falling). If it is badly oversold and today opens above yesterday's close, buy a CE (a bet on rising).

That is it. There is no trend filter, no volume check, no ADX, no VWAP, no open-interest gate, no confirmation candle and no second timeframe. This was a deliberate decision — the strategy is meant to be simple enough that you can always explain why a trade happened.

One trade a day, decided in the first minutes. The gap only exists at the open. By 10:00 the "gap" is just ordinary price movement, so GAPS makes its decision once, inside the entry window, and then does nothing else all day except manage the trade.

2. Words you'll see (plain meanings)

WordWhat it actually means
GapThe jump between yesterday's closing price and today's opening price. Yesterday closed 25,628 and today opens 25,554 → a 74-point gap down. No trading happened in between; the market just reopened lower.
RSIA 0–100 "how stretched is this?" meter. Near 100 means it has been going up relentlessly. Near 0 means the opposite. Most days it sits between 30 and 70.
RSI source = EMANormally RSI is calculated on closing prices. Here it is calculated on the EMA21 line instead — a smoothed version of price. Smoothing twice makes the meter far steadier, which is the only reason it can ever reach 90 or 10. On raw closes it almost never gets there.
EMA21An average of the last 21 bars that weights recent bars more heavily. It looks like a line drawn through the middle of price. Two different ones are used here — see the warning below.
PE / CEPE makes money if the market falls. CE makes money if it rises. GAPS always buys one of them, never sells.
Slightly ITMThe option strike is picked one step "in the money", which moves more closely with the index than a far-away strike would.
Careful — there are two different EMA21s in this strategy, and they do unrelated jobs.
  • GAPS_EMA_LENGTH=21 — on the daily chart. Its only job is to feed the RSI. It is not an exit level.
  • GAPS_TRAIL_EMA_LENGTH=21 — on the 5-minute chart. This is the trailing stop that gets you out.

They are separate settings on purpose: changing how smooth the RSI is must never quietly move your stop-loss.

3. Step 1 — Is the market stretched?

At the open the bot fetches roughly 400 days of daily NIFTY candles, draws the EMA21 through them, and then runs RSI over that line. If the answer is above 90 the market is badly overbought. Below 10, badly oversold. Anywhere in between and today is simply a no-trade day.

Which day's RSI? Today's. Not yesterday's.

At 09:15 today's daily candle has only just been born, and the one price it has is the opening price. So the bot builds today's daily bar from today's open and measures the RSI including it.

It deliberately uses the open and not the live moving price. The open is fixed the moment the market opens, so Paper, Live, Backtest and Replay all arrive at the identical number. A live-price RSI would drift every second and no replay could ever reproduce your decision.

The chart below is a real run of the engine. The blue line is the daily EMA21 — the line the RSI is measured on. Notice how it climbs almost every single day: that relentlessness is what pins the RSI at an extreme.

The setup. Weeks of grinding higher push RSI(14 on EMA21) to its ceiling. Yesterday closed at 25,628.49. Today opens at 25,554.49 — a 74-point gap down, straight into the strength. That is the trigger.

4. Step 2 — Did it gap the other way?

The gap is measured against yesterday's closing price — the last daily candle that actually finished before today. Today's forming candle is thrown away when working out "yesterday", so Paper, Live, Backtest and Replay all read the same bar.

Today's RSIWhat the market did at the openAction
above 90 (overbought)opens below yesterday's close — a gap DOWNBUY PE
below 10 (oversold)opens above yesterday's close — a gap UPBUY CE
between 10 and 90anythingno trade
above 90gaps UP (same direction)no trade
below 10gaps DOWN (same direction)no trade

Both halves must agree. An overbought market that gaps up is just carrying on — that is not the crowd changing its mind, so GAPS stands aside.

The gap is also your risk. Whatever the gap measures, that is the stop-loss distance. A 74-point gap means a 74-point stop. A 20-point gap means a 20-point stop. You never choose the number — the market hands it to you every morning.

5. Step 3 — The trade, with real numbers

Carrying on the example above, here is exactly what the bot works out:

ThingValueWhere it comes from
Yesterday's close25,628.49last finished daily candle
Today's open25,554.49today's first price
The gap−74.00 ptsopen minus yesterday's close
Today's RSIabove 90RSI(14) on the daily EMA21, including today's open

Stop-loss distance74 ptsthe gap
Stop-loss level25,628.49entry + 74 (a PE is stopped when price rises)
Option boughtslightly-ITM PEGAPS_ITM_STEPS=1
Why the stop is a distance, not a fixed price.

If the bot fills exactly at the open, "74 points above entry" and "yesterday's close" are the same price — 25,628.49. They only separate if the fill lands a little later in the entry window.

Say price slipped to 25,534 before the order went through. Measuring to yesterday's close would now risk 94 points instead of 74. Measuring the gap from the actual fill keeps it at exactly 74 — your risk stays what the market offered, and does not quietly grow because of a slow fill.

6. Step 4 — How we get out

Three exits, and nothing else. No profit target, no breakeven shuffle, no time-based exit.

ExitWhen it firesChecked
StopPrice moves the gap distance against you (74 pts here)every tick
TrailA 5-minute candle closes back through the 5-minute EMA21 — for a PE, a close above the line; for a CE, a close belowon candle close only, never a wick
Time15:15 — anything still open is squared off—

The trail is the interesting one. It is not a fixed price: the EMA21 is recalculated on every 5-minute candle, so as price falls the line follows it down and locks in more of the move. You stay in as long as the fall keeps going, and leave on the first candle that closes back the wrong side of it.

The same day, minute by minute. Bought the PE at the open at 25,554.49 with the stop (orange dashed) at 25,628.49. Price falls all morning and the green EMA21 trails down above it. The low of the day is 25,325.52. At 13:10 a candle closes at 25,357.45 — back above the trail at 25,354.56 — and the trade is closed. The stop was never touched.

Notice what the trail did. It gave back some of the very best price — the low was 25,325 and the exit was 25,357, about 32 points worse. That is the cost of never trying to guess the exact bottom. In exchange, it captured roughly 197 points of a 229-point move without you having to make a single decision.
A trailing stop can also cut a loss early. If price moves against you steadily rather than in one jump, the trail will often trigger before the 74-point stop is reached. That is deliberate — it is a stop that tightens as the picture changes, so many losing days cost less than the full gap.

7. Days it says no

Most days GAPS does nothing, and every refusal is written to the skip log with its reason. This is normal and expected — the setup needs two uncommon things to line up on the same morning.

Same overbought market, opposite morning. The RSI is just as stretched, but today opened above yesterday's close. The market is still charging in the same direction, so there is no change of mind to trade. Skipped, and logged as "needs a gap DOWN for the PE setup".

What you'll see in the skip logWhat it means
"is inside the band [10, 90] — not an extreme"Ordinary day. This is the most common message by far.
"needs a gap DOWN for the PE setup"Overbought, but it gapped up. Same-direction gaps are not traded.
"needs a gap UP for the CE setup"Oversold, but it gapped down.
"Warming up — n/35 closed daily candles needed"Not enough daily history yet to compute RSI-on-EMA21. It refuses rather than guessing.
"Daily trade budget spent"Already traded today. GAPS decides once.
"Candle series is NOT daily"A safety check — the engine was handed intraday candles where daily ones were expected, and refused to compute a fake daily RSI over them.
Expect a low trade count. A market has to be genuinely stretched and reverse overnight. Over a 30-day backtest you may see only a handful of trades, sometimes none. That is the strategy working as designed, not a bug — but it also means you need a long test range before any result means anything.

8. Safety limits

LimitDefaultWhat it does
GAPS_MAX_DAILY_TRADES1One trade per day. The strategy decides once at the open anyway.
GAPS_MAX_DAILY_LOSS5000Stop trading for the day once losses reach ₹5,000.
GAPS_MAX_WEEKLY_LOSS0 (off)When set, a rolling Monday-to-today cap read from the day-by-day logs.
GAPS_LOSS_STREAK_SKIP3After 3 losses in a row, pause.
Portfolio capsharedThe account-wide daily loss limit applies to GAPS like every other strategy.

There is deliberately no VIX filter and no open-interest gate. Other strategies in this bot have them; GAPS does not, because none was asked for and adding unrequested filters changes a strategy into something you did not design.

It will not open a trade it cannot protect. If the stop level cannot be worked out for any reason, the entry is abandoned and logged, rather than opening a position with no stop-loss.

9. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

All four run the same rules from one file, src/strategies/gaps.js. There is no second copy of the logic anywhere.

ModePageWhat it does
Paper/gaps-paper/statusLive prices, simulated money. This is the reference version — if anything ever disagrees, Paper is right.
Live/gaps-liveRuns Paper and places real Fyers orders off its decisions. Live therefore cannot drift from Paper — it is the same engine.
Backtest/gaps-backtestReplays historical days through the same signal. Option prices are simulated, so treat rupees as directional, not exact.
Replay/replayFeeds a recorded day's real ticks back through the Paper engine — the honest way to check a change before going live.
Real orders need three separate switches. GAPS_LIVE_ENABLED=true, and the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false, and GAPS_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. By default nothing places a real order — it only logs the order it would have sent. Leave it that way until Paper and Replay agree on a recorded session.

11. What's still missing

This strategy has never traded a real session. Every number in this guide comes from the engine running on constructed data, not from live results. There is no win rate, no profit factor and no edge estimate here, because none has been measured yet.

Before trusting it with money:

  1. Run it in Paper for a stretch of sessions. Because setups are rare, a handful of days tells you almost nothing — expect to wait.
  2. Use Replay on those recorded days and confirm the replayed decisions match what Paper actually did.
  3. Only then consider the live switches — and even then, leave LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=true for a few days first and read the "order I would have placed" log lines.
Known limitations, stated plainly.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated. There is no historical option-price data, so option premiums are modelled from the index move. Direction is meaningful; the exact ₹ is not.
  • Very few trades. Any statistic drawn from a short range will be noise.
  • No regime awareness. There is no VIX or open-interest filter, so a chaotic day is treated exactly like a calm one.
  • Gap size is not filtered. A 3-point gap qualifies just as much as a 74-point one — and a 3-point gap means a 3-point stop, which real spreads and slippage will take out almost immediately. If live results disappoint, a minimum-gap setting is the first thing worth discussing.
