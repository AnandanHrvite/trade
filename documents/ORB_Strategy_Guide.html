⚙1. ORB in one minute

The whole idea
  • From 9:15 to 9:30 NIFTY draws a small box: the highest and lowest price of the first 15 minutes.
  • At 9:30 the box is locked. It never changes again that day.
  • If a 5-minute candle closes above the box, that is a breakout up. If one closes below, that is a breakout down.
  • We never buy that first candle. We wait for the next candle to close even further. That is our proof.
  • Proof up → buy a CE (call option). Proof down → buy a PE (put option).
  • Two quick checks must agree: RSI and SuperTrend must point the same way as the trade.
  • The stop goes at the far end of the breakout candle.
  • One trade a day. No new trades after 11:30. Anything still open is sold at 15:15.
Read this before you trade real money. A long backtest (1 Jan 2024 → 11 Aug 2026) showed ORB losing money: 329 trades, 20.7% winners, −₹1,78,963, and every year negative on its own. The rules below are explained because the engine runs them, not because they are proven to make money. Section 12 has the details.

🕔3. Step 1 — Draw the opening box

  1. At 9:15 the market opens.
  2. The bot takes the first three 5-minute candles: 9:15, 9:20 and 9:25.
  3. Their highest high is the top of the box (ORH). Their lowest low is the bottom (ORL).
  4. At 9:30 the box is locked for the whole day.
  5. Nothing is traded while the box is being drawn.

What you see: the last 40 minutes of yesterday (grey), then today's open. Yesterday closed at 24,995.3 and today opened at 25,022. The blue box is the 9:15–9:30 range: top 25,063, bottom 25,008, so it is 55 points tall. At 09:45 a candle closes at 25,076, above the box (yellow dot). Why it matters: the box is the only reference ORB uses. Every later decision is "did a candle close outside these two lines?"

Two box checks exist but are switched off The code can skip a day when the box is too tall (ORB_OR_ATR_MAX, box height vs a 15-minute ATR, or ORB_OR_MAX_PTS, a cap in points) or when the morning gap is huge (ORB_GAP_OR_MULT, gap vs box height). All three ship at 0 = off, so every box is tradable. If you set them, a missing ATR or a missing yesterday's close lets the day through instead of blocking it.

🔄5. Step 3 — Break, then proof

  1. Wait for the first candle that closes outside the box. That is the breakout candle.
  2. Do not buy it.
  3. Look at the very next candle. For a CE it must close higher than the breakout candle's close. For a PE it must close lower.
  4. Check RSI and SuperTrend on that candle.
  5. All good → buy on that candle's close.
  6. Place the stop at the breakout candle's low (CE) or high (PE).

A CE example

What you see: box 25,008–25,063. At 09:45 the breakout candle closes at 25,076 (yellow dot). At 09:50 the next candle closes at 25,089 — higher than 25,076, so that is the proof. RSI is 70.26 (needs 51 or more) and the green dashed SuperTrend line is below price (bullish). BUY CE at 25,089. The red stop line is the breakout candle's low, 25,044. The trade ends at 10:15 at 25,113.53 when the global profit lock fires (Section 9 explains it). Why it matters: waiting one candle filters out pokes above the box that fall straight back.

The same rules, going down

What you see: box 24,704–24,748. At 09:35 a candle closes at 24,694, below the box bottom (yellow dot). The next candle closes lower still: BUY PE at 24,681. RSI is 31.56 (a PE needs 49 or less) and SuperTrend is red (bearish) above price. The stop is the breakout candle's high, 24,714. The trade ends at 10:10 at 24,654.55 through the profit lock. Why it matters: a PE is the exact mirror of a CE — same steps, opposite direction.

Stricter proof is available ORB_CONFIRM_MODE ships as close (just close beyond the breakout candle's close). Set it to extend and the next candle must also make a higher high (lower low for PE) and close back beyond the box edge.
The bot keeps looking after a weak poke With ORB_BREAKOUT_RESCAN on (default), a close outside the box that fails the body/colour/VWAP tests is skipped and the bot keeps watching for a better one. At the default settings those tests are off, so the first close outside the box is the breakout. The breakout chosen for the day does not change after that — the engine commits to it.
The "target" line on the ORB page does nothing The page draws a target 1.5 × the box height beyond the edge. It is only a picture. There is no target exit.

↻6. The backup path: retest or resume

Sometimes the next candle does not close further out. The bot does not give up at once. It stays ready for up to 6 more candles (ORB_RETEST_MAX_WAIT) and buys if one of these happens first:

  • Resume: a candle closes beyond the breakout candle's close after all.
  • Retest that holds: a candle dips back to the box edge — within 10% of the box height, at least 5 points — but still closes outside the box.

Two things end the wait without a trade:

  • The 6 candles run out.
  • A candle closes back through the far side of the box (below ORL for a CE, above ORH for a PE).

What you see: the breakout candle closes at 25,076 at 09:45. The next candle (grey dot) closes at 25,070 — not higher, so no buy yet. At 09:55 a candle dips to 25,061, within 5.5 points of the box top 25,063, and closes at 25,071, still above the box. That is a retest that held: BUY CE at 25,071, stop 25,044. Exit 25,094.66 at 10:15 by the profit lock. Why it matters: a pause after the break does not have to cost you the trade.

What you see: a breakout at 09:45 (close 25,076). The next candle closes at 25,061, so no proof. Price drifts back inside the box and no candle resumes or retests. After 6 waiting candles the engine writes "Retest window expired — no trade today". Later NIFTY climbs to 25,129.6, but ORB has already committed to this breakout and will not chase it. Why it matters: this rule costs some moves like this one. It is the price of trading only the first clean break.

Correction from the older guide The older guide said "a close back inside the box cancels the day". The code is different: closing inside the box only uses up waiting candles. The day is cancelled early only by a close through the opposite edge (below ORL for a CE). Otherwise the 6-candle wait decides.

🚫7. Filters that say "not today"

RSI blocks the trade

What you see: yesterday closed at 25,184.6; today opened at 25,010, a big gap down. Price then breaks above the box top 25,024 at 09:40 and the next candle confirms at 25,040. But RSI on that candle is only 34.66, below 51 — the gap down still dominates the last 14 closes. The engine says: "RSI gate blocked CE: RSI 34.66 vs min 51". Why it matters: once the proof candle is refused, the day's breakout is used up. ORB never buys late on a later candle.

SuperTrend blocks the trade

What you see: yesterday fell hard, so SuperTrend (red dashed line, above price) is bearish. Today price breaks above the box top 25,046 and confirms at 25,057. RSI is 52.86, which passes. But SuperTrend is still bearish at 25,076.13, above the price, so the CE is refused: "SuperTrend gate blocked CE: SuperTrend(10,3) is bearish, CE needs bullish". Why it matters: ORB will not buy a CE against a falling SuperTrend, or a PE against a rising one.

Too late, or a sideways morning

What you see: a 67-point box (25,004–25,071). All morning every candle closes inside it, so there is no breakout. At 11:35 a candle finally closes at 25,080, above the box — but the window ended at 11:30. The engine says: "Past 11:30 IST — no new ORB entries (breakout is stale)". Why it matters: a choppy morning is a no-trade day. ORB treats a breakout after 11:30 as stale.

VIX too high (only if you switch it on)

What you see: the same breakout as the CE example, with India VIX in the lower panel (illustrative values). The engine gives a CE signal at 09:50, but VIX is 23.4, above the 22 ceiling. With ORB_VIX_ENABLED on, the trade is skipped. Why it matters: high VIX means wild swings and costly options. The filter ships off, so by default VIX is ignored.

🎯8. Step 4 — Pick the option

  • Strike: one step (50 points) in-the-money. For a CE that is the strike just below price. For a PE it is the strike just above. Delta is about 0.6. Set by ORB_ITM_STEPS (0 = at-the-money).
  • Expiry: chosen automatically.
  • Price band: skip if the premium is below ₹120 or above ₹400. This check is off by default (ORB_PREMIUM_GATE_ENABLED).
  • Spread: skip if the bid-ask spread is wider than 2 points. If the broker sends no bid/ask, this check is skipped.
  • Size: one lot (global lot settings).
The backtest cannot run these checks Old market data has no option chain. So the backtest cannot check the price band, the spread or OI, and it guesses the option price (starting at ₹240, ORB_BT_SEED_PREMIUM) and charges 1.5 points of slippage each way (ORB_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS). Paper therefore takes the same number of trades or fewer than the backtest — never more.

🚪9. Step 5 — When we sell

There is no fixed profit target. Several rules watch the trade and the first one to fire closes it. They run in this order:

On every price tick:

  1. Rupee loss cap — off by default (ORB_MAX_TRADE_LOSS = 0).
  2. Option melt-down stop — the premium falls 35% below what we paid.
  3. Global profit lock — once the premium is up 8%, a floor follows the best price. It keeps 85% of the gain while the gain is under 20%, 80% under 40%, then 70%. If the premium falls to the floor, sell.
  4. Global breakeven — once the premium has been up 5%, sell if it falls back to the entry price. (Used only when the profit lock has not armed.)
  5. Hard stop — NIFTY touches the stop level (the breakout candle's far end).

When a 5-minute candle closes:

  1. Strong candle against us — off by default.
  2. Move the stop to entry (ORB's own breakeven) — off by default.
  3. Candle trail / SuperTrend trail — both off by default.
  4. EMA 20 trail — a candle closes back across EMA 20 (below it for a CE, above for a PE). It only works after price has first closed on the right side of the line once.

At 15:15: anything still open is sold.

Which exit usually wins? The global profit lock arms at +8% of premium — about 26.7 NIFTY points on a ₹200 option with 0.6 delta — and then only allows a small give-back. So on winning trades it usually fires long before a candle closes below EMA 20. The EMA 20 trail mostly ends trades that never got going. The profit lock and breakeven are shared by all strategies (PROFIT_LOCK_*, BREAKEVEN_*); the older ORB guide did not mention them.

Exit 1 — the hard stop (a losing trade)

What you see: BUY CE at 25,089 at 09:50. The next candle stalls. Then two red candles fall through the box top. At 10:05 NIFTY touches 25,044, the breakout candle's low: sold at the stop. That is 45 points, roughly ₹1,755 on one 65-unit lot at 0.6 delta. The premium never got 5% up (best ₹204.8 on a ₹200 entry), so the breakeven never armed. Why it matters: this is what a normal ORB loser looks like.

Exit 2 — the global profit lock

What you see: the CE trade from Section 5, with the option premium in the lower panel (₹200 entry, 0.6 delta assumed). The premium peaks at ₹217.43, above the ₹216 arm line (+8%). The floor is then 85% of the gain: ₹214.82. A small dip takes the premium to ₹214.72 and the trade is sold at 25,113.53. Why it matters: the lock banks a small win quickly. It also means ORB rarely rides a whole trend day at the default settings.

Exit 3 — the EMA 20 trail

What you see: BUY CE at 25,084. Price creeps up a few points (premium best ₹205.4, never +5%), then sags. The stop is far away at 25,026 because the breakout candle was long. At 10:25 a candle closes at 25,049, below the orange EMA 20 line (25,050.8): sold, 35 points down. Why it matters: the EMA trail cuts a trade that is going nowhere before it reaches the full stop.

Exit 4 — the 15:15 square-off

What you see: a late-morning breakout. BUY CE at 25,088 at 11:05. NIFTY then trends up all afternoon and every candle closes above EMA 20, so nothing sells. At 15:15 the trade is closed at 25,289.75, 201.75 points up. Important: this picture was made with the global profit lock and breakeven switched off. With the defaults, the profit lock would have sold this trade much earlier on one of the small dips. Why it matters: no ORB trade is ever held overnight.

Why the EMA trail must "arm" first The EMA trail starts working only after a candle has closed on the right side of EMA 20 (above it for a CE). Without that, a CE bought below a still-high EMA (for example after a gap down) would be sold on its very first candle.
Rupee cap arithmetic, for when you switch it on One lot is 65 units. At 0.6 delta, 1 NIFTY point ≈ 65 × ₹0.60 = ₹39. So a ₹1,500 cap allows only about 38 points against you. A stop wider than that would never be reached — the cap closes the trade first.

↺10. A second try after a stop (off by default)

Normally a stopped-out day is finished. ORB_REENTRY_AFTER_SL (default 0) allows extra tries, but only after a stop-out:

  • A hard stop, the rupee cap or the melt-down stop counts as a stop-out.
  • An EMA-trail exit, a profit-lock exit or the 15:15 square-off does not — those mean the move ended, not that the breakout was wrong.
  • After the stop, every candle up to the stop candle is ignored. A fresh close outside the same box starts the whole process again.

What you see: with re-entry set to 1. First trade: BUY CE at 25,089, stopped at 25,044 at 10:00. Price recovers, and at 10:15 a new candle closes above the box top again (yellow dot). The next candle confirms: BUY CE at 25,093, stop 25,058 (the new breakout candle's low). Exit 25,120.7 by the profit lock. Why it matters: raising ORB_MAX_DAILY_TRADES alone never gives a second trade; this setting does. It is a hypothesis — measure it before leaving it on.

Known quirk A stop that was moved first (for example by a trail) still counts as a stop-out, so it can earn a re-entry even though the move simply ended.

The brakes remember Weekly and streak results are saved in ~/trading-data/orb_risk_state.json, so a restart does not reset them. Paper and Live keep separate records.

📦12. How much to trust the numbers

Long backtest: ORB loses money 1 Jan 2024 → 11 Aug 2026: 329 trades, 20.7% winners, −₹1,78,963, profit factor 0.39, worst drawdown −₹1,79,110. By year: 2024 −₹95,275 · 2025 −₹51,420 · Jan–Jul 2026 −₹32,268. Four out of five trades lost about ₹1,130 each. Even a flat trade costs about ₹420 in spread and time decay.

These figures come from earlier backtest runs (scripts/orbValidate.js, scripts/orbSweep.js). They were measured before the global profit lock and breakeven were added in September 2026, so today's exits behave differently. Re-run the scripts before relying on any number.

Why the early numbers looked good

  • The first sample was only 39 sessions (Mar–Apr 2026): 9 trades, 33% wins, about +₹3,415.
  • One trade was 211% of the profit. Without it, the result was −₹3,786.
  • The chance that there was no real edge was about 37%.
  • Older claims ("+346 points, no losers", "≈ ₹6,737", "≈ ₹3,112") ignored costs or modelled the exits wrongly.

The "narrow box" idea did not survive

In 2025 and 2026 a box of 70 points or less looked much better. In 2024 the opposite was true. Two years agreeing and one disagreeing is what an over-fitted rule looks like. That is why ORB_OR_MAX_PTS ships off.

Do not tune on a small sample Tightening a filter on 39 sessions makes every number look better, simply because it drops small losers while the 2–3 big winners survive. Use node scripts/orbSweep.js --from … --to …, which tests on data it was not tuned on.

⚙13. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

Paper — /orb-paper

Real prices, pretend money. Paper is the official version of the rules. If Live or Backtest acts differently, they are fixed to match Paper.

Live — /orb-live

Real Fyers orders, behind two switches: ORB_LIVE_ENABLED must be on and the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN must be off. ORB_LIVE_DRY_RUN keeps ORB alone in pretend mode. While dry-run is on, the bot logs the order it would send. An optional exchange-side backup stop (HARNESS_EXCHANGE_SL_ENABLED, off) can rest at the exchange in case the bot crashes.

Backtest — /orb-backtest

Runs the same entry and exit code over past candles. It guesses option prices, charges slippage, fills a stop that was jumped over at the jump price, and cannot run the option-chain checks.

Replay — /replay

Re-runs a recorded paper day tick by tick, with the settings of that day or with your current settings. The safe way to test a change.

⚠15. What's still missing

  • No proven edge. The long backtest lost money in every year.
  • The exit stack changed after that backtest. The global profit lock and breakeven (September 2026) now end most winners early. Their effect on ORB has not been measured on a long sample.
  • Backtest option prices are guesses, and the backtest cannot run the spread, price band or OI checks.
  • The EMA 20 length is not validated. Anything from 13 to 55 was within noise on the first sample.
  • The stop you set is not always the stop you get. Turn on ORB_MAX_TRADE_LOSS and it usually binds before the chart stop.
