⚙ 1. The idea in one minute

Every morning, in its first 15 minutes (9:15 to 9:30), NIFTY draws a small high-and-low box. Think of it as the market "making up its mind." This strategy watches that box and asks one question: does the price break out of the box and keep running?

  • If price breaks above the box and keeps going up → we buy a CE (call option) to profit from the rise.
  • If price breaks below the box and keeps falling → we buy a PE (put option) to profit from the drop.

We buy only one option at a time (no pairs, no hedges), and normally only one trade for the whole day. The whole point is to catch a trending day — a day that picks a direction in the morning and runs with it — and to skip the days that just wobble sideways and trap you.

A whole ORB trend day — one trade, held for four hours. The first three candles (9:15–9:30) draw the blue dashed opening range box; it is narrow, which is exactly what ORB likes. Price coils just under the top, then at 09:55 a decisive green candle closes clearly above the box and above the purple fair-value (VWAP) line (yellow dot = the breakout candle). We still don't buy. Only when the next candle pushes higher do we get our proof → BUY CE at 25,170 (10:00). NIFTY then trends up all day; we ride the orange EMA20 line and sell only when a candle finally closes back below EMA20 → EXIT 25,347 (14:25), about +177 spot points. On roughly 3 days out of 4 the box never breaks convincingly and we don't trade at all.

One engine, rebuilt on 26 July 2026. There used to be three versions (V1, V2, V3) sitting behind switches. They are all gone. Today there is exactly one ORB engine and one set of rules — the ones in this guide. Anything that was tested and did not help (the extra indicators, the "must clear yesterday's high" rule, the "must close near the candle's tip" rule, the minimum box size) was deleted from the code rather than left behind a toggle, so there is nothing hidden to switch back to.

Its goal is simple: catch the strong trend days and throw away the fake breakouts — not to trade more often. It buys options that are slightly in-the-money (explained below), and it measures everything against how much NIFTY is normally moving that day, so the same settings keep working whether the market is calm or wild.

Why only one trade a day?

The morning box only forms once. A "breakout" that shows up at 2 PM is usually a completely different situation (news, a late surge) and tends to be a coin-flip. So ORB commits to a single breakout: the first clean one it sees. If that one doesn't work out, it does not go looking for a second try. Trying to squeeze in more trades just adds afternoon chop to the results.

Read this before you trust any profit number here. ORB has only been measured on 39 real sessions, which produced 9 trades. That is far too few to prove anything. On that sample it made about ₹3,415 — but one single trade was 211% of that, so if you delete just that one trade the strategy loses ₹3,786. Treat every setting below as a sensible starting guess, not a tuned optimum. Section 10 explains this honestly and in full.

📚 2. Words you'll see (plain meanings)

A few terms come up again and again. Here's what each one means, in everyday language — keep this handy and the rest of the guide reads easily.

TermWhat it really means
Opening range (OR)The high and low of NIFTY's first 15 minutes. The "box" the market draws while it makes up its mind. "OR width" = how tall the box is, in points.
CE / PECE (call) makes money when NIFTY goes up. PE (put) makes money when NIFTY goes down. We only ever buy one of them at a time.
CandleA 5-minute price bar showing where NIFTY opened, closed, and its high/low in that 5 minutes. A "green" candle closed higher than it opened; "red" closed lower.
ATR"Average True Range" — simply how much NIFTY normally moves in a bar. If ATR is 30, a typical 5-minute candle spans about 30 points. We use it as a ruler: a "big" move on a calm day is small on a wild day, and ATR tells us which is which.
VWAPThe day's fair-value line — the average price weighted by how much traded there. If price is above VWAP, buyers are in control; below it, sellers are. We only buy CE above the line, PE below it.
In-the-money (ITM) optionAn option that already has real value baked in, so it moves almost point-for-point with NIFTY and loses value slowly. We buy slightly ITM because it tracks a trend better than a cheap far-away option.
PremiumThe price you pay for the option (in ₹ per unit). "Premium collapsed 35%" means the option lost about a third of its value.
Bid-ask spreadThe gap between the buy price and the sell price of an option. A wide gap means you lose money just entering and exiting — so we skip options with a wide spread.
VIXThe market's "fear gauge." High VIX = jumpy, expensive options and bigger swings. We can tell the strategy to sit out when VIX is too high.
EMA20A smoothed average of the last several closes (the "20" is how many). We use it as a moving trend line: as long as price stays on the right side of it, the trend is still alive.

🕔 3. Step 1 — Draw the opening box

From 9:15 to 9:30, the strategy watches every price tick and records the highest high and lowest low. At 9:30 sharp, the box is locked for the rest of the day — the top of the box (ORH) and bottom (ORL) never change again, no matter what happens later.

SettingDefaultWhat it does
ORB_RANGE_START09:15When the box starts (market open).
ORB_RANGE_END09:30When the box locks. After this, the top and bottom are fixed.
ORB_ENTRY_END11:30Latest we'll take a new trade. A breakout after this is "too late" — the day is half over.
ORB_FORCED_EXIT15:15If we're still holding anything at this time, sell it — win or lose. We never carry overnight.
ORB_EXPIRY_DAY_ONLYfalseIf turned on, only trade on weekly-expiry day (Tuesday).
The "normal move" ruler is fixed at 14 bars and is no longer a setting. ATR always uses 14 bars, the breakout cushion is always 0.15× the box (or 0.30× ATR, whichever is bigger), and the retest tolerance is always 10% of the box with a 5-point floor. These used to be env keys; they were removed because they are structural choices, not dials, and having them as keys only let the three modes drift apart. If you see ORB_ATR_PERIOD, ORB_BUFFER_*, ORB_RETEST_TOL_* or ORB_TARGET_RANGE_MULT still sitting in your .env, they are dead text — the code ignores them.

First question: is the box a good one?

Not every morning box is worth trading. Before looking for a breakout, the strategy checks the box itself and can decide to skip the whole day:

  • Is the box too wide? If the box is more than 2.5× the normal 15-minute move, the big move already happened before 9:30 and we would just be chasing it — skip the day.
  • There is deliberately NO minimum box size. This changed: the old engine also skipped days where the box was smaller than 0.7× the normal move. Testing showed the two best trades in the whole sample came from the two narrowest boxes, so a minimum was pointing exactly the wrong way. It was deleted.
  • Did the market gap open crazily? If NIFTY opened miles away from yesterday's close (more than 3× the box height), that's usually news or panic that has already spent its energy — skip the day.
Real numbers. Say the normal 15-minute move (ATR15) today is 40 points.
• Box is 60 pts tall → 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5× → under 2.5 → day is tradable.
• Box is 110 pts tall → 110 ÷ 40 = 2.75× → over 2.5 → skip the whole day.
• Box is 60 pts and NIFTY opened 200 pts above yesterday's close → 200 > 3 × 60 = 180 → skip the whole day (news gap).
Why measure against "the normal move" instead of fixed points? A 40-point box means something very different on a sleepy day than on a stormy one. By comparing the box to how much NIFTY is actually moving today, the same settings keep making sense in any market — you never have to re-tune numbers when volatility changes.
Both day-checks "fail open." If the ruler isn't ready yet (not enough past bars for ATR) or yesterday's close isn't in the loaded history, the check is skipped rather than treated as a fail. Not knowing the gap is not proof of a bad gap.

A day ORB refuses to trade. The opening 15 minutes swung 112 points — nearly 2.8× the normal 15-min move. All the energy was already spent before 9:30, so no breakout from this box is worth buying. ORB draws the box, marks the day SKIPPED, and does nothing for the rest of the session. Roughly speaking, ORB stands aside on most days: on the 39-session sample it traded only 9 times.

▶ 4. Step 2 — The checklist before a trade

For a trade to happen, every box below has to tick "yes," in order. If any one fails, we skip — and the reason is written into the log so you can see exactly why nothing traded today.

1
Are we inside the trading window?
Nothing before the box locks, nothing after the morning is over.
time is 09:30–11:30 IST
2
Haven't we already traded today?
One trade per day. A loss is not a reason to try again.
trades today = 0
3
Is the box too wide?
A huge opening range means the move already happened. There is no minimum — narrow boxes are welcome (they were the best trades).
box height ≤ 2.5× the normal 15-min move
4
Did the market open calmly?
No giant news-gap that already burned off the day's move.
gap from yesterday's close ≤ 3× the box height
5
A real break, not a toe over the line
The first 5-min candle to close clearly past the box edge (past a small cushion) is the day's one and only breakout. Nothing later can replace it.
close beyond the edge by max(0.15×box, 0.30×ATR5, 1pt)
6
Was that breakout candle decisive?
Three checks on the breakout candle itself: right colour (green for CE, red for PE), a solid body (not a tiny doji), and closing on the correct side of the fair-value line. If any fails, the whole day is over — we don't look for another breakout.
right colour · body ≥ 0.6×ATR5 · CE above VWAP, PE below VWAP
7
Wait for the next candle to agree
We never buy the breakout candle itself. The next candle must push further: a higher high AND a higher close, still beyond the box edge and still on the right side of VWAP (mirrored for PE). This one extra step is what keeps us on real trend days and off fakeouts.
next candle extends the move → BUY on its close
8
If it hesitates — stay armed for a few candles
If that next candle stalls, we don't give up. For up to 6 more candles we can still enter on a fresh push or a pullback-and-hold of the edge. A close back inside the box cancels the day. (Section 5.)
resume or retest within 6 candles · close back inside the box = day cancelled
9
Is the option worth buying?
Pick a slightly in-the-money strike, check its price is in a sensible range, and that the buy/sell gap (spread) is tight. Live and practice only — history has no option prices, so the backtest can't run these.
premium inside the band · spread ≤ 2 pts · optional crowd-positioning (OI) check
What was deleted, and why. The older engine also demanded EMA20/50 + ADX + RSI agreement, a break past yesterday's high/low ("fresh ground"), a close in the top/bottom fifth of the breakout candle, a minimum box size, and wick/volume checks. Every one of them was measured and removed:
  • EMA / ADX / RSI — the engine carrying them took zero trades in 39 sessions. A strategy that never trades cannot be validated.
  • "Fresh ground" (yesterday's high/low) — it kept 7 trades worth 0% wins and cut 6 trades worth +290 points, including both winners. A filter that removes the entire right tail of a right-tail strategy is structurally wrong.
  • "Close near the tip" — removing it took the sample from +133 to +346 spot points.
  • Minimum box size — it never fired, and the two winners came from the two narrowest boxes.
Want to see exactly why nothing traded? Set ORB_DEBUG_TRACE=true and every 5-minute candle prints the full checklist to /logs — each gate with PASS / FAIL / SKIP, its actual numbers, and the final decision. The same funnel is always saved into the skip log as one short line, e.g. time window:P,trade budget:P,OR ready:P,OR vs ATR15:P,gap sanity:P,breakout:F — so a quiet day can be diagnosed from the log file alone. The trace is noisy; turn it off again afterwards.

🔄 5. Step 3 — Wait for proof, then buy

The strategy never jumps on the breakout candle itself. It waits for proof. There are two ways it can decide to actually buy:

The main way — confirmation

After a decisive breakout candle, we look at the very next 5-minute candle. For a CE it must make a higher high AND a higher close, still above the box top and still above the fair-value line. (For a PE: lower low, lower close, below the box bottom, below VWAP.) That's our proof — we buy when that candle closes. This catches the days that break out and just keep going.

Real numbers. Box top = 25,275. Cushion = 9 pts, so a real break needs a close above 25,284.
9:40 candle closes at 25,302, green, big body → that's the breakout. We do not buy.
9:45 candle: high 25,328 (higher than 25,306) and close 25,322 (higher than 25,302) → proof — BUY CE at 25,322.
If instead 9:45 had closed at 25,294 — still above the box, but not higher than the breakout close — no buy yet; we move to the backup window below.

The backup way — resume or retest

Sometimes the next candle stalls instead of pushing. Rather than give up, the strategy stays armed for up to 6 more candles (ORB_RETEST_MAX_WAIT) and can still get in if price:

  • Pushes on again in the breakout direction — a fresh higher-high-and-higher-close beyond the edge (a "resume"), or
  • Dips back to the box edge, touches it, and holds — the candle wicks down to within 10% of the box height (minimum 5 points) of the edge but still closes beyond it (a "retest that holds").

Either way the candle must still be on the right side of VWAP. If any candle closes back inside the box, the breakout is dead and the day is cancelled — no more attempts. If the 6 candles run out with neither happening, the day is also over.

Important: the backup never blocks a trade. If price simply rockets away and never comes back to retest, we still buy on the confirmation candle — we don't wait for a pullback that may never come. We tested forcing a retest before every trade and it made results clearly worse: demanding a pullback selected the weak, stall-and-fade breakouts and threw away the strong ones that never look back — exactly the trades that pay for the whole strategy. Setting ORB_RETEST_MAX_WAIT=0 turns the backup off entirely and leaves only confirmed entries.

Why we wait for proof. Price poked above the box top on one green candle — but it never got clearly above the fair-value (VWAP) line, and the very next candle slammed straight back down inside the box. The confirmation never came, so we never bought. The orange dot marks the failed poke: a classic fakeout that the confirmation-plus-VWAP rule quietly filters out.

The backup path in action. The breakout candle (orange dot, 9:40) was decisive, but the very next candle stalled — no higher high, so no buy. We stayed armed. Two candles later price dipped back and kissed the box top from above, then closed back above it — a retest that held → BUY CE at 25,291. Had that dip closed below 25,275 (inside the box) instead, the day would simply have been cancelled.

🎯 6. Step 4 — Pick the option

Once we've decided to trade, we still have to choose which option to buy and check it's worth trading.

CheckDefaultIn plain words
Which strike1 step in-the-moneyWe pick a strike one step inside the money (for CE that's a lower strike, for PE a higher one). This option already has real value, so it follows NIFTY almost point-for-point and loses value slowly — much better for riding a trend than a cheap far-out option.
Price range₹120 – ₹400Skip if the option is too cheap (a near-worthless lottery ticket) or too pricey. Keeps us in options that actually move well. Switch the whole check off with ORB_PREMIUM_GATE_ENABLED.
Buy/sell gap≤ 2 pointsSkip if the gap between the buy and sell price is wide — a wide gap quietly eats your profit on entry and exit. If the broker sends no bid/ask at all, this check is skipped rather than failed.

These three checks do NOT exist in the backtest — so the backtest always takes MORE trades than reality. History has no option chain, so the premium band, the spread check and the OI filter simply cannot run there. Whatever they block in practice, paper takes fewer trades than the backtest says — never more. The /orb-backtest page now prints which of these gates are switched on, so the gap is visible on screen instead of implied.
In the backtest, there are no real option prices either. History doesn't record option quotes, so the backtest estimates the option: it starts from a typical slightly-ITM price (₹240) and models how it would move with NIFTY. To stay honest it also charges a spread/slippage haircut on both entry and exit (ORB_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS, default 1.5 pts each way) and fills a blown stop at the gap-open when a candle jumps clean through it, not at the stop price — so a violent bar is costed realistically. Backtest profit is a rough approximation, not the fills you'd get live — always confirm against paper trading.

🚫 7. Step 5 — When we sell

The exits are built to let a winner run while cutting the losers quickly. There is no fixed profit target — we don't cap the trade at "+40%" and walk away. Instead, a handful of rules watch the trade, and whichever one triggers first closes it:

What sells us outDefaultIn plain words
Starting stop-losssee belowThe wider of two things: our entry candle's own low (for CE; its high for PE), and 1.5× the normal 5-min move away from entry. Then it is tightened to whatever the ₹1,500 per-trade budget actually allows — see the box under this table.
Move to break-evenmax(20 pts, 0.5×box)Once the trade is that far in profit, we slide the stop to our entry price. From here the trade can't turn into a loss. On a 60-point box that's max(20, 30) = 30 points.
Trend trail (EMA20)EMA 20The main exit. We ride the trend and only sell when a candle closes back across the moving trend line. One quick dip that closes back on-side does not shake us out. It also has to "arm" first — see the note below.
Strong candle against usbody ≥ 0.3×boxIf a big candle slams back the other way and closes back inside the box, that's a clear rejection — we exit. On a 60-point box, any opposite candle with an 18-point body qualifies.
Rupee loss cap₹1,500A hard floor checked on every tick: if the trade is down ₹1,500, we're out, no matter what the candles say. In practice this is the stop that ends almost every losing ORB trade.
Option melted down−35%If the option price collapses 35% from what we paid (even if NIFTY hasn't hit our spot stop), we bail. This catches the "the option bled out on time-decay" losses.
End of day15:15Anything still open at 3:15 PM is sold. We never hold overnight.
The most important thing to understand about ORB's stop. The strategy asks for a fairly wide stop (typically 50–83 NIFTY points). But ₹1,500 on a 65-lot, slightly-ITM option only survives about 38 points of adverse move. The rupee budget is therefore tighter, and it is what really ends the trade.

Previously the dashboard showed you the wide stop while the rupee cap quietly closed the trade much earlier. Now the two are reconciled before the trade even opens: the shown stop is clamped to whatever ₹1,500 allows, and the log says so whenever it clamps. What you see is what executes.

Practical consequence: ORB_SL_ATR_MULT is currently inert — every value from 1.0 to 2.5 gives an identical result, because the rupee cap binds first. To actually change your risk, change ORB_MAX_TRADE_LOSS, not the ATR multiplier. On the 39-session sample the budget clamped the stop on 9 of 9 trades.
Real numbers — how ₹1,500 becomes 38 points. Lot size 65. A slightly-ITM option moves about ₹0.60 for every 1 point NIFTY moves (that's its "delta"). So 1 point of NIFTY = 65 × 0.60 = ₹39 of P&L. ₹1,500 ÷ ₹39 ≈ 38 points. If you want the full 50–83 point stop, you need roughly ₹2,300 in ORB_MAX_TRADE_LOSS — that is a capital decision, so the code deliberately keeps the safer default.

What a losing ORB day actually looks like. Everything went right at the entry — decisive breakout, next candle confirmed, BUY CE at 25,322. Then it simply turned. The strategy's own stop sat at 25,277 (45 pts away), but the ₹1,500 budget only allows about 38 points, so the stop that was actually placed was 25,284 — and that is where the trade ended, for a loss of roughly ₹1,500. This is the normal shape of an ORB loser: the rupee cap, not the chart, ends the trade.

Why the EMA trail has to "arm" first. The trail only starts working once price has closed on the correct side of EMA20 at least once. Without that, a CE bought on a gap-down morning — with EMA20 still high from previous days — would be stopped out on its very first candle. Until the trail arms, the trade is protected by the hard stop, break-even, the opposite-candle rule and the rupee cap instead.
There's a "target" line on the chart, but it's just for looking. You'll see a target level drawn on the ORB page (1.5× the box height beyond the edge). Nothing sells when price reaches it — there is no target exit at all. The trend trail decides how far a winner runs.

A worked example — a CE trend day

The iconic ORB picture. The blue dashed box is the 9:15–9:30 opening range. Price coiled inside it, then a big green candle closed clearly above the box top and above the fair-value (VWAP) line (orange dot = the breakout candle). We still didn't buy — only when the next candle pushed higher did we get our proof → BUY CE at 25,322. We then rode the orange EMA20 trend line up and sold the moment a candle closed back below EMA20 → EXIT 25,328. No fixed target — the trend trail decided how far the winner ran.

Why no fixed target? If we always sold at "+40%," we'd throw away the big trend days — and a few of those big days are what make the whole strategy profitable over a month. The trend trail keeps us in the move as long as it lasts, while the break-even, rupee-cap and meltdown stops make sure a bad trade dies small. The real skill is in the entry checklist — the exit just decides how much of a good entry we get to keep.

🔅 8. The "market too jumpy" filter (VIX)

VIX is the market's fear gauge. When it's high, options get expensive and swings get violent — a riskier time to trade. ORB has its own optional VIX filter to sit out those days.

SettingDefaultIn plain words
ORB_VIX_ENABLEDfalseThe on/off switch. Off by default, so VIX is ignored unless you turn it on.
ORB_VIX_MAX_ENTRY22If VIX is above 22, don't trade at all today — too jumpy.
ORB_VIX_STRONG_ONLY18Between 18 and 22, only take the very best setups.
Why ORB has its own VIX limits: because we hold the trade for hours with no fixed target, our option's value is especially sensitive to how jumpy the market is. High-VIX days can swing hard against us, so ORB uses its own ceiling rather than the shared one.

🛡 9. Safety limits & the losing-streak brake

Several limits protect the account. Think of them as circuit-breakers of increasing size — per trade, per day, and per week.

LimitDefaultIn plain words
One trade a day1After today's trade, we're done — a failed trade is not a reason to try again.
Most we'll lose on one trade₹1,500Hard stop on a single trade's loss.
Option-meltdown stop−35%Exit if the option loses 35% of its value (time-decay / volatility crush).
Daily loss brake₹3,000Stop taking new trades once the day's loss hits ₹3,000. (With one trade a day, rarely matters.)
Weekly loss brake₹9,000If the week's losses reach ₹9,000, sit out the rest of the week (Monday–Friday).
Losing-streak brake4 daysAfter 4 losing days in a row, take a one-day break to reset.
Latest new trade11:30No fresh entries after 11:30 — the day's too far gone.
Sell everything by15:15Close any open trade at 3:15 PM.
Skip wide spreads2 ptsDon't trade an option whose buy/sell gap is too wide.
The weekly & streak brakes remember across days. The strategy saves its recent losses to disk (~/trading-data/orb_risk_state.json), so they survive a restart, and it will stop itself from trading during a bad run — even across days. Practice and live mode keep separate memories, so a rough patch in practice never freezes your live trading, and vice-versa.
Careful with the daily-loss brake. ORB_MAX_DAILY_LOSS is only checked when you are flat — it stops the next trade, it never closes an open one. The thing that caps a single open trade is ORB_MAX_TRADE_LOSS (₹1,500). Since ORB takes one trade a day, the daily brake almost never fires.

Crash-recovery (survives a restart)

ORB now writes its open trade to disk the moment it's opened (.active_orb_position.json) and clears it on close. If the server crashes or is redeployed with a trade still open, on the next boot the system re-reads that snapshot, checks it against your Fyers positions, and Telegrams you if it finds an orphan — and only clears the snapshot once the broker book is proven readable and flat. ORB previously had no such memory (a crash left an untracked position); it now matches the other five engines.

Portfolio-wide daily loss cap default OFF Separate from ORB's own ORB_MAX_DAILY_LOSS, there's now a single cap across all six strategies (PORTFOLIO_MAX_DAILY_LOSS, default 0 = off). It adds up today's realised P&L from every practice mode; once the combined loss hits the cap, it blocks new entries in every strategy (ORB included) for the rest of the day. It only ever blocks — it never places or changes an order — and if it can't read the totals it fails open (lets trading continue).

This section exists because it is the most useful thing in the whole guide. ORB looks profitable. The honest answer is: we don't know yet, and here is exactly why.

Confidence range−₹1,288 to +₹2,511 per tradeThe true average could easily be negative.
Chance there is no edge≈ 37%Better than a coin flip, nowhere near proof.
Biggest single trade211% of the total profitRemove that one trade and the strategy loses ₹3,786.
Read that last row again. Strip one trade out of nine and ORB loses money. This is not a validated edge — it is a right-tail lottery ticket measured over two months. To be 95% confident you would need roughly 147 trades, which at this rate is about 637 sessions — 2.5 years. Until then, every number on this page is a hint, not a fact.

Numbers you may have seen before — all of them were too optimistic

Old claimWhy it was wrong
"+346 spot points, no losers"Ignored trading costs completely.
"≈ ₹6,737"Used a flat 20-point break-even instead of the real max(20, 0.5×box) rule.

If a number here disagrees with something you remember, trust scripts/orbValidate.js.

The most promising untested idea

A narrow opening box may be the entire edge. On this sample, days where the box was under 1.5× the normal move gave 4 trades, 75% wins, +₹10,700. Days where the box was 1.5× or wider gave 5 trades, 0% wins, −₹7,285. That is mechanically sensible — a quiet open stores energy that the breakout releases. But it is only 9 trades, so ORB_OR_ATR_MAX deliberately stays at 2.5 rather than being tuned down to fit. Test this first on a long sample; it is the most likely real improvement in the whole design.

Why we don't just tune the settings until the numbers look good

Because on this sample they always do — and it means nothing. Tightening ORB_BODY_ATR_MULT or ORB_OR_ATR_MAX improves every metric, all the way from profit factor 1.4 up to 14.6. That is not the settings getting better; it is the filter dropping small scratch trades while the 2–3 known winners survive at every threshold. That is the sample choosing itself. Do not tune these on 39 sessions.
Which parts are safe-ish, which aren't. The break-even rule is the single most valuable exit component (removing it cost 106 points and created a −77-point worst case) and it works equally well anywhere from 10 to 25 points — so it is not a fitted number. The EMA trail is not that stable: EMA9 is clearly too tight, and EMA34 actually beat the shipped EMA20 on this sample. 20 was kept because it was already there, not because it won — anything from 13 to 55 is inside the noise. The 11:30 cut-off is a structural choice (an "opening range" breakout goes stale as the day wears on), not a measured one; extending it to 14:30 looked better in rupees but also produced the single worst trade.

⚙ 11. Paper, Live & Backtest

Paper — /orb-paper (practice)

Real live prices, pretend money. No real orders are ever placed. This is the "official" version of the strategy — whenever live or backtest behaves differently, paper is treated as correct. Every trade is saved to your trading-data folder, and every price tick can be recorded so you can replay the exact day later.

Live — /orb-live (real money)

Real orders through Fyers — but locked behind two safety switches so it can never fire by accident: ORB_LIVE_ENABLED must be on and the global dry-run switch must be off. While dry-run is on (the default), the system shows the order it would place but doesn't actually send it. Only turn dry-run off after you've checked that live and paper make the same decisions on a replayed day.

Optional exchange-side disaster stop default OFF For live (harness) trades you can also rest a backstop stop-loss at the exchange (HARNESS_EXCHANGE_SL_ENABLED, with HARNESS_SL_PCT the trigger as a % of your entry premium). It's a safety net in case the bot crashes mid-trade — the normal in-app stops stay in charge. The resting order is always cancelled before any normal sell, so it can't fire on a position you're already exiting. It places real orders, so validate it on a dry-run session first.

Backtest — /orb-backtest (test on the past)

Runs the strategy over past dates to see how it would have done. Since history has no option prices, it estimates the option's price and movement (see Section 6). You get a downloadable summary at the end.

Replay — /replay (re-run a recorded day)

Takes a day you recorded in paper mode and runs it again, tick by tick. You can replay it with the original settings (to reproduce exactly what happened) or with your current settings (to test a tweak against the same real prices, after market hours). Great for safely trying out changes.

⚠ 13. What's still missing

  • The sample is far too small. 9 trades. One of them is 211% of all the profit. This is the single biggest limitation and no amount of tuning fixes it — only more sessions do. (Section 10.)
  • Backtest option prices are estimated, and the backtest cannot run three of the entry checks (premium band, spread, OI) because history has no option chain. So it always shows more trades than practice takes.
  • Results are lumpy by design. ORB's profit comes from a handful of big trend days. Expect quiet, flat stretches between them — judge it over months, not days.
  • The stop you configure isn't the stop you get. ORB_SL_ATR_MULT is inert while ORB_MAX_TRADE_LOSS is tighter. That is now visible and logged, but it still surprises people. (Section 7.)
  • The trail period is not validated. EMA34 beat the shipped EMA20 in-sample. 20 was kept as the incumbent, not because it won.
Fixed since the last edition of this guide: the two conflicting stops are now reconciled before the trade opens (what the dashboard shows is what executes); the whole entry checklist is traceable per candle and saved into the skip log; the backtest now runs its exits in the same order as practice, charges slippage on both sides and fills a gapped-through stop at the gap-open; ORB survives a server restart with an open trade; and the paper vs live routes were audited and 8 behaviour differences closed — with practice treated as the correct one.
