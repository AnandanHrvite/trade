Trend Pullback (TREND_PB)

Wait for a clear trend. Let the price dip back to a fair spot. Buy an option only when a strong candle shows the trend has started again. Then let a trailing stop decide when to sell.

Updated 2026-10-09 · every chart is an example built from made-up candles, and the real engine code was run on those candles to place each entry

In one minute
  • Step 1 — trend (15-minute chart). Peaks and dips must both be rising (or both falling). EMA20 must be above EMA50 and rising (mirror for down). Price must be on the right side of VWAP. If not, no trade.
  • Step 2 — pullback (5-minute chart). Price must dip back to the EMA20 line for at least 2 candles, but not fall more than 1.5 × ATR below it.
  • Step 3 — resumption candle. A green candle (red for a PE) with a body of at least 0.5 × ATR, closing above EMA20 and above the previous candle's high. Buy on its close.
  • Which option: CE in an uptrend, PE in a downtrend, 1 strike in the money. Price must be ₹120–₹400 and the bid-ask gap 2 points or less.
  • Exits: stop at the dip low (kept 8–30 points away) → breakeven at +1R → chandelier trail 2.5 × ATR → close back across EMA20 → time-stop (6 candles, still within 12 points) → 15:15 square-off. Backstop: option down 35%. No target, no partial exits.
  • Limits: entries 09:45–14:30, max 3 trades and ₹5,000 loss a day, pause after 3 losses in a row. VIX filter is off by default.
Read this first. Every chart below is an example — not a real trade. The candles are invented. The bot's real signal code (trend_pb.js) was run on them, so each BUY sits on a candle the code really accepts. The exits follow the paper engine's rules. Option prices are a simple model: the option moves about ₹0.6 for each NIFTY point. These pictures show how the rules work, not whether the strategy makes money.

1. Words you will see

WordPlain meaning
CandleOne bar on the chart. It shows the open, high, low and close for a slice of time. Green = closed higher than it opened. Red = closed lower. The body is the thick part between open and close. Times on the charts are each candle's start time.
TrendThe market's main direction. Up, down, or unclear. This bot only trades in the trend's direction.
Swing high / swing lowA peak or a dip on the chart. Here a 15-minute candle is a swing high when the 2 candles on each side of it all have lower highs (mirror for a swing low).
EMAExponential moving average. A smooth line of recent average price. EMA20 uses about the last 20 candles. EMA50 uses about 50, so it moves more slowly.
VWAPThe day's average price so far, counted from 09:15. Above it, buyers are winning the day. Below it, sellers are.
ATRAverage True Range. How far NIFTY normally moves in one candle. ATR 12 means a normal 5-minute candle spans about 12 points. The bot uses it as a ruler, so its rules fit both calm and wild days.
PullbackA short move against the trend. A small dip in an uptrend, a small bounce in a downtrend.
CE / PECE = Call option, gains when NIFTY rises. PE = Put option, gains when NIFTY falls.
PremiumThe price of the option, in ₹ per unit.
ITMIn the money. An option that already has real value. It follows NIFTY more closely and loses value to time more slowly than a cheap far-away option.
Stop / stop-lossA price where the bot sells to cap a loss. Here every stop is measured on the NIFTY index price (spot), not on the option.
RThe risk on the trade: the gap between the buy price and the first stop. "+1R" means the trade is up by as much as it first risked.
BreakevenMoving the stop up to the buy price. After that the trade cannot turn into a real loss (apart from costs).
Chandelier trailA stop that hangs a fixed distance below the best price reached, like a chandelier hangs below a ceiling. It moves up with price and never moves back down.
Time-stopSelling a trade that has gone nowhere for too long. An option loses value every minute, so a stuck trade slowly bleeds.
VIXIndia VIX, the market's "fear gauge". High VIX = big swings and costly options.
Square-offClosing every open trade at a set time. Nothing is held overnight.

2. A whole Trend Pullback day

Here is the full idea on one chart. Read it left to right.

What you see: an example day, not a real trade. The 15-minute trend is up. Price climbs, then rests above the orange EMA20 line. At 10:25 the dip has not come down to the line yet (grey dot), so the bot waits. The dip finally reaches the line at 25,358.57. At 10:40 a green candle closes at 25,383.38, above EMA20 (25,356.06) and above the previous candle's high (25,374.61). Its body is 8.88 points, more than the 6.05 needed (0.5 × ATR 12.1). BUY CE. The first stop is the dip low, 25,358.57 (24.81 points of risk). The yellow dotted stop climbs behind price. Breakeven arms after the 11:05 candle. The best price is 25,470.91. At 13:35 price slips back to the trail at 25,444.75. Sell: +61.37 NIFTY points, about +₹37 on an example ₹200 option, roughly ₹2,400 on one 65-unit lot before charges. Why it matters: the bot waited through several "almost" moments and bought only when all three steps lined up.

One quirk: no volume check. Many strategies confirm a move with trading volume. The NIFTY index itself has no real volume, so this bot checks the size of the candle body instead. A big body means buyers (or sellers) really showed up.

3. Step 1 — Is there a real trend?

The bot first builds 15-minute candles from its 5-minute candles. It only reads 15-minute candles that have fully closed. For an uptrend all four checks must pass. A downtrend is the exact mirror.

  1. Rising staircase. The last swing high is higher than the one before. The last swing low is higher than the one before.
  2. EMA20 above EMA50 on the 15-minute chart.
  3. EMA20 rising — its latest value is above the one before.
  4. Price above VWAP — the 5-minute close is above the day's average price. (This check can be switched off.)

Uptrend → only CE trades today. Downtrend → only PE trades. Anything else → no trade.

What you see: example 15-minute candles over about one and a half days, not a real trade. The green dots are the last two swing highs: 25,219 then 25,250.30 — a higher high. The yellow dots are the last two swing lows: 25,172.65 then 25,175.99 — a higher low (only just, but higher still counts). EMA20 (25,199.74) is above EMA50 (25,102.76) and rising. Price (25,251.91) is above VWAP (25,229.37). Four out of four: CE only. Why it matters: any one check can be fooled. Needing all four keeps the bot out of messy days.

What you see: an example choppy day, not a real trade. From 10:00 to 14:30 NIFTY wanders in a 41-point box (24,772.63 to 24,813.46). The real engine was asked on every candle: on 56 of the day's 75 candles it found no established 15-minute trend, and it never gave a signal. Zero trades. Why it matters: most days look like this. Doing nothing is the strategy working as designed.

4. Step 2 — Wait for the pullback

The bot does not chase price. It waits on the 5-minute chart for a dip back to the EMA20 (5-minute) line. It looks at the 6 candles just before the current one (today's candles only). For a CE:

  1. Find the highest candle in those 6. That is where the dip starts.
  2. At least 2 candles must come after that peak. One candle is a flicker, not a pause.
  3. The dip's lowest point must reach the line: at or below EMA20 + 0.25 × ATR.
  4. The dip must not be too deep: not lower than EMA20 − 1.5 × ATR. Deeper means the trend may be breaking.

For a PE it is the mirror: a bounce up from the lowest candle, reaching EMA20 − 0.25 × ATR, and not higher than EMA20 + 1.5 × ATR.

What you see: a close-up of the day in section 2, an example, not a real trade. The green band is the pullback zone: from 1.5 × ATR below EMA20 up to 0.25 × ATR above it. Before 10:35 the dips stay above the band. The 10:35 candle dips to 25,358.57, inside the band (the top of the zone was 25,359.09). The next candle is the buy. Why it matters: the zone is measured in ATR, so the same rule works on a quiet day and on a wild one.

What you see: an example uptrend, not a real trade. From 10:15 the price rests, but it stays above the zone. At 10:20 the dip low is 25,244.84 while the zone top is 25,228.04 — too high. At 10:35 the dip low 25,232.30 misses the zone top 25,231.67 by just 0.63 points. Still no. At 10:40 the dip counts, but that candle's body is only 0.58 points. At 10:45 a solid green candle closes at 25,248.96. BUY CE. Why it matters: buying while price is stretched far above the line means paying full price. The bot waits for a discount.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. The red dashed line is the deepest a dip may go (EMA20 − 1.5 × ATR). The 09:40 candle dips to 25,281.94. When the engine checks at 09:45, the limit is 25,284.67, so the dip went too deep. It looks like a break, not a pause, and the engine says no at 09:45 and 09:50. The bot waits. Later a normal dip reaches EMA20 at 25,323.45, and at 10:30 a green candle closes at 25,351.94. BUY CE. Why it matters: a dip that crashes through the line is a warning, not a bargain.

5. Step 3 — The resumption candle

A pullback alone is not a reason to buy. The dip could keep going. The bot waits for one closed 5-minute candle that shows the trend is back. For a CE, that candle must:

RulePlain meaning
Be greenIt closes above its open.
Have a body of at least 0.5 × ATRA decisive candle. This is the stand-in for a volume check.
Close above EMA20 (5-minute)Price has climbed back over the line.
Close above the previous candle's highA fresh push, not a sideways shuffle.

For a PE: a red candle, body at least 0.5 × ATR, closing below EMA20 and below the previous candle's low. The bot decides only on closed candles, then buys at the next live price. A spike inside a candle that fades away does not count.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. The trend is up and the dip has reached the line. At 10:15 a green candle has a body of 5.75 points but needs 6.75 (0.5 × ATR). At 10:25 the body is 6.45 against 6.61 needed — just short. At 10:40 the body is 6.59 against 6.27 needed, the close 25,256.74 is above EMA20 (25,244.15) and above the previous high (25,253.76). BUY CE. Why it matters: weak candles show a half-hearted bounce. The body rule filters them out.

6. Step 4 — Pick the option

CheckDefaultPlain meaning
Which option1 strike in the moneyThe at-the-money strike moved 1 step (50 points) into the money: a lower strike for a CE, a higher one for a PE. This is roughly a "delta 0.6" option.
Price band₹120 – ₹400Too cheap barely moves. Too costly ties up money. Outside the band = skip.
Bid-ask gap2 points or lessA wide gap is a cost you pay going in and again coming out. If the broker sends no bid/ask, this check lets the trade through.
Money availableshared poolThe cost (quantity × option price) must fit in the shared Fyers capital pool, or the entry is refused.

Size is a fixed number of lots (the app's lot setting; one NIFTY lot is 65 units by default). The bot never bets bigger on a "better" setup. Once in the trade, the option's price is streamed live; a regular price poll is the fallback.

If the app is set to trade NIFTY futures instead of options, the price band, the bid-ask check and the 35% premium backstop do not apply. The other rules stay the same.

7. Step 5 — How it sells

There is no profit target and no partial exit. The whole position rides until one rule fires. Whichever fires first wins.

RuleDefaultWhen it is checkedPlain meaning
First stopdip low, 8–30 pts awayevery tickThe stop starts at the pullback low (CE) or high (PE). If that is closer than 8 points it is pushed out to 8. If it is farther than 30 it is pulled in to 30.
Breakeven+1Rcandle closeWhen a 5-minute candle closes 1R in profit, the stop moves to the buy price.
Chandelier trail2.5 × ATRcandle closeThe stop is raised to (best price so far − 2.5 × ATR). It only ever moves in your favour.
Trend failedEMA20 (5-minute)candle closeA candle closes back below EMA20 (above it for a PE). Only counts after price has first closed on the right side of the line.
Time-stop6 candles, 12 ptscandle closeFrom the 6th candle held onward, if the close is still within 12 points of the buy price (either way), sell.
Square-off15:15every tickAnything open at 15:15 is sold.
Premium backstop−35%every tickIf the option price falls 35% below the buy price, sell at once — whatever the index is doing.
Per-trade ₹ capoffevery tickOptional. Sell if the open loss reaches a rupee amount you set.

The stop, breakeven and trail all share one stop line on the NIFTY index price. The stop is checked on every tick. The breakeven, trail, EMA and time rules are updated when each 5-minute candle closes. The app-wide profit lock that some other strategies use is not part of Trend Pullback.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. BUY CE at 11:30 at 25,251.91, first stop 25,235.59 (16.32 points of risk). Breakeven arms after the 11:40 candle and the trail starts after 11:50. The yellow stop climbs all afternoon but price never falls back to it, and no candle closes under EMA20. At 15:15 the square-off sells at 25,365.95. +114.04 points, about +₹68 on an example ₹200 option. Why it matters: with no fixed target, a strong trend day can be held to the end of the day.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. BUY CE at 10:55 at 25,162.26, first stop 25,137.84. Price rises to 25,170.34, then fades. The 11:10 candle closes at 25,152.86, below EMA20 (25,153.53). The trend has failed, so the bot sells on that close. −9.40 points, much less than the 24.42 points the stop would have cost. Why it matters: the trend line often warns before the stop is hit.

8. The PE (falling market) trade

What you see: an example falling day, not a real trade. The 15-minute trend is down, so only PE trades are allowed. Price bounces up to EMA20 (the bounce high is 24,696.38). At 10:40 a red candle closes at 24,684.44, below EMA20 (24,691.50) and below the previous low (24,685.86). Body 10.22 points against 6.29 needed. BUY PE. The first stop is the bounce high, 24,696.38 (11.94 points). Breakeven arms after 10:55. The trail hangs 2.5 × ATR above the lowest price (24,545.81) and comes down with it. At 14:05 a bounce reaches it at 24,573.40. +111.04 points, about +₹67 on an example ₹210 option. Why it matters: the PE trade is the CE trade upside down. Nothing new to learn.

  1. 10:05 – 10:30 — the bounce. The engine says "not enough bounce candles yet", then "candle not red".
  2. 10:40 — red resumption candle. Buy PE at 24,684.44. Stop 24,696.38.
  3. 10:55 — a candle closes 11.94+ points lower. Stop moves to 24,684.44 (breakeven).
  4. 11:05 onward — the trail takes over and steps down.
  5. 14:05 — price bounces into the trail at 24,573.40. Sell.

9. Losing and flat trades

What you see: an example losing trade, not a real one. BUY CE at 10:40 at 25,281.25. The dip low, 25,267.53, is the stop (13.72 points). Price never gets far. At 11:00 it falls to the stop. −13.72 points, about −₹8 on the option, roughly −₹535 on one lot before charges. Why it matters: the stop sits where the setup is proven wrong. Small, quick losses are part of the plan.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. BUY CE at 09:45 at 25,208.04, stop 25,194.10 (13.94 points = 1R). The 09:55 candle closes more than 13.94 points higher, so the stop moves to 25,208.04. Price peaks at 25,230.10, then falls back. At 10:15 it touches the breakeven stop. 0 points — a scratch, before costs. Why it matters: once a trade is up by 1R it can no longer become a full loss.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. BUY CE at 11:55 at 25,187.01. The dip low (25,154.47) is 32.54 points away, more than the 30-point maximum, so the first stop is pulled in to 25,157.01. Then NIFTY goes nowhere. After the 6th candle (12:25) the close is 25,179.67 — only 7.34 points from the buy price, inside the 12-point "flat" band. Time-stop: −7.34 points. Why it matters: an option is a melting ice cube. A stuck trade bleeds value even when the index does nothing.

What you see: an example of a rare event, not a real trade. Same entry as above, ₹200 option. NIFTY barely moves, but the option's price (lower panel) drops fast: ₹182, ₹158, then ₹128. That is below ₹130 (35% under ₹200), so the bot sells at once at the 12:10 candle. This can happen in a sudden volatility crush or a gap. Why it matters: every other stop watches the index. This one watches the option itself, as a last line of defence.

10. Filters that block a trade

What you see: an example, not a real trade. The shaded first 30 minutes are off-limits: no entry is allowed on a candle that starts before 09:45. The 09:45 candle is the first one that may trigger. Here it does: it closes at 25,273.20, above EMA20 and above the previous high. BUY CE. Why it matters: the open is noisy. At the other end, no new trade can start on a candle from 14:30 onward, and everything is sold at 15:15.

What you see: an example, not a real trade. The lower panel is India VIX. At 09:50 the price rules give a CE signal (orange dot). But in this example the VIX filter is switched on, and VIX is 23.4 — above the 22 limit. The trade is skipped. Why it matters: on very jumpy days options are costly and swings are wild. The filter is off by default, so normally this signal would be taken.

11. Safety limits

LimitDefaultPlain meaning
Trades per day3After 3 entries, no more today. Most days take fewer, often none.
Daily loss₹5,000Once today's closed loss reaches ₹5,000, no new trades today. An open trade is still managed normally.
Losing streak3 tradesAfter 3 losing trades in a row today, no new trades today. A win resets the count. A trade that ends exactly flat leaves it unchanged. 0 = off.
Whole-book loss capoffShared by all strategies. When the combined loss of the day reaches the cap, every strategy stops taking new trades. It never places or changes an order.
Entry window09:45 – 14:30No new trades outside this window.
Square-off15:15Every open trade is sold. Nothing overnight.

12. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

ModePageWhat happens
Paper/trend-pb-paperLive prices, pretend money. No real orders. This is the official version: when live or backtest differ, paper is treated as correct.
Live/trend-pb-liveWraps the paper engine and copies its decisions to Fyers. A real order goes out only when all are true: Trend Pullback live is on, the global live dry-run is off, this strategy's own dry-run override is not on, and Fyers is logged in. Out of the box it is in dry-run: it logs the order it would place and sends nothing.
Backtest/trend-pb-backtestRuns the rules over past days. History has no option prices, so it models one from a ₹240 starting price and takes 1.5 points of cost off each side. It fills at the open when a candle gaps past the stop. It also models the daily loss, losing-streak and VIX limits. It compares against a "dumb baseline" (just buy in the trend's direction) and runs a walk-forward test. The whole-book cap is not modelled.
History/trend-pb-paper/historyPast paper sessions and their trades.
Replay/replayRe-runs a recorded day tick by tick. Use it to check that paper and live decide the same thing.
Survives a restart. When a trade opens, its details are saved to a small file. If the server restarts with a trade open, the app checks that file against the broker on boot. It picks the trade back up, or warns you on Telegram if something does not match.

14. What we do not know yet

  • The edge is not proven. The numbers in the rules are design choices, not values fitted to a long record of real trades. Judge it over weeks of paper sessions.
  • Backtest option prices are a model. History has no option quotes. Check results against paper and Replay.
  • It trades rarely, on purpose. Trend, pullback and resumption must line up in that order. Many days it takes nothing.
