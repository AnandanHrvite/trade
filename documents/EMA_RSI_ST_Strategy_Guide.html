EMA_RSI_ST

A trend-following strategy on 5-minute NIFTY candles. It buys a Call (CE) when the trend, the momentum and the SuperTrend line all point up. It buys a Put (PE) when all three point down. Then it waits one candle for proof, and rides the move with a stop that only ever moves in its favour.

Updated 2026-10-08 · every chart is an example built from made-up candles and checked against the real engine code

In one minute
  • Four checks on a closed 5-minute candle. For a CE: EMA20 above EMA50, RSI between 52 and 70, SuperTrend green, and the candle closed above EMA20. For a PE: the mirror — EMA20 below EMA50, RSI between 30 and 48, SuperTrend red, close below EMA20.
  • Wait for proof. That candle only "arms" the trade. The bot buys during the next candle, the moment price goes past the armed candle's close. No cross in that one candle = no trade.
  • What it buys: one NIFTY option at the money or one step in the money (never out of the money). Default quantity: 1 lot = 65.
  • First stop: the low (CE) or high (PE) of the candle before the signal candle. At every candle close the stop moves up (CE) or down (PE) to the tighter of EMA21 and the 3-candle low/high. It never moves back.
  • Other ways out: a 25-point hard cap, a 25% option-price stop, the app-wide profit lock (+8%) and breakeven stop (+5%), still losing after 2 candles, a candle touching EMA21, an opposite signal, and the 14:30 square-off.
  • Limits: new trades only 10:30–14:00. Max 5 trades and ₹3,000 loss a day. No entry when India VIX is above 20.
Read this first. Every chart below is an example — not a real trade. The candles are invented, but the indicator lines are computed from them, and the trades were produced by running the bot's own rule code over them. Option prices in the examples use a simple model (₹200 at entry, moving ₹0.55 for each NIFTY point). The real bot reads the live option price. Defaults quoted are the Settings-page defaults.

Words you will see

WordPlain meaning
CandleOne bar on the chart. Here each candle is 5 minutes of NIFTY price: where it opened, its high, its low, and where it closed.
EMAExponential Moving Average — a smooth line that follows the average price, giving more weight to recent candles. EMA20 uses about 20 candles, EMA50 about 50. When the faster EMA20 is above the slower EMA50, the trend is up.
EMA21 (trail line)A 21-candle EMA built from each candle's average of open, high, low and close. It is not used to enter. It is used to move the stop and to exit.
RSIRelative Strength Index, 0 to 100, over 14 candles. Above 50 means buyers are pushing; below 50 means sellers are. Above 70 is "overheated", below 30 is "washed out".
SuperTrendA line that sits below price in an uptrend (green) and above price in a downtrend (red). It uses ATR (average candle size) over 10 candles × 3. When price closes through it, it flips colour.
CE / PECE = Call option, gains when NIFTY rises. PE = Put option, gains when NIFTY falls. This bot only buys options.
PremiumThe price of the option, in rupees per unit. Profit = (sell premium − buy premium) × quantity.
Stop / trailA stop is the price where the bot sells to limit a loss. A trailing stop moves along behind price as the trade goes your way, so it can lock in profit.
VIXIndia VIX, the market's "fear gauge". High VIX = wild, jumpy prices.

Step 1 — The four checks

The bot looks only at closed 5-minute candles. When a candle closes, it asks four questions:

#For a CE (Call)For a PE (Put)
1. TrendEMA20 is above EMA50EMA20 is below EMA50
2. MomentumRSI is above 52 and below 70RSI is below 48 and above 30
3. SuperTrendGreen (line under price)Red (line over price)
4. CloseThe candle closed above EMA20The candle closed below EMA20

All four must be true on the same candle. The candle must also close inside the entry window, 10:30 to 14:00. The bot needs at least 55 candles of history first (EMA50 plus a small buffer), so it loads earlier days at start-up.

What you see: at 10:30 all four checks are true — EMA20 (blue) over EMA50 (orange), SuperTrend green under price, RSI 69.1 inside the 52–70 band, and the close (24,513.60) above EMA20 (24,480.65). The yellow line is that close: the trigger. At 10:35 price goes past it and the bot buys a CE. The grey area is before 10:30, when no trade can start. Why it matters: one or two checks are common; all four at once is the setup. Example — not a real trade.

  • Optional stricter trend check. Turn on the "triple stack" and the bot also wants EMA9 above EMA20 above EMA50 (CE) or the reverse (PE). Then check 4 uses EMA9 instead of EMA20. Off by default.
  • Check 4 can be switched off. It exists because the lines can stay stacked up from an earlier move while price dips below EMA20. Without it, the bot would buy calls into those dips.

Step 2 — Wait one candle for proof

A candle that passes all four checks does not buy. It arms a trade. Its close becomes the trigger.

  1. During the very next candle, if price goes strictly past the trigger (above it for CE, below it for PE), the bot buys at once, at that price. It does not wait for the candle to close.
  2. If that next candle never crosses, the arm is dropped.
  3. If that next candle itself passes all four checks, it re-arms with its own close as the new trigger.
  4. If the next candle starts outside the entry window, the arm is dropped.

What you see: 12:25 arms a CE (trigger 24,348.25). The 12:30 candle never gets above it — but it passes the checks itself, so it re-arms at its close, 24,341. During 12:35 price goes past 24,341 and the bot buys at 24,342.20. The trade is later closed by the profit lock at 13:15. Why it matters: a setup that price will not follow through on is skipped for free. Example — not a real trade.

You can turn this proof step off. Then the bot buys during the candle as soon as the four checks are true on the forming candle.

Step 3 — The first stop and the trail

Where the stop starts

  • CE: the low of the candle before the signal candle. PE: that candle's high.
  • If that level is on the wrong side of the buy price (for example a CE stop above the entry), the bot moves it to the nearest earlier low (CE) or high (PE) that does protect the trade. If none exists, it does not trade.
  • Option: set the first stop to EMA21 instead (only when EMA21 is on the protective side).

How it moves

At every candle close, the bot works out two levels: EMA21, and the lowest low of the last 3 candles (CE) or highest high (PE). The new stop is whichever is closer to price. The stop only tightens — it never moves away. Between closes, every price tick is checked against it.

What you see: a PE signal at 11:00 and a buy at 11:05 (24,477.10). The first stop is the high of the candle before the signal candle, 24,493.05 (it tightens a fraction at the next close). Price rises back through it at 11:15 and the trade is sold at a small loss. Why it matters: this is the normal, small loss — the stop is close, so the loss is small. Example — not a real trade.

A winning CE trade

What you see: the CE from Step 1, bought at 24,517.95. The orange line is the stop: it starts at 24,494.85 and climbs behind price at each close (purple = EMA21). At 11:35 the trade is closed by the profit lock at 24,595.65, before the stop is reached. Why it matters: on a winner the stop only follows; it never gives the gain back in one go. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: the same trade's option premium. Bought at ₹200. It passes +8% (₹216), which arms the profit lock, and peaks at ₹254.61. The lock now keeps 80% of the gain (peak gain was between +20% and +40%), so the floor is about ₹243.69. Premium dips to ₹242.74 and the trade is sold. About ₹2,695 net on one lot of 65. Why it matters: once a trade has run, most of the gain is protected. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a long CE trend. Bought 10:55 at 25,125.80. The stop (orange) steps up under the candles all day. At 13:15 price falls into it and the trade is sold at 25,330.85 — about 205 NIFTY points. In the model, premium ₹200 → ₹310.52. Why it matters: there is no profit target; the trail lets a trend day pay. Example — not a real trade.

A PE trade

What you see: a falling market. At 10:30 EMA20 is under EMA50, SuperTrend is red, RSI is between 30 and 48, and the candle closed under EMA20. Bought a PE at 10:35 (24,206.45). The stop follows from above. At 12:20 price bounces into it and the trade is sold at 24,090 — about 116 points in our favour. Why it matters: PE is the exact mirror of CE. Example — not a real trade.

Every way a trade ends

Checked on every price tick, in this order:

  1. 25-point cap — NIFTY moved 25 points against the entry.
  2. Profit lock (all strategies) — premium rose +8%, then fell back to the locked floor (see below).
  3. Breakeven stop (all strategies) — premium rose +5%, then fell back to the entry price.
  4. Option stop — premium fell 25% below what was paid.
  5. Price stop — NIFTY touched the stop line (first stop or trail).

Checked when each candle closes, in this order:

  1. Negative-candle stop — after 2 candles the trade is still losing.
  2. EMA21 touch — the candle's range touched EMA21 (not on the entry candle).
  3. Opposite signal — a PE signal while holding a CE, or the reverse.
  4. Square-off — at 14:30 anything open is sold.
The profit lock, in numbers. Arms once premium reaches entry +8%. From then the trade is sold if premium falls to the floor. The floor is the higher of entry +5% and a share of the best gain: 85% of it while the peak gain is under +20%, 80% under +40%, and 70% above that. Example: bought ₹200, peak ₹260 (+30%) → floor = 200 + 60 × 0.80 = ₹248.

What you see: a PE bought at ₹200 (13:35). Premium reaches ₹211.08 (+5.5%), which arms the breakeven stop, but never ₹216, so the profit lock never arms. Premium slides back and the trade is sold at ₹198.65 at 14:00 — the first tick at or below entry. Why it matters: a small winner does not turn into a real loss. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a PE bought at 24,592.55. Its candle stop is far away (24,636.60, 44 points). Price jumps up; at 24,617.55 — 25 points against — the hard cap sells it. Why it matters: the cap limits the damage when the candle stop is wide. After this stop, new PE trades are paused for 2 candles. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a CE bought at 13:50 (24,902.75). The stop is not hit, but after 2 closed candles (14:00 close) the trade is still slightly red, so it is sold at the close, 24,901.45. Why it matters: good trades usually work quickly; a trade going nowhere is cut while the loss is tiny. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a PE bought at 13:55. The 14:00 candle's range reaches up to the purple EMA21 line, so the trade is sold at that candle's close (25,097.15). Why it matters: in the default "touch" mode, price coming back to EMA21 is treated as the move fading. Switch to "cross & close" and only a close on the wrong side of EMA21 ends the trade. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a PE bought at 11:55 that keeps working. No new trades after 14:00 (shaded). The 14:25 candle closes at 14:30, the square-off time, so the trade is sold at 24,092.40 — about 222 points of profit. Why it matters: nothing is held into the last hour or overnight. Example — not a real trade.

Trades it refuses

What you see: at 13:30 the trend is up, SuperTrend is green and the close is above EMA20 — but RSI is 70.7. A CE needs RSI below 70, so nothing is armed. Why it matters: buying after a move is already stretched often means buying the top. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: at 13:00 EMA20 is above EMA50 and SuperTrend is green, but the candle closed at 25,119.20, below EMA20 (25,122.70). Check 4 fails, so no CE. Why it matters: stacked lines alone can be left over from an earlier move. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: the same CE setup as Step 1, but India VIX is 22. Each time a confirmation cross happens (dots), the entry is blocked because VIX is above 20. Why it matters: in a very jumpy market, stops get hit by noise. Example — not a real trade.

Cool-downs after a stop

  • Same side: after a price stop, the 25-point cap or the option stop, that side (CE or PE) is paused for 2 candles.
  • Opposite side: after any exit except an opposite-signal exit, the square-off or a manual exit, the other side is paused for 2 candles. This stops a quick CE → PE → CE flip in a choppy market.
  • No new entry on the same candle where a stop was hit.

What you see: a PE at 13:25 stops out within the same candle. For the next 2 candles new PE crosses (dots at 13:30 and 13:35) are refused. A second PE at 13:50 also stops out, and the 13:55 cross is refused. After 14:00 no new trades start. Why it matters: it stops the bot from buying the same failing idea again and again. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a PE stops out at 12:25. A CE cross at 12:35 is refused (dot) by the opposite-side pause. The CE taken at 12:40 is cut by the negative-candle stop; so is another at 12:55. A PE at 13:30 then wins via the profit lock. Why it matters: the pause is short — it slows flip-flopping but does not stop real reversals. Example — not a real trade.

Bad days and quiet days

What you see: a sideways morning. Five trades, all small losses (stop, breakeven, stop, stop, breakeven), about −₹1,246 in total. After the fifth trade the daily limit of 5 is used up (shaded) and the bot stops for the day. Why it matters: trend strategies lose small, often, in chop. The daily trade cap and ₹3,000 loss cap keep a bad day contained. Example — not a real trade.

What you see: a quiet day. Only once (12:45) do all four checks line up, and the next candle never crosses the trigger, so the arm expires. No trade all day. Why it matters: zero trades is a normal, correct outcome. Example — not a real trade.

Safety limits

  • Entry window: 10:30 to 14:00. The session itself runs 09:15 to 15:30; no new entry in its last 10 minutes either.
  • Square-off: 14:30. At 15:30 the session stops and anything still open is closed.
  • Max 5 trades a day and max ₹3,000 loss a day (counted with earlier trades today, so a restart does not reset it).
  • VIX filter: on by default; blocks entries when India VIX is above 20.
  • Chop guard (off by default): after N losses in a row, stop for the day.
  • Portfolio cap (off by default): one rupee loss cap across all strategies combined.
  • Open-interest filter (off by default): in live-market sessions, refuse trades that fight a big build-up of option positions.
  • Money check: paper entries are checked against the shared Zerodha capital pool; an entry it cannot fund is refused.

Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

  • Paper — simulated trades on the real live feed. This is the reference: the other modes are made to match it.
  • Live — real orders through Zerodha. Needs two switches: EMA_RSI_ST_LIVE_ENABLED on and the app-wide dry-run brake off. While the brake is on (default), the bot only logs the order it would have sent.
  • Backtest — runs the same rules on past candles. It has no real option prices, so premium is estimated from NIFTY moves.
  • Replay — replays a recorded day tick by tick through the paper engine, to check a change.
