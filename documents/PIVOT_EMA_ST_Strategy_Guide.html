PIVOT_EMA_ST

Draw yesterday's pivot lines on today's chart. Buy a CE when the fast average (EMA20) crosses above the slow one (EMA50) inside the zone between the pivot and R1, the candle closes in that zone, and SuperTrend is green. Buy a PE on the mirror picture between S1 and the pivot. The SuperTrend line is the stop, and it is only checked when a candle closes.

1. In one minute

  • Before the open, the bot works out three lines from yesterday's high, low and close: PP (the pivot, in the middle), R1 (above it) and S1 (below it). They do not move all day.
  • It watches closed candles — 3-minute candles by default — with two moving averages, EMA20 and EMA50, and the SuperTrend (10, 3) line.
  • Buy a CE when one candle ticks all three boxes: it closes between PP and R1; EMA20 crosses above EMA50 on that very candle, and the crossing point is also between PP and R1; and SuperTrend is green.
  • Buy a PE on the mirror: closes between S1 and PP, EMA20 crosses below EMA50 on that candle with the crossing point between S1 and PP, SuperTrend red.
  • A close above R1 or below S1 is never traded, whatever else the chart says.
  • The stop is the SuperTrend line. It only moves your way, and it is only checked when a candle closes.
  • The app-wide common rules also apply: profit lock, breakeven stop, a 2-candle wait after any exit, and a bid-ask spread check. New trades 10:00–14:00, everything closed at 15:00.
  • Up to 3 trades a day, ₹5,000 daily loss limit, 1 lot. Orders go to Zerodha; prices come from Fyers. Live is dry-run unless you switch three things.
  • The same rules run on NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, SAIL and on MCX crude, gold and silver (paper only, MCX hours).

3. Yesterday's pivot lines

Each morning the bot takes yesterday's daily high (H), low (L) and close (C) and works out:

PP
PP = (H + L + C) ÷ 3 — the pivot, roughly yesterday's average price.
R1
R1 = 2 × PP − L — the first line above.
S1
S1 = 2 × PP − H — the first line below.
Real example (from the stored 5-minute candles). On 12 Nov 2025 NIFTY's high was 25,934.55, its low 25,781.15 and its close 25,874.00. PP = (25,934.55 + 25,781.15 + 25,874.00) ÷ 3 = 25,863.23. R1 = 2 × 25,863.23 − 25,781.15 = 25,945.32. S1 = 2 × 25,863.23 − 25,934.55 = 25,791.92. Those three lines are fixed for all of 13 Nov 2025 (the CE example in sections 4 and 9).
Where the candle closesWhat the bot may do
Above R1Nothing. No-trade zone.
Between PP and R1Buy a CE, if the other two boxes tick.
Between S1 and PPBuy a PE, if the other two boxes tick.
Below S1Nothing. No-trade zone.
Exactly on PP, R1 or S1Nothing — on a line is not inside a zone.
  • If yesterday's high/low/close cannot be loaded, there are no lines and the bot refuses to trade.
  • If the lines it has are older than the last session on its chart (for example, the daily feed is missing yesterday's bar), it refuses them and fetches them again. Lines from a newer day are fine — that happens after a special session such as Muhurat trading, whose candles are not on the normal chart.
  • BANK NIFTY and SAIL use their own yesterday; the commodity pages use the traded future's previous MCX day.

4. How a trade starts

Everything is read on one closed candle — the signal candle. A wick does not count.

Buy a CE (call) — all three on the same candle
1
Zone. The candle closes above PP and below R1.
2
Cross, inside the zone. EMA20 crosses above EMA50 on this candle (one candle earlier EMA20 was at or below EMA50, now it is above), and the cross level is also between PP and R1. A cross on an earlier candle does not count. A cross that happens below PP does not count, even if the candle then closes above PP.
3
SuperTrend green on this candle (its line is under the close). That line's value is the first stop.
Real example (13 Nov 2025, shown on 5-minute candles). The 10:05 candle closes at 25,894.90 — above PP 25,863.23 and below R1 25,945.32. Box 1 ticks. One candle earlier EMA20 was 25,872.81 and EMA50 25,873.38 (fast under slow). On this candle EMA20 is 25,874.91 and EMA50 25,874.23 (fast over slow), so the lines crossed on this candle, at 25,874.57 — between PP and R1. Box 2 ticks. SuperTrend turns green on this same candle, at 25,815.74. Box 3 ticks. Buy the ATM CE at the next tick (NIFTY 25,896.20 at 10:10). Stop = 25,815.74, 80.46 points below.
Buy a PE (put) — the mirror
  • The candle closes above S1 and below PP.
  • EMA20 crosses below EMA50 on this candle, and the cross level is between S1 and PP.
  • SuperTrend is red (its line is over the close). That value is the first stop.
Real example (26 Feb 2026, 5-minute candles). The 11:45 candle closes at 25,483.35, between S1 25,387.03 and PP 25,519.82. EMA20 goes from 25,519.15 (above EMA50 25,517.19) to 25,515.74 (below EMA50 25,515.87) — a cross down on this candle, at 25,515.81. That is only 4.01 points under PP, but it is inside the PE zone. SuperTrend is red at 25,553.96. Buy the ATM PE at the next tick (NIFTY 25,483.95 at 11:50). Stop = 25,553.96.
Two ways a good-looking candle is still refused.
  • The close is outside the zone. On 1 Sep 2025 the 09:55 candle closed at 24,547.50 — 11.67 points above R1 24,535.83. EMA20 crossed above EMA50 on it at 24,506.50 (inside PP–R1) and SuperTrend was green. The close was above R1, so the bot did nothing.
  • The cross happened outside the zone. On 19 Aug 2025 the 10:10 candle closed at 24,921.90, just inside PP 24,919.63–R1 24,986.42, and SuperTrend was green. EMA20 crossed above EMA50 on it — but at 24,904.35, 15.28 points below PP. The lines crossed in the PE zone, so there was no CE.
Both are charted in section 9.
  • No waiting candle. Paper and Live buy on the first price tick after the signal candle closes.
  • Time. The signal candle must close between 10:00 and 14:00.
  • The bot also needs: no trade already open, fewer than 3 trades today, today's loss under ₹5,000, and 2 candles passed since the last exit.

5. How a trade ends

Checked when a candle closes (the strategy's own exit):

  1. SuperTrend changes colour against the trade (red on a CE, green on a PE) → sell.
  2. The candle closes beyond the stop (below it on a CE, above it on a PE) → sell.
  3. Otherwise the stop moves up to the new SuperTrend value (down, on a PE) — only if that is tighter. It never moves back.

The sale is at the first tick after that candle closes. In the NIFTY data below, rule 1 and rule 2 always happened together: all 154 strategy exits were a SuperTrend colour change.

The SuperTrend stop is never checked tick by tick. A candle that dips through the line but closes back on the right side does not end the trade (the CE chart in section 9 shows three). The flip side: a candle that jumps a long way through the line is only sold after it closes, so a loss can be bigger than the gap to the line (the PE chart shows one).

Checked on every tick (the app's common rules, on the option price — section 6): the profit lock and the breakeven stop. And at 15:00 anything still open is sold.

There is no fixed target, no option-price stop of its own on the NIFTY / BANK NIFTY / SAIL pages, and nothing about R2 or S2. Once a trade is open, R1 and S1 no longer matter — the CE example runs above R1 and is kept.

8. Starting it, and Live

  • Start All (Paper) on the Dashboard starts every switched-on paper strategy, one after another. These three NSE pages are switched on by default, so they start with it. While MCX is open it also starts the switched-on commodity pages; after the NSE close only the commodity half runs. The Real-Time monitor has a separate Start All (Commodity) button.
  • Each page also has its own Start and Stop.
  • Live is a wrapper around the Paper page: Paper decides, Live copies each entry and exit to Zerodha. It needs both logins — Fyers for prices, Zerodha for orders. Its menu item is hidden by default.
  • A real order needs all three: the strategy's …_LIVE_ENABLED on, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN off, and the strategy's …_LIVE_DRY_RUN off. Out of the box the global dry-run is on, so Live only writes the order it would have placed to the log.
  • No Backtest page and no Replay — your choice when the strategy was set up. The only evidence before paper trading is the spot-point check in section 10.

9. Real chart examples

How to read the charts. These are real NIFTY days, run candle by candle through the real engine the way Paper does: decide on the close, buy at the next candle's open (standing in for the first tick), check and trail the stop on each close, square off at 15:00. These examples use 5-minute candles (PIVOT_EMA_ST_TF=5), because only 5-minute history is stored. The live default is 3-minute, so on a live day the same idea plays out on smaller candles, at different times and prices. Dashed lines: R1 (blue), PP (grey), S1 (pink), built from the previous day's 5-minute candles. Yellow = EMA20, purple = EMA50, green/red = SuperTrend, white dashes = the stop in force. Results are in NIFTY points. Option prices are not shown, so the tick-level profit lock and breakeven stop are not drawn.

A winner (CE)

What you see: 13 Nov 2025. Lines from 12 Nov: PP 25,863.23, R1 25,945.32, S1 25,791.92. The 10:05 candle closes at 25,894.90, inside PP–R1. EMA20 crosses above EMA50 on it (25,874.91 over 25,874.23) at a cross level of 25,874.57, also inside PP–R1. SuperTrend turns green on the same candle, at 25,815.74 → BUY CE at 25,896.20 (10:10 open). The green line climbs under price and the stop is moved up 19 times, to 25,947.65 by 12:20. From the 10:55 candle NIFTY closes above R1 (high 25,987.60) — that does not matter once a trade is open. The 12:35, 12:40 and 12:45 candles all dip below the stop (the 12:35 low is 25,942.05) but close above it — no exit. The 12:50 candle closes at 25,942.70, below the stop, and SuperTrend turns red → EXIT at 25,942.00 (12:55 open), +45.80 points. Why it matters: with no target, the trailing line is what lets a move run — and a close-only stop rides out wicks.

A loser (PE)

What you see: 26 Feb 2026. Lines from 25 Feb: PP 25,519.82, R1 25,611.43, S1 25,387.03. The 11:45 candle closes at 25,483.35, inside S1–PP. EMA20 crosses below EMA50 on it (25,515.74 under 25,515.87) at 25,515.81 — 4.01 points under PP, so inside the PE zone. SuperTrend is red at 25,553.96 → BUY PE at 25,483.95 (11:50 open), 70.01 points under the stop. NIFTY falls to 25,400.95 by 12:20, 83 points in the trade's favour, and the red line follows it down 6 times, to 25,489.49 by 13:15. Then NIFTY goes sideways. The 14:40 candle jumps from 25,473.80 to close at 25,513.00 — 23.51 points through the stop — and SuperTrend turns green → EXIT at 25,514.80 (14:45 open), −30.85 points. Why it matters: a close-only stop can be passed by a lot in one candle, and here a trade that was 83 points up still ended in a loss. On a real day the tick-level profit lock and breakeven stop (on the option price, not drawn here) may have sold it earlier.

No trade: the close was above R1

What you see: 1 Sep 2025. Lines from 29 Aug: PP 24,470.27, R1 24,535.83, S1 24,368.08. NIFTY opens below PP, jumps into the CE zone in the first candle, and the 09:30 candle closes above R1. The 09:55 candle closes at 10:00 — the first moment entries are allowed — at 24,547.50, 11.67 points above R1. EMA20 crosses above EMA50 on it (24,507.22 over 24,505.78) at 24,506.50, inside PP–R1, and SuperTrend is green at 24,475.58. Two boxes tick, but the close is above R1 → no trade. It was the day's only EMA cross, so nothing traded all day. Here the rule cost a winner: NIFTY kept rising to 24,635.60, and the same entry and exit rules would have made +85.15 points (24,547.55 at 10:00 → 24,632.70 at 15:00). Why it matters: the R1/S1 rule is a hard no, even when the trend is strong.

No trade: the lines crossed below PP

What you see: 19 Aug 2025. Lines from 18 Aug: PP 24,919.63, R1 24,986.42, S1 24,817.27. The 10:10 candle closes at 24,921.90, just inside PP–R1, and SuperTrend is green at 24,861.12. EMA20 crosses above EMA50 on it (24,904.67 over 24,904.03) — but the cross level is 24,904.35, 15.28 points below PP, in the PE zone (the orange dot). Two boxes tick → no trade, and nothing else traded that day. Taken anyway, the same exit rules would have made +33.65 points (24,921.20 at 10:15 → 24,954.85 at 13:55). Why it matters: a close in the zone is not enough — the cross itself has to happen there. On the data this rule turns away more setups than any rule except the close zone (section 10).

10. What the NIFTY data says

How this was measured: the engine (default settings, except 5-minute candles) was walked candle by candle over stored NIFTY 5-minute candles from 1 Jan 2018 to 31 Jul 2026. The first 5 days were warm-up (the engine loads 5 sessions on 5-minute candles), so 2,115 trading days (8 Jan 2018 – 31 Jul 2026, 102.7 months) could trade. Decide on the close, buy at the next open, trail on each close, sell at the next open after an exit close, square off at 15:00, at most 3 trades a day, wait 2 candles after an exit. This is a spot-point simulation — not a backtest page, and not rupees. The profit lock, breakeven stop, spread check and ₹5,000 loss limit need option prices, which this data does not have, so they were not applied. No costs were charged. The live default (3-minute) was not measured, and neither were BANK NIFTY, SAIL or the commodity pages.

The two zone rules remove the most. Setups turned away only because the lines crossed outside the zone (close in the zone, SuperTrend agreeing, no trade open) came up 342 times; taken one at a time with the same exits, 161 of them (47.1%) would have won, +5.14 points each on average — about the same as the trades taken. Setups turned away only for closing above R1 / below S1 came up 40 times: 17 (42.5%) would have won, +0.02 points on average. So on this data both zone rules mainly mean fewer trades, not better ones. (Each of these is counted on its own; taking one would have blocked some others.)

Common rules (shared by every strategy)

SettingDefaultWhat it does
PROFIT_LOCK_ENABLEDtrueProfit Lock (all strategies). One rule for EVERY strategy, now and in future: once the option premium has risen by the arm %, never let the trade be sold below the floor %. Measured on 346 paper trades — losers peaked ~3 min after entry at ~+6pt while winners peaked ~25 min in at ~+39pt, so a small early gain is a reversal signature. It never caps the upside: a runner that keeps climbing is left to the strategy's own trail.
PROFIT_LOCK_ARM_PCT8Arm at (% premium gain) (1–100). The lock arms once premium touches entry + this %. Must be greater than the floor %, or the lock stays inert.
PROFIT_LOCK_FLOOR_PCT5Locked floor (% premium gain) (0–99). Once armed, the trade is closed if premium falls back to entry + this %. Replaying 344 trades at arm 8 / floor 5 changed 72 exits and turned the active book from -Rs6,028 to +Rs32,110 — treat that as a hypothesis to confirm in paper, not a promise.
PROFIT_LOCK_TRAIL_PCT70Trail — keep % of best gain (0–95). Once the lock is armed, its floor rises with the peak: the trade is closed if premium falls below entry + this % of its best gain so far. Example at 70: entry 142.75, peak 330 → floor 273.82. Only moves up. 0 = off (fixed floor only).
PROFIT_LOCK_TRAIL_TIERS20:85,40:80Trail tiers — tighter on small peaks. Keep more of a small gain, less of a big one. Format "peak%:keep%" pairs. Default 20:85,40:80 — below +20% keep 85% of the gain, below +40% keep 80%, above that the trail % above. Example: entry 100, peak 110 → floor 108.5; peak 150 → floor 135. The floor never drops as the peak rises. Blank = flat trail only.
BREAKEVEN_STOP_ENABLEDtrueBreakeven Stop (all strategies). Once the option premium has risen by the arm %, never let the trade be sold below the entry premium — a winner cannot round-trip into a loss. Catches what the Profit Lock misses: a move that goes +5% and dies arms this but never arms an +8% lock. Measured on 321 trades: lock alone +Rs32,110, lock plus this +Rs44,973. It killed only 6 real winners (Rs499 total), because 66 of the 72 winners that dipped had dipped BEFORE arming. Note a breakeven exit still pays ~Rs71 of charges.
BREAKEVEN_ARM_PCT5Arm at (% premium gain) (1–100). The breakeven arms once premium touches entry + this %. Lower scores better in replay (+Rs81,100 at +2%) but that is the overfitting trap and it fires on noise — 5 is the conservative default.

12. What's still missing

  • It has never traded. No paper session and no live order on NIFTY, BANK NIFTY, SAIL or the commodity pages.
  • No backtest page and no Replay — by your choice. The only test is the spot-point simulation in section 10.
  • The numbers are NIFTY spot points on 5-minute candles. Not option rupees, no costs. The live default is 3-minute, which was not measured (there is no 3-minute history). BANK NIFTY, SAIL and the commodities were not measured at all.
  • The tick-level rules were not tested. Profit lock, breakeven and the spread check need real option ticks. They will change the results above, most likely by cutting some winners short — 103 of the 257 trades were held to 15:00.
  • The stop is wide and close-only. The middle first stop is about 48 points (about ₹1,560 a lot at delta 0.5), and 38 of 147 losers lost more than that first gap. PIVOT_EMA_ST_ST_MULT is the lever: smaller = tighter stop but more noise exits.
  • Thin before costs, and slow. +4.58 points a trade on average, at about 2.5 trades a month on 5-minute NIFTY candles. 30 NIFTY paper trades would take about a year at that pace (3-minute candles and the other three instruments will add more).
  • Next step: paper-trade clean sessions on the defaults, run Live in dry-run next to Paper and check its logged orders match Paper's trades, and only then think about live.
