Prev ORB Scalp

Yesterday's high and low are two lines the whole market watches. If today's first 15 minutes close outside one of them, the day has picked a side. The bot then waits for a small 3-minute candle to confirm, aims for one candle's worth of points, and lets a trailing stop take any extra.

1. In one minute

  • Mark yesterday's high and yesterday's low on NIFTY 50.
  • Build today's first 15-minute candle (09:15–09:30).
  • It closes above yesterday's high → CE day (only a call can be bought). It closes below yesterday's low → PE day (only a put). Anywhere in between → no trade today.
  • From 09:30, watch 3-minute candles. The first one that closes beyond the 09:15 candle's high (CE day) or low (PE day) is the break candle. Buy at its close.
  • Stop = the other end of the break candle. Target = entry ± the break candle's size.
  • At the target the trade is not closed. The stop jumps to the target, then follows each 3-minute candle.
  • One trade a day. No entry after 14:30. Everything is closed at 15:15.

Read this first. This strategy has never traded — no paper sessions and no live orders yet. The only numbers we have come from a backtest on 5-minute data (Section 10), and that backtest lost money. All live orders are off by default.

3. Step 1 — Yesterday's high and low

  1. Before trading starts, the bot looks at yesterday's NIFTY 50 candles.
  2. It writes down the highest high and the lowest low. Nothing else from yesterday is used.
  3. If yesterday's data looks incomplete (its last candle ends before 15:00), the bot does not trade. A wrong high or low is worse than no trade.

What you see: the last hour of yesterday (grey), then today's first five 3-minute candles inside the blue box. Yesterday's high was 24,896.15 and its low 24,791.15. Together the five candles make the 09:15 candle: open 24,908, high 24,930, low 24,895, close 24,922 (yellow line). The close is above yesterday's high, so today is a CE day. Why it matters: one comparison at 09:30 decides the direction for the whole day.

4. Step 2 — The 09:15 candle picks the side

09:15 candle closes…Result
above yesterday's highCE day — only a call can be bought today
below yesterday's lowPE day — only a put can be bought today
anywhere between themNo trade today
  • Only the close counts. A wick above yesterday's high that closes back inside is a no-trade day.
  • Only the 09:15 candle counts. A later candle crossing yesterday's high does not create a setup.
  • If one of the five 3-minute candles is missing, the bot waits and checks again on the next candle. It does not guess.

5. Step 3 — The entry candle

  1. From 09:30, look at every 3-minute candle as it closes.
  2. CE day: the first candle that closes above the 09:15 candle's high. PE day: the first one that closes below its low.
  3. That candle is the break candle. The trade is entered at its close (Paper and Live buy on the first price tick after the close).
  4. Only the first such close counts. If it is missed or refused, the setup is used up for the day.
  5. The break candle must close by 14:30.
  6. The option bought is one strike (50 points) in-the-money (PREV_ORB_SCALP_ITM_STEPS). Fyers supplies prices; orders go to Zerodha.

What you see: a CE day. The 09:15 candle's high is 24,930 (blue line). The 09:33 candle closes at 24,940, the first close above it: BUY CE at 24,940. Its low 24,924 becomes the stop (red). Its size is 19 points, so the target is 24,940 + 19 = 24,959 (green). Why it matters: entry, stop and target all come from one candle, so they are fixed the moment it closes.

When a valid signal is still not filled
  • If NIFTY has already moved through the stop by the time the order would fill, the entry is cancelled.
  • If the break candle only reaches the bot late (in a delayed data batch), it is not filled at a stale price, and the setup is used up.
  • If the bot cannot get an option price, it retries every 5 seconds until the next candle closes.
  • The capital pool must be able to pay for the lot.

6. Step 4 — Stop and target

CE tradePE trade
Entrybreak candle's closebreak candle's close
Stopbreak candle's lowbreak candle's high
Targetentry + candle sizeentry − candle size
  • Candle size = break candle's high minus its low.
  • The entry sits inside the candle, so the stop is never further away than the target.
  • Both are checked against live NIFTY on every price tick.
  • A break candle with zero size, or one that closes exactly at its own low (CE) or high (PE), is refused — the stop would equal the entry.

7. Step 5 — After the target

With PREV_ORB_SCALP_TRAIL_AFTER_TARGET on (the default):

  1. When NIFTY reaches the target, the trade stays open.
  2. The stop jumps to the target price. The profit is now protected.
  3. After each 3-minute candle closes, the stop moves to that candle's low (CE) or high (PE) — only if that is tighter.
  4. The trade ends when NIFTY touches the stop.

With the setting off, the trade simply closes at the target.

What you see: the same CE trade. At 09:39 NIFTY reaches the target 24,959 (green dot) and the red stop line jumps up to it. Then it steps up under each new 3-minute low. At 10:06 a candle dips to the trailed stop: EXIT at 25,026, 86 points from the entry — about ₹3,354 on one 65-unit lot at 0.6 delta. The target gave 19 points; the trail added the rest. Why it matters: on a strong day the trail can be worth far more than the target.

What you see: the same day with Trail After Target switched off. The trade closes the moment NIFTY touches 24,959: 19 points, about ₹741. Why it matters: trail off takes a small, quick profit and gives up the big days. Trail on (the default) keeps the door open.

The global profit lock also watches the trade. It looks at the option premium on every tick. Once the premium is up 8%, a floor is set. The floor keeps at least +5%, and it rises with the best price: it keeps 85% of the best gain while the gain is under 20%, 80% while it is under 40%, and 70% after that. If the premium falls to the floor, the trade is closed. Example: entry ₹260, best ₹286 (+10%) → floor ₹282.10. At 0.6 delta, +8% on a ₹260 option is about 35 NIFTY points.

8. Every way a trade ends

On every price tick the bot checks, in this order: profit lock → stop → target. The trail moves when a 3-minute candle closes. At 15:15 everything is closed.

Stop hit before the target (a loss)

What you see: a PE day. The 09:15 candle closed at 24,604, below yesterday's low 24,625.8. The 09:30 candle closes at 24,590, below the 09:15 low 24,596: BUY PE at 24,590, stop 24,607, target 24,571. Price bounces instead. At 09:36 NIFTY touches 24,607: stopped out, 17 points, about ₹663. At 09:54 another candle closes below the 09:15 low (grey dot), but the day is finished. Why it matters: one trade a day, and a second break never gets a second chance.

Target reached, then straight back to it

What you see: a CE day (09:15 candle closed 24,715, above yesterday's high 24,693.55). BUY CE at 24,726 on the 09:30 candle. At 09:36 NIFTY touches the target 24,741 and the stop jumps there. The next candle drops back and touches it: EXIT at 24,741, +15 points, about ₹585. Why it matters: once the target is touched, the trade cannot turn into a loss (apart from gaps and slippage).

The profit lock closes it

What you see: a PE day, with the option premium in the lower panel (₹260 entry, 0.6 delta assumed). BUY PE at 24,649. NIFTY falls fast and the target 24,629 is reached. The premium peaks at ₹282.2, above the +8% line (₹280.80), so the lock arms and keeps 85% of the gain. A quick bounce pulls the premium back to ₹278.75 and the trade closes at 24,617.75, before the trailed stop is touched. Result about +₹1,219. Why it matters: on a fast move the profit lock often acts before the 3-minute trail.

Square-off at 15:15

What you see: a PE day where the first close below the 09:15 low 24,101 only comes at 13:57. BUY PE at 24,095, stop 24,114, target 24,073. The afternoon goes nowhere: neither the stop nor the target is touched. At 15:15 the trade is closed at 24,090.6 (+4.4 points). Why it matters: nothing is carried overnight, whatever the trade is doing.

9. Days with no trade

Most days end without a trade. Here are the three common reasons.

The 09:15 candle closes inside yesterday's range

What you see: yesterday's high was 24,469.05. The 09:15 candle goes as high as 24,493 but closes at 24,460 (yellow line) — back inside the range. So the bot writes "no trade today" at 09:30. Later NIFTY climbs to 24,509.3, well above yesterday's high, but a later move never counts. Why it matters: a wick is not a close. This rule keeps the bot out of mornings that could not decide.

A side is picked, but the break never comes

What you see: the 09:15 candle closed at 24,382, above yesterday's high 24,366.4, so it is a CE day. The break level is the 09:15 high, 24,400. Price slides and drifts below it. In this example no 3-minute candle closes above 24,400 for the rest of the day, so there is no trade. Why it matters: the setup is only half of the rule. Without a break candle, nothing is bought.

The break comes too late

What you see: a CE day (09:15 candle closed 24,190, above yesterday's high 24,174.05). The break level is 24,208. All day no candle closes above it — until the 14:42 candle, which closes at 24,209.2 at 14:45. That is after the 14:30 cut-off, so it is refused, and because only the first break counts, the day is over. Why it matters: a late break leaves little time before the 15:15 square-off.

12. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

ModeWhat it does
PaperLive data, pretend orders. This is the official version of the rules. The other modes are made to match it.
BacktestPast candles. Buys at the next candle's open (the same moment as Paper's first tick after the close). Inside a candle it checks the stop before the target, so a candle that touched both counts as a loss. Charges slippage each way and assumes an entry premium of ₹260. The profit lock is estimated by turning the premium floor into a NIFTY level.
LiveReal Zerodha orders through the paper-wrapping harness. Needs three things: LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false (global), PREV_ORB_SCALP_LIVE_ENABLED=true, and PREV_ORB_SCALP_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. It ships in dry-run.
ReplayRe-runs a recorded day tick by tick so a change can be tested on a real session.

Every decision reads only closed 3-minute candles from the history feed. The live price is used only to test the stop, target and profit lock.

14. What's still missing

  • It has never traded. No paper session, no live order.
  • The backtest used 5-minute candles. Real 3-minute break candles are smaller, so stops and targets are tighter and more trades may stop out on noise.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated from a flat premium and delta.
  • The profit lock now trails. The rising floor was added after the backtest above, so its effect here is not measured.
  • On the data we have, the rules lose money. Collect clean paper sessions on real 3-minute candles, compare Paper and Live on /replay, and only then think about live.
