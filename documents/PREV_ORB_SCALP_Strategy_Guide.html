Prev ORB Scalp

Yesterday's high and low are the two lines everyone on the street is watching. If the market opens and its first fifteen minutes close outside one of those lines, the day has picked a side. This strategy waits for the next small candle to confirm the move, rides it for one candle's worth of points, and then lets a trailing stop decide how much more it gets.

Updated 2026-09-27  ·  plain-English edition  ·  every chart and every number below was produced by running the real engine over real NIFTY data

Read this first: this strategy has never traded. Not one paper session, not one live order. Every figure here comes from running src/strategies/prev_orb_scalp.js and its backtest over the repo's cached NIFTY history, January 2018 to July 2026. That history has 5-minute candles only, so every chart and number in this guide uses 5-minute bars as a stand-in for the 3-minute chart the strategy actually trades. Every rupee figure is simulated. And the honest result, in section 9, is a loss.

1. The idea in one minute

Every morning the market has two obvious reference lines: yesterday's highest price and yesterday's lowest price. Most days NIFTY opens and wanders around inside that range. Some days it opens and immediately pushes through one side and stays there. Those are the days this strategy wants.

It does not guess. It asks one question at 09:30 — did the first 15 minutes of the day close outside yesterday's range? — and one more question on every small candle after that: has price now closed beyond the edge of that first 15-minute candle? The first time the answer is yes, it buys an option in that direction.

Mark yesterday's HIGH & LOW→ 09:15 15-min candle closes outside → side picked→ First 3-min close beyond that candle → buy→ Stop = that candle's other end · target = its size→ At target: lock it, then trail

Below yesterday's low it buys a Put (PE). Above yesterday's high it buys a Call (CE). One trade a day at most, and most days — more than half, as section 9 shows — there is no trade at all.

Two charts, one data feed. The engine is fed only 3-minute NIFTY 50 spot candles. The 15-minute candle is built from the first five of them (first open, highest high, lowest low, last close) — exactly the candle a 15-minute chart draws. Yesterday's high and low come from the same feed. There is no VIX gate, no open-interest filter, no RSI, no moving average, no volume test and no ATR. Three price levels and one candle are the entire input.

3. Step 1 — Yesterday's high and low

Before the market opens, the engine looks back at the previous session and writes down two numbers: its highest high and its lowest low. Nothing else from yesterday is used — not the close, not the open, not any pivot.

The engine is strict about these two numbers being right. If yesterday's data looks incomplete — the last candle it has closes before 15:00 — it refuses to trade that day rather than use a range that may be missing part of the afternoon. On the charts below yesterday's high is the dashed purple line and yesterday's low the dashed pink line.

4. Step 2 — The 09:15 candle picks the side

At 09:30 the first 15-minute candle closes, and the engine compares its close with yesterday's two lines:

Closes BELOW yesterday's LOW
PE day

Only a Put can be bought today.

Closes ABOVE yesterday's HIGH
CE day

Only a Call can be bought today.

Anything else — the close lands anywhere inside yesterday's range — means no trade today. It does not matter what happens later. A candle at 11:00 that breaks yesterday's high does not count; only the 09:15 candle can set the day up. It is the close that counts, not a wick: a 09:15 candle that pokes above yesterday's high and closes back inside is a no-trade day.

Here is a real one. Look how close it came.

7 July 2026 — inside yesterday's range, no trade. Yesterday's high was 24,458.65. The 09:15 candle went as high as 24,488.45 but closed at 24,455.85 — 2.8 points short of yesterday's high. That is inside the range, so the engine wrote "no trade today" at 09:30 and stopped looking. Later in the morning price did climb to 24,530.9, but a later break never counts. (Drawn with 5-minute bars; the 09:15 candle is the three bars in the blue box.)

This happens a lot. Over 8½ years of real sessions, the 09:15 candle closed inside yesterday's range on 1,138 of 2,120 days — 53.7%. On those days the strategy does nothing, by design.

5. Step 3 — The entry candle

On a PE or CE day, the engine now watches every 3-minute candle from 09:30 on and asks one thing: did this candle close beyond the 09:15 candle's edge?

  • PE day: the first 3-minute candle that closes below the 09:15 candle's LOW.
  • CE day: the first 3-minute candle that closes above the 09:15 candle's HIGH.

The trade is entered at that candle's close. Paper and Live buy on the first tick after the close; the backtest buys at the next candle's open, which is the same moment in the market.

Only the first close counts. If the session was started late and missed that candle, or the entry was refused for any reason, the day is over. A later close beyond the same level is not a second chance. This keeps Paper, Backtest and Replay agreeing on the one candle that could trade.

Two more limits:

  • No entries after 14:30 (PREV_ORB_SCALP_ENTRY_END), judged on when the break candle closes. In the 8½-year run this blocked 22 days whose first break came too late.
  • One trade a day. After it closes, win or lose, nothing more happens until tomorrow.

Sometimes the break never comes: the 09:15 candle closes outside yesterday's range, but price never closes beyond the 09:15 candle's own edge. That happened on 262 of the setup days in the backtest.

The option bought is one strike in-the-money (PREV_ORB_SCALP_ITM_STEPS = 1, one 50-point step), roughly delta 0.6. Orders go to Zerodha; Fyers supplies the candles and option prices.

6. Step 4 — Stop and target

Both come straight off the break candle. Nothing is calculated from anything else.

PE tradeCE trade
EntryBreak candle's closeBreak candle's close
StopBreak candle's HIGHBreak candle's LOW
TargetEntry minus the candle's sizeEntry plus the candle's size

A real-numbers example (the PE trade in section 8): the break candle had a high of 18,049.7, a low of 18,024.7 and closed at 18,032.8. So:

  • Entry = 18,032.8
  • Stop = the candle's high = 18,049.7 (16.9 points away)
  • Candle size = 18,049.7 − 18,024.7 = 25 points
  • Target = 18,032.8 − 25 = 18,007.8

Because the entry is the close, which sits somewhere inside the candle, the stop is never further away than the target. In this example you risk 16.9 points to make 25.

Stop and target are both checked against live NIFTY spot on every tick. A trade is also refused if the break candle has zero size, or closed exactly at its own high (PE) or low (CE) — that would put the stop at the entry price.

How big are these candles? On 5-minute bars (the stand-in), the median break candle was 20.65 points, and 58 of 690 (8.4%) were under 10 points. Real 3-minute candles will be smaller, so expect tighter stops and targets live.

7. Step 5 — After the target

With PREV_ORB_SCALP_TRAIL_AFTER_TARGET ON (the default), reaching the target does not close the trade. Instead:

  1. The stop jumps to the target level. From here the trade can no longer end below the target (ignoring gaps and slippage).
  2. After every closed 3-minute candle, the stop moves to that candle's HIGH (PE) or LOW (CE) — but only if that is tighter. It never moves backwards.
  3. The trade ends when spot touches the trailed stop.

With the setting OFF, the trade simply exits at the target.

The global profit lock also applies. The repo-wide premium lock in utils/tradeGuards.js watches the option price itself. Once the premium is up 8%, if it falls back to only +5%, the trade is closed. Example: bought at ₹260, the lock arms at ₹280.80 and would close the trade if the premium drops back to ₹273. At delta 0.55 that is roughly a 38-point spot move to arm and a floor about 24 points in your favour.

And whatever else is happening, anything still open is closed at 15:15 (PREV_ORB_SCALP_FORCED_EXIT). Nothing is carried overnight.

8. Three real trades

These are real sessions, run through the real engine and backtest. Each is drawn with 5-minute bars standing in for the 3-minute chart. On every chart: dashed purple/pink = yesterday's high/low, the blue box = the 09:15 candle, dashed blue = the break level, solid red = the stop (it steps as it trails), dashed green = the target.

A Put that hit the target and trailed

25 January 2023 — PE day, target hit, then trailed. Yesterday's low was 18,078.7. The 09:15 candle closed at 18,043.7 — below it, so PE day, break level = its low 18,034.2. The 09:30 candle closed 18,032.8, the first close below that: BUY PE at 18,032.8, stop 18,049.7, target 18,007.8 (25-point candle). At 09:50 the target was reached; the stop jumped to 18,007.8 and then followed each candle's high down: 18,004.2 → 18,002.8 → 17,996.8 → 17,969.9 → 17,955.4. The 10:20 candle rose to touch it: EXIT 17,955.4, 77.4 spot points from the entry — the target gave 25 of them, the trail another 52.4. Simulated: +₹2,451. Price kept falling to 17,909.5 afterwards; the trail does give some back.

A Call on a CE day

2 September 2025 — CE day, target hit, then trailed. Yesterday's high was 24,635.6. The 09:15 candle closed 24,653.55, above it: CE day, break level = its high 24,685.85. The 09:30 candle closed just under at 24,676.6 — not yet. The 09:35 candle closed 24,687.55: BUY CE at 24,687.55, stop at its low 24,675.2 (12.35 points), target 24,705.6 (18.05-point candle). The 09:50 candle reached the target; the stop moved to it and then trailed each candle's low up to 24,734.3. The 10:10 candle dipped to it: EXIT 24,734.3, 46.75 spot points. Simulated: +₹1,354.

A Put that was stopped out

30 June 2026 — PE day, stopped out. Yesterday's low was 23,924.55. The 09:15 candle fell hard and closed 23,896.9, below it: PE day, break level 23,890.8. The very next candle, 09:30, closed 23,873.3: BUY PE at 23,873.3, stop 23,899.5 (26.2 points), target 23,841.25 (32.05-point candle). Price slipped to 23,851.95 at 09:50 — about 10.7 points short of the target — then turned. The 10:00 candle rose to 23,914.95 and took out the stop: EXIT 23,899.5. Simulated: −₹1,244. This is the most common ending: 358 of 690 backtest trades (51.9%) finished exactly like this.

Read that plainly: once a trade reaches its target it almost always wins (94.0% of those 302 did). The problem is that more than half of all trades never get there — 113 of the 358 stops were hit inside the same candle the trade was filled on.

The index moved the right way — the costs ate it

Measured on NIFTY spot, fill to exit, the 690 trades made +2,688 points in total, about +3.9 points a trade. So the rules do pick the right direction slightly more than the wrong one. But one lot of a delta-0.55 option turns 3.9 points into only about ₹139. Against that, each trade pays about ₹195 in the assumed slippage (1.5 points each way on 65 units) and about ₹93 in brokerage and taxes. That leaves roughly −₹149 a trade, and 690 × −₹149 is the −₹1.03 lakh.

Two good years out of nine is not an edge. 2024 and 2025 were profitable; the other seven were not, and 2026 so far is back to losing. The early years look worst partly because the backtest uses today's lot size (65) and a flat ₹260 premium for every year, when NIFTY was less than half today's level in 2018 — so treat the year-by-year rupees as rough. But the overall picture does not change: on this stand-in data, the rules as written lose money once realistic costs are charged. The single best trade (5 August 2024, +₹7,060) was only 3.1% of gross wins, so the result is not one lucky day either way.

There is no daily-loss limit because there is never more than one trade a day: the stop is the daily limit.

Deliberately NOT in this strategy. No VIX filter, no open-interest filter, no RSI, no moving averages, no volume test, no ATR, no VWAP, no gap filter, no retest of the level, no second confirmation candle, no minimum or maximum candle size, no stop buffer, no breakeven move before the target, and no second trade. The rules were specified without any of them. Adding one would make it a different strategy, and should be tested as one.

11. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

ModeWhat it does
PaperLive data, simulated orders. The canonical surface — every decision, fill and exit rule is defined here, and the other modes are made to match it, never the reverse.
BacktestHistorical candles. Fills at the next candle's open, tests the stop before the target inside each candle (so a candle that touched both books the loss), charges a PREV_ORB_SCALP_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS haircut each way and assumes a PREV_ORB_SCALP_BT_SEED_PREMIUM entry premium. The profit lock is approximated by converting the premium floor back into a spot level.
LiveReal Zerodha orders through the paper-wrapping harness. Triple-gated: it needs LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false (the global switch), PREV_ORB_SCALP_LIVE_ENABLED=true (this strategy), and PREV_ORB_SCALP_LIVE_DRY_RUN not set to true (a hold-back for this strategy alone). It ships dry.
ReplayRe-runs a recorded session tick by tick, so a change can be tested against a day that actually happened.
The modes agree by construction. Every value a decision reads — yesterday's range, the 09:15 candle, the break candle — comes from closed 3-minute candles from the history endpoint. The live price is only used to test the already-fixed stop and target. The rules live in one file, src/strategies/prev_orb_scalp.js; Paper, Backtest and Live all call it.

13. What's still missing

It has never traded. Not once. No paper session, no live order. Everything here describes what the code does, checked by running it — not what it earns.
  • The data is a 5-minute stand-in. The strategy trades 3-minute candles; the only cached history is 5-minute. With 3-minute bars the 09:15 candle is the same, but the break candle usually comes sooner and is smaller, so stops and targets are tighter and more trades may stop out on noise. The numbers in section 9 will not be the 3-minute numbers.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated. There is no historical option chain: the premium is a flat ₹260 moved by delta 0.55, with a 1.5-point haircut each way and today's 65-unit lot for every year. Treat it as a direction, never as a result.
  • The profit lock in the backtest is an approximation. Live, the lock watches the real option price tick by tick. The backtest turns the premium floor into a spot level and checks it against candle highs and lows, which cannot see the order things happened inside a candle.
  • On this data the rules lose money. Profit factor 0.69 over 690 trades, with only 2024 and 2025 positive. The spot direction is slightly right (+3.9 points a trade); costs are the difference. Paper sessions should confirm or reject that before any live thought.
The order of work. Collect clean paper sessions on real 3-minute candles first. Diff them against /replay to confirm Paper and Live make identical decisions on a recorded day. Only then consider a live gate — and given section 9, not before a clean paper sample says something different.
