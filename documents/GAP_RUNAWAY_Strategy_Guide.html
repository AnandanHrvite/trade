GAP_runAway__Kirubakaran

Look at yesterday's daily candle. If the daily RSI (worked out on the EMA21 line) is below 10 and NIFTY opens higher today, buy a CE. If the RSI is above 90 and NIFTY opens lower, buy a PE. The stop is yesterday's close — if price goes back and fills the gap, you are out. A 5-minute candle closing back through EMA21 ends the trade. Everything is closed by 15:15.

1. In one minute

  • Every morning the bot reads yesterday's closed daily candle on NIFTY: the close, the EMA21 of the daily close, and an RSI(10) worked out on that EMA21 line (not on the close).
  • RSI below 10 = oversold. RSI above 90 = overbought. Anything in between = no trade today.
  • Gap = today's opening price minus yesterday's close.
  • Buy the ATM CE: oversold yesterday and today opens above yesterday's close (gap up). Buy the ATM PE: overbought yesterday and today opens below it (gap down). Any size of gap counts.
  • Entry only between 09:15 and 09:30. One trade a day, no second try.
  • Stop = yesterday's close, checked on every tick. If price goes back to it (the gap “fills”), the trade is closed.
  • Trail = EMA21 on the 5-minute chart. A 5-minute candle that closes back through it ends the trade (a wick does not).
  • Anything still open is closed at 15:15. Nothing is held overnight.
  • 1 lot (its own setting). Orders go to Zerodha; prices come from Fyers. Live is dry-run unless you switch three things.
  • The same rules also run as a stock scanner over the F&O list, paper only, trading shares intraday (section 7).
Yesterday's daily RSI-on-EMA21 < 10 or > 90
→
Today gaps the opposite way
→
Buy at the open
→
Out if the gap fills, a 5-min close crosses EMA21, or 15:15
Read this first. This strategy is brand new and has never traded — no paper session and no live order, on NIFTY or on stocks. Live orders are off by default. On 8½ years of stored NIFTY data the rules lost points: 375 trades, 28.3% winners, −10.41 points a trade on average, before any costs, and every single year was negative (section 10). That is the main finding of this guide. Results are NIFTY spot points, not option rupees. There is no backtest page.

3. The daily setup

Before the open, the bot loads about 400 calendar days of NIFTY daily candles and looks only at the ones that have closed. Today's candle is never used for the setup, so nothing during the day can change it.

1
EMA21 of the daily close.
2
RSI(10) on that EMA21 line — in TradingView terms, RSI length 10 with Source: EMA.
3
Read both on yesterday's candle. Below 10 → oversold. Above 90 → overbought. Otherwise → nothing today.
4
Keep yesterday's close. It is used twice: to measure the gap, and as the stop.
Real example. On 18 Nov 2024 NIFTY closed at 23,465.30, far below its daily EMA21 of 24,205.24. The RSI on the EMA21 read 3.2 — below 10, so 19 Nov started oversold: a gap up would buy a CE.
  • The bot refuses to decide if it has fewer than 100 closed daily candles, or if the newest one is more than 7 days old (a broken feed).
  • The numbers come from the same engine the paper page uses, so the setup card on the page shows exactly what the decision read.

4. How a trade starts

The decision is made once, as soon as today's official open is known (the open of today's 09:15 one-minute candle; if that cannot be fetched within about 20 seconds, the first live tick after 09:15 is used and logged).

Buy the ATM CE (call)
1
Yesterday's RSI on EMA21 is below 10.
2
Today opens above yesterday's close (any gap bigger than 0, and at least GAP_RUNAWAY_MIN_GAP_PTS, which is 0 by default).
Buy the ATM PE (put)
1
Yesterday's RSI on EMA21 is above 90.
2
Today opens below yesterday's close.
Real example (19 Nov 2024). Oversold (RSI 3.2). Yesterday's close 23,465.30, today's open 23,529.55 → gap up 64.25 points. Buy the ATM CE. Stop = 23,465.30, 64.25 points below the open.
Real example (22 May 2025). Overbought (RSI 97.27). Yesterday's close 24,800.95, today's open 24,733.95 → gap down 67 points. Buy the ATM PE. Stop = 24,800.95.
  • Wrong-way gap = no trade. Oversold but a gap down, or overbought but a gap up, does nothing all day (example in section 9).
  • Window. The fill is the first price after the open, between 09:15 and 09:30. If the option cannot be filled (no symbol or no premium) it retries every 5 seconds until 09:30, then gives up for the day.
  • Already filled = no trade. If NIFTY is already back at yesterday's close when the fill happens, the entry is refused — otherwise the stop would close it on the first tick.
  • One trade a day. After any exit, nothing more until tomorrow.
  • Strike. Exactly ATM at the moment of entry. The app's global strike offsets do not apply.

5. How a trade ends

Whichever happens first:

  1. Gap stop — on every tick. CE: NIFTY spot at or below yesterday's close. PE: at or above it. The stop is a fixed price level, not a distance, and it never moves.
  2. EMA21 trail — on each closed 5-minute candle. CE: the candle closes below the 5-minute EMA21. PE: it closes above it. A wick through the line does not count.
  3. 15:15 square-off. Anything still open is sold.
The size of the gap is the size of your risk. A 64-point gap means the stop is 64 points away. An 8-point gap means the stop is 8 points away. On a typical day the first 5-minute candle alone is about 48 points tall (median over 2,118 stored days), so a stop that close sits well inside the opening noise. On the stored data, 109 of the 158 stops happened inside the first 5-minute candle (09:15–09:20), and 155 of 158 before 10:00. GAP_RUNAWAY_MIN_GAP_PTS is the setting that skips tiny gaps (section 10 shows what it did on this data).

There is no fixed target, no option-price stop, no profit lock and no breakeven. The EMA21 trail is the only thing that takes profit.

6. What does not apply

You chose that none of the app's Common Rules apply to this strategy. It keeps its own settings for everything.

Common ruleHere
Profit lock, breakeven stopOff. Only the gap stop, the EMA21 trail and 15:15.
Wait after any exitNot needed. One trade a day, no re-entry.
Bid-ask spread checkOff. The entry is not skipped for a wide spread.
10:00–14:00 trade window, 15:00 exitOff. Own window 09:15–09:30, own square-off 15:15.
Lot MultiplierOff. Own GAP_RUNAWAY_LOTS (1 lot = 65 qty).
Paper capital pool, portfolio capOff. Own paper capital (GAP_RUNAWAY_CAPITAL).
VIX, OI, volume, trend filtersNone. No confirmation candle either.

Two shared things are still used, because they are facts, not rules: the common option expiry (to pick the contract) and the brokerage/charges model.

The video holds for days; this build does not. The reference video keeps the trade for several days. You chose intraday: everything is closed at 15:15. That is a real difference from the video, and the numbers in section 10 are for the intraday version only.

7. The stock scanner

The same engine runs over a list of stocks, with its own settings (same names with GAP_RUNAWAY_STK_ in front). Page: /gap_runaway_stk-paper, under STOCKS. Paper only — it never sends an order.

  1. Scan (at Start, before 09:15). Every stock in the list (default: all F&O stocks, about 220, roughly 75 seconds) gets its daily candles and yesterday's RSI on EMA21. Only stocks with RSI below 10 or above 90 stay on the list.
  2. Open. From 09:15 it reads those stocks' quotes. Today's open = the quote's official open. If the quote's “previous close” differs from the daily history by more than 2% (an unadjusted split or stale data), that stock is skipped.
  3. Pick. Every stock that gives a signal is ranked by the size of its gap in %, biggest first. Up to 3 trades a day, one per stock. A stock whose price has already filled its gap is skipped and the next one takes the slot.
  4. Trade. Gap up after oversold → buy the shares. Gap down after overbought → short-sell the shares. Intraday (MIS). Shares = ₹1,00,000 ÷ price, rounded down: a ₹2,500 stock → 40 shares.
  5. Exit. The same three: price back at yesterday's close, a 5-minute close back through EMA21, or 15:15.
  6. P&L = share price move × shares, minus Zerodha intraday-equity charges.

For a stock, GAP_RUNAWAY_STK_MIN_GAP_PTS is in rupees of share price (0 by default = any gap). A restart in the middle of the day restores that day's open paper positions on the next Start. It has not been run on real stock data yet — nothing in section 10 is about stocks.

8. Starting it, and Live

  • Start All (Paper) on the Dashboard starts both pages (NIFTY and the stock scanner) while their Mode switches are on, which they are by default. Each page also has its own Start and Stop.
  • Start before 09:15 so the daily setup is ready when the market opens. The entry window closes at 09:30; starting later means no trade that day.
  • NIFTY paper page: /gap_runaway-paper. If it is stopped and started again (or the app restarts) in the middle of the day, it counts that day's earlier trades from disk, so the day does not get a second trade.
  • Live (/gap_runaway-live) wraps the Paper page: Paper decides, Live copies each entry and exit to Zerodha. It needs both logins — Fyers for prices, Zerodha for orders. Its menu item is hidden by default.
  • A real order needs all three: GAP_RUNAWAY_LIVE_ENABLED on, the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN off, and GAP_RUNAWAY_LIVE_DRY_RUN not on. Out of the box the global dry-run is on, so Live only writes the order it would have placed to the log.
  • No Backtest page and no Replay — your choice when the strategy was set up. The stock scanner has no Live at all.

9. Real chart examples

How to read the charts. These are real NIFTY days from the stored 5-minute candles, run through the real engine with default settings. Daily candles are built from those 5-minute candles (first open, highest high, lowest low, last close of each day). Each example has two charts. Top: the last 40 daily candles, the daily EMA21 (yellow) and, underneath, the RSI on EMA21 (purple) with the 90 and 10 lines; the yellow dot in the RSI panel is yesterday's reading. Bottom: the trade day on 5-minute candles, with the last hour of yesterday on the left (grey band), the 5-minute EMA21 (yellow) and yesterday's close as a white dashed line — the stop. The buy is at the 09:15 candle's open (standing in for today's official open). A stop is filled at yesterday's close; a trail exit at the close of the candle that crossed EMA21. Results are NIFTY points, not option rupees, and no costs are taken off.

A winner (CE) — 19 Nov 2024

What you see: NIFTY daily, 40 sessions to 19 Nov 2024. Price has fallen well below its daily EMA21 (24,205.24 on 18 Nov 2024). The RSI on EMA21 has been under 10 for days — 19 Nov 2024 is the 10th session in a row that starts oversold — and reads 3.2 on 18 Nov 2024. That day closes at 23,465.30. 19 Nov 2024 opens at 23,529.55, 64.25 points higher (+0.27%) → oversold + gap up = CE. Why it matters: the whole decision is made from yesterday's daily candle and today's first price.

What you see: 19 Nov 2024, 5-minute candles. Yesterday closed at 23,465.30 (white dashes = the stop). BUY CE at 23,529.55 (09:15 open), 64.25 points above the stop. The first candle dips to 23,517.10 — still above the stop — and closes at 23,590.05. NIFTY climbs to 23,780.65, staying above the yellow EMA21. The 12:40 candle closes at 23,732.75, below EMA21 23,739.98 → EXIT 23,732.75, +203.20 points. Later the same day NIFTY fell all the way back to 23,464.80 and closed at 23,468.70 — the gap filled after the exit. Why it matters: with no target, the EMA21 trail is the only thing that banks a profit, and here it got out before the fall.

A winner (PE) — 22 May 2025

What you see: NIFTY daily, 40 sessions to 22 May 2025. Price is above its daily EMA21 (24,419.02 on 21 May 2025) and the RSI on EMA21 is pinned near the top: 97.27 on 21 May 2025, the 14th session in a row that starts overbought. 21 May 2025 closes at 24,800.95; 22 May 2025 opens at 24,733.95, 67.00 points lower (−0.27%) → overbought + gap down = PE. Why it matters: an RSI on a smooth line can sit above 90 for weeks, so “overbought” is common, not rare.

What you see: 22 May 2025, 5-minute candles. Stop = yesterday's close 24,800.95. BUY PE at 24,733.95 (09:15 open). The first candle only reaches 24,737.50 — 63.45 points short of the stop — and closes at 24,643.05. NIFTY keeps falling under the EMA21 to 24,509.10. The 13:30 candle closes at 24,552.90, just 0.41 above EMA21 24,552.49 → EXIT 24,552.90, +181.05 points. Afterwards NIFTY dipped again to 24,462.40 and closed the day at 24,637.00; held to 15:15 (open 24,606.35) the trade would have made +127.60. Why it matters: a close-only trail can exit on a candle that is barely over the line — here by 0.41 of a point.

A gap-stop loser — 9 Jul 2025

10. Real-data check

How this was measured: the engine (default settings) was walked over the stored NIFTY 5-minute candles from 1 Jan 2018 to 31 Jul 2026 — 2,120 trading days. The engine needs 100 closed daily candles first, so the first day it could decide was 29 May 2018: 2,020 days could trade. Each day: yesterday's RSI and close from dailySetup, the decision from getSignal with the 09:15 candle's open, buy at that open, then candle by candle — 15:15 square-off at that candle's open; else a touch of yesterday's close exits at that level; else a close through the 5-minute EMA21 exits at that close. This is a spot-point check — not a backtest page, and not rupees. No option prices, no slippage, no charges.

Does a minimum gap help? (spot points, before costs)

GAP_RUNAWAY_MIN_GAP_PTS skips gaps smaller than its value. Same check, three values:

A bigger minimum gap gives a few more winners in %, but a bigger gap is also a wider stop, so the average loser grows faster. On this data, 20 and 40 points did not make the average trade positive — it got worse per trade. These are spot points before costs; option results were not measured.

In plain words: about 3 trades in 10 won. Winners were bigger than losers, but not big enough: the average trade lost 10.41 NIFTY points before costs. As a rough idea only, at delta 0.5 on one lot of 65 that is about ₹338 a trade, and brokerage, the spread and time decay would come on top. Is it just bad luck? Drawing 375 trades at random from these same 375, 20,000 times over, only 1.6% of the draws came out positive. So: as built (intraday, any gap), the rules did not have an edge on NIFTY.

12. What's still missing

  • It has never traded. No paper session and no live order, on NIFTY or on stocks.
  • No backtest page and not replayable — by your choice. The only test is the spot-point check in section 10.
  • The stock scanner has not been run on real stock data. Nothing above says whether it works on stocks.
  • The daily RSI can differ a little from TradingView. The EMA and the RSI are both started (“seeded”) from the first candles they see, so on a short history they read slightly differently from TradingView's. The engine refuses to decide below 100 daily candles and loads about 400 calendar days (roughly 270 sessions) so the seed has faded. Near the 90 / 10 line, a small difference can still flip a day.
  • Option prices, slippage and charges are not in the numbers. Section 10 is NIFTY spot points. A real option moves less than spot (delta), loses value through the day, and every trade pays brokerage and the spread.
  • 5-minute candles hide the order of the first move. When the first candle touches both the stop and a lower/higher price, the check counts it as a stop. A real tick feed decides that differently on some days.
  • Intraday only. The video holds positions for days; this build closes everything at 15:15, by your choice. The video's version was not tested here.
  • The result on the data is negative. −10.41 points a trade before costs, every year negative at the default settings, and raising the minimum gap to 20 or 40 points did not turn it positive (section 10). Paper-trading it as it stands will most likely show the same.
