NIFTY OPTIONS — Price Action Strategy

The bot waits for one of four classic chart shapes, waits again for the breakout to be re-tested, and only then buys one CE or PE.

In one minute

  1. The bot watches the NIFTY 50 index on 5-minute candles. A candle is one bar that shows where price opened, closed, and its high and low in those 5 minutes.
  2. It looks for four chart shapes only: Double Bottom (W) and Ascending Triangle mean "up" — it buys a CE (call option, gains when NIFTY rises). Double Top (M) and Descending Triangle mean "down" — it buys a PE (put option, gains when NIFTY falls).
  3. A shape is complete when a candle breaks the key line (the neckline or the flat side of the triangle) with a body of at least 5 points.
  4. It does not buy the breakout. It waits up to 4 candles for price to come back near the line (within 10 points) and close on the breakout side. That is the retest. It buys at that candle's close.
  5. The stop-loss (the price where we accept we are wrong) goes just past the pattern, but never closer than 8 or further than 25 points from entry.
  6. When the trade is up about ₹300, the stop moves to the entry price (breakeven). At every candle close the stop also trails up to the latest swing low (CE) or down to the latest swing high (PE). There is no fixed target.
  7. New trades only from 09:21 to 14:30. Anything open is sold at 15:20. Daily brakes: 30 trades, ₹2,000 loss.

1 How to read the charts in this guide

Every chart below is drawn like a TradingView chart. All of them are made-up examples. Their numbers were produced by running the bot's real rules over the candles you see, so every BUY and EXIT sits where the code would put it.

What the marks mean

  • Green candle: closed higher than it opened. Red candle: closed lower.
  • The thin line above and below a candle (the wick) shows the highest and lowest price in those 5 minutes.
  • ▲ BUY = we bought. ▼ EXIT = we sold.
  • Yellow dot = the breakout candle. Grey dots = the twin lows or highs of a pattern.
  • Orange dashed line = our stop-loss while we hold. Watch it step up (CE) or down (PE).
  • Shaded column = the time we hold the option.

Points and rupees

All prices are NIFTY index points, not option prices. The option the bot buys is at-the-money (ATM) — the strike nearest to NIFTY. It moves roughly 0.55 rupee per NIFTY point, and one NIFTY lot is 65 units. So a 10-point NIFTY move is about ₹350 on one lot before costs. The rupee figures in captions use this rough rule and subtract about ₹50 of charges.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: NIFTY falls to 24,065, bounces, and falls again to 24,069 — two lows only 4 points apart. That is a Double Bottom (W). The 11:35 candle closes above the neckline (24,126). The bot waits. The next candle dips back toward the line and closes above it — the retest — so it buys a CE at 24,134. The stop (orange) starts at 24,109, jumps to breakeven, then steps up under each new swing low. A dip at 13:40 touches the trailed stop: EXIT 24,193, about +59 points (roughly ₹2,059 on one lot). Why it matters: this one chart shows every rule in order. Most days have no such shape, and the bot does not trade.

3 Step 1 — The bot marks the highs and lows

Every pattern is built from small peaks and valleys. So first the bot finds them.

  1. At each candle close, the bot looks back over the last 30 candles (about 2½ hours).
  2. A candle is a swing high if its high is above the high of the candle just before it and the candle just after it.
  3. A candle is a swing low if its low is below the low of the candle just before it and the candle just after it.
  4. The newest candle can never be a swing point yet — it needs a candle after it.
  5. Two levels count as "equal" when they are within 12 points of each other. Markets rarely hit the exact same price twice.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a wavy morning. Red dots are swing highs, green dots are swing lows. The shaded area is the 30-candle look-back. The highs (24,186, 24,154, 24,193, 24,144, 24,182) never repeat within 12 points of the one before, so there is no flat ceiling and no M. The last two lows, 24,092 and 24,096, are within 12 points — a possible W — but price never closes above the high between them, so nothing happens. Why it matters: a shape alone is not a trade. The bot needs the breakout candle too. The same swing points later move the trailing stop.

4 Step 2 — The four patterns

These four shapes are the only entry signals. Each one can be switched off on its own in Settings. The bot always uses the last two swing highs and the last two swing lows it found.

PatternShapeThe breakout candle must…We buy
Double Bottom (W)Two swing lows within 12 pts, at least 5 candles apartopen at or below the neckline and close above itCE
Double Top (M)Two swing highs within 12 pts, at least 5 candles apartopen at or above the neckline and close below itPE
Ascending TriangleTwo swing highs within 12 pts (flat ceiling) and the second swing low higher than the firstopen at or below the ceiling (average of the two highs) and close above itCE
Descending TriangleTwo swing lows within 12 pts (flat floor) and the second swing high lower than the firstopen at or above the floor (average of the two lows) and close below itPE
One extra check for all four

The breakout candle's body must be at least 5 points. That is the only extra check. There is no RSI, no ADX and, unless you switch on the trend filter, no moving average. If two shapes appear on the same candle, the bot checks them in this order and takes the first: Double Top, Double Bottom, Ascending Triangle, Descending Triangle.

Pattern 1 — Double Bottom (W) → CE

Sellers push price down to a level twice and fail twice. Buyers defended that floor. When price climbs over the peak between the two lows (the neckline), the "W" is complete.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: lows at 23,946 and 23,953 — 6 points apart, inside the 12-point band, and 11 candles apart. The neckline is the highest high between them, 24,002. The 11:20 candle closes above it (yellow dot). The 11:25 candle dips back near the line and closes above it, so the bot buys a CE at 24,006. The trail later exits at 24,047 (+41 pts). Why it matters: the two lows must be close in price and spread out in time — two dips one candle apart are just noise.

Pattern 2 — Double Top (M) → PE

The mirror image. Buyers push to a ceiling twice and fail. When price drops under the valley between the two highs, the "M" is complete.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: highs at 24,396 and 24,392 (4 pts apart). The neckline is the lowest low between them, 24,337. The 11:20 candle closes below it. The next candle pokes back up near the line and closes below it — the retest — so the bot buys a PE at 24,334. The stop starts 25 points above (24,359) because the twin highs are too far away, then steps down over each new swing high. EXIT 24,296, +38 pts. Why it matters: for a PE, "in our favour" means down — the stop moves down, never up.

Pattern 3 — Ascending Triangle → CE

A flat ceiling with rising floors. Sellers hold the same price, but buyers step in higher each time. The squeeze usually breaks upward.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: two highs at 24,184 and 24,185 make a flat ceiling (average 24,184). The lows rise from 24,116 to 24,144. The 11:25 candle closes above the ceiling; the next one retests it and the bot buys a CE at 24,190. EXIT on the trail at 24,223 (+33 pts). Why it matters: the stop for a triangle sits under the latest rising low — here that is 49 points away, so the 25-point cap applies.

Pattern 4 — Descending Triangle → PE

A flat floor with falling ceilings. Buyers defend the same price, but sellers push in lower each time. The squeeze usually breaks downward.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: lows at 24,209 and 24,207 form a flat floor (average 24,208). Highs fall from 24,276 to 24,250. The 11:25 candle breaks below the floor; the retest candle closes back under it and the bot buys a PE at 24,200. EXIT 24,163 (+37 pts). Why it matters: triangles have no minimum gap between the two touches — only the double patterns need 5 candles.

A breakout candle that is too small

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a good W (lows 24,047 and 24,050, neckline 24,101). But the candle that crosses the neckline at 11:20 has a body of only 3 points. The rule needs 5. The next candle is already above the neckline when it opens, so it cannot be a breakout candle either. Why it matters: no breakout is recorded, so there is nothing to retest and no trade all day.

5 Step 3 — Wait for the retest, then buy

Many breakouts snap straight back. So the bot does not buy the breakout candle. It parks the breakout and waits for proof.

  1. A breakout is found. The bot remembers the side (CE or PE), the line, and where the stop would go. It does not buy.
  2. On each of the next candles it asks three questions, in this order:
Question (CE example; PE is the mirror)If yes
Did the candle close more than 10 points below the line?Breakout failed — forget it.
Did the candle's low come within 10 points of the line (or below it) and close above the line?Retest — buy a CE at this candle's close.
Have we already waited 4 candles?Too slow — forget it.

A waiting breakout is never bought after the entry window closes — the bot stops checking at 14:30. In the charts above every winning trade entered on the retest candle — the very next candle after the breakout.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a W breaks out above 24,101 at 11:20. The very next candle closes at 24,084 — more than 10 points back below the line (red dashed). The bot forgets the breakout. Price keeps falling. Why it matters: buying the breakout candle here would have been a loss. This is the trap the retest rule avoids.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: the breakout at 11:20 is strong. Price never comes back into the 10-point retest zone (purple). After 4 candles the bot gives up at 11:40 and logs "Retest not seen in 4 candles". Why it matters: this is the cost of being careful — some good moves are missed. The strategy accepts that on purpose.

Patterns on 5-minute charts are often tall, so in practice the 25-point cap is used a lot. Every trade chart in this guide hits that cap. The stop is watched on every price tick of NIFTY, not only at candle close. When it is touched, the bot sells at once.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a textbook W and a clean retest — BUY CE at 24,106. The pattern's own stop (grey line, 24,046) is about 60 points away, so the stop is pulled in to 24,081 (25 points). Price never gains enough for breakeven. It slides and touches the stop at 11:50: −25 points, about ₹944 lost on one lot. Then the bot pauses for 2 candles (amber). Why it matters: losing trades are normal. The cap decides how much each one costs.

7 Step 5 — How a trade ends

There is no profit target. The stop does all the work: it starts at the pattern, jumps to breakeven, then trails behind the swings.

What happensWhen it is checkedPlain words
Stop hitEvery tickNIFTY touches the current stop — first stop, breakeven stop or trailed stop. The trade is sold.
BreakevenEvery tickThe first time the trade's best profit reaches ₹300, the stop moves to the NIFTY entry price +1 point (CE) or −1 point (PE). It only moves if that is tighter than the current stop.
Swing trailEvery candle closeThe stop moves to the latest swing low (CE) or swing high (PE) — but only if that level is tighter than the current stop and still on the right side of the price. It never loosens. It runs from the start of the trade, with or without breakeven.
Square-offClockAt 15:20 (10 minutes before the 15:30 stop time) any open trade is sold.

₹300 is small. With one lot it is about 9–10 NIFTY points of move, so breakeven usually arms within a candle or two of a good entry.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: BUY CE at 24,106. The 11:35 candle pushes the profit past ₹300, so the stop jumps from 24,081 to 24,107 (entry + 1). Price then fades back and touches it: EXIT 24,107. Why it matters: without breakeven this trade would have kept falling toward the 25-point stop. Instead it ends about flat — a small loss of roughly ₹14 from costs only.

The overview chart in Section 1 shows the swing trail in full: the stop moved to 24,135 (breakeven), then to 24,148, 24,169, 24,193 as new swing lows formed, and the trade ended when price dipped to the last one.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a W in the afternoon. BUY CE at 24,106 at 14:20. Price climbs smoothly, with no pullback deep enough to make a new swing low, so the stop stays at breakeven. At 15:20 the bot sells anyway: EXIT 24,156 (+50 pts). Why it matters: the bot never holds an option overnight.

Things this strategy does not have

No profit target, no time-stop, no partial booking, no "premium" stop. The all-strategy premium profit-lock in Settings is not used by this strategy's paper engine. Exits are only: stop (first, breakeven or trailed) and the 15:20 square-off.

8 Filters that can block a trade

Even a perfect pattern and retest can be refused. Here is every check, in plain words.

Time window: 09:21 to 14:30

The bot only looks for entries at candle closes between 09:21 and 14:30. In practice the first check is when the 09:20 candle closes (09:25) and the last is when the 14:20 candle closes (14:25). A breakout still waiting for its retest at 14:30 is never bought.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a neat W forms after lunch, and the breakout candle closes at 14:35. The entry window is already shut (red shade), so the bot does not even look. Why it matters: late trades have little time left before the 15:20 square-off.

Optional trend filter (off by default)

Switch on PA_TREND_FILTER_ENABLED and the bot reads the trend from a 20-candle EMA of closes:

  • Close above the EMA → trend is UP. Close below → DOWN. (An optional "flat band" around the EMA counts as neither.)
  • Triangles must agree with the trend: Ascending Triangle CE only in an UP trend, Descending Triangle PE only in a DOWN trend.
  • Double Top / Bottom must sit at the edge of the recent range: the twin high must be within 12 points of the highest swing high in the last 30 candles (and the twin low within 12 points of the lowest swing low).

Example — not a real trade
What you see: NIFTY rallies, then makes a small Descending Triangle — a flat floor near 24,387 under highs of 24,413, 24,406 and 24,400. The 11:10 candle closes below the floor at 24,380. But the close is still above the 20-EMA (blue, about 24,364), so the trend reads UP and the PE is blocked: "Descending Triangle PE blocked — trend bias UP". Why it matters: price then rallies to new highs. (With the filter off, the bot would have parked this breakdown — and here the retest rule would also have dropped it, because the next candles closed back above the floor.)

Optional VIX filter (off by default)

Switch on PA_VIX_ENABLED and the bot will not open a trade while India VIX is above 20. The check happens after the retest says "buy". If VIX data cannot be fetched, the default is to block (VIX_FAIL_MODE=closed). It is skipped in replay simulations.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a W, a breakout and a clean retest at 11:25 — the rules say "BUY CE". The lower panel shows India VIX at 22.4, above the 20 line, so the entry is refused. The waiting breakout is used up, so there is no second chance. Why it matters: on jumpy days option prices are expensive and stops get hit more. The cost: sometimes you miss a good move, like here.

10 A no-trade day

Most of the time the bot is waiting. That is normal.

Example — not a real trade
What you see: a full choppy morning. Swing highs at 24,250, 24,226, 24,262, 24,229, 24,265, 24,234 and swing lows at 24,186, 24,161, 24,197, 24,166, 24,203, 24,172. 11 Worked example, step by step

The overview chart from Section 1, told as a story. All numbers are from that example.

  1. 10:05 — a swing low at 24,065.
  2. 11:10 — a second swing low at 24,069. That is 4 points from the first (inside 12) and 13 candles later (at least 5). The highest point between them is 24,126 — the neckline.
  3. 11:35 candle — opens below the neckline, closes above it at 24,142, with a body over 5 points. Breakout recorded. No buy yet.
  4. 11:40 candle — dips to 24,124 (within 10 points of the neckline) and closes at 24,134, above it. Retest! Buy one lot of the ATM CE.
  5. Stop: the pattern says average low − 3 = 24,064. That is 70 points away — more than 25 — so the stop is pulled in to 24,109.
  6. 11:45 — the trade's profit passes ₹300. Stop moves to entry + 1 = 24,135. The trade can no longer become a real loss.
  7. 12:20 close — a new swing low is confirmed. Stop moves up to 24,148.
  8. 12:50 close — a new swing low is confirmed. Stop moves up to 24,169.
  9. 13:20 close — a new swing low is confirmed. Stop moves up to 24,193.
  10. 13:40 — price dips and touches 24,193. Sold. Result: +59 NIFTY points, roughly ₹2,059 on one lot after costs.
  11. Because the exit was a stop (a trailed one), the bot now pauses 2 candles before it looks for the next setup.

12 Paper, Live and Backtest

All three use the same pattern engine. Paper is the reference: when the others differ, paper is treated as correct.

Paper — /pa-paper

Live NIFTY ticks, pretend money. Option prices come from real live quotes. Trades are saved to ~/trading-data and an open position survives a restart. Paper entries are funded from the shared Fyers capital pool.

Live — /pa-live

Real Fyers orders. A real order needs all of these: PA_ENABLED=true, LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN=false, PA_LIVE_ENABLED=true and PA_LIVE_DRY_RUN=false. Otherwise the order is only logged ("dry run"). A hard stop-loss order is placed at the broker with each real entry. /pa-live-harness is another way to run live: it runs the paper engine and copies each paper decision to the broker, so live matches paper by design.

Backtest — /pa-backtest

Past candles from Fyers. There are no old option prices, so the option is estimated: it moves 0.55 × the NIFTY move (delta) and loses about 10 points a day to time decay (theta). A signal fills at the next candle's open, with 1.5 points of slippage each way by default. If a candle gaps past the stop, the fill is at the worse gap price.

14 Good to know

A safe way to start

  1. Run /pa-backtest and /pa-pattern-backtest to see each pattern's history.
  2. Paper-trade for at least two weeks on /pa-paper.
  3. Check one recorded day in /replay and confirm Paper and Live make the same calls.
  4. Only then turn off the dry-run switches.

If you want to tune it

  • Fewer, cleaner patterns: lower PA_CHART_PATTERN_TOL or raise PA_MIN_BODY.
  • More entries: raise PA_RETEST_TOL_PTS or PA_RETEST_MAX_WAIT.
  • Risk per trade: change PA_MAX_SL_PTS / PA_MIN_SL_PTS.
  • Tune only on trades made after the last rule change.

The whole strategy in six lines

  1. Four shapes only: W and Ascending Triangle → CE; M and Descending Triangle → PE.
  2. The breakout candle needs a 5-point body.
  3. Buy on the retest, never on the breakout.
  4. Stop past the pattern, squeezed to 8–25 points.
  5. Breakeven at ₹300, then trail behind swing points. No target.
  6. Window 09:21–14:30, sell all at 15:20, stop for the day at −₹2,000.
