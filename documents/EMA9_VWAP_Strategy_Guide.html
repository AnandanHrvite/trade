⚙ 1. The idea in one minute

Look at NIFTY on a 5-minute chart. Draw two things on it.

  1. A fast line. It is the average of the last 9 closing prices, so it hugs the price.
  2. A channel around the day's fair price. It shows the normal range for today.

Most of the time the fast line stays inside the channel. That is a quiet market. Now and then it breaks out. A break-out usually means buyers or sellers have taken charge.

  • Fast line breaks out of the top → buyers in charge → the bot buys a CE (a call option, which gains when NIFTY rises).
  • Fast line breaks out of the bottom → sellers in charge → the bot buys a PE (a put option, which gains when NIFTY falls).

There is no fixed target. The bot stays in while the move lasts. It gets out when the move shows signs of ending.

What you see: a whole morning. The yellow fast line stays inside the purple channel until late morning. At the 11:55 candle it closes above the top edge, so the bot buys a CE at 24,542. Price climbs. At the 13:25 candle a red candle closes below the lows of the two candles before it. That is the "sharp reversal" exit, at 24,584. The trade made about +42 NIFTY points. Why it matters: this is the whole strategy on one screen — wait, break out, ride, get out.

📈 3. The two lines on the chart

The fast line (EMA 9)

It is a smooth average of the last 9 candle closes. Recent candles count more. So it turns fast and stays close to price. It is the line that does the crossing.

The channel (VWAP ± 1 sigma)

  1. From 09:15, the bot takes each candle's typical price (HLC3).
  2. It averages them. That is the fair-value line (the dotted middle line).
  3. It measures how spread out they are. That is one sigma.
  4. Top edge = fair value + 1 sigma. Bottom edge = fair value − 1 sigma.

The channel starts again every morning at 09:15. Yesterday does not count. On a calm day the channel is narrow. On a busy day it gets wider.

What you see: a quiet morning. The channel starts as a single line at 09:15. By 09:45 it is about 10 points wide. By 11:15 it is about 20 points wide. The yellow fast line wanders but never closes outside the channel, so there is no trade. The grey area before 10:30 is the time when no new trades are allowed anyway. Why it matters: most of the day looks like this. Doing nothing is normal.

This VWAP will not match TradingView's numberA normal VWAP gives more weight to candles with more volume. This one gives every candle the same weight and never reads volume. The reason: the live NIFTY tick feed carries no index volume, but past data does. A volume-based formula made Paper and Backtest draw different channels for the same day. Equal weight is the only formula every mode can compute the same way. So compare the crosses with TradingView, not the exact line values.
The first candle of the day is skippedAt 09:15 there is no earlier candle from today to compare with. The bot reports "warming up" and waits. It never compares today's fast line with yesterday's channel.

▶ 4. When the bot buys

One event starts a trade: the fast line breaks out of the channel. The bot checks it when each 5-minute candle closes. It compares two candles in a row.

CE
Buy a CE
Last candle: the fast line was at or below the top edge. This candle: it closed above the top edge.
was inside → now above the top
PE
Buy a PE
Last candle: the fast line was at or above the bottom edge. This candle: it closed below the bottom edge.
was inside → now below the bottom

A line that was already outside does not count. It must step out on this candle.

CE example, close up

What you see: the same day as Section 1, zoomed in. The purple dot marks the 11:50 candle: the fast line (24,500.27) is just under the top edge (24,500.86). On the 11:55 candle the fast line (24,508.64) closes above the top edge (24,504.70). The lower panel shows the gap between them. It crosses zero right there. The bot buys a CE at the close, 24,542. Why it matters: "was inside, now outside" is the whole signal.

PE example

What you see: price slides lower. On the 11:35 candle the fast line closes just below the bottom edge, so the bot buys a PE at 24,707. NIFTY keeps falling. At 13:15 a green candle closes above the highs of the two candles before it. That is the PE version of the sharp-reversal exit. The bot sells at 24,613, about +94 points in its favour. Why it matters: the PE side is the exact mirror of the CE side.

STRONG and WEAK break-outs

The bot measures how far the fast line went past the edge, in sigma. That way the ruler fits today's market.

  • At least 0.25 sigma past the edge → STRONG.
  • Less than that → WEAK.

By default both kinds trade. The grade is written in the log. If you switch on the strength filter, WEAK break-outs are skipped.

What you see: a very quiet day. At 10:35 the fast line pokes 0.11 points above the top edge. Sigma is about 1 point, so that is 0.11 sigma → WEAK. The trade goes nowhere and the line falls back inside at 10:45, so the bot sells for about 0 points. At 11:40 the fast line breaks out by 0.95 points with sigma at 3.8, which is 0.25 sigma → STRONG. That trade ends at 13:05 on a reversal candle, about +23 points. The lower panel shows the gap between the fast line and the top edge. Why it matters: with the strength filter ON, only the second trade would have been taken.

Only between 10:30 and 14:30

The channel starts at 09:15. But new trades are allowed only when the signal candle closes between 10:30 and 14:30. The first hour is often noisy. After 14:30 there is too little time left. A trade that is already open can stay open after 14:30.

The time is taken from the candle, not from the wall clock. The 10:25 candle closes at 10:30, so it is the first one that may trade. Paper, Live and Backtest all use this same rule.

What you see: at 09:45 the fast line closes just above a very narrow channel. That is a real CE signal, but it is before 10:30, so the bot skips it (red dot) and only writes it in the skip log. Later, at 11:35, a cleaner break-out happens inside the window and the bot buys a CE at 25,038. At the right edge (12:30) the trade is still open at 25,077. Why it matters: the same signal is judged by the clock. Early signals are ignored on purpose.

Exactly when the buy happens

By default the bot buys at the close of the candle that crossed. Two optional switches change this. Both are OFF.

SettingDefaultWhen turned ON
EMA9VWAP_INTRACANDLE_ENTRYfalseBuy as soon as the cross shows up inside a candle that is still forming, without waiting for the close.
EMA9VWAP_CONFIRM_CANDLE_ENABLEDfalseWait. The next candle must trade past the signal candle's close (above it for a CE, below it for a PE). Then buy. If the next candle does not do it, the signal is dropped.
What the bot writes in the logEach entry has a line like: EMA9 24508.64 crossed ABOVE VWAP top 24504.7 (VWAP 24491.98 +1σ) | break 3.94pt = STRONG. It shows the fast line, the edge, the fair-value line and the grade.

The bot buys one lot of the option nearest the money, using the shared strike settings.

📍 5. When the bot sells

There is no fixed target. The bot sells on the first of these. Some are checked on every price tick, some when a candle closes.

1
Shared profit lock and breakeven stop — every tick, on the option price
Once the premium is up 5%, the trade may not fall back below the price paid. Once it is up 8%, a rising floor keeps most of the gain. These two rules are global: they apply to every strategy in the app. Both are ON by default.
BREAKEVEN_STOP_ENABLED = true · PROFIT_LOCK_ENABLED = true
2
Sharp reversal candle — at candle close (ON by default)
CE: a red candle that closes below the lows of both of the two candles before it. PE: a green candle that closes above the highs of both of the two candles before it.
EMA9VWAP_REVERSAL_EXIT_ENABLED = true
3
Fast line back inside the channel — at candle close
CE: the fast line closes back below the top edge. PE: it closes back above the bottom edge. The reason for the trade is gone.
CE: EMA9 back below top · PE: EMA9 back above bottom
4
Square-off at 15:15
Anything still open is sold when the candle ending at 15:15 closes. At 15:30 the engine stops for the day; if anything were somehow still open, it is sold then.
EMA9VWAP_EOD_EXIT_TIME = 15:15 · EMA9VWAP_STOP_TIME = 15:30

At candle close the order is: reversal first, then the fast-line exit, then the 15:15 check.

Exit by the fast line falling back inside

What you see: at 11:50 the fast line closes above the top edge and the bot buys a CE at 24,171. The move runs for two hours. No candle is sharp enough to trigger the reversal exit. At the 13:55 candle the fast line (24,216.82) closes back below the top edge (24,217.69). The bot sells at 24,206, about +35 points. Why it matters: this is the "the burst is over" exit. It is slower than the reversal exit, because a 9-candle average takes time to turn.

Exit by a sharp reversal candle

The charts in Sections 1 and 4 (PE example) both end this way. In Section 1 the exit candle closed at 24,584.32. The two candles before it had lows of 24,586.24 and 24,586.02. It was red and closed below both, so the bot sold.

Why the reversal exit is ONThe fast line is slow to fold back. Without the reversal exit, a trade often gives back a large part of its gain before the line comes back inside. If you prefer to hold only to the channel signal, set EMA9VWAP_REVERSAL_EXIT_ENABLED=false.

Exit by the profit lock (shared rule)

What you see: the option premium after a CE buy, on a 1-minute chart. The bot paid ₹146.90 at 12:01. At ₹158.65 (+8%) the profit lock arms. The pink dashed floor then rises with the best price. The premium peaks at ₹175.96 (about +20%). Below a +20% gain the floor keeps 85% of the gain, so it sits at ₹171.60. At 12:23 the premium drops to ₹171.54 and the bot sells. Why it matters: the lock works on the option price, tick by tick. It often ends a trade before any chart rule does.

Best gain so farFloor keepsExample (paid ₹100)

+8% to +20%85% of the gain, and never less than +5%peak ₹110 → floor ₹108.50

Exit by the breakeven stop (shared rule)

What you see: a PE bought at ₹150.90. The premium rises to ₹160.13, about +6%. That is past +5%, so the breakeven stop is armed. It is short of +8% (₹162.97), so the profit lock never arms. The premium then slides back. At 11:25 it touches ₹150.70, at or below the price paid, and the bot sells. Why it matters: a small winner is not allowed to turn into a real loss. The exit is about flat, but charges still apply.

Exit at 15:15

What you see: an afternoon break-out. At 13:55 the fast line closes above the top edge and the bot buys a CE at 24,427. The move keeps going. No reversal candle and no fold-back happens. When the 15:10 candle closes (at 15:15) the bot sells at 24,452, about +25 points. The grey area is after the square-off. Why it matters: the bot never holds overnight, even when the trade is still working.

🛡 7. Filters that block a trade

Even with a clean break-out, the bot checks these before buying. If any one says no, there is no trade. The skip is written to the skip log with its reason.

  1. Time window (Section 4): signal candle must close between 10:30 and 14:30.
  2. VIX filter.
  3. Daily loss limit and the all-strategy loss cap.
  4. Losing-streak brake (off by default).
  5. Trades-per-day limit.
  6. Bid-ask spread check on the option.
  7. Same-side pause and opposite-side cool-off.
  8. Capital check: the shared money pool must be able to pay for the option.

VIX filter

VIX is the market's fear gauge. Above 20 the bot takes no new trades. The filter is ON by default (it follows the global switch).

What you see: at 12:00 the fast line (24,277.01) closes above the top edge (24,274.77). That is a CE signal inside the time window. But the lower panel shows VIX at about 20.9, above the red line at 20. The bot skips it (red dot). Why it matters: on jumpy days options are expensive and moves reverse fast. The bot sits those days out.

The "STRONG only" VIX band does nothing hereOther strategies allow only STRONG signals when VIX is between 16 and 20. This strategy has the keys for it, but the paper engine always reports its signal as STRONG to the VIX check. So in practice only the hard limit (20) blocks. If VIX data cannot be read, entries are blocked (VIX_FAIL_MODE=closed).

Opposite-side cool-off

After a trade closes, the bot may not buy the opposite side for 3 candles. This stops it from flipping CE → PE into the same messy candle. It does not apply after the 15:15 square-off or a manual exit.

What you see: a very quiet morning with a thin channel. At 10:45 the bot buys a CE at 24,607. At 10:55 a red candle closes below the two lows before it, so the bot sells at 24,594, about −13 points. Two candles later, at 11:05, the fast line (24,594.33) closes below the bottom edge (24,594.54). That is a PE signal. The cool-off lasts 15 minutes from the exit, until 11:15, so the PE is skipped (red dot). After that the fast line stays below the channel. It never steps out again, so no PE trade happens at all. Why it matters: the same red candle that ends a CE often looks like a PE signal. The cool-off waits for the dust to settle.

A choppy day, and the loss brakes

What you see: NIFTY swings up and down by 50–90 points without a trend. The bot buys a CE at 10:45 and loses about 25 points on a reversal at 11:00. It buys a PE at 11:35 and loses 14 points at 11:40. It buys a PE again at 12:55 and loses about 7 points at 13:00. All three break-outs were WEAK and all three ended on reversal candles. Why it matters: choppy days are where this strategy loses. The brakes below limit the damage.

BrakeSettingDefaultWhat it does
Daily loss limitEMA9VWAP_MAX_DAILY_LOSS₹5,000Once today's loss for this strategy reaches ₹5,000, no new trades today.
Trades per dayEMA9VWAP_MAX_DAILY_TRADES20No new trades after 20 in a day.
Losing streakEMA9VWAP_MAX_CONSEC_LOSSES0 (off)Set it to N. After N losses in a row, the bot stops new trades for the rest of the day. A win resets the count. (On 5-minute candles a 4-candle pause also starts, but the stop-for-the-day rule wins.)
All-strategy capPORTFOLIO_MAX_DAILY_LOSS0 (off)When set, once the whole app's loss for the day reaches it, no strategy may open a new trade.

Spread and open-interest checks

  • Spread: before buying, the bot reads the option's bid and ask. If the gap is more than 2 points (MAX_BID_ASK_SPREAD_PTS), it skips the trade. This runs in Paper and Live on the real market.
  • Open interest: optional (EMA9VWAP_OI_ENABLED, off). It blocks a buy that fights a clear build-up of positions against you. It also needs the app-wide OI switch.

🧮 8. A worked example with numbers

This follows the Section 1 chart, step by step, with default settings.

11:50  Candle closes.
         Top edge          = 24,500.86
         Fast line (EMA 9) = 24,500.27   → still inside. No trade.

11:55  Candle closes at 24,542.13.
         Fair value        = 24,491.98
         Sigma             = 12.72
         Top edge          = 24,491.98 + 12.72 = 24,504.70
         Fast line         = 24,508.64   → now ABOVE the top edge.
         Break = 24,508.64 − 24,504.70 = 3.94 points = 0.31 sigma → STRONG
         Time window: candle closes at 12:00 → inside 10:30–14:30 ✔
         VIX below 20 ✔   No cool-off ✔   Under the loss limit ✔
       → BUY one lot of a CE at the close.

12:00–13:20  NIFTY climbs to about 24,600. The fast line stays above the top edge.
       No reversal candle. Nothing to do.

13:25  Candle: open 24,588.13, close 24,584.32 (red).
         Lows of the two candles before it: 24,586.24 and 24,586.02
         Close is below both → sharp reversal exit.
       → SELL at the close. NIFTY gain = 24,584.32 − 24,542.13 = +42.19 points.

Roughly, the option gains a little over half of that, about 23 premium points. On one lot of 65 that is about ₹1,500 before costs. If the premium had risen 8% or more first, the profit lock could have ended the trade earlier, on the option price.

After this exit, a PE signal in the next 3 candles would be skipped by the cool-off.

⚙ 9. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

Paper — /ema9vwap-paper

Real live prices, pretend money. This is the reference version: if Live or Backtest behaves differently, Paper is treated as correct. Trades are saved in your ~/trading-data folder, with a day-by-day log that also records the settings in use. If the app restarts while a trade is open, a small saved snapshot lets it warn you on start-up, so an open position is never forgotten.

Live — /ema9vwap-live

The same brain as Paper, plus real Zerodha orders. Real orders need all of these:

  1. EMA9VWAP_LIVE_ENABLED = true.
  2. The global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN = false (it is true by default).
  3. EMA9VWAP_LIVE_DRY_RUN not true. When true, this one strategy only logs the orders it would place.

Turn real orders on only after a recorded day shows Paper and Live making the same decisions in /replay.

Backtest — /ema9vwap-backtest

Runs the same rules over past days, using the same entry window, exits, cool-offs, VIX and loss brakes. Past data has no option prices, so the option-based rules (profit lock, breakeven, option-price stop) are turned into NIFTY-point equivalents. Each side of a trade is charged 1.5 points of slippage (BACKTEST_SLIPPAGE_PTS). Expect small differences from Paper.

Replay — /replay

Re-runs a recorded day tick by tick. Use the original settings to reproduce the day, or your current settings to test a change on real prices.

⚠ 11. Honest notes & limits

  • Choppy days hurt. The chop chart in Section 7 is the typical losing day. Several WEAK break-outs in a row is a warning sign.
  • The fair-value line is equal-weight on purpose. It will not equal TradingView's VWAP. Compare crosses, not levels.
  • The shared profit lock and breakeven stop change this strategy. It used to have no stop at all. Now most winners end on the option-price floor. Do not compare new results with old ones without keeping this in mind.
  • The "STRONG only" VIX band is not used by this strategy, even though the keys exist.
  • Backtest is an approximation of the option-price rules. It turns them into NIFTY points. Paper and Replay are the true test.
  • Do not tune on old trades made before a bug fix. Collect clean sessions first.
