EMA9_CROSS

When a 5-minute candle closes above the EMA9 line, buy a call. When a candle closes back below it, sell — and buy a put if that close is a fresh cross down. The first stop is the common premium stop every strategy uses.

1. In one minute

  • One line on the chart: EMA9 of the 5-minute closes.
  • Buy CE when a candle closes above EMA9 and the candle before it closed at or below it. Buy PE on the mirror.
  • Only on a finished candle — never in the middle of one. Strike: ATM. New entries 10:30–14:00 (10:00–14:00 while the Common Trade Window is on).
  • Exit when a candle closes back on the other side of EMA9. If that close is a fresh cross, flip to the other side on the same close.
  • The first stop is the common premium stop (and the profit lock, if it is on) from Settings → Common Rules.
  • After a stop-out, no new trade until the next fresh cross.

Read this first. This strategy has never traded — no paper sessions, no live orders, and there is no live mode for it. On the recorded data the backtest lost money (Section 6).

2. The entry

SidePrevious candleThis candle
CE (call)closed at or below EMA9closes above EMA9
PE (put)closed at or above EMA9closes below EMA9
  • A candle that only stays above EMA9 is not a cross. The bot waits for a real cross.
  • A close exactly on EMA9 is neither an entry nor an exit.
  • EMA9 is warmed up on the last 5 calendar days of candles, so it matches the chart from the first candle of the day.
  • Paper buys at the first tick after the candle closes. The backtest buys at that candle's close — the same moment.

4. The flip

On 25 Jun 2026 the 11:05 candle closed above EMA9 at 24189.95 and the bot bought a CE. The 12:20 candle closed at 24226.05, below EMA9: the CE was sold (+₹1,066.46) and, because that close was a fresh cross down, a PE was bought on the same close. The PE was sold when the 13:15 candle closed back above EMA9 at 24196.8 (+₹822.62).

Yellow line = EMA9. Buy and exit marks sit on the candle whose close made the decision. This run had the common wait-after-exit switched off, so the flip could happen.

The flip needs the common "Wait after any exit" rule off. That rule (EXIT_REENTRY_COOLDOWN_*, on by default, 2 candles) blocks every new entry for a while after any exit — including the flip. With it on, the bot exits and then waits for the next fresh cross. To allow flips for this strategy only, turn on its Own Common Rules in Settings and switch the wait off there.

5. Sideways days

An EMA9 cross happens all the time when the market goes sideways. On 1 Jul 2026 there were 15 trades and only 1 made money (−₹9,249 for the day, flips allowed).

Each small cross buys, and the next small cross sells at a loss. This is the main weakness of the rule.

Plain verdict: on this data the rule loses money either way. A typical trade lasts 3 candles and loses a few points: price crosses EMA9 and crosses back before the option can gain. The premium stop almost never fires (3 times) because the EMA9 exit comes first. Allowing flips doubles the trades and the loss.

9. What's still missing

  • It has never traded. No paper session, no live order.
  • There is no live mode.
  • Backtest rupees are simulated. Every trade starts at the same seed premium and moves 0.55 per NIFTY point.
  • On the data we have, the rules lose money. Collect clean paper sessions and check them on /replay before trusting any number here.
