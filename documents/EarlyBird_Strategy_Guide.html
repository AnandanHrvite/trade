Strategy 14 · Fyers · CASH EQUITY on F&O stocks · Plain-English edition

EarlyBird

Every strategy in this repo buys a NIFTY option. This one does not. EarlyBird reads the market's first fifteen minutes, decides from NIFTY alone whether today is a buying day or a selling day, and then buys — or short-sells — the actual shares of F&O stocks that opened the same way. The decision is made at 09:30 and the rest of the day only executes it.

Updated 2026-08-30  ·  plain-English edition  ·  every chart and every number below was produced by running the real engine over real cached NIFTY data

Read this first: this strategy has never traded — not once. No live order, no paper session, zero trades in existence. Everything measured in this guide comes from running src/strategies/early_bird.js over 2,118 cached NIFTY sessions. And there is a second, larger caveat that you must carry through the whole document: there is no cached per-stock data in this repo and the Fyers token is expired, so no stock could be fetched. Every "trade" figure below uses NIFTY itself standing in as if it were a stock. The index's opening candle does not behave like a single stock's opening candle, so those figures show that the machinery works end-to-end — they are not this strategy's expected profit or loss.

1. The idea in one minute

The first fifteen minutes of the trading day are the loudest fifteen minutes. Overnight news, foreign markets, and every order that queued up while the exchange was shut all land at once. By 09:30 that fight has produced a single candle, and EarlyBird's whole belief is that this one candle tells you which side won — and that whoever won the opening usually keeps pushing for the next few hours.

So the strategy asks three questions at 09:30 and then stops asking questions:

Did NIFTY print a one-sided opening candle?→ Which stocks opened the same way?→ Place a pending order just past each one's candle→ Stop just past the other end→ Everything closed by 1 pm

If NIFTY's opening candle is a strong green one, today is a buy day and the strategy only buys. If it is a strong red one, today is a short day and the strategy only short-sells. If the candle is indecisive — a small body, or a long wick showing the other side pushed back hard — there is no trading at all that day. That happens more than half the time.

One candle, one decision, one time. The engine reads exactly one bar per symbol per day — the 09:15–09:30 fifteen-minute candle — and never re-evaluates it. That is what "EarlyBird" means. There is no second look at 11:00 and no re-entry if a stock stops out.

2. Why this one is unlike every other strategy here

This matters enough to spell out, because almost every habit you have from the other guides is wrong here.

Strike / expiryChosen every tradeNone. There is no strike, no expiry, no premium and no lot size

The size warning that comes with cash equity. An option costs you the premium and no more. One hundred shares of a ₹3,000 stock is a ₹300,000 position, and five of those at once is one and a half million rupees of exposure. The strategy's rupee risk per stock is small — 100 shares times a stop of a few rupees — but the capital and margin required is not, and a short sale carries the extra obligation of having to buy the shares back. Read Safety limits before turning anything on.

4. Step 1 — What counts as a signal candle

The original strategy describes three drawings for a bullish opening candle: a full green body with no wicks at all; a green candle with a long lower wick and no upper wick; and a green candle with a small upper wick and a long lower wick. The bearish versions are the mirror images.

All three drawings share exactly one property, and the engine implements that property rather than the three pictures: the wick pointing against the move is small, and the body is real. Two numbers say it:

GREEN candle (close > open)
  upper wick (high - close)  ≤  30% of the candle's range   ← EARLYBIRD_MAX_OPPOSING_WICK_PCT
  body       (close - open)  ≥  40% of the candle's range   ← EARLYBIRD_MIN_BODY_PCT

RED candle (close < open)
  lower wick (open - low)    ≤  30% of the range
  body       (open - close)  ≥  40% of the range

Wicks are measured as a percentage of the candle's own range, never in rupees. That is what lets the identical rule work on a 60-point NIFTY bar and on a ₹900 stock bar — "30% of the range" means the same thing on both.

Why not "no wick at all", as drawing 1 shows? Because on real 15-minute data a literally wickless index candle is close to nonexistent. A rule that fires twice a year cannot be measured, tuned, or trusted. 30% and 40% is the engine's reading of "mostly one-directional", which is the thing all three drawings actually have in common. Both numbers are settings you can tighten toward zero if you want the strict picture.

Here is a real one. NIFTY's 09:15 candle on 28 March 2024 opened at 22163.60, ran to 22224.60 and closed at 22217.70 — a 61.00-point range with a 54.10-point body, so the body is 88.7% of the range and the upper wick is only 11.3%. Both tests pass comfortably.

A signal candle — 28 March 2024. The first bar of the day is green, almost all body, with a 6.90-point upper wick. The engine labels it BULLISH, small opposing wick (drawing 3), and everything for the rest of the day follows from it. Note that the market kept climbing to 22516 by mid-afternoon — but this strategy stops looking at 09:30.

5. Step 2 — NIFTY picks the side, and only the side

The very same shape test is applied to NIFTY's opening candle first. The answer is one of three things:

  • Green and it passes → today is a BUY day. The strategy may only buy stocks.
  • Red and it passes → today is a SHORT day. The strategy may only short-sell stocks.
  • It fails → no trading today. The scan does not even run.

That is the entire role of the index. NIFTY is a switch with three positions, and no money ever goes into it.

6. Step 3 — At least one stock has to agree

Having a direction is not enough. At 09:30 the engine fetches the opening 15-minute candle for every symbol in the chosen universe — EARLYBIRD_UNIVERSE, default FNO, roughly 220 names — and runs the identical shape test on each one. A stock is a candidate only if its own candle passes and points the same way as NIFTY. A green stock on a short day is discarded, however pretty its candle.

By default, one confirming stock is enough (EARLYBIRD_MIN_CONFIRMING_STOCKS). The original rule is simply "the stock has to make a similar pattern to NIFTY", so a single name that qualifies is reason enough to trade that name. Raising this number turns the setting into a market-breadth gate: "don't trade unless at least 20 stocks opened this way".

If more stocks confirm than EARLYBIRD_MAX_CONCURRENT allows (default 5), the engine sorts them by tightest stop first and takes the top five. With a fixed 100-share size, the tightest stop is simply the smallest rupee loss, and the cap has to choose somehow.

7. Step 4 — The 2% gap rule

A stock that opened far away from yesterday's close is reacting to its own news, not to the market's mood, and its opening candle means something different. So any stock whose 09:15 open is more than EARLYBIRD_MAX_GAP_PCT (default 2%) away from the previous day's closing price, in either direction, is dropped before its shape is even examined.

An unknown previous close is a refusal, not a pass. If the engine cannot fetch yesterday's close for a symbol, that symbol is dropped — it does not assume there was no gap. "We don't know the gap" is not the same as "there is no gap", and treating them the same is how a strategy quietly starts trading the exact stocks it was written to avoid.

8. Step 5 — Entry, stop and target

Every level is read off the stock's own signal candle — not NIFTY's — and every level is frozen at 09:30. Nothing moves for the rest of the day.

BUY day (LONG)
  entry  = signal candle HIGH + ₹5      ← EARLYBIRD_ENTRY_BUFFER_PTS
  stop   = signal candle LOW  - ₹5
  risk   = entry - stop
  target = entry + risk × 2             ← EARLYBIRD_TARGET_RR

SHORT day (SHORT)
  entry  = signal candle LOW  - ₹5
  stop   = signal candle HIGH + ₹5
  risk   = stop - entry
  target = entry - risk × 2

The ₹5 buffer is the "a little above the breakout candle" from the original rules, and it is applied to both ends — past the extreme you are entering on, and past the extreme you are wrong at.

The entry is a pending stop order. Nothing is bought at 09:30. The order sits there and fills the first time price trades through the level, and it fills at the level — not at whatever price a tick happened to print. That last detail is what keeps the backtest honest: a 40-rupee jump through the level would otherwise hand the simulation a fill the real market never offered.

Here is that day in full. Entry 22229.60 (the 22224.60 high plus 5), stop 22158.60 (the 22163.60 low minus 5), so the risk is 71.00 points and the target is 22229.60 + 142.00 = 22371.60.

A complete LONG — 28 March 2024. The pending order filled on the very next bar at 22229.60. The stop at 22158.60 was never approached. The target 22371.60 was reached on the 11:15 bar, which ran to 22373.50 — a 142.00-point gain, exactly 2R. NIFTY is standing in for a stock here, because no per-stock candles are cached in this repo; the mechanics are the strategy's, the instrument is not.

And here is the other kind of day. On 6 June 2025 NIFTY's opening candle was red and passed the test (body 48.0%, lower wick 15.5%), making it a short day. The short entry was 24718.25, the stop 24768.35, risk 50.10.

A complete SHORT that lost — 6 June 2025. Filled at 24718.25 on the 09:30 bar. Two bars later, at 10:00, price ran to 24817.10 and took out the stop at 24768.35. That is exactly −1R, and the market then climbed to 25029.50 by 11:45 — the stop did its job. The target 24618.05 was never in sight. Again, NIFTY standing in for a stock.

9. Step 6 — The big-candle stop

Sometimes the opening candle is enormous. A stop placed at its far wick would be so wide that a single trade risks far more than intended, and the 1:2 target would sit somewhere price will not reach before lunch. The original rules cover this with a note: if the breakout candle is too big, ignore the wick.

The engine's version: if the wick-to-wick risk exceeds EARLYBIRD_MAX_SL_PTS (default ₹60), the stop moves off the wick and onto the candle's body edge instead.

LONG   stop = min(open, close) - ₹5      ← the bottom of the body, not the low
SHORT  stop = max(open, close) + ₹5      ← the top of the body, not the high
It can only ever tighten. The body is always inside the wick, so this rule never widens a stop — and the engine additionally refuses to apply it if the body edge somehow came out no tighter. It is also applied before the target is computed, so a body-edge stop produces a correspondingly nearer target. The 1:2 is honoured against the risk actually taken, not against the risk you avoided.

A real example, 29 January 2025. NIFTY opened at 23026.75, spiked to 23050.05, fell to 22978.35 and closed at 22987.70 — a red candle that passes (body 54.5%, lower wick 13.0%), so a short day. The wick stop would be 23050.05 + 5 = 23055.05, which against the entry at 22973.35 is a risk of 81.70 — over the ₹60 limit. So the stop moves to the body top, 23026.75 + 5 = 23031.75, cutting the risk to 58.40.

The big-candle rule, and an order that never filled — 29 January 2025. Two things happen on this chart. First, the wick stop (81.70 risk) is rejected in favour of the body-edge stop (58.40). Second, the trade never happened at all: the short entry sat at 22973.35 and the lowest price in the entry window was 22976.50, on the 09:30 bar. Price then went straight up and the pending order was cancelled unfilled at 10:45. NIFTY standing in for a stock.

That "never filled" outcome is not rare and it is not a failure — it is the pending order doing its job. On the proxy measurement below, 18.9% of confirmed setups never triggered at all.

10. Step 7 — How a position ends

There are exactly three ways out. No trailing stop, no breakeven shift, no partial exits.

ExitWhenWhere it fills
TargetPrice reaches the 1:2 levelAt the target level
StopPrice reaches the stop levelAt the stop level — unless the bar opened beyond it, in which case at the open, because the better price was never available
1 pmEARLYBIRD_FORCED_EXIT, default 13:00At the market, whatever it is

Two time rules bracket the day. No new entry is taken after EARLYBIRD_ENTRY_END (default 10:45) — a pending order not triggered by then is cancelled. And everything still open is squared off at 13:00, win or lose.

When one bar could have done both. A single 15-minute bar can contain both the stop and the target. The bar cannot tell you which came first, so the backtest takes the stop — always. Assuming the good one arrived first is exactly how a backtest flatters itself. Same reasoning for a bar that opens beyond the stop: it fills at the open, never at the better level.

11. The days it refuses

More than half of all sessions produce no trade at all, and the reason is almost always one of two things. Both are worth seeing, because they are what the strategy is for — the whole premise is that an indecisive opening tells you nothing.

The body is too small. On 6 January 2025 NIFTY's opening candle had a 73.55-point range — a big, busy fifteen minutes — but it opened at 24045.80 and closed at 24036.15, a body of just 9.65 points, or 13.1% of the range. Price went everywhere and finished nowhere.

Refused for a small body — 6 January 2025. The engine's own words: body 13.12% of range < required 40%. And it was right to refuse in a useful way — the market then fell all day, so a naive red-candle reading would have been directionally lucky here, but on a candle that genuinely showed no conviction at 09:30.

The opposing wick is too long. On 14 January 2025 the opening candle was green and its body was 40.7% — enough on its own. But it had run to 23227.20 and closed at 23193.10, leaving a 34.10-point upper wick, 51.0% of the range. Buyers pushed, sellers pushed back harder, and by the close of the candle most of the gain was gone.

Refused for a long opposing wick — 14 January 2025. The engine's words: upper wick 51.01% of range > allowed 30% — sellers pushed back too hard. This is the test doing exactly what it is for: a candle that looks green in a list of closes but was a fight in reality.

C. The 1 pm exit is cutting most trades short

Look again at the exit mix: 62.9% of proxy trades ended because the clock struck 13:00, not because the strategy was right or wrong. Only 6.6% reached the 1:2 target. That is the shape of a system whose target is further away than its holding time allows.

Re-running the identical proxy with only EARLYBIRD_FORCED_EXIT changed from 13:00 to 15:30, and nothing else touched:

Forced exitTargetStopForcedWin rateAvg R
13:00 (the rule)6.6%30.4%62.9%50.2%+0.026R
15:30 (test only)15.2%40.9%43.9%45.6%+0.082R

Holding to the close roughly tripled proxy expectancy (0.082 ÷ 0.026 = 3.3×) — while lowering the win rate from 50.2% to 45.6%, because more trades got the time to reach their stop as well as their target. More losers, bigger winners, better net.

This is an observation, not a recommendation. The 1 pm square-off is the user's stated rule and the engine's default, and it stays that way. It is also measured on the proxy, so the 3.3× is not a number to bank on. What this section is for is naming EARLYBIRD_FORCED_EXIT as the highest-leverage tunable in the whole strategy, and pointing out that it should be tested on real stock data once a Fyers token is available — not changed on the strength of an index proxy.

13. What is deliberately not here

Most strategies in this repo accumulate filters. EarlyBird has none, on purpose — the original rules did not ask for them, and adding them would be inventing a different strategy. None of the following exists anywhere in the engine:

Not implementedWhy not
VIX gateNot in the rules. Every other strategy here has one; this one does not.
Open-interest gateNot in the rules — and this is cash equity, where option OI is not the relevant signal anyway.
RSI, ADX, moving averages, ATRNot in the rules. There is not one indicator calculation in the engine.
Volume filterNot in the rules.
Confirmation candleThe pending stop order is the confirmation — price has to trade through the level.
Multi-timeframe checkOne bar per symbol per day is the whole design.
Trailing stop / breakeven shiftThe stop is frozen for the life of the trade.
Partial exits / scaling outNot asked for, and partials would change the risk profile entirely.
Re-entryOne attempt per stock per day. A stock that stops out is done.
Risk-based position sizingA flat 100 shares, exactly as specified — not "shares such that risk is ₹X".

14. Safety limits

The strategy rules themselves have no risk management beyond the per-trade stop, so the day-breakers are separate settings layered on top. All of them are checked before a new entry.

SettingDefaultWhat it stops
EARLYBIRD_MAX_CONCURRENT5How many stocks may be held at once. Five × 100 shares of a mid-priced name is a large capital commitment.
EARLYBIRD_MAX_DAILY_TRADES5Total entries per day across all stocks.
EARLYBIRD_MAX_DAILY_LOSSES3Day ends after this many stop-outs.
EARLYBIRD_MAX_DAILY_LOSS₹5,000Day ends after this much loss.
EARLYBIRD_DAILY_PROFIT_LOCK0 (off)Optionally stop once a profit target for the day is banked.
EARLYBIRD_MAX_WEEKLY_LOSS0 (off)Optionally stop for the whole week.
The stop is the only per-trade protection, and it is a level, not a guarantee. There is no trailing stop and no breakeven move. If a stock gaps through the stop on the next print, the fill is at that print. The engine models this honestly — a bar that opens beyond the stop fills at the open — but honest modelling of a bad fill is still a bad fill.

15. Paper, Live, Backtest & Replay

All four modes call the same file, src/strategies/early_bird.js. Not one threshold comparison lives in a route. What differs between them is only where prices come from.

Why a quote poll instead of the shared socket

Every other strategy here rides the shared Fyers WebSocket, which carries NIFTY spot and the option strikes in play. EarlyBird cannot: it needs prices for up to five arbitrary equity symbols chosen fresh each morning, and pushing equity symbols through the shared index feed would change a connection that six other engines depend on.

So after 09:30 the engine polls REST quotes for the shortlist only — the handful of names with a pending order or an open position — in batches of EARLYBIRD_QUOTE_CHUNK (20) every EARLYBIRD_POLL_MS (2,000 ms). A quote older than EARLYBIRD_QUOTE_STALE_SEC (30s) stops being used for exit decisions and the failure is shown on the status page, so a position is never managed off a frozen price.

The 09:30 scan

Once per day, and only once, the engine fetches the opening candle and the previous daily close for every symbol in the universe. Two API calls per symbol, roughly 220 symbols, rate-limited to EARLYBIRD_SCAN_RPS (8/second) and EARLYBIRD_SCAN_RPM (180/minute), EARLYBIRD_SCAN_CONCURRENCY (4) at a time. A full scan takes about two minutes — comfortably inside the entry window that closes at 10:45.

Why the rate limits are not optional. Fyers answers an over-fast history request with no data — which is indistinguishable from a delisted stock. An over-fast scan does not error; it quietly reports most of the universe as having no opening candle, and the day looks like it had no confirming stocks. If a scan comes back near-empty, suspect the rate limit before you suspect the market.

Live is double-gated

A real order requires both EARLYBIRD_LIVE_ENABLED and the global LIVE_HARNESS_DRY_RUN being off, and EARLYBIRD_LIVE_DRY_RUN can keep it simulated regardless. All three ship in the safe position. This strategy has never placed an order of any kind, so the correct sequence is: paper sessions first, then a /replay diff showing paper and live decisions match on a recorded session, and only then the harness.

The cost model — a known gap, stated plainly

src/utils/charges.js has exactly two paths, options and futures. It has no cash-equity-intraday path. Equity intraday charges are structurally different — STT is 0.025% on the sell side only versus the far higher options rate, and the exchange transaction charge is an order of magnitude smaller — so reusing the options path would be wrong by a large factor in both directions.

Both routes therefore compute equity charges locally, from their own settings: EARLYBIRD_STT_PCT, EARLYBIRD_TXN_PCT, EARLYBIRD_SEBI_PCT, EARLYBIRD_STAMP_PCT and EARLYBIRD_BROKERAGE_PER_ORDER in Paper; the parallel EARLYBIRD_CHG_* keys in Backtest. If charges.js ever grows an equity path, both local implementations should be deleted in favour of it.

Backtest additionally applies EARLYBIRD_BT_SLIPPAGE_PTS (default ₹0.05) to both the entry and the exit — five paise a side, one tick on a liquid F&O cash name. That is deliberately a floor, not a claim. Raise it to find out whether an edge survives a wider touch.

17. What's still missing

An honest list of everything this guide could not establish. None of these are hypothetical concerns — each one is a specific thing that is untested or unmeasured today.

1. It has never traded. At all. Not one live order, not one paper session, no trade records anywhere on disk. Every number in this guide is a simulation of the rules over historical candles. The strategy is code that has never met a market.
2. There is no real stock data behind any lifecycle figure. This is the big one. The repo caches NIFTY candles only, and the Fyers token is expired, so no stock's opening candle could be fetched — not one. Every trade statistic in Section 12B (the 81.1% trigger rate, the 6.6%/30.4%/62.9% exit mix, the 50.2% win rate, the +0.026R) was produced by feeding NIFTY to the engine as though it were a stock. The index is an average of fifty names; it gaps less, trends more smoothly and has a completely different opening-candle character from any single stock. Those figures demonstrate the machinery runs end to end. They are not the strategy's expected performance and should never be quoted as such.
3. Every rupee figure is simulated. There is no live fill, no live slippage, no live rejection, no real short-sale borrow. The backtest applies a ₹0.05-per-side slippage floor, which is one tick on a liquid name and deliberately optimistic for anything less liquid.
4. The 1 pm exit truncates most trades — measured, unresolved. 62.9% of proxy trades ended at the bell rather than at a stop or target, and only 6.6% reached the 1:2. Moving the exit to 15:30 on the same proxy roughly tripled expectancy (+0.026R to +0.082R). That is presented as an observation, not a recommendation. 13:00 is the user's rule and remains the default; the finding is measured on the proxy, so it needs re-testing on real stock data before anyone touches EARLYBIRD_FORCED_EXIT.
5. charges.js has no equity-intraday path. It handles options and futures only. Both EarlyBird routes therefore compute equity charges locally, from their own EARLYBIRD_*_PCT / EARLYBIRD_CHG_* settings. That is two implementations of the same arithmetic that can drift apart, and neither has been reconciled against a real Fyers contract note.
6. The 220-symbol scan has never run against a live token. The rate limiting, the two-calls-per-symbol pattern, the ~2-minute completion time and the retry behaviour are all reasoned and coded but never exercised at full scale on a working Fyers session. The failure mode is specifically unpleasant: Fyers answers an over-fast request with "no data", which looks exactly like a delisted stock, so a mis-tuned scan reports an empty universe rather than an error.
7. Several operational unknowns that only live trading answers. Whether a cash-equity short is actually permitted and marginable on the account for every F&O name; whether five simultaneous positions fit the available capital; whether the quote poll keeps up when five names are moving at once; and whether a pending stop order placed at 09:30 behaves at the broker the way the engine assumes.

The order these should be resolved in

  1. Refresh the Fyers token — nothing else on this list can move until stock candles can be fetched.
  2. Re-run the backtest on real F&O stocks, and replace every Section 12B figure with a real one. Expect them to differ substantially.
  3. Run paper sessions and watch the 09:30 scan complete at full universe size.
  4. Diff paper against live-dry-run in /replay on a recorded session; they must agree before the harness is touched.
  5. Only then revisit EARLYBIRD_FORCED_EXIT and the other tunables, on real data.
