Strategy 14 · Fyers · F&O stocks (cash) and/or one NIFTY option · Simple-English edition

EarlyBird

EarlyBird looks at one candle only: the first 15 minutes of the day, from 09:15 to 09:30. If that candle is strong and one-sided, the bot trades in that direction. If it is not, the bot does nothing all day. All trades are closed by 1 pm.

Updated 2026-10-08 · every rule and default on this page was checked against src/strategies/early_bird.js and the paper route src/routes/earlyBirdPaper.js · all charts are made-up examples, not real trades

In one minute
  1. At 09:30 the bot reads NIFTY's first 15-minute candle.
  2. A strong green candle makes it a BUY day. A strong red candle makes it a SHORT day. Anything else means no trade today.
  3. What it trades depends on one setting, What To Trade:
    • stock (default) — it buys, or short-sells, the shares of F&O stocks whose own first candle looks the same as NIFTY's.
    • option — it buys one NIFTY CE on a BUY day, or one NIFTY PE on a SHORT day. No stocks are checked.
    • both — it does both, side by side.
  4. Entry is just past the candle's high (BUY) or low (SHORT). Stop is just past the other end. Target is 2 times the risk.
  5. No new trades after 10:45. Everything still open is closed at 13:00.
  6. The stop never moves. No trailing, no partial exits, no second try.

3. Step 1 — The signal candle

At 09:30 the first 15-minute candle has just closed. The bot checks its shape. It uses the same test for NIFTY and for every stock.

The idea is simple: the candle must move mostly one way. Two checks say this in numbers:

  1. The body must be big. The body must be at least 40% of the candle's range.
  2. The opposing wick must be small. The wick that fights the colour must be at most 30% of the range.

The wick on the winning side may be long. On a green candle, a long lower wick is fine — it shows buyers threw back a dip. Both checks use percentages of the candle's own range. So the same rule works on a 60-point NIFTY candle and on a ₹20 stock candle.

CandleResult
Green, body ≥ 40%, upper wick ≤ 30%BULLISH → BUY day
Red, body ≥ 40%, lower wick ≤ 30%BEARISH → SHORT day
Open equals close (a doji)No direction → no trade
High equals low (flat)Nothing to read → no trade
Anything elseNo signal → no trade

The bot also names the shape in its log, just for you to read:

  • Drawing 1 — full body: both wicks are 5% of the range or less.
  • Drawing 2 — long helpful wick: the wick on the winning side is 25% of the range or more.
  • Drawing 3 — small opposing wick: every other passing candle.

What you see: NIFTY's 09:15 candle opens at 24,480 and closes at 24,536.9. The body is 95% of the range. Both wicks are tiny. Why it matters: this is the cleanest kind of signal (drawing 1). The day becomes a BUY day. The bot does not look at any later candle to decide the direction.

What you see: a red opening candle. It spiked up to 24,731, then fell and closed at 24,656. The lower wick is only 7.4% of the range. Why it matters: on a red candle, the long upper wick is the helpful one — sellers rejected the high. The opposing (lower) wick is small, so this passes. The day becomes a SHORT day.

Why not demand "no wick at all"? On real 15-minute data, a candle with zero wicks almost never happens. A rule that fires twice a year cannot be tested. So the bot uses "body at least 40%, opposing wick at most 30%". You can make both numbers stricter in Settings.

4. Days with no trade

If NIFTY's first candle fails, the bot does nothing for the whole day. No stock scan. No option. This is normal. An earlier offline study (see section 16) found that about half of all days have no signal.

What you see: the first candle has an 81-point range but only an 8-point body (9.9%). Price went up and down and ended near where it started. The rest of the day is choppy too. Why it matters: a small body means neither side won. The body check needs 40%, so the bot stays out all day. On a choppy day like this, staying out is the point.

What you see: a green candle with a 41.7% body, which is enough. But it ran up to 24,268 and closed at 24,235. That upper wick is 45.8% of the range. Why it matters: sellers pushed back hard. The opposing wick limit is 30%, so this candle is refused even though it is green.

A day can also have no trade for other reasons:

  • Stock mode: fewer stocks matched than the "Stocks That Must Confirm" setting (default 1).
  • The pending order never filled by 10:45 (see section 10).
  • A safety limit closed the day early (see section 12).
  • The bot is started after 13:00 — it refuses to start.
  • An optional minimum candle range is set and the candle is smaller. This is off by default.

5. Step 2 — Picking stocks (stock mode)

This step only runs when What To Trade is stock or both.

  1. At 09:30 the bot loads the first candle of every stock in the chosen list. The default list is FNO, about 220 stocks. You can also pick NIFTY50 or NIFTY100.
  2. Each stock gets the same shape test as NIFTY.
  3. A stock counts only if its candle passes and points the same way as NIFTY.
  4. At least 1 stock must match (setting "Stocks That Must Confirm"). Raise it if you want many stocks to agree first.
  5. If more stocks match than "Max Positions At Once" (default 5), the bot keeps the ones with the smallest stop distance first. With a fixed 100 shares, a smaller stop means a smaller rupee loss.

What you see: NIFTY gave a BUY day. This stock printed a clean red first candle. Why it matters: the candle is strong, but it points the wrong way. The bot skips it — "not aligned". It never short-sells on a BUY day or buys on a SHORT day.

6. Step 3 — The 2% gap rule

A stock that opens far from yesterday's close is usually reacting to its own news, not to the market. So any stock whose 09:15 open is more than 2% away from yesterday's close is dropped. This works both ways: a gap up or a gap down.

This check runs before the shape check. A gapped stock is dropped even with a perfect candle. If the bot cannot find yesterday's close, it also drops the stock. "We don't know the gap" is not the same as "there is no gap".

What you see: the stock closes yesterday at 3,120. Today it opens at 3,205, which is 2.72% higher. Its first candle is a nice green one. Why it matters: the open is above the +2% line (3,182.4), so the stock is dropped before its candle is even read. The option leg never uses this rule.

7. Step 4 — Entry, stop and target

All three levels come from the signal candle. They are fixed at 09:30 and never move. The buffer is 5 (₹5 on a stock, 5 points on NIFTY).

BUY day (long)SHORT day (short)
Entrycandle high + 5candle low − 5
Stopcandle low − 5candle high + 5
Riskentry − stopstop − entry
Targetentry + 2 × riskentry − 2 × risk

Nothing is bought at 09:30. The entry is a pending stop order. It fills the first time price trades through the entry level. The fill is recorded at the level, not at the price of the tick that broke it. A real resting stop order would fill there too.

What you see: a BUY day. The stock's first candle has high 2,928 and low 2,903. Entry is 2,933, stop 2,898, risk ₹35, target 2,933 + 70 = 3,003. The 09:45 candle breaks 2,933 and the order fills. Price climbs and touches 3,003 at 12:00. Why it matters: this is a full winning trade. 100 shares × ₹70 = ₹7,000 before charges.

What you see: a SHORT day. The stock's red first candle has low 2,188 and high 2,213. The bot short-sells at 2,183. Then price turns and rallies through the stop at 2,218 at 10:30. Why it matters: this is a normal losing trade. The loss is exactly one risk unit: 100 × ₹35 = ₹3,500. That stock is done for the day — no second try.

8. Step 5 — The big-candle stop

Sometimes the first candle is huge. A stop at the far wick would risk too much, and a 1:2 target would be very far away. So there is one extra rule:

  • If the risk from wick to wick is more than 60 (₹60 on a stock, 60 points on NIFTY), the stop moves from the wick to the edge of the body.
  • BUY: stop = lower of open/close − 5. SHORT: stop = higher of open/close + 5.
  • The target is then worked out from this new, smaller risk.

This rule can only make the stop tighter, never wider. Note that it does not force the risk under 60 — the new risk can still be above 60 if the body itself is big.

What you see: the first candle has a long lower wick, from 4,082 up to 4,172. The wick stop would be 4,077, a ₹100 risk. That is over ₹60, so the stop moves to the body bottom: open 4,130 − 5 = 4,125. Risk drops to ₹52 and the target becomes 4,177 + 104 = 4,281. Why it matters: the trade risks half as much, and the closer target is reached at 12:00. 100 shares × ₹104 = ₹10,400 before charges.

9. The option leg: CE and PE

This runs when What To Trade is option or both. It is simpler than the stock leg:

  1. NIFTY's first candle must pass the shape test. That is the only entry condition. No stock has to agree.
  2. BUY day → buy a NIFTY CE. SHORT day → buy a NIFTY PE.
  3. Entry, stop and target are worked out from NIFTY's candle with the same table as above, including the big-candle rule.
  4. All three levels are NIFTY spot levels. The bot watches the index, not the option price, to decide entry and exit.
  5. When spot breaks the entry level, the bot picks the strike (1 step in the money by default), fetches the live premium and buys 1 lot.
  6. Only one option trade per day. No re-entry.

Why spot levels? An option's price also moves with time decay and volatility. A stop on the premium could not be checked again later from recorded data. Spot levels can.

What you see: NIFTY's first candle (high 24,589, low 24,546) passes as bullish. Spot entry is 24,594, stop 24,541 (risk 53 points), target 24,700. Spot breaks 24,594 at 09:30 and the bot buys a CE at about ₹182. Spot reaches 24,700 at 11:15 and the CE is sold at about ₹243. The lower panel shows the CE premium. Why it matters: the exit is decided by spot, but the profit is on the premium: (243.15 − 182) × 65 ≈ ₹3,975 before charges. Premium numbers here are illustrative.

What you see: a bearish first candle (high 24,709, low 24,660). Spot entry 24,655, stop 24,714, target 24,537. The bot buys a PE at about ₹176. NIFTY drifts lower but never reaches the target. At 13:00 the trade is closed with the PE at about ₹213. Why it matters: this is a time exit. A PE gains when NIFTY falls, so the profit is still (exit − entry) premium × qty ≈ ₹2,396. The bot never holds past 13:00.

10. The entry window and orders that never fill

  • The earliest entry is 09:30, right after the signal candle closes.
  • The latest entry is 10:45. A pending order that has not filled by then is cancelled.
  • A move through the level after 10:45 does not count.

What you see: the pending buy sits at 1,491. From 09:30 to 10:45 the highs reach 1,489.4 at most. At 10:45 the order is cancelled. Price breaks 1,491 later, around 11:15. Why it matters: no trade is taken. The late move is outside the window, so it is ignored. "Never filled" is a normal outcome, not an error.

11. How a trade ends

There are only three ways out. Whichever comes first wins.

ExitWhenPrice recorded
TargetPrice reaches entry ± 2 × riskThe target level
StopPrice reaches the stopThe stop level. In the backtest, if a candle opens past the stop, the open price is used.
13:00 square-offStill open at 13:00The market price at that moment

For the option leg, "price" means NIFTY spot for the decision. The money is booked on the option premium.

What you see: a long from 2,471 with the stop at 2,441. The 11:30 candle closes at 2,441.9, just above the stop. The 11:45 candle opens lower, at 2,438.2. Why it matters: the better price of 2,441 was never available. The backtest books the exit at 2,438.2. Live prices can jump past a stop too; the stop is a level, not a guarantee.

When one candle touches both stop and target. A 15-minute candle cannot tell you which came first. The backtest always assumes the stop came first. Assuming the happy outcome is how backtests lie.

12. Safety limits for the day

The strategy itself only has the per-trade stop. These extra limits sit on top. They count trades from both legs together.

LimitDefaultWhat happens
Max positions at once5No more than 5 stocks open together. (The option leg is one extra trade, once a day.)
Max trades per day5After 5 entries, no new entries.
Stop-outs that end the day3After 3 stop-losses, no new entries. 0 = off.
Max daily loss₹5,000When the day's P&L reaches −₹5,000, no new entries. 0 = off.
Daily profit lock0 (off)When set, stop new entries once the day's profit reaches it.
Max weekly loss0 (off)When set, checked when a session starts. If the week is already down that much, no trading that day.
Capital check—Before each entry the bot checks the shared capital pool. If there is not enough money, that entry is refused and not retried that day.

Daily limits are checked after every exit. When one trips, all pending orders (stock and option) are cancelled. Trades that are already open are not dumped. They still run to their own stop, target or 13:00 exit.

13. What is deliberately not here

The original rules asked for none of these, so the code has none of them:

  • No VIX filter, no open-interest filter.
  • No indicators at all: no RSI, ADX, moving average, ATR or volume filter.
  • No second timeframe and no extra confirmation candle. The pending order itself is the confirmation.
  • No trailing stop and no move to breakeven. The stop is frozen.
  • No partial exits.
  • No re-entry. Each stock gets one try a day. The option leg gets one try a day.
  • No risk-based sizing. Always 100 shares per stock and 1 lot for the option, unless you change the settings.

14. Paper, Live, Backtest and Replay

All four modes use the same rule file, src/strategies/early_bird.js. Only the source of prices changes.

ModePagePricesOrders
Paper/early-bird-paperStocks: a quote poll of the short list every 2 seconds. Option leg: the shared NIFTY spot feed, plus a premium quote at entry and exit.Simulated
Live/early-bird-liveRuns the paper engine and copies each entry and exit as a Fyers order.Real only when all three switches allow it
Backtest/early-bird-backtest15-minute history bars. Option premiums are modelled from the spot move (delta and time decay), not real premiums.Simulated
Replay/replayRecorded ticksSimulated

Live needs three switches

  1. EarlyBird Live Orders must be ON (default OFF).
  2. The global live harness dry-run must be OFF (default ON).
  3. EarlyBird Live DRY-RUN override must be OFF. When ON, it keeps everything simulated.

The safe order is: run paper sessions, compare paper and live dry-run on a recorded day in Replay, and only then think about real orders. The option leg in particular has never been run against a live broker.

The 09:30 stock scan

The bot fetches two things per stock: today's first candle and yesterday's close. About 220 stocks, 4 at a time, limited to 8 requests a second and 180 a minute. It takes about two minutes. If you push the rate higher, Fyers answers "no data", which looks like a stock with no candle. If a scan comes back almost empty, suspect the rate limit first.

Costs

The repo's shared charges file only knows options and futures. Cash-share intraday costs are very different, so the stock leg works out its own costs from the settings below (brokerage, STT on the sell side, exchange fee, SEBI fee, stamp duty on the buy side, GST). The option leg uses the normal options charges. The backtest also applies slippage (default ₹0.05, or 0.05 points on NIFTY) against you on both entry and exit.

16. What we know and do not know

How often a day qualifies

An earlier offline study (August 2026) ran the NIFTY signal test over 2,118 past sessions of cached NIFTY data. It has not been re-run for this edition.

ResultDaysShare
BUY day44721.1%

Of the no-signal days, about three in four failed the body check. So "Minimum Body %" is the setting that changes trade frequency the most.

Trade results: treat with care

The same study had no stock data, so it fed NIFTY in as if it were a stock. On that stand-in: about 81% of setups filled, 19% never filled, and of the filled trades 6.6% hit target, 30.4% hit the stop and 62.9% were closed at 13:00. Average result was about +0.03R — close to zero. These numbers show the machinery works. They are not what this strategy will earn on real stocks.

That study also noted that most trades end on the 13:00 clock, not at target or stop. 13:00 is the rule as written and stays the default. Test any change to it on real stock data first.

Open questions

  • Track record: at the last check this strategy had no live trades. Look at the History page for current paper results.
  • Real stock backtests are needed before trusting any profit number.
  • Option backtests use a modelled premium, not real option prices.
  • Costs for the stock leg are worked out locally and have not been matched to a real contract note.
  • Broker questions only live trading can answer: is an intraday short allowed on every stock, is there enough margin for five positions, does the quote poll keep up.
