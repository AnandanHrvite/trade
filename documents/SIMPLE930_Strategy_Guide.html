NIFTY options · 1-minute option-price charts · Live orders via Zerodha · Plain-English edition

SIMPLE_9:30

Most strategies read the NIFTY chart. This one reads the option price only. At 09:25 the bot picks one call and one put that cost close to ₹180. The first one to trade above ₹180 by 09:35 is bought. One trade a day, at most.

Updated 8 October 2026 · every chart is an example, not a real trade

In one minute
  1. 09:25 — pick. Round NIFTY to the nearest 50 (the ATM strike). Look at in-the-money strikes on both sides. Keep the one CE and the one PE whose price is closest to ₹180. They do not change for the rest of the day.
  2. 09:25–09:35 — buy. The first of the two to trade above ₹180 is bought at once. If neither does by 09:35, no trade today.
  3. Stop. 20 points below the price actually paid. It starts to follow the price (trail) only after the premium touches ₹220.
  4. Shared guards. Once the premium is up 5%, it may not fall back below the price paid (breakeven). Once it is up 8%, a rising floor keeps most of the gain (profit lock).
  5. 09:45 — box check. If the premium has stayed between ₹160 and ₹220 the whole time, sell it, win or lose.
  6. 15:15 — square-off. Anything still open is sold.

1. The idea

An option that costs about ₹180 and is in the money moves almost point-for-point with NIFTY. It is still cheap enough that a strong opening move can push it through ₹180.

So the bot does not guess the direction. It watches one call and one put. Whichever one the market pushes above ₹180 first shows the direction. That one gets bought.

What you see: a 24300 CE on a strong morning. The grey dashed line is the PE the bot was also watching. At 09:29 the CE trades above ₹180 and the bot buys it at ₹180.18. The PE is falling at the same time. The blue dot at 09:44 is where the premium touches ₹220. That breaks the box and arms the trail. The premium peaks at ₹236.73. At 09:57 it falls to the profit-lock floor (₹225.42, which keeps 80% of the gain) and the bot sells at ₹225.28. That is +45.10 premium points. Why it matters: this is the day the strategy is built for. Note that the shared profit lock, not the 20-point trail, ended it.

3. Step 1 — 09:25, the pick

  1. At 09:25 the bot reads NIFTY once and rounds it to the ATM strike.
  2. It asks for prices on the ATM strike and 8 in-the-money strikes on each side.
  3. On each side it keeps the one strike whose price is closest to ₹180.
  4. That pair (one CE, one PE) is the day's watchlist. It never changes.

  • A missing price is reported, not guessed. A strike with no price is listed as missing. It is never treated as ₹0.
  • Ties go to the strike nearer the money, then to the lower strike.

4. Step 2 — the ₹180 break

From 09:25 to 09:35 the bot checks both prices about once a second. The first one to trade above ₹180 is bought at market. Exactly ₹180 is not enough.

Before buying, the bot asks for a fresh price. If that fresh price is back at ₹180 or below, it does not buy and keeps watching. It also refuses a price older than 15 seconds.

What you see: this time NIFTY falls. The 24500 PE (candles) climbs while the CE (grey dashed line) drops to about ₹173. At 09:31 the PE trades above ₹180 and the bot buys it at ₹180.10. It peaks at ₹200.30 and the profit lock sells it at ₹197.03 at 09:43. That is +16.93 points. Why it matters: the bot does not choose the direction. The market does, by pushing one leg through ₹180 first.

What you see: a jumpy open. On the very first reading at 09:25 both legs are above ₹180: the CE at ₹181.50 and the PE (grey dashed) at ₹180.80. The bot buys the one further above the line, the CE, at ₹181.50. Its stop is ₹161.50. The premium rises to ₹197.13 and the profit lock sells at ₹194.62 at 09:39, +13.12 points. Why it matters: when both legs qualify, the higher price wins. A fixed "always CE first" rule would tilt every such day to one side.

5. Step 3 — the stop and the trail

  • The stop starts 20 points below the price actually paid. Bought at ₹180.07 → stop ₹160.07. A worse fill does not get a wider stop.
  • The stop does not follow the price at first. It waits until the premium touches the top of the box, ₹220.
  • After that it trails 20 points behind the best price. It only moves up.

Why wait for ₹220? A trail that starts at once turns the 20-point stop into a tiny one after a few rupees of noise. Waiting means the trail only protects a real move.

What you see: the CE is bought at ₹180.07 at 09:28. Stop: ₹160.07 (red line). The premium rises only to ₹186.90. That is +3.8%, short of the +5% that would arm the breakeven stop. Then it falls. At 09:44 it touches ₹160.01 and the stop sells it. Loss: 20.06 points. Why it matters: this is the normal losing trade. The loss is fixed at about 20 points.

What you see: a very strong run. Bought at ₹180.22. At 09:40 the premium touches ₹220 (blue dot) and the red stop jumps from ₹160.22 to ₹200 and starts following the price. The premium peaks at ₹265.03, so the trail sits at ₹245.03. The pink profit-lock floor is lower, at ₹239.59 (above a +40% gain it keeps 70%). At 10:06 the premium falls to ₹244.91, hits the trail first, and the bot sells. +64.69 points. Why it matters: on very big runs, 20 points behind the peak is tighter than the profit lock, so the trail decides.

6. Step 4 — breakeven and profit lock

These two rules are shared by every strategy in the app. They act on the option price, on every check, before this strategy's own stop and box rules. Both are ON by default.

RuleArms when the premium is upThen the bot sells ifBought at ₹180
Breakeven stop5%the price comes back to what was paidarms at ₹189, sells at ₹180
Profit lock8%the price falls to the floor. The floor keeps 85% of the gain below +20%, 80% up to +40%, then 70%, and is never below +5%.arms at ₹194.40; peak ₹200 → floor ₹197
What this means for SIMPLE_9:30 The strategy's own rules say: stop 20 points down, trail only after ₹220. But the shared rules arm much earlier, at about ₹189 and ₹194. So in practice most winning trades are closed by the profit lock, not by the trail. The trail only takes over on very big runs (see the chart above).

What you see: bought at ₹180.09 at 09:28. The premium reaches ₹192.57, which is +6.9%. That arms the breakeven stop (pink line at the price paid). It stays short of ₹194.50, so the profit lock never arms. The premium slides back and at 09:43 touches ₹179.93. The bot sells, about flat (−0.16). Without this rule the trade would have stayed open until the 09:45 box check. Why it matters: a trade that was clearly winning is not allowed to become a loss.

What you see: bought at ₹180.12. The premium climbs to ₹202.71 (+12.5%), then turns. The profit-lock floor keeps 85% of that gain: ₹199.32. At 09:40 the premium touches ₹199.22 and the bot sells. +19.10 points. It never reached ₹220, so the strategy's own trail never started. Why it matters: without the shared lock, this trade would have run to the 09:45 box check and been sold wherever the price was then.

7. Step 5 — the 09:45 box

At 09:45 the bot asks one question about an open trade: has the premium been outside ₹160–₹220 at any moment since the buy? Touching ₹220 or ₹160 counts as outside.

  • Yes → the trade is moving. It is left alone. The stop, the trail and the shared guards look after it until 15:15.
  • No → the trade went nowhere. It is sold at market, win or lose.

What you see: bought at ₹180.06 at 09:28. For 17 minutes the premium stays between ₹176.99 and ₹188.17, well inside the box. It never got 5% up, so no shared guard armed. At 09:45 the bot sells at ₹182.40: +2.34 points, which costs money once charges are paid. Why it matters: a move that has not happened in 15 minutes probably will not. Selling early saves time decay and frees the day.

In the first chart (Section 1) the premium touched ₹220 at 09:44, one minute before the check. So the 09:45 rule did not apply to it.

About the ₹160 edge With a 20-point stop the trade is already sold at about ₹160 before the box floor matters. The ₹160 edge only becomes useful if you widen the stop (SIMPLE930_SL_PTS). It is still built in, because it is part of the rule.

8. Step 6 — 15:15 square-off

What you see: the last 45 minutes of a rare all-day trend. The CE was bought at ₹180.18 at 09:27 and touched ₹220 at 09:44. All day the premium kept making new highs without falling back to the trail (red) or the profit-lock floor (pink). At 15:15 the bot sells at ₹327.65, about +147 points. Why it matters: however well a trade is going, it is closed at 15:15. Nothing is held overnight.

9. Days with no trade

Neither leg reaches ₹180 by 09:35

This will be the most common day. At 09:36 the bot closes the day and writes the reason, and how close each leg got, to the skip log.

What you see: inside the yellow window (09:25–09:35) the CE gets to ₹179.09 and the PE to ₹177.17. Neither is above ₹180, so there is no trade. At 09:39 the CE does trade above ₹180 (red dot), but the window has shut. Why it matters: this is the rule working. A move that has not shown itself by 09:35 is treated as a different move. If you want those trades, the setting is SIMPLE930_ENTRY_END — change it only on evidence.

One trade a day

What you see: the CE is bought at ₹180.12 at 09:27 and drops fast. At 09:32 it hits the stop at ₹159.94. One minute later the PE (grey dashed) trades above ₹180 (red dot). It is still inside the entry window, but today's one trade is used. No second trade. Why it matters: the rule is one shot a day. A loss is not chased with the other side.

Other reasons the bot does not buy

  • The fresh price just before buying is back at ₹180 or lower.
  • The latest price is older than 15 seconds (stale data).
  • The stop cannot be worked out (it would be at or below ₹0).
  • The shared money pool cannot pay for the option.
  • Today is a weekend or an NSE holiday.
  • No usable strike was found on either side at 09:25.

There is no VIX filter, open-interest filter or indicator check in this strategy, on purpose.

10. The money, with numbers

One NIFTY lot is 65 units by default (NIFTY_LOT_SIZE), so one premium point on one lot is ₹65. Charges for a round trip are roughly ₹80 on one lot. The backtest also charges 1.5 points of slippage on each side.

The shape is simple: lose about ₹1,300 plus charges when wrong, lose only charges when nothing happens, and keep most of the move when right. The strategy needs its winners to average more than its 20-point losers. Nobody has measured that yet.

13. Honest notes & limits

  • No real trades yet. All numbers here come from made-up prices run through the real rule code.
  • ₹180, 09:35 and 09:45 are the operator's choices, not values fitted to data.
  • The shared guards change the strategy a lot. They close most winners well before ₹220. Judge results with that in mind.
  • Backtest and Paper differ. The backtest skips the shared guards, checks once per minute, and fills a stop at exactly the stop price. Paper checks about once a second.
  • On a fresh weekly expiry the whole ITM ladder can cost more than ₹180. The bot then watches the closest thing it has. SIMPLE930_MAX_PREMIUM_DIST exists for that day but is off.
